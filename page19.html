Troy Parrott is daring to dream of playing in a home European Championship after helping lift the Republic of Ireland to a 3-0 Nations League victory over 10-man Kosovo.

The Real Betis striker contributed two penalties on Tuesday night at the Aviva Stadium in Dublin, where Jack Moylan added a late cushion to ensure the Republic secured a coveted seeding spot in pot two for next month’s Euro 2028 draw.

Finishing runners-up in group B3 also assured them of a place in the Nations League League A/B playoffs, with one of Denmark, Wales, Croatia or Serbia awaiting them in March.

“I’ve been thinking about it since I was a kid, I think everyone has,” Parrott, asked about the 2028 championship, told RTÉ.

“Look, it’s not a guarantee that we are going to qualify for that tournament, so I think tonight was very important to get into pot two so that we have a better draw.

“We’ll be going into the qualifiers now trying to make sure that we qualify for the tournament.

“A tournament that’s one of the host nations being Ireland, without Ireland it would be heartbreaking for all of us, so I think it’s a mission now to make sure that we’re there.”

The 24-year-old was delighted to come out on top after what he branded a series of “nearly games”, including a 2-2 draw with group winners Austria at the Aviva Stadium last month and Saturday’s 1-0 loss in Linz.

The Republic were gifted an advantage in the 26th minute on Tuesday night, when Kreshnik Hajrizi was sent off for handling the ball off the line.

Parrott, who added his second penalty five minutes after the restart before Moylan put the game away with 15 minutes remaining, said: “It was disappointing that we didn’t win the group because that’s what we all wanted to do at the start.

“There’s been so many nearly matches where we’ve nearly won, we’ve played really well and haven’t won, and I think tonight we went out there with the mindset that no matter what we have to win.

“Obviously the red card helped us, but I have no doubt in my mind that group of players who were out on that pitch would have won that game.”

Republic boss Heimir Hallgrimsson brushed aside suggestions that his players should fear a Nations League promotion, insisting they all want to go up.

He said: “Everybody, and especially us, likes to play the top teams. To compare yourself with the best teams, that’s one thing.

“Second is those teams will expose your weaknesses, and that helps you in your growth, to play these teams, so we can continue our growth.

“We’re already looking forward to March. Tough teams in the pot. Whoever we draw is going to be a tough two games, and it’s good for this team at this time to play these matches instead of friendlies.

“We’re really excited for March, and looking forward to meeting the players again.”

FAA suspends installation of router that led to a ground stop of flights across the US

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The router malfunctioned and flooded other routers with data, prompting the FAA to order the ground stop.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has suspended the installation of a router model made by Hewlett-Packard Enterprise-owned Juniper Networks, which was part of its modernization efforts. As The New York Times reports, the agency believes the router played a major role in the issue that caused it to order a ground stop for all flights in the US on November 13. In addition to suspending new installations, it has also disabled more than 60 routers that had already been installed.

If you'll recall, Transport Secretary Sean Duffy blamed the ground stop on old hardware the first time he spoke about the event. But Duffy revealed a day later that the issue was connected to the modernization of America's aviation facilities.

He said a contractor from tech company L3Harris was working on switching telecommunication lines from old copper wires to fiber and on installing the new router at an FAA site in El Dorado, Arkansas. That router started flooding other routers in the air traffic control system with data. Another router in Amarillo, Texas had also gone haywire, and the FAA responded by disabling both of them.

The agency said the router was tested for a year before installations started. "Sixty-eight times it worked well," he sad at a news conference. "This one router started to broadcast into our network, and it started to overload the network." In aviation, routers are used to transmit data, including radar feeds, flight plans and weather information.

Despite knowing that it was the router that caused the issue, FAA administrator Bryan Bedford said they still don't know what triggered it. The Senate Commerce, Science and Transportation Committee's Democratic members are urging the agency to find out why it had malfunctioned. They also asked the FAA to report to Congress on how it's going to prevent the same thing from happening in the future.

Duffy said he was told by national security agencies that the issue wasn't caused by a cyberattack, but he still asked the FBI to be part of the team analyzing the router from Arkansas. The Transport Secretary has also renewed his calls for Congress to approve an additional $50 billion in funding to modernize America's air traffic control system. "I would just tell you, the longer we stay on an old rickety network, the less safe it becomes," he said. "We do have to get this work done. Otherwise, staying on the old system for longer is going to mean more risk as opposed to fixing the system, and you're going to reduce the risk."

While conventional wisdom might claim that slow-burn horror is always more critically acclaimed than hardcore scares, one Netflix masterpiece with a flawless critical rating proves this isn’t strictly true. It can sometimes seem that, despite horror gaining a critical foothold in recent years, certain entries into the genre are still taken a lot more seriously by reviewers than others.

To be fair, this is partially a reasonable response. The Scream spinoff Scream: The Series was one of the franchise’s most divisive entries for years before 2026’s risible Scream 7, but this was mainly because the show abandoned the self-aware meta humor that made the franchise famous in the first place. Similarly, the fact that Peacock’s Friday the 13th prequel, Crystal Lake, received drastically better reviews than the rest of the franchise is most likely because the spinoff is simply far better than those earlier slasher movies.

That said, it is not unreasonable to note that horror stories which incorporate a metaphor about the protagonist’s trauma, no matter how tacked-on or perfunctory, often receive more credit than genre entries that simply revel in scaring their audience. Similarly, if Mike Flanagan’s successful Carrie reboot proved anything, it was that sympathetic, slow-burn horror typically fares better than jump-scare-fuelled shockfests. At least, until 2019’s Netflix hit Marianne is taken into consideration.

Co-written by future Cobweb creator Samuel Bodin alongside Quoc Dang Tran, Marianne sees Victoire Du Bois’s conflicted horror author Emma return to her small hometown to find out the truth behind the mysterious death of her childhood friend, Caroline. Soon, Emma realizes to her horror that the characters in her horror novels are seemingly starting to manifest in the real world, including a particularly odious evil witch.

This setup will likely sound familiar to anyone who has seen director John Carpenter’s criminally underrated In the Mouth of Madness, a riff on Lovecraft where Sam Neill’s troubled horror author gets trapped in his own fictional world. However, that classic movie, much like many screen adaptations of Lovecraft’s work, starts out slowly and only reveals the extent of its horror as In the Mouth of Madness reaches its wild finale.

In contrast, Marianne takes a huge risk that massively pays off when the show’s pilot features a slew of terrifying jump scares and gruesome scenes. Right from the off, Marianne makes it perfectly clear that, despite the popularity of movies like The Witch and Get Out at the time of the show’s release, this is no subtle, slow-burn horror story at all.

This seems like a terrible choice, since it opens Marianne up to accusations of focusing more on jump scares than character development and atmosphere. Ever since the classic 1990 It miniseries and Twin Peaks, most critically adored horror shows are patient slow burns like Mike Flanagan's Netflix shows The Haunting of Hill House and Midnight Mass, The Walking Dead franchise’s outings, or The Terror.

Occasionally, viewers will be blessed with a horror show that goes full-throttle from start to finish, like the infamously absurd American Horror Story, certain episodes of anthologies like Masters of Horror or Tales from the Crypt, or, in recent years, streaming successes like Brand New Cherry Flavor. However, these are few and far between, and much more likely to face criticism for starting strong but gradually losing steam or sense.

In contrast, Marianne’s decision to pair a fast and furious start with a slower, more atmospheric middle and a terrifying ending blends both approaches perfectly. It’s a pacing approach that is unique to this Netflix masterpiece, and one that means the 100% Rotten Tomatoes critical rating success of Marianne has rarely been matched in the seven years since the show debuted.

Published Nov 18, 2026 4:00 AM EST

An isolated  group of primates calls the rugged, sandstone Ennedi plateau in northern Chad, home. In this region in the middle of the Sahara Desert about the size of Switzerland, the olive baboons (Papio anubis) have lived a separate life away from other primate species. At 40 to 60 pounds, some olive baboons are about the size of our early hominin ancestors. But their size does not keep them safe from hungry Verreaux’s eagles (Aquila verreauxii).

New research published today in the American Journal of Biological Anthropology details how these eagles appear to be preying upon these nearly human-sized baboons. The discovery is reshaping how scientists think about predator-prey interactions during human evolution.

Our ancestors were vulnerable to several predators, including crocodiles, big cats, and even eagles. However, scientists believed that our species (Homo sapiens) and our hominin ancestors have been escaping these predators for roughly the past 2.5 million years. No primate over 28.6 pounds (13 kilograms) has been confirmed to be killed by raptors, so our body sizes appeared to be too heavy for even the large eagles with a 71- to 86-inch-long wingspan to pick up.

In this study, the team describes skulls and bones of adult male olive baboons that bear the signature marks of eagle predation. There were talon punctures to the skulls, and various scrapes and “rakes” on the shoulder bones.

“We saw the eagles while we were searching for the baboons, but we never imagined that they might be a threat to them,” Catherine Hobaiter, a study co-author and primatologist at the University of St. Andrews in Scotland, said in a statement. “Then we found the first skull with a puncture mark, and then another, and another.”

With these remains, the team was able to calculate how much the deceased baboons weighed. One individual was over 57.3 pounds (26 kilograms), well within the range of a hominin species.

“Having worked in this region for about three years, I had the opportunity to visit these gueltas (pockets of water that form within rock formations) and often observed Verreaux’s eagles. I had never imagined that these predators would target primates of this size until the research team stumbled upon evidence,” added Dr. Jean Didier Akpona from the African Parks Network. “This discovery leads us to explore further the mystery that surrounds the gueltas of the Sahara Desert.”

To defend against eagles, our primate ancestors would “mob” or charge the attacker to try to drive them away. The modern primates living in places with eagle predators often move in large groups mixed with other primate species. This also mirrors the evolutionary history of our early ancestors, who lived alongside other hominin species and other primates. While some conflict arose, plenty of cooperative advantages to sticking together also happened—like mobbing eagles.

The team hopes to learn more about the lives of the Ennedi baboons to see what other evolutionary secrets they may be holding.

“Baboons are some of the best studied monkeys in Africa, but our very first observations from Ennedi upended all sorts of notions about what these baboons should be doing—from their diet to their social lives, I can’t wait to see what new ‘rules’ they’ll break next!” Hobaiter concluded.

Accelerating development for massive Git repositories

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Android’s source code is big – the Android 17 open source release has 1,084 Git repositories, 1.62 million files, and takes 300 GB for a built workspace. Additionally, in a typical Android source tree with hardware vendor code and first-party applications, the size increases by 2-3x. Traditionally, it took over 30 minutes to fetch Android Open Source Project (AOSP) source code and well over an hour to build on even highly capable developer workstations. As software development evolved over the last 2 years, modern coding harnesses and LLMs have accelerated the development of many projects. These tools are used today for Android operating system development, but the long sync and build times make them less efficient. Both human developers and agents need quick setup, parallel workspaces, and fast build-and-test iteration times.

We built Another Build File Service (ABFS) to address these bottlenecks. ABFS is a full suite of software across a Linux kernel filesystem module, user-space daemons, and server-side infrastructure. ABFS uses content-addressability across the entire stack to lazy-load and cache source code, developer workspaces, intermediate build files, and build results.

Today, we are excited to announce that ABFS is open source on GitHub.The impact of ABFS to Android is:

ABFS already powers parallel workflows for developers and agents in Android, but it is designed to scale to other massive Git-based projects.

In the Android build system, developers define build rules in Blueprint files (Android.bp), Soong compiles them into Ninja files, and Ninja executes the final build graph (see the Android build overview). Similar to the Make build tool, Ninja is timestamp-based. If a build step’s input file is newer than the existing output file, Ninja rebuilds the output file.

Analyzing the AOSP code for Android 17 shows the sheer disk footprint and file count involved in a single checkout and build.

In a typical Android tree that includes hardware-specific repositories, board support packages, first-party applications, form-factor-specific code, etc., the code size is 2–3 times the size of AOSP (over 3,000 Git repositories), compounded across multiple active SKU and legacy maintenance branches.

Building the Cuttlefish emulator target executes a build graph with 192,249 Ninja actions. While local incremental builds are fast once a tree is already built, every new workspace, branch switch, or ephemeral agent session starts cold.Even though typical developer changes touch only 1-10 different Git projects, someone building locally without any remote execution might waste up to 80 minutes on a 64-core machine repeating work their CI system already built. Even if a developer edits a core library like system/libbase, 97% of the build graph is identical to the CI build.

Sharing cached build outputs across CI and local machines is also tricky in a codebase of Android's age and rate of change. Build rules occasionally introduce hidden dependencies (such as passing an undeclared schema file inside raw compiler flags), over-declared inputs, or undeclared outputs. In timestamp-based builds, hidden dependencies silently produce stale incremental artifacts, forcing developers to wipe output directories and run clean builds.

ABFS makes building Android fast by replacing massive local source checkouts with a virtual, content-addressable filesystem (CASFS). ABFS streams files on demand only when a build actually touches them and utilizes shared build caching to help eliminate redundant downloads and compilation.

On the server side, Git-pushers continuously ingest new Git objects from Gerrit or other Git hosts into a horizontally scalable gRPC ABFS Content-Addressable Storage (CAS) Server that indexes Git content, workspace snapshots, and build step results. On the developer host, a local Least Recently Used (LRU) Cacheman daemon, Mount Daemon, and Tap utility connect user-space build tools to the kernel filesystem so checkouts and builds stream only what they need. See the full ABFS architecture on GitHub.

At the core of the client is a virtual filesystem called Content-Addressable Storage Filesystem (CASFS), a Linux kernel module that exposes Git SHA content hashes via extended attributes. When a file's hash is set, CASFS populates its metadata immediately and downloads file bytes via Cacheman only when needed:

Initial checkouts download only lightweight directory metadata prepopulated with Git hashes. CASFS uses Git's object model natively, allowing standard git and repo operations to work transparently.

CASFS can also tap build processes. Tapping records every file actually read, written, or deleted into lists called patches, which are stored alongside cached build outputs. Ninja-go replaces timestamp checks with two-phase content-addressable caching:

Ninja-go accelerates Ninja-based builds like Android and Chrome; other build systems can integrate with ABFS's caching APIs using the same model (see the two-phase caching walkthrough on GitHub).

Each mounted checkout attaches to a persistent workspace that eagerly syncs modified files, git state, and repo state through Cacheman to the ABFS CAS Server—keeping mounts synchronized across sessions or across multiple developer hosts.

8 Biggest Snubs in the 2026 Game Awards Nominations

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Game Awards has announced its official list of nominees for this year’s prizes. Among the nominees are plenty of obvious contenders, such as Grand Theft Auto 6, Resident Evil Requiem, and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. There was stiff competition across several categories, meaning that some worthy games — and Polygon favorites — didn’t make the shortlist for certain prizes. These are the biggest TGA nomination snubs of 2026.

As one eagle-eyed Resetera user pointed out, Sony just broke a streak that dates all the way back to 2015. This marks the first time in 11 years that a first-party PlayStation release hasn't received a Game of the Year nomination. During that 11-year streak, Sony took home three GOTY trophies: God of War (2018), The Last of Us Part 2 (2020), and Astro Bot (2024). This year's big first-party game from PlayStation was Marvel's Wolverine, which is currently sitting at a 75 on Metacritic and incited a whole lot of debate about difficulty and UI design. The game was nominated in two categories: Best Performance for Liam McIntyre and Innovation in Accessibility.

There were a ton of fantastic video game performances this year, and somebody’s favorite was bound to get left out. Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave fan favorite Jimmie Yamaguchi gave an outstanding performance as the oddball scholar Fabio, and by every measure, it deserved a nomination. In less capable hands, Fabio’s starry-eyed devotion to Fortune’s Weave co-protagonist Dietrich could have been played for laughs, but Yamaguchi makes the budding romance between these men believable and heartfelt.

Manni L. Perez was nominated for GTA 6, presumably for portraying Lucia Caminos. (Funnily enough, developer Rockstar Games has not yet officially confirmed Perez is actually playing Lucia, despite longstanding online rumors.) Apparently, the actor who played co-lead Jason Duval didn’t make enough of an impact with players to score a nomination.

Blizzard’s World of Warcraft has had a massive year, with the launch of its reimagined Classic experience, World of Warcraft: Forever. The team also released major updates for the modern version of the beloved MMORPG, like March’s Midnight expansion. Those changes weren’t enough to move the needle with voters, and WoW was also left out of the Best Community Support category.

The popular farming sim, which entered early access in 2024 before its full release this year, may have more than 26,000 “overwhelmingly positive” reviews on Steam, but that wasn’t enough for it to snag a nomination in any category. Best Debut Indie might have been Fields of Mistria’s best shot at a prize, but it wouldn’t have been out of place in Best Sim/Strategy or the broader Best Indie category, either.

Despite a wobbly third act, Fortune’s Weave is one of our favorite games of 2026, one of the best games available for the Nintendo Switch 2, and one of the best-reviewed games of the year. Surprisingly, developer Intelligent Systems’ epic strategy RPG only managed to pick up one nomination, for Best Role-Playing Game. It stands a decent chance of taking this one home: other nominees include Final Fantasy Resonance, Monster Hunter Stories 3, The Blood of Dawnwalker, and Trails in the Sky 2nd Chapter.

Pearl Abyss’s open-world adventure game was an early-year frontrunner for a GOTY nom, even after middling reviews and a rocky launch. While it wasn’t a critical darling, it did have a pretty massive online following that made the prospect of several nominations seem likely. Surprisingly, Crimson Desert will compete in just one category: Best Community Support.

Bungie’s extraction shooter managed to snag a nomination for Best Multiplayer Game, which it frankly stands little chance of winning against a field that includes two indie chart-toppers and a well-regarded Call of Duty. However, the stylish game was overlooked in the Best Art Direction category, perhaps partly due to the shadow cast by a 2025 plagiarism scandal where Bungie admitted to using an artist’s designs without proper credit. (Bungie later credited her as a Visual Design Consultant.) Marathon’s retro-future meets Y2K cyberpunk aesthetic truly stands out in a sea of Unreal Engine 5 homogeneity, and it’s a shame to see it overlooked here.

Both the Best Indie and Best Debut Indie categories have some stiff competition this year. However, no independent game managed to get a nomination for Game of the Year in 2026. While there weren’t a ton of indie contenders for the top category this year, Polygon’s Oli Welsh had the co-op adventure Big Walk pegged as a dark horse, though developer House House’s recent AI controversy likely didn’t help matters.

The lack of an indie GOTY nominee represents a pretty massive shift from the 2025 crop, which included a record-setting three games developed by independent studios: Hades 2, Hollow Knight: Silksong, and Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. Sandfall Interactive’s much-loved RPG managed to take home the top prize and a total of nine Game Awards, the most wins by a single game. 2024 also saw one indie crack the category, Balatro, though the top honors went to Astro Bot.

Sean Connery Wasn’t the First James Bond: Meet Barry Nelson in the ‘Casino Royale’ You’ve Never Seen

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Ever since George Lazenby first replaced Sean Connery as James Bond in 1969’s “On Her Majesty’s Secret Service,” fans have argued over who makes the best 007 — and those arguments are likely to resume once Robert Pattinson takes the screen as the latest Bond in director Denis Villeneuve’s new iteration. Although Craig has his partisans, Connery tends to be the consensus choice for the best Bond, though I’ve argued here for the merits of 1970s and ’80s Roger Moore. All the Bonds have their virtues, but Connery benefited from being there first — for anyone who discovered the series in its initial heyday, Connery simply is James Bond. Yet Connery actually wasn’t the first actor to play Bond. That honor goes to journeyman MGM contract player Barry Nelson, who played Bond in a “Casino Royale” adaptation eight years before Connery made the character famous in “Dr. No.” Connery may have been the first 007 to grace the big screen, but Nelson established Bond on the small screen in an episode of the CBS anthology series “Climax!” opposite Peter Lorre in the Le Chiffre role that would later be inhabited by Orson Welles (in the goofy 1967 “Casino Royale”) and Mads Mikkelsen (in the 2006 adaptation that introduced Craig to the series).

The 1954 “Casino Royale” is a fascinating artifact that includes elements that would go on to characterize future James Bond movies, while also going in a few other very different directions. The key difference is in the scale, stripped down by necessity for television production. The hour-long “Climax!” episode is all interiors, taking place entirely in the hotel and casino of the title. It’s more a James Bond play than a movie, with Bond and Le Chiffre facing off in a series of verbal — and ultimately physical — jousts in confined spaces, and it’s surprisingly effective.

It’s also a very strange beginning for a franchise that would come to be known for its globetrotting locales and elaborate action set pieces. Not that this “Casino Royale” was officially part of the James Bond franchise as we know it. In fact, although there have been three adaptations of Ian Fleming’s first James Bond novel, only the third incarnation with Daniel Craig was from EON Productions, the company that controlled the character from “Dr. No” through the Craig era before selling the rights to Amazon.

The 1954 “Casino Royale” came about when producer Gregory Ratoff optioned the rights to Fleming’s novel in May of that year. The novel wasn’t yet a hit, and Fleming was sorely in need of cash, which led him to make one of the worst deals in movie-rights history when he sold the book outright to Ratoff for $6,000 after the TV episode aired. This left “Casino Royale” out of the deal when EON secured the screen rights to James Bond as a character, so Bond producers Albert Broccoli and Harry Saltzman could only sit back and grit their teeth when Ratoff turned “Casino Royale” into a comedy in 1967 and cast Peter Sellers, David Niven, Woody Allen and others as rotating Bonds.

EON ultimately made a deal to bring “Casino Royale” under their umbrella and used the novel to reboot the franchise with Craig, but in 1954 it served as the source for a much more modest affair. The biggest similarities between the 1954 “Climax!” episode and the Daniel Craig movie come in the time spent on extended gambling sequences, and the centering of a brutal torture scene. Given censorship restrictions of the time, Nelson’s torture in a bathtub is considerably less graphic than the mallet to the testicles inflicted on Craig, but it’s rough enough stuff for a 1950s TV production.

Certain aspects of Nelson’s characterization, like Bond’s mix of sexual swagger and icy indifference, would later be adopted by most future 007s, to varying degrees. The biggest difference between Nelson and future Bonds is a simple one: He’s American. Although Broccoli and Saltzman flirted with the idea of an American Bond throughout the series’ history (going so far as to cast James Brolin in “Octopussy” before Moore decided to return at the last minute), to date they’ve never actually pulled the trigger on casting outside the UK.

Nelson is probably best known to modern audiences as the affable hotel manager Stuart Ullman in Stanley Kubrick’s “The Shining,” but in the 1950s he was a sturdy character actor and occasional leading man who bounced around television after an initial sojourn as a studio contract player. As a young man, he appeared in several important MGM films, including “Shadow of the Thin Man,” “Bataan,” and “A Guy Named Joe” (the source material for Steven Spielberg’s “Always”). Later on, he popped up in “Airport” and had a successful Broadway career. He was nominated for a Tony acting opposite Liza Minnelli in Martin Scorsese’s 1977 production “The Act.”

In 1954, though, he was in the middle of a prolific and productive era as a TV stalwart, both as a series regular (on “The Hunter” and “My Favorite Husband”) and a frequent guest star. He doesn’t exhibit a ton of range as American “Combined Intelligence” agent “Jimmy” Bond, but that too is in keeping with the overall conception of the character, at least until Craig tried to bring a little more emotional depth to the role.

In Americanizing Bond, the “Climax!” edition of “Casino Royale” ends up making a number of related changes that are small but make the film feel just a bit off for fans of the EON productions. American Felix Leiter (later played by Jeffrey Wright) becomes Brit Clarence Leiter, for example. Although there’s none of the Bond gadgetry that would become famous later, there are hints of what’s to come in the form of props like a villain’s cane that hides a gun, and the opening credits for the “Climax!” series itself feature a gun barrel pointed at the viewer that predicts the iconic graphics of later Bond title sequences.

The 1954 “Casino Royale” is noteworthy for another reason: its composer, Jerry Goldsmith, who would ultimately score hundreds of major films, including “Planet of the Apes,” “Chinatown,” “Basic Instinct,” and most of Joe Dante’s work. Yet in some ways what’s fascinating about “Casino Royale” is how unremarkable it is — it’s a decent, solid piece of entertainment from an era when proliferating anthology shows provided similar programs every week.

If Fleming had his way, it would have only been the start for 007 on television; he wrote several outlines for episodes of a James Bond series before CBS canceled the project. Luckily, he repurposed most of what he wrote as short stories for his 1960 book “For Your Eyes Only,” and television’s loss was cinema’s gain. By 1962, James Bond belonged to the movies — and to Sean Connery.

The order does not merely prohibit threats or harassment. A judicial order "forbidding certain communications when issued in advance of the time that such communications are to occur" is a prior restraint…. "Any system of prior restraints on communication bears a heavy presumption against its constitutional validity." Such restraints are unconstitutional "except in exceptional circumstances." .As the Supreme Court held in Organization for a Better Austin v. The remedy for false or injurious statements lies in "actions under libel laws providing for redress and punishment, and not in proceedings to restrain the publication." [But t]he October 12 Order … does not impose consequences for particular statements adjudicated unlawful. It prohibits Mr. Hughes from speaking about entire categories of people in the future. That is a prior restraint in its most direct form which is presumed unconstitutional….

Test Masters Educ. Servs., Inc. v. Singh (5th Cir. 2005) … recognized that a court may enjoin "harassing communication" and "repeated invasions of privacy." But an injunction prohibiting all communication, rather than limiting itself to "threatening or harassing conduct," "swept too broadly" and constituted an unconstitutional restraint. Id.…

Plaintiffs cannot justify the restraint merely by characterizing Mr. Hughes's commentary as malicious, coercive, or injurious. The Supreme Court rejected that reasoning in Keefe. There, the Court held that an individual's "interest in being free from public criticism of his business practices" did not justify an injunction against speech…. Plaintiffs may have remedies for unlawful conduct. But those remedies do not include an order forbidding Mr. Hughes from speaking about them at all….

The absence of notice presents a separate constitutional defect. In Carroll v. President & Commissioners of Princess Anne (1968), the Supreme Court set aside a short ex parte order restraining expression because of "a basic infirmity in the procedure by which it was obtained." The order "was issued ex parte, without notice to petitioners and without any effort, however informal, to invite or permit their participation in the proceedings."

There is a place in our jurisprudence for ex parte issuance, without notice, of temporary restraining orders of short duration; There is no place within the area of basic freedoms guaranteed by the First Amendment for such orders where no showing is made that it is impossible to serve or to notify the opposing parties and to give them an opportunity to participate.

The Court also rejected the suggestion that a later hearing could cure the problem. The availability of a subsequent opportunity to be heard "does not overcome the infirmity in the absence of a showing of justification for the ex parte nature of the proceedings."

The reason is straightforward. "The value of judicial decision-making … is substantially diluted where the process is ex parte, because the Court does not have available the fundamental instrument for judicial judgment: "an adversary proceeding in which both parties may participate." And where First Amendment freedoms are at stake, any order must be "couched in the narrowest terms that will accomplish the pin-pointed objective."

The October 12 Order fails both principles. It was entered without notice or an adversary hearing. It contains no finding that notifying Mr. Hughes was impossible. And far from identifying a narrow category of unlawful speech, it prohibits virtually any online commentary concerning the Plaintiffs, their families, or their counsel.

Carroll is directly controlling on the constitutional defect. An ex parte order restraining expression cannot stand without a showing that notice and participation were impossible. No such showing appears here….

Instead of wading through decades of defense bureaucracy and drawn-out congressional appropriations, USAFA secured its next-generation aerobatic aircraft in just 10 months, cutting through red tape with the help of a private donor group led by Andrew McKenna.

The T-19A is a derivative of the commercial GB1 GameBird. By skipping the traditional procurement, leadership bypassed the budget overruns and timeline slips that have paralyzed programs like the Boeing T-7A Red Hawk. The lightweight plane is purpose-built for agility thanks to its designer, German aerobatic champion Philipp Steinbach. He is a co-founder of Game Aerospace Composites in Bentonville, Arkansas, which produces the off-the-shelf civilian variant certified to pull a staggering load of ten G-forces.

The Kestrel went from concept to delivery in 10 months using $3 million in private donations, whereas the Red Hawk has spent over a decade in development using billions in taxpayer funds and still faces multi-year delays. The T-19A, or GB1 GameBird, is the only American-built two-seat aircraft approved for Unlimited-category competition aerobatics. Its all-carbon monocoque airframe lacks traditional mechanical wing-attach joints, relying on a continuous composite spar cured inside an autoclave. This gives the plane an operating empty weight of roughly 1,290 pounds (585 kg) while enabling symmetric flight loads of up to 10-G at its aerobatic maximum limit.

Under the hood, a 583-cubic-inch Lycoming AEIO-580-B1A flat-six piston engine delivers 303 horsepower (226 kW) to a four-blade composite MT-Propeller system. An inverted oil and fuel system keeps the engine running under sustained negative-g flight and zero-g push-overs. To make the transition, USAFA pulled six active-duty instructor pilots from the 306th Flying Training Group to stand up the first training cadre. These officers will shape a new syllabus to train cadets and establish a demonstration and competition team. The initiative comes as the wider military struggles with a persistent shortfall of roughly 2,000 operational pilots, forcing leadership to seek shortcuts to build stick-and-rudder skills earlier in pilot development.

The Academy's existing fleet jumps from unpowered TG-16A gliders and basic T-53A/T-41D piston trainers straight to the UPT's T-6A Texan II turboprops after graduation. USAF Chief of Staff General Ken Wilsbach and Academy Superintendent Lt. Gen. Paul Moga secured the four airframes thanks to a $3 million donation coordinated by philanthropist Andrew McKenna. The Kestrel will act as an intermediate high-performance step, teaching cadets how to manage high power-to-weight ratios, rapid throttle response, and aggressive energy management. Standard trainers are built for stability and prohibit extreme maneuvers. Because the Kestrel is certified for unlimited aerobatics, instructors can deliberately put cadets into inverted spins, vertical stalls, and disorienting attitudes, teaching them instinctive recovery skills that directly prevent real-world loss-of-control accidents.

The Air Force is facing a persistent pilot deficit and needs to push students through UPT faster without lowering standards. By giving top cadets advanced stick-and-rudder proficiency during their junior and senior years at the Academy, they arrive at UPT needing fewer remedial rides and moving through the syllabus faster. Friedlander and his team received a traditional blessing and farewell on Utirik before sailing north to Bikar, which lies about 72 miles (116 km) from the nearest inhabited atoll. Bokak is even more isolated, about 239 miles (385 km) away. This remoteness is part of the reason the atolls have remained relatively untouched by human influence. Friedlander wanted to carry out an ecological assessment of everything from the land habitats to the reefs and the open sea. That meant studying the structure and biodiversity of these ecosystems, documenting how well they had been preserved and how the two atolls differed, and identifying the conservation measures they would need in the future.

“The remote northern atolls of Bikar and Bokak represent some of the least disturbed coral reef ecosystems remaining in the tropical Pacific,” Friedlander and his co-authors wrote in a study recently published in Frontiers in Marine Science. “Both atolls are uninhabited and geographically isolated from major population centers and industrial development. Previous expeditions documented portions of their terrestrial habitats, seabird colonies, sea turtles, and shallow coral reefs.”

The team’s first stop was Bikar, which curves across the water like a crescent moon. There, the researchers used a variety of sampling methods, including scuba surveys, photography, underwater video, deep-sea cameras, and a submarine, to record which species were present and in what numbers. Polynesian rats, which humans introduced, were sometimes seen scurrying through the island’s old-growth Pisonia forests. Even so, native species still thrive. Seabirds such as masked boobies, great frigatebirds, black noddies, and white terns filled the skies, while shorebirds like the migratory Pacific golden plover pecked at the sand in search of small invertebrates. Bikar’s beaches are also a nesting ground for green sea turtles; the team recorded 132 nest pits there. Its reefs are full of fish, including several species of snapper and grouper. Giant clams, which host photosynthetic algae, filter the water and are vital to the health of the reef.

Farther north and even more isolated, Bokak is an oasis that has seen very little human interference. Its drier, savanna-like landscape is covered in tufts of bunchgrass and tree heliotropes, which bear clusters of small white flowers. Many of the seabirds that soar over Bikar are also found on Bokak. Bokak’s waters are even richer than Bikar’s: its reefs are dominated by coral and hold more fish by weight. Friedlander dove in to investigate. He came across a school of humphead parrotfish, a threatened species once thought to have nearly vanished from the islands. He also saw an unusual reversal of the typical food pyramid. Reef sharks and black jacks outweigh their prey in total biomass. Because these top predators feed so often, they keep energy and nutrients moving quickly through the reef, which helps the ecosystem run efficiently.

After Bokak, Friedlander’s team had one more location to survey, and it offered a sharp contrast. From 1946 to 1958, Bikini Atoll was a center of U.S. nuclear testing, starting with Operation Crossroads. When the islanders were evacuated before the first tests, they took only a few changes of clothes, not knowing that they would never be able to resettle their home for good. The series of detonations devastated the atoll. From the ocean’s glassy surface, Bikini seems to be ringed by cities of coral. But divers who swam closer found that many of these formations, which had taken thousands of years to grow, were little more than skeletons. Still, Bikini Atoll is not without hope. After searching long enough, Friedlander found patches of live coral that had settled on the dead reef and were swarming with fish. It was a sign of how resilient life can be.

Even Bikar and Bokak can’t fully escape what is happening elsewhere. Their remoteness protects them from most direct human pressures, but climate change is slowly reaching them, too. When the water gets too hot, corals expel the symbiotic algae that feed them and give them their bright colors, a process known as coral bleaching. Leftover radiation from the nuclear-testing era is still a source of contamination in the region. Meanwhile, currents carry in trash from other countries, and plastics break down into microplastics. People are responding with conservation measures. In 2025, two years after the expedition, the government of the Marshall Islands designated the waters around Bikar and Bokak, about 18,500 square miles (48,000 square km), as the country’s first national marine sanctuary, as part of the community-led Reimaanlok conservation framework. Despite the threats that come from far away, Friedlander is optimistic.

“As relatively intact tropical marine ecosystems become increasingly rare worldwide,” he and his co-authors concluded, “remote atolls such as Bikar and Bokak serve not only as regional conservation priorities but also as globally important ecological reference systems for understanding the structure and future trajectories of tropical oceanic ecosystems in the Anthropocene.”

IEEE Celebrates 10 Years of Incorporating Ethics Into AI Systems

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Standards and certifications foster socially responsible products

Kathy Pretz is the editor in chief of The Institute, IEEE's member publication

Policymakers, technologists, and members of the public have raised concerns about the harm AI’s rapid advancement might cause to society. Their concerns include bias, data privacy violations, the spread of misinformation, and losing control of AI agents.

Those issues are relatively new to many people, but the IEEE Standards Association has been working for the past decade on ways to prioritize the responsible development of AI systems.

In 2016 IEEE SA launched the IEEE Global Initiative for Ethical Considerations in Autonomous and Intelligent Systems (A/IS) and published the Ethically Aligned Design report. EAD’s mission is to encourage everyone involved in designing autonomous and intelligent systems to prioritize human well-being by applying ethical considerations for the benefit of humanity.

Developed by a multidisciplinary community of experts from around the world, the report outlined eight foundational principles for A/IS. They include human rights, people’s well-being, and transparency. More than 100 leading AI ethics experts provided input. EAD’s Version II report was released in 2017. Both versions solicited public input and received hundreds of pages of feedback, according to John C. Havens, who was the global initiative’s founding executive director, and is now the global director for IEEE Planet Positive 2030. And by 2019, there were more than 400 contributors and the community continues to grow.

“Ethically Aligned Design was the first of its kind in the sense of a report that was more than a list of principles and from an organization with the credibility of IEEE,” Havens says. “At the time it was released, there wasn’t anything like it.”

Since its launch, a series of standards has been developed to provide practical guidelines on designing, building, and deploying technology that prioritizes human values. Several AI ethics certification programs have been introduced.

This year marks the 10th anniversary of EAD’s first draft. To celebrate, IEEE SA recently launched a website, a podcast series, and a blog that reflect on the report’s achievements and impact.

The word ethics is often used interchangeably with morality, but that is not the case in responsible AI systems design, according to EAD’s first version. The report includes insights from classical ethics traditions and value-based design methodologies to offer actionable recommendations for engineers developing AI tools.

The report established eight foundational general principles for designing autonomous systems: respecting human rights, prioritizing increased well-being as success criteria for development, empowering individuals with data agency to have control over their identity, effectiveness, transparency, accountability, awareness of misuse, and competence for the system creators.

The principles shift applied ethics to the design stage of AI systems, compared with being a concern after they’re released, the authors wrote.

The IEEE 7000 series of standards has been developed based on EAD’s framework. Currently the series consists of 29 projects, 11 of which have been published, and more are still being added. They cover topics such as ethical system design model process (IEEE 7000-2021), AI system transparency (IEEE 7001-2021), data privacy (IEEE 7002-2022), algorithmic bias considerations (IEEE 7003-2024), the impact on people’s well-being (IEEE 7010-2020), machine-readable personal privacy requirements (IEEE 7012-2025), and systems that mimic human empathy (IEEE 7014-2024).

IEEE SA offers a suite of ethics in AI programs including IEEE CertifAIEd, which includes two certifications: one for individuals and one for products.

The professional certification provides individuals with skills to assess autonomous and intelligent systems for adherence to IEEE’s methodology and ethics criteria.

“Ethically Aligned Design was the first of its kind in the sense of a report that was more than a list of principles and from an organization with the credibility of IEEE.” —John C. Havens, founding executive director of the global initiative

The product certification program assesses whether an organization’s AI tool or system conforms to the criteria in the IEEE Ontological Specifications. A CertifiAIEd assessor evaluates the product to ensure it meets the criteria. Upon completion of the assessment, the owner submits it to the IEEE Conformity Assessment Program, which can certify it and issue the certification mark.

IEEE SA also recently released its Responsible AI for Children certification program. This program certifies that AI products for children have been independently tested and verified to meet design and ethics requirements.

The IEEE Artificial Intelligence Systems (AIS) for Global Flourishing Collaborative Industry Collections group was formed to provide the resources, knowledge, and tools needed to prioritize people’s well-being to developers of AI systems. It has more than 200 members.

The collaborative describes its work as a practical, systems-level framework for responsible technology design that translates flourishing into applied methods, metrics, and tools that can be used throughout the AIS lifecycle.

The group is establishing guidelines for how to design, evaluate, and govern systems that can measurably improve human well-being and ecological sustainability.

Subcommittees are focused on cognition, education, faith, planetary care, spirituality, and other issues.

“The group is building directly off the well-being chapter of Ethically Aligned Design and the IEEE 7010 standard (Recommended Practice for Assessing the Impact of Autonomous and Intelligent Systems on Human Well-Being),” Havens says. “That’s on purpose because if human and planetary flourishing aren’t addressed first in design, then human well-being and planetary sustainability are afterthoughts.”

To mark the 10-year anniversary, IEEE SA created the five-part “Ethically Aligned Design: 10 Years of Impact” podcast series. It launched on 21 October. New episodes are being released every two weeks.

The episodes center around the vision for EAD, the process for selecting its foundational principles, and how they were translated into the IEEE 7000 standards.

The first podcast, “Origins of Ethically Aligned Design,” explored early efforts to make ethics a practical part of AI and autonomous system design. The experts discussed challenges, debates, global collaboration, and the real-world impact that helped move responsible technology from ethical principles toward actionable standards and practices.

The second podcast, “The Chapters and Committees” focuses on the multidisciplinary groups that worked on turning broad human values into practical guidance for AI and autonomous systems.

The podcast discussions serve as material for a thought leadership program that also includes blog articles about the IEEE 7000 series and educational resources to continue to raise awareness and support of this groundbreaking work.

Gravitational waves have revealed a population of black holes that may have been born when masssive stars failed to go supernova.

Most of us know the origin story of black holes, or we thought we did. Massive stars run out of the fuel for nuclear fusion and their cores collapse. As this happens, the stars' outer layers expand outwards. Shockwaves from the core collapse strike the outer layers, triggering a supernova explosion. The collapsed core creates a black hole.

However, new research based on gravitational wave data collected by LIGO (Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory) and its gravitational wave detector partners, Virgo and KAGRA (Kamioka Gravitational Wave Detector), suggests this might not be the complete picture of black hole birth. The team behind the research suggests some massive stars go out with a whimper rather than a bang and that these "failed supernovas" can also spawn black holes.

The hint of this comes from the over 300 black hole collisions spotted by LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA since the detectors first "heard" ripples in space from these distant events in 2015.

"A vast majority of the merging black holes in the universe are about 10 times the mass of our sun," LIGO team member Katerina Chatziioannou, of the California Institute of Technology (Caltech), said in a statement. "Our data are showing that this group of black holes may have a common origin — they may be a distinct subpopulation that failed to go supernova.

"These results represent a connection between the gravitational-wave data and underlying astrophysics we are trying to extract."

Currently, there is no hard evidence of failed supernovas, but their existence is well supported theoretically. In fact, scientists think there is a vast population of massive stars out there that imploded rather than exploded, directly collapsing into black holes. And these may be black holes with around ten times the mass of the sun (10 solar masses) found in merging pairs.

Chatziioannou led a team that examined gravitational wave data to confirm this concept. To do this, they looked at the distribution of the masses of 257 black hole mergers detected by the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA network over the past 10 years.

This revealed a peak of 10-solar-mass black holes that represented 75% of the entire sample. If this was expected, what the team found that wasn't predicted was a lack of black holes with masses 12 to 16 times the mass of the sun.

"We see 10 solar-mass black holes merging all the time," team member Isaac Legred of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign said. "But why are, say, 14 solar-mass black holes so rare? The gap we see in the distribution of masses indicates there may be a special cocktail of internal physics that goes into forming the abundance of 10 solar-mass black holes."

The researchers also looked at the spin rates of black holes in mergers, a telltale signature of how they formed. This revealed that 10-solar-mass black holes seemed to have a distinct range of spin speeds. This indicates a common origin, perhaps the implosion of a massive star without a supernova explosion.

"We can look up at the sky and try to study historical records of massive stars that failed to explode, but it's challenging because they just vanish without exploding, leaving a dark patch of sky," Legred concluded. "With this study, we show the gravitational-wave data are consistent with most binary black holes forming from a very special kind of failed supernova, but more research is needed.

"Black holes are born in some of the most extreme environments in the universe, so the prospect of better understanding their formation is very exciting."

The team's research was published on Tuesday (Nov. 10) in the journal Physical Review D.

Hardware-wallet maker Trezor has disclosed a breach at shipping partner Skladon affecting data connected with approximately 27,000 customer orders. The incident is serious because a delivery address and product purchase history can reveal who may own crypto. It is also important not to turn a data-exposure story into a false claim that customer wallets were drained. Trezor says its own systems were not compromised, and the disclosed records did not include wallet backups, PINs or passphrases.

Trezor says Skladon informed it on November 15 of unauthorized access linked to an incident on November 7. The data at issue concerned orders placed between August 9 and November 7, 2026. The company estimates roughly 27,000 affected orders and says the investigation is continuing. The records included names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, email addresses and information about orders and products bought. A limited number of earlier August customers may also have been affected even though their information had been anonymized within Trezor’s own systems before the notification process.

Someone with a buyer’s name, address and exact device order can craft a convincing phishing message or impersonate customer support. The same combination may create physical-security concerns if a criminal believes the household controls valuable assets. None of this requires compromising the hardware wallet’s cryptographic security. Trezor says it never requests wallet recovery data by telephone and warns affected users to treat unexpected calls, texts, emails and letters with suspicion. If a message uses authentic order details, that does not make its instructions legitimate.

The company says it has contacted affected customers it can still reach and notified a data-protection authority. It is introducing or expanding anonymous pickup at collection points in much of Europe, reducing the amount of residential delivery information involved in future orders. Trezor also says it is working to shorten customer-data retention from 90 to 45 days, with further reductions under consideration. Those steps address the long-term exposure of operational records rather than claiming that a damaged secret key can be repaired after the fact.

Customers should go directly to the vendor’s known website for official support rather than follow contact details supplied in an unsolicited message. They should never type a recovery phrase into a web form or share it with a caller. Buyers can also consider whether their delivery address and household security require extra attention. Anyone receiving threats should contact appropriate local authorities. The precise number of affected people, rather than orders, may differ, and the final breach scope may change as investigators establish what was exported.

A legitimate package or delivery notice is especially convincing because it can refer to a real order. Criminals might use the information for emails, texts, calls or fraudulent web pages that look like shipping support. Wallet owners should be wary of any message that asks them to type a recovery phrase, install a replacement wallet application or approve an unexpected transaction. Those requests are unrelated to normal delivery updates. The strongest precaution is to verify a message through a separately opened official website, not by following a link in the message itself.

What exactly are Flock cameras and how can you spot one?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Flock cameras are everywhere, and they’re easier to miss than you think.

Basic descriptions of Flock cameras fall woefully short of their true capabilities and potential uses. Most people are familiar with the automated license plate reader (ALPR) version, usually pole-mounted and paired with a solar panel. If that were all there was to it, this would be a very short article. But Flock Safety (Flock Group Inc.) has become deeply intertwined with the defining terminology of ALPR cameras.

In truth, there are more than a dozen manufacturers and vendors of ALPRs; their products just aren't called Flock cameras, and each has its own aesthetic characteristics. For all intents and purposes, however, they are identical. If you see Motorola on the side of one, it likely does much of what a Flock does: read plates and log vehicles.

Most are vertically oval, narrow in width and fairly small, roughly the size of your hand. They're designed to capture your license plate and the time and place you passed by. Most are also accompanied by a solar panel. Leonardo and Genetec make the most visually distinct versions. Leonardo's are blockier, rectangular in shape, with some elongated versions. Genetec's are horizontally oval, with the same narrow width as Flock's variations, only flipped. Motorola manufactures a mix of the two.

The problem is that some Flock cameras can easily be mistaken for a standard traffic camera and vice versa. Because of this, it's difficult to accept the absolute accuracy of a project like DeFlock, which uses volunteers to identify Flock cameras visually. There's no centralized or formal verification system to independently confirm the sightings.

Flock also sells more than ALPRs. Depending on what the customer wants, the system can log details like roof racks on cars and bumper stickers. Some setups have microphones and others can recognize things like clothes and beards.

Flock cameras usually turn up where traffic funnels. They're often mounted on poles at intersections and busy traffic areas. Since many homeowners associations (HOAs) are major Flock camera customers, it shouldn't be a surprise to find them at neighborhood entrances. Shopping centers aren't off the list of potential locations either. The aesthetics aren't irrelevant, but since several manufacturers make them, there are a few other telltale signs to look for as well.

Flock cameras are meant to capture license plates, so look at where they're aimed. Check for Flock's bird logo on the camera and a solar panel above it. Because most are self-powered, there's usually little to no additional equipment nearby. Red light and speed cameras are typically larger and wired to traffic signals or enforcement poles. White, elongated cameras are another lookalike, but they mostly help traffic signals adjust to traffic flow. Some states also mount signs next to speed cameras so they're easy to identify.

Keep in mind that there are other variations of Flock cameras out there. The Flock Condor is a mounted sphere that rotates 360 degrees and tracks people. Naturally, these are mounted near areas with heavy pedestrian traffic. Flock also makes a mobile security trailer, a gunshot detector and a drone-as-first-responder. Because companies are using video integration tools to convert standard IP cameras into plate readers, it will be increasingly difficult to identify Flock or Flock-type cameras.

As a rule of thumb, if it's pointed at you, it's potentially watching you. But for a broader view, check the Atlas of Surveillance, a searchable database from the Electronic Frontier Foundation that shows what technology law enforcement agencies use. Search your town or county to see whether police are using plate readers.

While some services like MapQuest can help you find Flock cameras in your neighborhood, be wary of crowdsourced maps. DeFlock uses OpenStreetMap data collected from volunteers, but there's no official public list, so a missing pin doesn't mean there isn't a Flock camera sitting there.

Pins can also be wrong, since it's easy to confuse a Flock camera with the countless security and traffic camera variations. A Flock camera doesn't have to belong to a public agency. Flock and Flock competitors also sell to HOAs and retailers.

If you want to find every Flock camera in your city, county or state, you have to put in the work. Submit a public records request, attend the next city council meeting or ask your HOA and local businesses.

More than just a football club: Cardinale’s vision to make AC Milan a multi-billion empire

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In case you weren’t aware from his repeatedly bold comments in the media, Gerry Cardinale has big plans for AC Milan.

The RedBird Capital founder and chief has made one concept very clear: while he is at the helm, Milan are no longer just a football club, but a platform that aims to integrate sports, infrastructure, and entertainment. Of course AC Milan will be at the centre of the web, but the idea is to expand.

The proof of this comes from a three-letter acronym: MSE. It stands for Milan Sports & Entertainment and it is the new holding company created by RedBird. The idea, simply put, is to bring together investments and activities in various sectors under one umbrella.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport recall, Cardinale explained to the Financial Times that the assets involved could be valued in the mid-single-digit billions, meaning several billion dollars. So, how does he plan to get there?

Cardinale linked the creation of MSE to the process initiated with the acquisition of Milan: “Milan Sports & Entertainment is the culmination of this work and of what we have built since we first invested in the club four years ago.”

In his interview with La Gazzetta, Cardinale clarified that the new structure is also created to support the Rossoneri’s sporting ambitions: “The driving force behind operations like this is to provide Milan with the financial strength needed to compete in the transfer market and secure the best players in the world.”

There are examples of this kind of model working already. Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, AEG and Fenway Sports Group are among the most prominent and – given his background in the USA – Cardinale knows each of them well.

According to CNBC valuations, KSE was worth $26.05bn in 2026, FSG $15.37bn, and AEG $11.99bn in 2025, excluding music and hotel businesses.

KSE boasts franchises such as the Los Angeles Rams, Denver Nuggets, Colorado Avalanche and Arsenal, as well as venues such as SoFi Stadium, Ball Arena, and Emirates Stadium. The group combines sports, infrastructure and real estate development such as Hollywood Park, the district built around SoFi Stadium.

AEG – founded by Philip Anschutz – have instead built its model on the management and development of venues. It owns or manages venues such as Crypto.com Arena and The O2 in London plus the Los Angeles Kings and LA Galaxy, and operates in concert promotion and ticketing. The LA LIVE project developed around Crypto.com Arena puts the venue at the centre of a greater ecosystem.

Fenway Sports Group – born around the Boston Red Sox and Liverpool FC – have developed a holding company with interests in media, marketing, real estate, and entertainment. It’s a model Cardinale knows first hand: in 2021, RedBird invested approximately $750m in FSG, acquiring a stake of around 11%, based on an enterprise valuation of $7.35bn.

MSE in theory can harness elements of all three of these experiences: KSE’s integration of sports and infrastructure, AEG ‘s commercial development of facilities and FSG ‘s multi-sport approach. The main difference is the purpose declared by Cardinale: to use all of it to funnel money back into Milan.

From the stadium shared with Inter to the possibility of an arena in San Donato, including the new NBA Europe venture and even tennis: MSE’s scope includes various initiatives that go beyond the playing field.

The main infrastructure project is the future Generali Stadium, the new 71,500-seat facility designed by Foster + Partners and Manica. Developed jointly by the two Milanese clubs, it represents a key element of the transformation of the San Siro area and will not be a Rossoneri-only facility.

The promoters intend for the stadium to host year-round activities, from matches to concerts, as well as catering, events, and hospitality services. In other words, like the stadiums and arenas mentioned above, it will become more than just a place to go for two hours on a Sunday.

In San Donato Milanese, RedBird are also considering the possibility of building a 20,000-seat arena. Cardinale explained that the fund already owns the land and has planning permission, but the idea is also linked to Milan’s potential involvement in NBA Europe. Another area of ​​focus concerns tennis, with the possibility of hosting a tournament in the city.

Finally, RedBird intend to develop a company dedicated to managing stadiums and arenas, with services including catering, merchandising and premium hospitality. The goal is to build commercial expertise to apply to the facilities connected to the group, but also to make it available to other owners and sports clubs.

This phase will be co-ordinated by Brett Yormark, appointed CEO of MSE and set to step down as head of the Big 12 (a conference in College Football) at the end of January 2027. He worked for 14 years at Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment, overseeing the development of the Brooklyn Nets and the Barclays Center. He has also gained experience in NASCAR and Roc Nation.

The choice to go for Yormark reflects the desire to entrust the platform to someone with expertise across multiple different fields in the sporting and commercial worlds.

All these grand plans are well and good, but how will ideas turn into money that drives AC Milan forward? MSE’s economic potential is measured not only through the club’s revenues, but also through the activities that RedBird intends to develop around sports, infrastructure and entertainment.

The starting point is obviously Milan as a club, which closed the 2025-26 financial year with revenues of €464.6m, over €100m of which came from sponsorships. The main infrastructure opportunity is the new San Siro, an investment of approximately €2.3bn shared with Inter.

Projections contained in the design documents indicate a potential annual revenue of approximately €120.4m for the facility as a whole (both for the Rossoneri and the Nerazzurri), through sporting, commercial, and complementary services

These are numbers that will be revised upwards by the two clubs when considering, for example, the record agreement with Generali for naming rights. It is worth a total of €650m over 25 years, divided into €10m per year for the first five years and €30m per season for the following 20, once the stadium is built.

One of the areas on which RedBird Capital are focusing most is hospitality, which is currently worth around €15m per year for the Rossoneri. The international benchmark has already been set by Legends Global, a company Cardinale knows well.

Legends are what the Milan owner sees as a model for developing premium services, restaurants, and commercial spaces. The most obvious example is at FC Barcelona: with the support of LG, the club have marketed nearly 5,000 VIP seats at Camp Nou, generating over €380m in contracted revenue.

They aim to generate another €700m through multi-year licenses for an additional 2,000 seats. This isn’t annual revenue, but rather contracts spread over 15 or 30 years, so a guaranteed flow of money coming in that Cardinale aims to replicate.

Another agreement which demonstrates the potential of this kind of arrangement is the one with Real Madrid for the Santiago Bernabéu. It involves an investment of approximately €360m in exchange for 20-year participation in some of the stadium’s businesses,.

Legends Global also operate in facilities and events management. Following the acquisition of ASM Global (who actually proposed renovating San Siro for non-football events in Italy) in 2024, the group claims a presence in over 450 venues, approximately 20,000 events, and 165 million guests annually. Their European partnerships include Arsenal, Chelsea and Porto.

To illustrate the potential it could hold for Italy as a whole, the English subsidiary reported revenues of £131.6m in 2024, of which £105.5m came from facility and event management and £26.1m from catering. Before the integration with ASM Global, Legends had reported revenues of $1.7bn in the period ending in March 2024, with 67% coming from food and beverage sales.

In Italy, a benchmark is Forumnet, who in 2024 generated €29.7m in revenue and €12.8m in EBITDA (Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation). They manage the Assago Forum which hosts approximately 60-70 concerts a year, demonstrating how a venue can generate activity well beyond the sports calendar.

As mentioned, an arena is being planned in San Donato with an estimated investment of around €400m, which could become the basis for a Milanese franchise in NBA Europe. For reference, Olimpia Milano – the current basketball team in the city – generate around €15m through ticketing, season tickets, sponsorships and merchandising.

For NBA Europe, projections speak of over $1.5bn to be distributed to the clubs in the first six years. A theoretical division among the 12 permanent franchises would equate to approximately $20.8m per team per year, but this is not fixed yet. Entry costs – estimated by the media to be between $500m and over $1bn – also remain to be determined.

Tennis represents another potential area for development. The ATP 500 in Halle generates around €5m in revenue, excluding television rights, while the ATP Finals in Turin generate around €60m. These are events with different dimensions and scopes, but they provide a scale.

The creation of MSE has a stated objective: to strengthen Milan’s financial capacity and reduce the gap with the richest clubs on the international scene, particularly those in the Premier League.

The gap is also evident when comparing revenues. AC Milan closed the 2025-26 season with €464.6m, a significant result considering their absence from European competitions , but still far from the top English clubs.

According to the Deloitte Football Money League, in the 2024-25 season, Liverpool generated €836.1m, Arsenal €821.7m and Manchester United €793.1m. Tottenham and Chelsea also exceeded €670m and €580m, respectively.

The comparison covers different financial years, but it still shows the magnitude of the gap. Liverpool generated revenues equal to approximately 1.8 times those of Milan, while Chelsea recorded a turnover approximately €120 higher.

The chasm shows how clubs in the Premier League have showcased an ability to generate commercial revenue, enhance stadiums and reach a global audience. The gap has only been growing, and TV rights will be another focus, though that is outside of just Cardinale’s hands.

Speaking of the owner, it is now obvious he sees MSE as the name that will house the tools to try build a real sporting and commercial world around Milan. It remains to be seen how effective it will be, but the starting point is there.
