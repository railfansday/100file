This guide builds a synthetic legal-entity entitlement model for an international bank with a Hong Kong entity and a Singapore entity. It uses Databricks Free Edition as a perpetual, no-cost, serverless-only learning environment.

The design isolates data, agents, ontology context, service identities, row access, column visibility, and cross-entity analytical outputs. The objective is to prevent an agent assigned to Entity HK from reading Entity SG records, while still allowing narrowly approved group analytics through controlled aggregate datasets or Databricks Clean Rooms.

The example uses institutional liquidity analysis, but the same controls apply to customer support, payments, treasury, risk, compliance, and regulatory reporting agents.

Agent Bricks is a Databricks capability for building and operating governed AI agents. Unity Catalog remains the authorization and governance layer for governed data objects.

“Legal-entity entitlement” is an enterprise access-control pattern, not a single Databricks switch with that exact product name. Implement it through catalogs, schemas, groups, service principals, grants, governed tags, ABAC policies, row filters, column masks, views, application authorization, and auditable agent tooling.

“Entitlement Interceptor” is a useful architectural description, but it is not necessary to invent a separate service. Unity Catalog evaluates object privileges and access policies before the query executes. ABAC policies are evaluated before the query reaches the runtime, and the runtime applies the resulting row and column restrictions.

Row filtering prevents unauthorized rows from appearing in query results. It should not be described as proof that an unauthorized row is never physically read by any internal storage component. The useful security guarantee is that the principal cannot retrieve unauthorized rows through the governed query path.

Genie Ontology provides governed business context and semantic meaning. It complements Unity Catalog authorization but does not replace grants, ABAC, row filters, masks, or clean-room controls.

Databricks Clean Rooms provides an isolated, no-trust collaboration environment using OpenSharing and serverless compute. It does not automatically add differential-privacy noise to every result. Databricks Agent Bricks Legal-Entity Entitlements on AWS for Hong Kong and Singapore Banking Operations

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For most of the history of international banking, legal-entity separation was visible in buildings, ledgers, licenses, management chains, and correspondent relationships. A Hong Kong branch and a Singapore branch could belong to the same banking group while remaining different regulated establishments with different books, customer obligations, data restrictions, capital expectations, reporting duties, and supervisory relationships. The group could consolidate financial results, but the operating rights of one entity did not automatically become the operating rights of another.

Cloud data architecture made physical consolidation easier. Data from Entity HK and Entity SG can now be stored in the same Amazon S3 estate, governed by the same Databricks account, and queried through the same analytical platform. That consolidation can improve resilience, consistency, and economics. It also creates a serious risk. A shared platform can tempt an institution to confuse common infrastructure with common authority.

The arrival of AI agents increases the consequence. A human analyst usually chooses a report, writes a query, and reviews a result. An agent can choose tools, generate SQL, retrieve documents, call models, combine evidence, and repeat the process without a person inspecting every intermediate step. A single overprivileged service principal can turn a well-designed agent into a cross-entity data-exfiltration mechanism. A correct final answer does not excuse an unauthorized intermediate retrieval. An agent that reads Singapore customer transactions before deciding they are irrelevant has already crossed the boundary.

Databricks Agent Bricks, Unity Catalog, Unity Gateway, Genie Agents, and Genie Ontology provide the ingredients for a stronger model. Agent Bricks can build and operate production agents using governed data, models, tools, and enterprise context. Unity Catalog can register agents and services as governed objects, assign ownership, enforce grants, apply row filters and column masks, and preserve lineage and audit. Genie Ontology can supply business meaning while respecting Unity Catalog permissions. Databricks Clean Rooms can support approved collaboration where parties must calculate shared outcomes without directly exposing each other’s underlying data.

The institution must assemble these capabilities around one principle: an agent receives authority because of the legal entity, role, purpose, and execution context of the request, not because it happens to run inside the group’s Databricks account.

This paper describes an AWS-based architecture for a global banking group operating Entity HK and Entity SG on one Databricks platform while preserving extreme legal-entity and semantic isolation. It explains the role of the three-level namespace, service principals, account groups, attribute-based access control, dynamic row filtering, column masking, governed tags, trusted execution, Genie Ontology, clean rooms, aggregation controls, differential privacy, Photon, and auditable agent execution.

It also corrects several dangerous simplifications. Unity Catalog does not expose a product object formally named an entitlement interceptor. Policy enforcement is part of Unity Catalog’s access-control and query-execution path. Photon can execute predicates efficiently, but an institution should not promise that every policy guarantees physical non-reading of disallowed rows. Pruning depends on predicate shape, statistics, file layout, expression complexity, and runtime. The security guarantee is that unauthorized rows and values are not returned to the principal. Physical file elimination is a performance property that must be verified separately.

Databricks Clean Rooms do not automatically restrict every calculation to SUM or AVG, and they do not automatically add differential-privacy noise to every result. Clean Rooms provide a no-trust collaboration environment, OpenSharing, isolated serverless execution, explicit code approval, output control, and governed assets. Aggregate-only interfaces and differential privacy must be deliberately designed, tested, and approved. Precision in these statements is not pedantry. It is the difference between a control and a hope.

Hong Kong and Singapore may be regional peers, but they are not interchangeable operating domains. Entity HK can be subject to Hong Kong banking, securities, privacy, outsourcing, recordkeeping, and supervisory requirements. Entity SG can be subject to Singapore banking, securities, personal-data, technology-risk, outsourcing, and recordkeeping requirements. The exact obligation depends on license, product, customer, booking model, processing location, and contractual arrangement. Platform architecture should not attempt to replace legal interpretation. It should make the approved interpretation enforceable.

A regional liquidity agent illustrates the problem. Group treasury may legitimately need a consolidated view of aggregate liquidity across Asia-Pacific. A Hong Kong desk agent may legitimately need Hong Kong balances, expected settlements, collateral, and limits. A Singapore agent needs the corresponding Singapore information. Consolidation does not imply that the Hong Kong agent may inspect Singapore customer-level transactions. Group treasury’s need for aggregate liquidity does not imply access to customer names or counterparty account numbers.

Traditional role-based access control can express some of this. The platform grants a Hong Kong group access to Hong Kong tables and a Singapore group access to Singapore tables. The model becomes fragile when data is physically shared, users hold several roles, applications use service principals, and hundreds of agents are created. Manual grants accumulate. A new table may receive a broad default. A copied view may omit a filter. An agent may call a function whose owner has broader access.

The institution therefore needs policy at several layers. Namespace isolation organizes ownership. Identity isolation establishes who is acting. ABAC interprets governed tags and context. Row filters constrain legal-entity records. Column masks protect sensitive fields. Service and tool permissions constrain what the agent can call. Genie Ontology constrains what semantic context the agent can retrieve. Clean Rooms constrain cross-party computation. Audit and tracing demonstrate what actually happened.

No layer should be treated as sufficient alone. Security comes from their composition.

Unity Catalog uses catalog.schema.table. This namespace is more than a naming convention. It is the hierarchy through which ownership, grants, policies, lineage, and discovery can be organized.

A legal-entity-first design can create catalogs such as bank_hk_prod, bank_sg_prod, and group_apac_shared. Within each legal-entity catalog, schemas can separate domains such as liquidity, payments, markets, credit, customer, finance, compliance, and agent_serving. Tables and views contain the governed records.

The Hong Kong liquidity position could be bank_hk_prod.liquidity.position_current. The Singapore equivalent could be bank_sg_prod.liquidity.position_current. A group aggregate approved for regional treasury could be group_apac_shared.treasury.liquidity_summary. The shared catalog should contain products deliberately published for group use, not direct shortcuts into entity-level base tables.

This model provides strong ownership clarity. Entity HK data owners control Hong Kong catalogs. Entity SG data owners control Singapore catalogs. Group owners control published shared products. A group administrator should not automatically become the business owner of every table simply because the administrator maintains the platform.

A second valid design stores common-format facts in a regional catalog with a mandatory legal_entity_code column. This improves shared engineering and can simplify group consolidation. It raises the importance of row-level policy and creates a larger blast radius if a policy is missing. The institution should use this model only when source contracts, policy inheritance, testing, and ownership are mature.

A hybrid design generally fits a global bank. Entity-owned raw and sensitive data remains in separate catalogs. Standardized, lower-sensitivity facts can enter a regional curated catalog with mandatory legal-entity attributes and ABAC. Deliberately aggregated products enter a group shared catalog. The platform avoids both extremes: complete duplication and uncontrolled centralization.

The namespace should also separate production from development. A service principal for a development agent should not silently receive production access through a common group. Development should use masked, synthetic, or approved branch data. Production grants should be explicit and time bounded where appropriate.

Every production Agent Brick instance should run under a dedicated service principal or a dedicated governed service identity, depending on the deployment pattern. Reusing one service principal across Hong Kong and Singapore destroys attribution and increases the blast radius of a credential or policy error.

A Hong Kong liquidity agent can use sp_agent_liquidity_hk_prod. The Singapore instance can use sp_agent_liquidity_sg_prod. A group aggregate agent can use sp_agent_liquidity_apac_aggregate_prod. Each identity should have an owner, legal entity, business purpose, environment, cost center, risk classification, creation date, review date, and retirement process.

The service principal should belong to account groups aligned with function and legal entity, such as group_entity_hk, group_liquidity_readers, and group_agent_prod. The Singapore principal should belong to group_entity_sg, not group_entity_hk. Group membership should be managed through an approved identity lifecycle and reviewed independently of application deployment.

Group membership is necessary but not sufficient. An agent can act for a user. When the use case requires user-specific authority, the platform should preserve the end user’s identity through a supported on-behalf-of mechanism rather than collapsing every request into the app service principal’s broad rights. The effective authority should be the intersection of the user, agent, legal entity, purpose, tool, and data asset.

This distinction matters for a regional manager. The manager may be authorized to see aggregate Hong Kong and Singapore liquidity but not customer-level Singapore transactions. If the request runs only as a powerful regional service principal, the agent can retrieve too much. If the execution carries the manager’s governed context and uses aggregate products, the platform can enforce the intended boundary.

Agent services registered through Unity Gateway should receive explicit EXECUTE grants. Registration provides discoverability, ownership, and consistent permissions. It does not make an agent safe. The service’s source connection, base path, runtime policy, model, tools, and underlying data privileges must all be reviewed.

Secrets should use OAuth or managed credentials rather than personal tokens. No prompt, notebook, environment file, or Markdown instruction should contain database credentials. Credential rotation should not require changing the agent’s semantic identity.

Role-based grants answer which principal can use which object. Attribute-based access control answers which policy should apply when object, identity, and data attributes meet defined conditions. This is essential at banking scale because a single manual grant model cannot safely express thousands of combinations.

Unity Catalog ABAC policies use governed tags and can apply row filters or column masks at catalog, schema, or table scope. ABAC is preferable to manually attaching a policy to every table when the institution needs consistent enforcement across many data products. A new tagged table can inherit the policy rather than depend on a developer remembering to add it.

The bank should define account-level governed tag keys such as legal_entity, jurisdiction, data_classification, customer_data, cross_border_restricted, purpose, books_records, and retention_class. Tag values should be controlled by authorized principals. An ordinary table owner should not be able to remove Classification.HighlyConfidential merely to make a query succeed.

Identity attributes should be sourced from the enterprise identity plane. Examples include home legal entity, approved jurisdictions, employment status, function, outsourcing company, and clearance. Databricks supports identity-attribute functions in specified ABAC policy conditions, subject to current limitations. Identity attributes should be treated as security data. Their source, freshness, and change approval require the same control as privileged group membership.

A Hong Kong row policy should not simply test whether the principal’s name contains hk. It should call a governed SQL function or policy condition that evaluates authoritative group or identity attributes. The row data must contain a canonical legal-entity code, not an unreliable free-text country field.

ABAC requires supported compute. Current Databricks documentation requires serverless compute, Databricks Runtime 16.4 or above on standard compute, or dedicated Runtime 16.4 or above with fine-grained access-control filtering enabled. Older unsupported runtimes should not be allowed to read ABAC-secured tables through an alternate path.

Policy scale also requires quota awareness. The institution should prefer reusable policies at catalog or schema scope and avoid one custom policy per table and agent. ABAC policy counts, principal limits, and tag propagation delay should be tested before the bank treats the design as limitless.

A row filter is a SQL function or policy expression evaluated at query time. Rows for which the function returns false are excluded from the result. For a shared regional table, the filter can compare legal_entity_code with the principal’s allowed entity set.

A simplified Hong Kong-only concept is easy to describe: return a row when legal_entity_code = 'HK' and the principal belongs to the Hong Kong entity group. Production logic is usually more nuanced. Some service principals need only HK. Some regional risk principals may see HK and SG aggregates but not detail. Some compliance investigators may receive approved temporary access. Some operational records belong to a booking entity, processing entity, and customer jurisdiction simultaneously.

The correct model uses explicit dimensions. booking_legal_entity_code identifies the entity holding the position. processing_legal_entity_code identifies the operational processor. customer_jurisdiction_code identifies the customer context. data_residency_class identifies storage or processing restrictions. A policy should use the dimension relevant to the approved purpose rather than one overloaded legal_jurisdiction_code.

The policy should fail closed. A null or unknown entity code should not become globally visible. It should be excluded from ordinary consumption and routed to data stewardship. An unresolved mapping is risk, not shared data.

When the agent sends SQL or uses a DataFrame API, Unity Catalog evaluates the effective principal and applicable policy. The engine executes the secured logical plan. Unauthorized rows do not appear in the result. The application should not be able to disable the policy through prompt wording, generated SQL, or a notebook parameter.

The physical behavior needs careful language. The optimizer can push a simple legal-entity predicate into scan planning, and Delta Lake or Apache Iceberg statistics and clustering can allow files containing only Singapore rows to be skipped for an HK-only query. Photon can execute eligible filters efficiently. However, a complex UDF, mixed-entity file, missing statistics, or unfavorable layout can require reading files that contain both allowed and disallowed rows before filtering them. Security is maintained because prohibited rows are not returned, but physical non-read cannot be universally guaranteed by row filtering alone.

If the legal requirement demands stronger physical or regional isolation, the bank should store entity data in separate catalogs, storage locations, workspaces, AWS accounts, or regions as required. Logical policy is not a substitute for an approved residency architecture.

Column masks transform a column’s value for principals who lack the required authority. The return type remains compatible with the underlying column. The mask can return the original value, a partial value, a token, a generalized category, or null.

Fields such as customer name, counterparty account, national identifier, payment reference, telephone number, email address, exact address, and internal investigation marker should be classified through governed tags. A column tagged data_classification=highly_confidential can inherit a mask policy at catalog or schema scope.

The mask should match the purpose. A liquidity agent rarely needs a customer’s name. It may need customer segment, currency, expected amount, settlement date, and confidence. Returning null is safer than returning a partially masked name if the name has no analytical purpose. A support workflow may require the last four digits of an account. A sanctions workflow may require the full legal name but only under approved handling.

Masking should occur before data becomes model context. Sending a full account number to a model and asking the model not to reveal it is not data protection. The SQL result, tool response, trace, cache, and prompt should contain only the authorized representation.

A tag does not enforce a policy by itself. The bank must create the governed tag, restrict assignment, attach the ABAC mask policy, and test the result through every supported runtime and application path. The platform should continuously scan for highly confidential columns without an active mask or a documented exception.

Photon is Databricks’ vectorized query engine for SQL and DataFrame workloads. It can accelerate scans, joins, aggregations, and filters on supported operations. In a legal-entity query, the secured plan can include the row predicate, and Photon can execute that plan efficiently.

The bank should nevertheless separate security assurance from performance assurance. The security test verifies that an HK principal cannot obtain SG rows or unmasked SG values. The performance test verifies that the plan prunes relevant files, reads an acceptable number of bytes, and meets latency objectives. Passing one does not imply passing the other.

Data layout should support the policy. Tables can use liquid clustering on high-value fields such as booking_legal_entity_code, as_of_date, and account_id where query evidence supports them. Files should avoid unnecessary mixing of entity records when the principal access pattern is entity selective. Data-skipping statistics should cover policy and filter fields. The team should inspect query profiles and plans under the real service principal.

The institution should not reveal physical plan details to users who do not need them if those details expose schema or policy internals. Security teams and performance engineers require enough telemetry to prove execution while preserving separation of duties.

Data authorization alone does not solve semantic cross-contamination. An HK agent can be restricted to HK rows yet still answer with a Singapore-specific business definition if the ontology or instructions combine both domains carelessly.

Genie Ontology is a unified context layer combining modeled Unity Catalog semantics with inferred context from metric views, dashboards, SQL queries, and Genie Agents. Snippets receive authority scores and are permission gated. Genie uses only context extracted from assets the principal is permitted to see.

The bank should create entity-specific authoritative assets. Hong Kong liquidity definitions belong in HK-owned metric views, pages, and Genie Agents. Singapore definitions belong in SG-owned assets. Group definitions belong in the shared catalog and should state how entity values are consolidated, converted, eliminated, and timestamped.

A term such as available liquidity can differ by legal entity because of local reserve, settlement, encumbrance, or funding treatment. The HK ontology should define available_liquidity_hk through the approved Hong Kong metric. The SG ontology should define its own metric. A group metric should not silently reuse one entity’s definition.

Authority should be explicit. A signed treasury policy outranks an old dashboard description. A certified metric view outranks an ad hoc query. A current legal interpretation outranks a conversational note. Genie Ontology can score authority and freshness, but the organization remains responsible for source hierarchy, ownership, effective date, and supersession.

The agent should cite the data and semantic sources used. A regional answer should state whether it is an HK detail, SG detail, or approved group aggregate. The user should not have to infer the legal scope from the conversation.

Ontology isolation needs testing. The team should issue the same ambiguous prompt under HK, SG, and regional identities and verify that the answer uses the correct definition and source. A response can contain no unauthorized rows and still be legally or economically wrong because it applied the wrong entity’s meaning.

The bank should not create one omnipotent APAC agent and rely on prompts to keep it within legal boundaries. It should create bounded agents with independent identities, data permissions, tool permissions, budgets, tests, and owners.

The HK liquidity Agent Brick should use the HK service principal, HK Genie Agent, HK ontology context, HK metric views, and approved HK tools. The SG instance should use equivalent SG assets. The regional aggregate agent should use only published group aggregate products and clean-room outputs where necessary. It should not hold direct read permission on entity-level customer tables.

The system prompt should state purpose and limits, but prompts are not security policy. A user can ask the HK agent to ignore its rules. Unity Catalog, Unity Gateway, and tool authorization must make the request impossible rather than merely discouraged.

Each tool should be registered and governed. A function that retrieves account detail should be inaccessible to the group aggregate agent. A tool that writes a liquidity proposal should write only to the appropriate legal-entity workflow. A communication tool should not send externally without human approval.

MLflow tracing and agent evaluation should record model calls, tool calls, retrieved assets, SQL, latency, errors, guardrails, and outcomes subject to sensitive-data controls. Traces should use governed storage, retention, and masking. The bank should not improve agent observability by creating a second unprotected PII repository.

The SG agent should face symmetric tests. The regional agent should be tested against attempts to infer entity-level detail from repeated aggregate queries.

The phrase trusted execution context should mean more than a secure cluster. It should describe an execution in which identity, code, data, model, tools, network, logging, and output policy are known and enforced.

For an Agent Brick, the trusted context includes the dedicated service principal, registered agent service, approved model service, approved MCP or function tools, Unity Catalog data grants, ABAC policies, network egress restrictions, environment, code or agent version, and trace destination. Every production invocation should be attributable to this context.

Trusted execution should also protect against confused-deputy behavior. A low-privilege user should not be able to cause a high-privilege agent to retrieve data and return it indirectly. On-behalf-of user context, result filtering, tool-specific authorization, and purpose checks reduce this risk.

The institution should maintain an execution manifest for every agent release. It should contain agent ID, legal entity, owner, code or configuration version, model, tools, data scopes, policy versions, network policy, benchmark result, budget, and rollback version. A change to any of these can alter effective authority and should trigger review.

Databricks Clean Rooms use Unity Catalog, OpenSharing, serverless compute, and isolated execution to let collaborators analyze sensitive data without giving one another direct access to the underlying assets. In an approval-based clean room, collaborators approve code before it runs. In a packaged clean room, a provider can supply hidden code that a consumer runs against its own shared assets under a different trust model.

Although Entity HK and Entity SG belong to one bank, a clean-room pattern can be valuable when local legal owners require explicit collaboration boundaries. Each entity contributes approved tables or views. Neither receives direct access to the other’s rows. Approved notebooks calculate group-level liquidity, concentration, overlap, or stress measures in the isolated central environment.

The clean room itself does not guarantee that output is safe. An approved notebook could return a single-row group whose value identifies one transaction. A sequence of queries could differ by one person and reveal that person’s data. A model could encode sensitive information in free text. Output governance must therefore be explicit.

The bank should allow only reviewed notebooks or packaged analyses. Code should aggregate through approved functions. Output should require minimum group sizes, suppress rare categories, round or bucket sensitive measures, cap dimensions, prevent arbitrary predicates, and log every run. Raw rows should never be returned to the regional agent.

Clean Rooms operate on serverless compute and have specific network and storage requirements. If an S3 bucket is protected by a firewall or private endpoint, the institution must configure supported secure connectivity such as SecureConnect or use an approved storage arrangement. Network policy is part of the clean-room trust model.

The bank should distinguish a clean room from a general internal data share. If a group function has approved direct aggregate access, a certified group table may be simpler. A clean room is justified when collaborating owners need a stronger no-direct-access boundary, code approval, and explicit output contract.

Differential privacy provides a mathematical bound on how much the presence or absence of one individual record can affect a result. It typically adds calibrated random noise to aggregates and consumes a privacy budget represented by epsilon. Lower epsilon generally means stronger privacy and lower accuracy. Delta is another parameter in approximate differential privacy.

Databricks Clean Rooms can host approved code that implements differential privacy, but the platform should not be described as automatically adding noise to every clean-room output unless the specific deployed solution does so. The bank must select a library or implementation, define contribution bounds, choose epsilon and delta, track cumulative budget, and validate utility and attack resistance.

A regional liquidity aggregate may not require differential privacy when values are legal-entity-level and no personal data is involved. Customer concentration, transaction behavior, or small counterparty groups can require stronger protection. The method should follow the disclosure risk.

A practical control begins by limiting each subject’s contribution. Without contribution bounds, one customer can dominate an aggregate and remain inferable even after noise. The system then enforces a minimum group size, calculates an aggregate, adds calibrated noise, records privacy-budget consumption, and prevents unlimited repeated queries.

The regional agent should receive only the approved noisy or thresholded output. It should not receive the pre-noise result, privacy seed, or raw clean-room table. Differential-privacy parameters and budget usage should be part of the audit record.

The bank should test utility with domain experts. Excessive noise in liquidity data can create unsafe treasury decisions. In some cases, exact entity-level aggregate values governed by contractual access are better than noisy figures. Privacy technology should reduce disclosure risk without manufacturing false precision or false uncertainty.

The first failure mode is treating a shared metastore as shared authority. Infrastructure scope does not determine legal permission.

The second failure mode is using one regional service principal for all agents. Shared identity destroys isolation and auditability.

The third failure mode is relying on prompt instructions. Security must be enforced below the agent.

AB-410: Building Intelligent Applications — How Microsoft's New AI-First Builder Exam Replaced PL-200

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The AB-410 exam earns the Microsoft Certified: Intelligent Applications Builder Associate certification, and it tests whether a Power Platform builder can design Dataverse data models, build model-driven and canvas apps, automate business logic with cloud flows, and wire AI Hub prompts, models, and Copilot Studio agents into real business solutions. This article explains the AB-410 exam format, its three domains and weights, the 700 passing score, the AI-first topics it adds over the retired PL-200, and a practical six-week study plan for working builders.

Microsoft announced the AB-410 beta on May 27, 2026, and the exam reached general availability at the end of July 2026. The PL-200 exam it replaces retired on August 31, 2026, and a PL-200 certification already held stays on the holder's Microsoft Learn transcript but cannot be renewed. That timeline matters for anyone still studying from PL-200 material: the old exam's core of Dataverse, model-driven apps, canvas apps, and cloud flows survives, but AB-410 tests it through an AI-first lens, and the newest fifth of the exam — AI Hub prompts and models, prompt columns, generative pages, and agents created from canvas apps — has no PL-200 equivalent at all.

The shift is not cosmetic. In the PL-200 era, a functional consultant proved they could model data, build an app, and automate a process. In the AB-410 era, the same builder is expected to decide when a Copilot Studio agent belongs in the solution, when a prompt column is the right way to surface AI-generated content in a table, and when a business process flow should hand off to an agent instead of a human approver. Microsoft's own audience profile for the exam says candidates build AI-powered solutions using Copilot, agents, natural language prompts, and low-code tools, and that they collaborate with governance teams on responsible AI principles. If your Power Platform experience predates AI Hub, this exam will expose that gap quickly — which is exactly why this article spends so much of its length there.

The AB-410 exam is a 120-minute Pearson VUE test with a 700 passing score, and the exam earns the Microsoft Certified: Intelligent Applications Builder Associate credential.

Three details in that table deserve emphasis before you plan anything else. First, Microsoft has not published a question count for AB-410, so any source quoting an exact number is guessing; the honest figure is the 40–60 range Microsoft publishes for its certification portfolio. Second, the 120-minute duration is the majority figure across prep sources and matches what the certification page states, but one established prep guide still lists approximately 100 minutes — the conflict is recorded in full later in this article. Third, the credential is valid for only twelve months, and the free renewal assessment opens six months before expiry, so AB-410 is a certification you maintain, not one you earn once and forget.

The AB-410 exam replaced the PL-200 exam on August 31, 2026, because Microsoft needed a credential that tests AI-integrated Power Platform building rather than traditional low-code app development alone.

PL-200 was the Microsoft Power Platform Functional Consultant Associate exam, and for years it was the standard credential for the people who configured Dataverse, built model-driven and canvas apps, and wired up Power Automate flows for a business. It did that job well. But the platform it certified changed underneath it: Copilot arrived inside app design, AI Hub arrived with prompts and models that drop straight into flows and forms, and agents became something a business app builder was expected to reason about rather than something a data scientist built in a separate tool. A credential that never asked about any of that stopped describing the job.

Microsoft's answer was not to bolt an AI module onto PL-200. AB-410 is a genuinely new exam built around a different role: the intelligent applications builder. The audience profile in the official study guide describes a professional who creates apps, data models, and flows that connect to agents, AI models and prompts, and visualizations — and who works with environment and security administrators on policies, roles, identity, and authentication, and with governance teams on responsible AI, application lifecycle management, solutions, pipelines, and monitoring. That collaboration list is itself a signal: AB-410 expects you to operate inside a larger, more structured delivery team, not as a solo app builder.

The launch followed a fast schedule. The beta opened in April 2026, and Microsoft offered the first 300 people who took the beta exam on or before June 17, 2026 an 80% discount with the code AB410KBMaui. General availability followed at the end of July 2026, and by October 2026 the certification page no longer carried a beta label. PL-200 registration closed ahead of the August 31, 2026 retirement, and Microsoft's own retirement table names Microsoft Certified: Intelligent Applications Builder Associate as the related new credential. If you hold PL-200, nothing is taken away — but the renewal path is gone, and the market signal has moved.

One more consequence of that newness is worth stating plainly: third-party preparation for AB-410 is still thin. As of September 2026, prep providers noted that Microsoft's own practice assessment had not yet shipped, and one guide still lists it as unavailable while another reports it live on Microsoft's AI Skills Navigator. The exam is young enough that the official study guide remains the single most reliable source, and every claim in this article is checked against it first.

The AB-410 exam measures three domains, and the business application logic and automation domain carries 40–45% of the exam, so study time should follow those published weights.

Microsoft publishes the weights as ranges, not fixed percentages, and the ranges have not changed between the guide's earlier version and its December 18, 2026 version. The three domains are Create a foundation for intelligent applications at 25–30%, Create intelligent applications at 25–30%, and Build business application logic and automation at 40–45%. The arithmetic is the whole study strategy: the third domain alone is worth nearly as much as the other two combined. A study plan that spends equal time on all three domains is a plan that under-invests in almost half the exam.

The AB-410 foundation domain covers 25–30% of the exam, and the domain tests AI-enabled solution design, environment choices, application lifecycle management, and Dataverse data modeling.

This domain splits into two skill groups, and both are decision-heavy rather than click-heavy. The first group, designing Power Platform solutions by using AI-enabled tools, asks you to analyze a business requirement and identify the right components and implementation options — canvas app, model-driven app, Copilot Studio agent, Power Pages site, or some combination — to evaluate built-in agents for inclusion in the solution, to recommend extensibility options when out-of-the-box capability falls short, to recommend environment types, and to deploy a solution and application lifecycle management strategy. The exam is testing judgment here: given a scenario, which pieces fit together, and which do not.

The second group is building data models in Dataverse, and it is where the exam shows its AI-era focus most clearly in this domain. You need to create and edit tables in the data workspace, create and modify standard tables, configure table properties, create and modify columns, and configure table relationships. Then come the two skills that separate AB-410 from PL-200: configuring prompt columns, which embed AI-generated content directly into a table column, and configuring row summaries, which generate AI summaries of record data. You also need public views, main forms, and table-level security configuration.

The throughline is that "foundation" does not mean basic. It means the decisions everything else in the solution depends on — get the data model or the solution architecture wrong here, and no amount of clever automation downstream fixes it. Study this domain by building a real Dataverse model for a real business process, including at least one prompt column and one row summary, rather than by reading about tables.

The AB-410 intelligent applications domain covers 25–30% of the exam, and the domain tests model-driven apps, canvas apps, generative pages, and Copilot Studio agent integration.

This domain covers the actual app-building work, split across model-driven and canvas apps, and it is worth treating each as a genuinely distinct skill set rather than two flavors of the same thing. For model-driven apps, you need to create and configure forms and views, create generative pages using natural language — a skill that reflects how much of modern Power Apps development now starts with a natural-language prompt rather than a blank canvas — compose a model-driven app from its component parts, configure access to forms, views, and the app itself, and create and configure charts and dashboards.

For canvas apps, the exam goes well beyond connecting a data source and adding controls. Expect content on designing apps for accessibility, performance, responsiveness, and usability — all four together, not just visual polish. You need to automate business processes directly from a canvas app, build reusable components including named formulas, user-defined functions, and component libraries, manage variables and collections correctly, implement proper error handling rather than letting a flow fail silently, and test canvas apps using Monitor to diagnose real runtime issues. One skill that specifically reflects the intelligent-applications framing of this exam is creating a Copilot Studio agent directly from a canvas app, a genuinely new workflow most PL-200-era candidates never had to learn.

The practical difference between the two app types is the exam's favorite trap. Model-driven apps are metadata-driven and Dataverse-centric: forms, views, and security roles do the work. Canvas apps are formula-driven and pixel-controlled: Power Fx, variables, collections, and components do the work. Scenario questions will present a requirement and ask which app type fits, or will describe a model-driven feature and ask how its canvas equivalent behaves. Knowing both deeply, and knowing where each stops, is the whole domain.

The AB-410 business logic and automation domain carries 40–45% of the exam, and the domain tests cloud flows, AI Hub prompts and models, business rules, and business process flows.

This is the domain that decides whether you pass, simply because of its sheer weight, and it covers three distinct technical areas that all deserve serious study time. The first is cloud flows: recommending the right trigger for a given scenario, evaluating and choosing appropriate connectors, managing approval processes through flows, configuring flow actions correctly, implementing control logic with conditions and loops, and — critically — testing and troubleshooting cloud flows when they do not behave as expected. That last skill is where a lot of real-world Power Automate work actually happens, and it is tested accordingly.

The second area is prompts and models in AI Hub, the most distinctly current content on the entire exam. You need to build a prompt from a template or from blank, consume that prompt inside both apps and cloud flows, add knowledge sources to ground a prompt's responses, customize prompt settings including which underlying model it uses, add structured inputs so a prompt can be parameterized rather than static, and consume a standalone AI model — not just a prompt — inside both apps and flows. If your prior Power Platform experience predates AI Hub, this is very likely your single biggest study gap, and it deserves dedicated hands-on time rather than passive reading.

The third area is business and process logic: configuring business rules and business process flows — both long-standing Power Platform capabilities that remain fully relevant — alongside calculated, rollup, and formula columns, and evaluating which use cases call for which kind of business logic. This is the domain where experienced functional consultants coming from PL-200-era knowledge will feel most at home, but it is worth resisting the temptation to skim it just because it feels familiar, since the exam still expects precision on when each logic type is the right tool.

The AB-410 exam tests AI Hub prompts and models as a skill, and the exam expects candidates to build prompts, add knowledge sources, and consume prompts inside apps and flows.

AI Hub is the single largest new surface in AB-410, and it is the area where PL-200 study material helps least. Start with the vocabulary, because the exam uses it precisely. A prompt is a reusable instruction you build from a template or from blank, with structured inputs that let it accept parameters instead of fixed text. A model is a standalone AI model you consume directly — for text generation, classification, extraction, or similar tasks — without wrapping it in a prompt first. Knowledge is the grounding material you attach to a prompt so its responses draw on your business data rather than on general training. Prompt settings include which underlying model the prompt runs on, and changing the model changes the behavior, cost, and output quality.

The exam tests the full lifecycle, not just the definitions. You build the prompt, you add knowledge to it, you customize its settings, and then you consume it — in a canvas app, in a model-driven app, and in a cloud flow — and you do the same for a standalone model. Scenario questions will ask where a prompt belongs: in the app that shows the result to the user, or in the flow that processes the record in the background. They will ask what happens when a prompt has no knowledge source attached, and what changes when you add one. They will ask which model setting to change when the output is wrong in a specific way.

The study method that works here is building, not reading. Create a prompt from a template, then rebuild the same prompt from blank. Add a knowledge source and test the same question with and without it, so you can see grounding change the answer. Consume the prompt in a canvas app control and then in a cloud flow action, and note how the inputs and outputs differ between the two surfaces. Then consume a standalone model in both places and note what a model gives you that a prompt does not. Two focused afternoons of this will teach you more about the AI Hub skills than a week of reading descriptions.

Responsible AI belongs in this section too, because the exam ties it to the same features. Building a solution that calls an AI Hub prompt or model, or that hands off to a Copilot Studio agent, introduces governance questions a traditional canvas-app-plus-Dataverse solution never raised: what data is the model grounded on, who can configure or modify a prompt's knowledge sources, and how is AI-generated content in a prompt column or row summary reviewed before it is trusted. The exam does not expect you to be a governance specialist, but it does expect you to think about these questions when you design a solution, and the audience profile explicitly calls out collaboration with governance teams on responsible AI principles.

The AB-410 exam tests model-driven apps and canvas apps as distinct skills, and the exam expects candidates to choose the right app type for each business requirement.

The cleanest way to hold the distinction is to ask who controls the layout. In a model-driven app, Dataverse metadata controls the layout: tables, forms, views, and security roles define what the user sees, and the app is assembled from those components. In a canvas app, you control the layout: every control is placed by hand, every behavior is written in Power Fx, and responsiveness, accessibility, and performance are your design decisions rather than platform defaults. The exam tests whether you know which model fits a given requirement, and the wrong choice in a scenario question is usually the app type that could technically do the job but fights the requirement.

Model-driven apps fit requirements that are data-shaped: a team working through records in a table, with forms for detail, views for lists, charts and dashboards for oversight, and role-based access deciding who sees which form or view. Generative pages fit here too — the exam specifically lists creating generative pages using natural language as a model-driven skill, which means describing the page you want and letting the platform generate it. Canvas apps fit requirements that are experience-shaped: a custom interface for a specific task, a mobile-first form for field workers, a screen that automates a business process inline, or a reusable component library shared across several apps.

The canvas-app skills list is the more demanding of the two, and it rewards hands-on practice. Named formulas, user-defined functions, and component libraries are relatively advanced Power Fx capabilities, and the exam lists all three. Variables and collections must be managed correctly — knowing when state lives in a variable, when it lives in a collection, and when it should live in neither because Dataverse should hold it. Error handling must be implemented deliberately, so a failed flow or connector call surfaces a message instead of failing silently. And Monitor is the named testing tool: you test a canvas app by running it under Monitor and reading what actually happened at runtime, not by staring at the formula bar.

Study both app types by building the same small business process twice — once as a model-driven app and once as a canvas app — and writing down every point where the two builds diverge. That comparison is the exam in miniature.

The AB-410 exam tests Dataverse modeling in depth, and the exam expects candidates to distinguish calculated, rollup, and formula columns and to configure prompt columns and row summaries.

Dataverse is the foundation the whole exam stands on, and the questions here punish shallow knowledge more than anywhere else except the automation domain. The column-type distinction is the classic trap, and it is worth learning cold. A calculated column computes its value from other columns on the same record, in real time, using a formula — it is for values derived from the record itself. A rollup column aggregates values from related records — a sum, count, average, minimum, or maximum across a relationship — and it is computed on a schedule, not instantly. A formula column is the newer type: it uses Power Fx, it can reference columns across related tables, and it behaves like a calculated column with a more expressive language. Scenario questions will describe a requirement and ask which column type fits; the wrong answers will always be plausible, and the deciding detail will be whether the value comes from the same record, from related records, or needs Power Fx expressions.

Relationships and security are the other half of the foundation. You need to configure table relationships correctly — one-to-many, many-to-many, and the self-referential cases that trip people up — and to configure table-level security so the right roles see the right records. The exam also tests the newer AI-flavored table features in this context: prompt columns that embed AI-generated content into a table, and row summaries that generate AI summaries of record data. Know what each one produces, where its output appears, and what governs it — a prompt column's output depends on its prompt and its inputs, and a row summary depends on the record's data.

Environment types belong here too, since the exam lists recommending environment types as a foundation skill. Development, test, and production environments exist for a reason, and the exam will present scenarios where the wrong environment choice creates a governance or data problem. Pair that with the solution and application lifecycle management strategy the exam also lists: solutions package components for movement between environments, pipelines automate the promotion, and the whole structure exists so a change can be tested before it reaches production users. Study Dataverse by modeling a real process end to end — tables, relationships, the right column types, security roles, a prompt column, and a row summary — and then moving it through environments as a solution.

The AB-410 exam tests cloud flows and business logic across its heaviest domain, and the exam expects candidates to pick correct triggers, connectors, approvals, and control logic for each scenario.

Cloud flows reward the candidate who has actually debugged one. The exam's skill list reads like a working consultant's week: recommend the right trigger for a scenario, evaluate and recommend connectors, manage approvals through flows, configure flow actions, implement control logic with conditions and loops, and test and troubleshoot flows. The trigger question is the most common trap — an automated trigger, an instant trigger, and a scheduled trigger each fit different requirements, and a scenario that says "when a record is created" is pointing at a different trigger than one that says "every Monday at 9 a.m." or "when a user presses a button in the app." Connectors are the second trap: standard connectors, premium connectors, and custom connectors have different licensing and availability implications, and the exam expects you to notice which one a scenario requires.

Approvals deserve their own study pass. Managing an approval process through a cloud flow means knowing the approval types, what happens when an approver does not respond, and how the flow branches on approval versus rejection. Control logic is the same story: conditions and loops are simple to describe and easy to get wrong in a scenario, especially when a loop must process a collection of records and handle the failure of a single item without failing the whole run. And troubleshooting is explicitly listed as a skill, so practice reading a failed flow run, finding the action that failed, and explaining why — that diagnostic sequence is itself an exam skill.

Business rules and business process flows are the quieter half of this domain, and they are easy to under-study because they feel familiar. A business rule applies logic at the form level: show or hide a field, set a value, lock a field, based on conditions on the record. A business process flow guides a user through stages of a process, with steps and branching, and it is for processes where the path matters as much as the outcome. The exam's skill is evaluating use cases for business logic — knowing when a business rule fits, when a business process flow fits, when a cloud flow fits, and when a calculated, rollup, or formula column is the right tool instead. The four options will all appear in scenario questions, and the deciding detail will be where the logic runs and who experiences it.

Databricks Lakebase Safekeeper, Pageserver, and LTAP Architecture

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This guide explains how Databricks Lakebase separates PostgreSQL compute from durable storage, how Safekeepers and Pageservers participate in write and read paths, how log sequence numbers support versioned page reconstruction and branching, and how Lakebase connects transactional data to lakehouse analytics.

The implementation exercises use Databricks Free Edition as a perpetual, no-cost, serverless-only learning environment. Free Edition is appropriate for learning PostgreSQL behavior, Lakebase concepts, synthetic transaction workloads, branches, Unity Catalog tables, and analytical queries. It is not a production database service-level environment.

Lakebase does not simply write PostgreSQL .tar data chunks to local compute disks. Its PostgreSQL compute layer is stateless with respect to durable database storage. Durable state is maintained by a distributed storage layer made up of Safekeepers, Pageservers, and cloud object storage.

Safekeepers replicate and persist PostgreSQL write-ahead log records before a transaction commit is acknowledged. Databricks documents quorum-based WAL replication across availability zones. The internal consensus and replication implementation should be described using the official term “quorum replication” unless a specific Databricks source explicitly identifies a Paxos or Raft implementation.

Pageservers ingest WAL, maintain versioned page history, reconstruct PostgreSQL pages at a requested log sequence number, and use cloud object storage as the durable storage foundation. Pageservers are not the same as an application-visible Delta Lake or Apache Iceberg table.

Lakebase LTAP can expose transactional changes to the lakehouse through managed capabilities such as Lakebase Change Data Feed. This is different from claiming that every PostgreSQL INSERT is synchronously converted into a Delta or Iceberg Parquet file before commit.

Operational and analytical workloads can use a unified storage and governance architecture, but “zero pipeline” does not mean zero processing, zero latency, or literal zero data movement. Managed services still capture changes, materialize analytical formats, track freshness, and handle schema evolution.

Absolute point-in-time synchronization between a front-office order book and a back-office risk calculation must not be assumed. Production systems require explicit snapshot identifiers, source commit positions, LSN watermarks, event-time controls, and measured freshness.

Traditional PostgreSQL combines the query process, buffer cache, WAL handling, and database data files around one host or tightly attached storage system.

Before acknowledging a commit, PostgreSQL writes the transaction's WAL record to durable storage. WAL provides a sequential recovery record and allows PostgreSQL to recover modified pages after a failure.

PostgreSQL also maintains relation data files that materialize the current state of table and index pages. A normal PostgreSQL page is commonly 8 KB.

The traditional architecture is operationally simple, but compute and storage scaling are coupled. Creating a full branch, read replica, or point-in-time copy can require substantial copying, replay, or storage provisioning.

Lakebase retains PostgreSQL transaction semantics while separating the compute and durable storage responsibilities.

The Lakebase compute node runs a PostgreSQL-compatible query engine.

The compute node is stateless with respect to authoritative durable database storage. It can cache pages in memory and maintain temporary local runtime state, but the durable database does not depend on one compute instance's local disk.

Stateless does not mean that a compute interruption is invisible to every application. Applications should use connection retry, idempotency, and transaction retry patterns appropriate to PostgreSQL.

When an application executes an INSERT, UPDATE, or DELETE, PostgreSQL changes pages in memory and emits WAL records describing the changes.

Instead of relying on one compute node's local WAL disk as the durable authority, the compute node streams WAL to multiple Safekeeper nodes.

A Safekeeper is a specialized WAL persistence service. It receives WAL, persists it, participates in replication, tracks LSN progress, and acknowledges durable receipt.

Lakebase acknowledges a committed transaction only after the required storage durability condition is satisfied. Databricks describes this as writing to multiple locations across availability zones and recording committed transactions durably before acknowledging them.

Exact exposed metric names can vary. Use the documented Lakebase monitoring interfaces rather than assuming all internal LSN values are available to clients.

Do not design an application around a fixed number of Safekeepers. Databricks manages the storage topology and redundancy.

Pageservers receive or retrieve durable WAL and transform it into a page-version history suitable for scalable reads and long-term storage.

The storage model is log structured and non-overwriting. Historical versions are retained under the service's retention and branching model rather than updating one authoritative page file in place.

A page version is addressed by a database object and page identity together with an LSN.

The internal implementation is managed by Databricks. Users should not build direct dependencies on private layer-file formats, local index structures, or undocumented storage APIs.

Incoming page changes and WAL-derived records are first accumulated in memory and organized for persistent storage.

When internal thresholds are reached, the storage engine creates immutable layer files.

A delta layer represents changes over a defined key and LSN range.

An image layer contains complete page images at a particular historical boundary. Image layers reduce how many deltas must be replayed for a read.

Compaction merges or reorganizes historical layers so reads do not need to traverse an unbounded sequence of small deltas.

Layer files can be uploaded to managed cloud object storage such as Amazon S3, Azure Data Lake Storage, or Google Cloud Storage, depending on the Databricks cloud.

Cloud object storage is the durable foundation. Pageservers provide the indexed and cached serving layer used by compute.

The thresholds, compaction schedules, index structures, and file sizes are service internals. Do not hard-code an assumption such as “every 1 GB creates one layer” into an institutional design unless the current Databricks documentation provides that contract.

When a required page is not present in the compute node's buffer cache, the compute layer requests the page from storage for a specific LSN.

The Pageserver finds the newest image layer at or before the requested LSN.

It then locates the relevant delta records after the image LSN and no later than the requested LSN.

The Pageserver replays those changes in order and reconstructs a standard PostgreSQL page.

The reconstructed page is returned to the compute node and can be cached in the PostgreSQL buffer cache.

This model supports historical page versions and branch timelines without maintaining a full physical copy for every compute or branch.

Do not promise that every page reconstruction completes in a fixed number of milliseconds. Latency depends on cache state, layer lookup, delta depth, object-storage access, network conditions, page history, and service load.

Lakebase branches use copy-on-write storage metadata.

A new branch references an existing parent timeline at a selected point. The branch initially shares the parent's historical storage rather than copying every database page.

New writes create a separate branch history.

A branch is not a Git repository. “Git-style branching” is an analogy for fast, isolated, copy-on-write histories. Database branches do not automatically provide text merges, semantic conflict resolution, or an unrestricted merge-to-production operation.

Amazon Resale

Formerly known as Amazon Warehouse, Amazon Resale is where Amazon sends customer returns. The trick to using Resale is understanding its strict grading system.

If you buy an item rated as Used—Like New, it means the product itself is in perfect, untouched condition, but the original shrink wrap is broken or the cardboard box has a minor dent.

You get a massive discount simply because the packaging isn’t pretty enough to sit on a retail shelf. If you’re buying tech gear or kitchen appliances where the box just goes straight into the recycling bin anyway, it’s the easiest way to save hundreds. Amazon Outlet

Unlike Resale, everything in Amazon Outlet is brand new. This is where Amazon dumps seasonal overstock and excess inventory that’s taking up too much physical space in its fulfillment centers.

Finding it is the hard part. It isn’t advertised on the home page. You have to type amazon.com/outlet directly into your browser or dig deep through the Deals and Savings menu on the mobile app.

Once you’re in, you’ll find everything from holiday decorations to winter apparel marked down by 50% to 70%. It’s the absolute best spot to hunt for stocking stuffers and white elephant gifts without breaking the bank.

Woot

Amazon bought Woot back in 2010, but it still operates as a standalone site at woot.com. It’s a dedicated daily deal platform that runs flash sales on electronics, tools, and apparel until the inventory sells out.

If you’re hunting for a refurbished television, a discounted sound bar, or cheap smart home gear, Woot should be your first stop every morning. Amazon Haul

If you’ve ever been tempted to buy cheap stocking stuffers off Temu or Shein but didn’t want to hand your credit card to an overseas app, you’ll be interested to know that Amazon built a direct competitor right inside its own ecosystem.

It’s called Amazon Haul, and the gimmick is simple: Every item comes from outside the U.S., which often means cheaper, direct-from-manufacturer pricing.

While his vast cinematic output is full of certified bangers even in 2026, there's nothing that hits quite as hard as his first true look back on things. NASA’s Curiosity rover, expected to last 23 months, has now climbed 1 kilometer up a Martian mountain 15 years after launch

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Curiosity rover has now spent more than 5,000 sols on Mars, gained 0.62 miles of elevation on Mount Sharp and drilled its 49th hole into Martian rock.

On November 26, 2011, NASA launched the Curiosity rover toward Mars. When it left Earth, it was the largest and most capable rover ever sent to the planet, according to NASA.

The car-size robot landed in Gale Crater on August 6, 2012. Its job was to find out whether Mars was ever able to support microbial life.

NASA described that first phase as a “23-month primary mission.” Fifteen years after launch, Curiosity is still working, and it is now one kilometer (.62 miles) up the side of a Martian mountain.

Curiosity did not need all 23 months. NASA said in 2013 that evidence of a past environment well suited to support microbial life came “within the first eight months” of the primary mission, from the first rock sample ever drilled on Mars.

“We now know Mars offered favorable conditions for microbial life billions of years ago,” the mission’s project scientist, John Grotzinger, said at the time.

On June 24, 2014, the rover completed its first Martian year, which lasts 687 Earth days. By then it had driven almost 8 kilometers (about 5 miles), according to a NASA video from the team, and was heading for the base of a mountain.

That mountain is Mount Sharp, a peak about 3 miles (5 kilometers) tall in the middle of Gale Crater. Curiosity has been climbing it since 2014.

On August 26, 2026, the rover “marked an elevation gain of 1 kilometer in its journey from Gale Crater on Mars up the slope of Mount Sharp,” NASA said.

That is one kilometer of height above the crater floor, not distance driven. NASA called the milestone the high point of the mission so far.

William Farrand, a senior research scientist at the Space Science Institute who was on the science team for the earlier Spirit and Opportunity rovers, put the climb in perspective on NASA’s Curiosity blog.

When the Spirit rover reached the top of Husband Hill in August 2005, it had climbed 106 meters (about 348 feet) above its landing site. Curiosity has passed 1,000 meters (about 0.62 miles).

Three days later, on August 29, Curiosity marked 5,000 Martian days, or sols, on Mars. Because a day on Mars lasts 24.6 hours, that is more than 5,137 Earth days, the blog noted.

The rover keeps photographing the mountain as it goes. Earth.com has reported on Curiosity’s photos of Martian cliffs with a layer in the wrong color.

The rover is still collecting samples. After drilling in late October, the team wrote on the mission blog: “We successfully drilled our 49th drill hole, called ‘Nazca.'”

The team chose that rock because it was full of small disc-shaped forms that they interpret as possible crystals. If they are crystals, the team wrote, they could be “very interesting indicators of the environmental conditions.”

In mid-November, the team said it was time to “hit the Martian road again and resume our climb up Mount Sharp.”

The rocks in its path are covered in small polygons with raised ridges, a texture that can form when water is lost from minerals, among other causes, according to the blog.

“After over 5,000 sols of exploring with Curiosity I’ve learned Mars is full of surprises,” said Abigail Fraeman, the mission’s deputy project scientist, in the post.

Curiosity has company on Mars. NASA’s younger rover, Perseverance, has now driven farther than any vehicle on another world.

Before The Lord of the Rings, Tolkien Wrote an Epic Christmas Saga for His Kids

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

When J.R.R. Tolkien first comes to mind, Middle-earth soon follows. The literary genius who crafted The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings is easily best known for his medieval-inspired work of high fantasy that wove everything from his personal experience during the Great War to his devoted Christian faith into one cohesive mythological tapestry.

However, sometime after he returned home and before he ever published his first novel, Tolkien spun fantastical tales to his children every holiday season leading up to Christmas, which would eventually be collected by his future daughter-in-law, editor Baillie Tolkien, in The Father Christmas Letters, published originally in 1976. Aside from showing another side to Tolkien, the letters also feature some ideas that would later shape Middle-earth.

As noted in the introduction to The Letters from Father Christmas, Tolkien's tradition of annual correspondence from the North Pole began in 1920 when the professor, under the guise of the holiday icon, sent his first letter to his oldest son, John. This continued as Tolkien and his wife, Edith, welcomed more children into their home throughout the decade, as Michael, Christopher, and Priscilla were eventually born. That first letter was quite brief but opened the door to the winter wonderland that Tolkien envisioned. Dated the 22nd of December, Father Christmas — who is later revealed to age with the holiday itself, and is as old as the very first Christmas in Bethlehem — sent Tolkien's eldest son a brief letter with artwork included (a colorful self-portrait and an image of his home in the North Pole). Of course, as the letters progress, more detail is added.

With each year, Father Christmas sent his regards to the children, and with each year, Tolkien infused letters with more hijinks and adventures than the last. The titular figure's own perspective is often juxtaposed with that of the North Polar Bear (also called the Great Polar Bear), his long-time friend and bumbling assistant, as well as his well-written Elven secretary, Ilbereth. Of course, other characters soon followed, as Tolkien expanded the rich, holiday mythology with North Polar Bear's nephews, the "Rory Bory Aylis" fireworks, the presence of Goblins, living snowmen, Red Elves, the Cliff House, and many other unique additions that enhance these glimpses into the love and care the author put into every one of his stories — even those he never thought the world would see.

What began decades earlier as a fun tradition between the professor and his children eventually concluded in 1943 with a final letter penned to his youngest child and only daughter, Priscilla. Here, Father Christmas and the North Polar Bear bid her farewell near the decline of the Second World War, not because they were going away, but because it was time for the tradition to end. Indeed, events from the war itself spill over into the final few letters, with Father Christmas even noting that the Goblins believed the conflict was enough cover to capture the North Pole — though they had not counted on Father Christmas' "Windbeam" and great number of allies. Tolkien used the battle as cover to explain why certain presents and stocking-stuffers did not end up in the Tolkien home on Christmas morning.

Of course, anyone familiar with Tolkien's work can see how some of the worldbuilding aspects of The Father Christmas Letters carried over into his larger legendarium. For one thing, he includes not only Elves and Goblins (as well as a reference to an Elven language), but also "The Man on the Moon," a folktale noted in The Fellowship of the Ring and other Tolkien works. Since The Hobbit itself was published in 1937, it's likely that Tolkien may have worked out some of these ideas in these letters before fastening them to his copy. Indeed, after the release of the novel, he further expands on the conflicts between the North Pole and the Goblin threat, though it could have just as easily had more to do with World War II than any connections to Middle-earth.

One might even go a step further with the influence of Tolkien's Father Christmas epic — which also dabbled in the same sort of rhythmic, poetic material one would find in The Lord of the Rings — on the world of fantasy at large. Of course, Tolkien wasn't the only one to connect the world of Christmas with the fantastical traditions of fairy tales and folklore. The Wizard of Oz author L. Frank Baum had already done so in his 1902 novel, The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus. Yet, Tolkien's efforts for his children may have had an effect on his longtime friend and fellow Inklings member C.S. Lewis. Although we do not know for certain if Lewis was at all privy to any of these letters, we do know that the author saw in Father Christmas the same sort of heroic, charitable, and iconic traits. So, just as he utilized Tolkien's invention of Númenor in his 1945 novel That Hideous Strength, so too did he put Father Christmas in The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe in 1950.

But Tolkien cared deeply about Father Christmas, and was quite careful regarding the way he portrayed the mythic holiday figure. According to George Sayer in his book Jack: A Life of C.S. Lewis, Tolkien detested The Chronicles of Narnia for Lewis' "careless" collection of different mythologies, pulling together too many ideas from different points of cultural and historical origin. That criticism was especially true regarding the appearance of Father Christmas himself. Tolkien may have been too harsh, but it's difficult to blame a man who spent decades writing Father Christmas tales for his children, which, for the author, recalled a time when they were young and interested in such fantastical adventures. Little did he know, children around the world would enjoy his stories all these years later. With the holidays at hand, there’s no better time to start a new Tolkien-flavored Christmas tradition — after all, who knows what sort of adventures it may inspire?

India uses $1.62 billion in Nepal aid to lock in regional influence—and perhaps ward off China, analysts say

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

India announced a $1.62 billion economic package for flood-hit Nepal on Nov. 23, pairing reconstruction grants with a credit line for energy projects, the latest move in a decades-old contest with China over the Himalayan country’s future.

The floods that hit Nepal in late August were among the worst disasters in the country’s recorded history, killing at least 1,300 people. An estimated 84,270 people, including over 32,000 children, were affected by the floods, according to UNICEF.

“It has been three months and families are still working hard to rebuild, but efforts are hampered by the loss of assets and livelihoods they depend on,” Andrea Berardo, the World Food Programme’s deputy country director in Nepal, said in a Nov. 24 statement. “With winter approaching in Nepal’s highlands, many families are still living in temporary shelters and are facing increasingly harsh conditions.”

The money is about more than rebuilding damaged roads and homes. India—already Nepal’s largest trading partner—is using disaster aid to reinforce its ties with a neighbor that China has courted for years, and to keep Nepal’s economic future closely linked to its own, analysts say.

India’s package to Nepal includes a $377 million grant for post-disaster reconstruction and new infrastructure projects, along with a $1.24 billion line of credit for energy projects, according to a joint statement by the two countries.

Nepal is likely to use the grant for reconstruction and the credit line—which carries preferential terms and sourcing requirements from India—for infrastructure, notes Constantino Xavier, a nonresident fellow at the Brookings Institution’s foreign policy program. The credit line could also foster cross-border supply chains and support Nepal’s industrialization and manufacturing ambitions, which are essential to creating jobs in one of Asia’s youngest countries.

The announcement followed talks in New Delhi between Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Nepalese Prime Minister Balendra Shah. It was the first official bilateral trip abroad for the 36-year-old rapper-turned-politician since he took office in March, after 2025’s youth-led protests against corruption.

“Our commitment will be to help affected families get their lives back on track, create new opportunities and further strengthen Nepal’s capacity to deal with future disasters,” Modi said to reporters.

Shah thanked India for consistently acting as a first responder to Nepal during “times of natural disasters and difficult hours”, and invited Modi to visit Nepal. India was the first foreign government to respond to the August floods, sending relief supplies and helping restore communications.

A Post-Disaster Needs Assessment released this month by Nepal’s National Planning Commission estimates recovery and reconstruction will cost 706.65 billion Nepali rupees ($5.2 billion)—more than 10% of Nepal’s 2025 GDP of $45.49 billion, according to the World Bank.

India and Nepal have maintained close economic, diplomatic and security ties since they signed the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship; bilateral trade reached $9.38 billion in the 2025–26 fiscal year.

After a 2015 earthquake in Nepal that killed more than 8,000 people, India donated $1 billion for post-earthquake reconstruction. Yet five months later, many Nepalis blamed India for a fuel shortage, accusing New Delhi of imposing an undeclared blockade after Nepal adopted a new constitution. India denied responsibility, saying protests on the Nepali side of the border were blocking its trucks.

India has long used money to play a central role in South Asia. It provided nearly $4 billion in aid to Sri Lanka in 2022, as the tourism-dependent island nation faced its worst economic crisis in more than seven decades.

“It reflects India’s growing geoeconomic engagement with its neighbors, especially in times of crises, as also during Sri Lanka’s 2022 financial default,” Xavier says of the Nepal pledge.

There may also be a geopolitical motivation behind India’s generosity. Kunda Dixit, former editor of the Nepali Times, told Reuters that India “may be trying to ensure that [Shah] does not lean too much towards China.”

China and Nepal share an 878-mile border across the Himalayas. China accounts for nearly half of Nepal’s foreign direct investment and is the country’s second-largest trading partner, though trade is heavily skewed in Beijing’s favor.

Beijing has also identified 10 projects for potential joint implementation under its Belt and Road Initiative, including a proposed railway linking Kathmandu with Tibet, road corridors and a cross-border electricity transmission line. China also responded to the floods by providing cash and relief supplies, and sending more than 20 rescue and forensic experts.

In Nepal, India disbursed more than four times as much aid as China in the 2024–25 fiscal year, according to Nepali government data cited by Reuters.

“I don’t think China will launch a checkbook competition with India in Nepal,” Yun Sun, a senior fellow and co-director of the China Program at the Stimson Center, says. “Influence and presence are measured by long-term, consistent involvement, not a one-time deal. The region would benefit from contributions from all great powers, instead of a single package.”

“Many of the smaller South Asian states, including Nepal, rely substantively on India for development and humanitarian assistance,” Michael Kugelman, a resident senior fellow for South Asia at the Atlantic Council, says. “India’s new aid package is the latest reminder that even with China deepening its footprint in South Asia, New Delhi continues to leverage reliance on its largesse to ensure ties with its neighbors—or at least most of them—remain in a good place.”

Moon phase today: What the Moon will look like on November 28, 2026

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Moon is looking noticeably less full now, although most of its surface is still illuminated. We're still in the Waning Gibbous phase, with the Moon getting a little smaller each night as we head towards the Third Quarter.

As of Saturday, Nov. 28, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Waning Gibbous phase, with 81% of its surface visible.

If you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Serenitatis, Oceanus Procellarum, as well as the Tycho Crater. With binoculars, you'll also see the Gassendi Crater, the Clavius Crater, and the Archimedes Crater. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 11 and 15 landing spots, plus the Fra Mauro Highlands.

In North America, the next Full Moon will take place on December 23. In other time zones it will fall on December 24.

It takes around 29.5 days for the Moon to move through all its phases, according to NASA. Throughout this period, the Moon's position relative to Earth and the Sun determines how much of its illuminated surface we can see. Starting with a New Moon, the visible portion gradually increases until it reaches a Full Moon, before shrinking again as the cycle comes back around.

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

First Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

He singles out the Philippines for its consistency in developing talent and keeping a presence abroad.

Isfansyah is also one of Indonesia’s leading independent producers. “I believe countries need to recognize cinema as an important part of their cultural strategy, not merely as an industry or a source of national prestige,” he says. “This requires long-term investment, institutional support, and a commitment to building sustainable connections with the international film community.”

JAFF’s 21st edition drew more than 1,000 submissions, from which the festival picked 254 films from 34 countries. The program runs under the theme “Influx,” opening with Lawrence Fajardo’s Filipino drama “Silenced Nights” and closing with Ueda Makoto’s Japanese comedy-drama “You Are the Film.”

Isfansyah says the current social, political and economic climate pushed the team to rethink what staging a festival should mean this year. “We did not want JAFF to become a celebration disconnected from the realities around us. This is not a moment for an excessively festive atmosphere or for pretending that everything is fine.”

Fajardo’s film, he says, speaks directly to present-day anxieties. Opening with it was meant to signal that “cinema must remain connected to the world we live in, and a festival should not be afraid to confront difficult realities.”

The closer is pitched partly at younger viewers, as a reminder of where JAFF came from. “JAFF began with a love for films like this, films that may not necessarily be grand in scale or production, but possess sincerity, imagination, and a strong cinematic voice,” Isfansyah says. “I think it is important to remember that a film does not have to be big to be powerful.”

Among the submissions, he noticed filmmakers growing bolder, especially in shorts, with more of them playing with form and blurring fiction and nonfiction. “Short films are increasingly becoming spaces for creative risk-taking,” he says, rather than mere stepping stones to features.

Many films were also more political, though not always in the sense of tackling institutions or specific events. “There is a growing urgency to tell stories that respond to the realities surrounding us.”

The “Influx” theme also nods to change across Asian cinema, from a new generation pushing against established storytelling to shifts in how films get financed and reach audiences. “However, I think it is important to look beyond the idea of change as simply a matter of technological or industrial transformation. Commercial hits, he adds, pull more people into cinemas, lift interest in local titles and draw investment, while challenging independent work keeps the industry diverse and credible.

Premieres matter, but they have never been JAFF’s main yardstick. What counts is whether a film merits a slot and whether it has already had a fair shot at the festival’s audience. “If we believe a film has not yet had the opportunity to reach JAFF’s viewers, we will be interested in bringing it here, regardless of whether it has already premiered elsewhere.”

As it enters its third decade, the festival wants to put back into the ecosystem that built it. This year it launched JAFF Kids, aimed at children and young audiences, and JAFF Awards, which honors individuals, institutions and initiatives in Indonesia and the wider region that have helped develop film culture. Earlier programs JAFF Community Forum and JAFF Market grew strong enough to become parallel events in their own right.

However large JAFF gets, Isfansyah says it has to keep resting on emerging talent, industry professionals and academics, and keep all of them talking to one another.

“For me, being community-driven does not mean remaining small. It means remembering that the festival exists because of the people who build it, participate in it, and give it meaning.”

The Oscar-winning Conclave was a surprising front-runner in a year dominated by flashier films like Anora and The Brutalist, and its source material is just as disarmingly good. The premise of a papal conclave held right after the pope’s death might feel a bit too somber, but Conclave unfolds like a tense political thriller that balances breakneck pacing with intricate detail. Mystic River is a book that delves beneath the surface, compelling us to understand how grief carries on into adulthood. There’s immense thematic weight to this book about the tussle between choice and fatalism in the face of injustice.

FIRST (the Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams) initially forecast a record number of CVEs in 2026. But here's the thing: the raw count almost doesn't matter anymore. What matters is which vulnerabilities are actually exploitable and relevant to your environment. That's a prioritization problem --- which means it's a human review time problem.

That's the part that keeps me up at night.

A June 2026 survey of 168 cybersecurity professionals conducted by Filigran at Infosecurity Europe found that 25% identified validating whether risks are real as one of their biggest time sinks. Another 26% pointed to chasing false positives and low-priority alerts.

Meanwhile, 41% identified AI-powered attacks at scale as their biggest security concern.

So defenders are dealing with an increasingly difficult prioritization problem while worrying about attackers using AI to scale their operations.

You can't hire your way out of every problem. Security teams have limited time, limited resources, and a growing number of findings competing for their attention.

So the obvious answer is "throw AI at it," right?

Let me be blunt: I think most AI-based audit tooling today would fail a production bar. Not because the models are dumb --- because the failure modes are brutal in this specific domain:

Hallucinated findings. An AI that invents a vulnerability isn't just wrong, it costs you engineering hours to debunk. Do that twice and your team ignores the tool forever --- which is worse than having no tool, because now you've got false confidence in your skepticism.

Missed real vulnerabilities. The flip side, and scarier. A missed SQLi in an audit report doesn't just sit there --- it can create the false impression that someone checked and everything is fine.

Non-reproducibility. Same codebase, same prompt, different findings on Tuesday than Monday. How do you regression-test an auditor whose judgment drifts? And how do you distinguish a genuine improvement from a change in model behavior?

No universally trusted evaluation baseline. We still don't have a universally accepted way to answer "was this audit actually good?" That makes evaluating AI auditors particularly difficult. We need reliable ways to measure detection rates, false positives, missed vulnerabilities, and consistency across repeated tests.

None of this is a reason to give up. It's the spec for the problem.

In the same Filigran survey, only 8% of security professionals said they'd trust AI to make security decisions without human approval --- and honestly? Good. I agree with the 92%.

Autonomy is not the goal. Trustworthy assistance is.

Not because AI can't help there --- because we haven't earned that level of trust yet.

What Security Teams Can Monitor for CVE-2026-123168

📅 Nov 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

CVE-2026-123168 is a SolarWinds Serv-U insufficient validation flaw that can escalate a high-privilege application account to root on Linux. SolarWinds fixed it in Serv-U 2026.4. Detection has to work around an important gap: the public advisory does not identify the exact malformed input or vulnerable request.

The GitHub Advisory Database scores the flaw 9.1 with CVSS 3.1 vector AV:N/AC:L/PR:H/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H. The vulnerable interaction is reachable over the network, has low complexity, and requires high privileges. No user interaction is required. An unauthenticated request stream is not the right detection premise.

Monitoring should focus on privileged Serv-U identities and the transition from application activity to host-level behavior. That does not mean every administrator login is suspicious. It means the logs should make an unusual pattern visible before a routine administrative action becomes a host compromise.

Collect authentication events, role changes, administrative actions, service starts, configuration changes, file-transfer metadata, and operating system process or file events from each Serv-U host. Preserve source time, user identity, session identifier, host, and version with the event. A log without an attributable identity or host cannot support a useful investigation.

For Linux, include authentication, sudo, process execution, and file-integrity sources where available. For Windows, include account, service, process, and file audit sources. The exact products vary by environment, but the coverage questions remain: who acted, on which server, with what privilege, and what changed on the host.

High-privilege accounts are part of the exploit condition. Alert on new administrator roles, restored accounts, permission grants, and authentication from unexpected locations. Review dormant privileged users and shared credentials. A login from a known administrator may be normal, while a dormant identity becoming active near a configuration change deserves review.

The public record does not specify whether one particular built-in role is required. Avoid signatures that assume a role name that the sources do not name. Use the vendor's permission model and the local administrative design to define the set of identities with high privilege.

The significant outcome is root access on Linux. Detection should therefore include new processes running as root, unexpected changes to service binaries or configuration, new local users or authorized keys, and modifications to audit or logging configuration. These events are generic because the public exploit detail is absent. A generic signal is still useful when it is correlated with Serv-U identity and session activity.

Do not automatically label a root event as exploitation of this CVE. Legitimate maintenance can produce the same effects. Correlation, change records, and the installed version help separate expected administration from an intrusion.

INCIBE lists Serv-U 2026.3 and earlier as affected. Security telemetry should carry the current Serv-U release so an alert can identify whether the host was patched. The 2026.4 release notes confirm the fix. A log search across unversioned hosts will produce results that cannot be prioritized.

Record the patch state separately from the detection state. A host can have suspicious activity and a current version, or an old version and no observed alert. Each fact answers a different question.

The broad count can help identify externally observable instances, but it cannot prove a version, role, or compromise. The zero filter is not evidence that internal monitoring is unnecessary.

External exposure and internal telemetry should be joined through the asset inventory. If an external match corresponds to a known host, verify the version and management path. If no match exists, continue to inspect internal management networks, because non-public instances can still carry the flaw.

For a suspected case, collect the Serv-U version, relevant sessions, role assignments, host authentication, process and file events, configuration changes, and network connections around the alert. Record the collection time and preserve logs before cleanup. The exact malformed request may not be visible, so the investigation should rely on identity, host, and outcome rather than a single packet signature.

The absence of a public exploit signature should not lead teams to invent one. A disciplined packet based on the known privilege boundary is more defensible.

TORONTO — To say the Nets have had a disappointing start to the season would be an understatement.

But on Thanksgiving, the Nets can at least be happy about struggling Egor Dëmin having a bounce-back performance. Probably the best outing of his heretofore underwhelming campaign.

"The guys off the bench, Egor, Noah [Clowney], they were amazing," coach Jordi Fernández said after the Nets' slump-busting victory in Milwaukee.

The Nets snapped their five-game skid that had been tied for the worst in the NBA. And Dëmin showed promising signs of snapping out of his own not-unrelated malaise, coming into Friday's game in Toronto.

Perhaps most auspicious was the fact that it wasn't just a hot shooting showing, but an all-around night.

Part of Dëmin's allure as the eighth overall pick was his size, a 6-foot-9 guard who could not just space the floor but rebound and use his wingspan on defense.

That's happened all too infrequently so far. But it did Wednesday against the Bucks.

Dëmin finished with 15 points, four assists, three rebounds and a game-high plus-15. And despite being 0-for-5 from deep, Dëmin — who has struggled horribly inside the arc — was 5-for-6 from 2-point range. He even added feisty defense.

For a player too often faced with the question of "What can he do but shoot?" this was the answer. One that has to get repeated, starting against the Raptors.

Egor Dëmin, who scored 15 points in the Nets' win over the Bucks, drives past Ryan Rollins during the victory. AP

"Yes, he went back to the simple things and started with this physicality on defense," Fernández said. "They felt him the whole game, his length. Then he can rebound. He can be in the gaps. And then offensively, just simple.

"And I know he's capable of [hitting] every shot. Every 3 that he takes, I think it's going in. He was 0-for-5, but guess what? I think all his shots were great. So that's the way I want him to play: with energy, touching the paint, chasing the ball with his teammates, four assists. Proud of him, for sure."

Dëmin went into Milwaukee having averaged 11.6 points on just 32.2 percent shooting over his prior five games.

And his woes were the worst on the other end of the court, posting a horrid 137.9 defensive rating during that skid. Dëmin was an aggregate minus-57, with Fernández admitting the Russian needed to be better in terms of his physicality.

Dëmin acknowledged a need to improve and did so against the Bucks. His defensive engagement helped spark a game-changing third-quarter spurt and helped the Nets run — his blown dunk notwithstanding.

"Before anything else, I've got to be present mentally for this team, especially because I'm trying to step in front as a leader role, and help Julius [Randle] out from that standpoint — from that kind of perspective — and help Mike [Porter Jr.] and older guys out with the leadership role," Dëmin said.

"So first off, I've got to be in my own mind out there present, and enjoy the game. And for me, it all came from the defensive side of the game. Because we were getting stops and getting in transition — not counting that missed dunk — but we were getting wide-open opportunities that we were able to execute."

Dëmin had a block and a steal in the same game for the first time this season. He had three deflections and got in passing lanes.

And when Dëmin checked in with 5:16 left in the third and the Nets down 65-60, he helped ignite a 13-2 run. He had five points and two assists in just 1 ¹/₂ minutes, giving them the lead for good.

"Everybody was able to just put their minds together and not just focus on team defense but their own island, how we say 'guard your yard,' " Dëmin said. "That's where it came from."

The next goal is for Dëmin and the Nets not to lose it before Friday's game in Toronto.

All 12 Episodes Of Disney+'s Sci-Fi Masterpiece Are Perfect From Start To Finish

📅 Nov 27, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Disney+ crafted a sci-fi masterpiece with a 12-episode series. Disney+’s sci-fi shows tend to be spread across larger franchises, from Star Wars to Alien. Where these two franchises are concerned, they tend to produce some of the best shows on Disney+, regardless of genre; Alien: Earth received great reviews from audiences and critics alike, continuing the franchise in genuinely intriguing ways that recent feature films have neglected.

Moreover, Star Wars’ best TV show is Andor, which transcended its franchise to become one of the strongest written pieces of small-screen fiction ever made. Evidently, when it comes to big-budget, franchise sci-fi stories, Disney+ has the ability to house some true masterpieces. Another example of this came in the Marvel Cinematic Universe across 12 episodes, spread over two seasons. In 2021 and 2023, respectively, Loki seasons 1 and 2 were released on Disney+, with the collective show now being ranked among the MCU’s best TV shows.

For several reasons, Loki can be classed as a true sci-fi masterpiece. Its broader narrative rests on the shoulders of sci-fi tropes like time travel, multiversal exploration, time loops, temporal gadgets, bureaucracy, and so on. Of course, these elements ground it completely in science fiction, while the quality with which they are delivered solidifies its masterpiece status. Loki is endlessly original in how it explores both the sci-fi genre and how this can exist within the MCU, with the character writing equaling its uniqueness.

Across 12 episodes, the show doesn’t have a drop of filler, meaning everything ties to either the central narrative, the characters that inhabit it, or deeper sci-fi themes that need fleshing out. Moreover, Loki ties in seamlessly to the wider MCU, all while riding the line between setup and working as a self-contained story. Loki managed to balance all these aspects seamlessly, becoming one of the best MCU shows, one of the best on Disney+, and one of the strongest modern sci-fi masterpieces.

Ironically, the changes to the wider MCU narrative that could have rendered Loki pointless actually improve the show. Originally, Loki was utilized as a way to introduce Kang the Conqueror to the MCU as the Multiverse Saga’s big villain. The original story of Avengers: The Kang Dynasty would’ve stemmed from this. However, after Jonathan Majors’ assault charges, Marvel Studios pivoted from Kang to Doctor Doom, subsequently changing Avengers: The Kang Dynasty to Avengers: Doomsday.

Kevin Feige finally confirms what the original Kang and Doctor Doom plans were going to be for Doomsday before The Kang Dynasty became Doomsday.

Although Loki worked as a self-contained story using Kang variants like He Who Remains and Victor Timely, it still established Kang as a wider multiversal threat. The MCU’s switch to Doom could have rendered the show’s narrative obsolete as a result, but it actually had the opposite effect. Now, Loki works even better as a self-contained sci-fi story; the arcs of both He Who Remains and Victor Timely were wrapped up within Loki’s 12 episodes, the finale of which established that the TVA was keeping tabs on any further variants.

Moreover, Loki ended with its titular character as the god of the multiverse who oversees the branching timelines, which has been retroactively folded into Avengers: Doomsday’s narrative. Despite the change-ups behind the scenes at Marvel Studios, Loki still works on two fronts, both as a self-contained sci-fi story and one that left threads dangling to be explored in the future.

Another positive impact that the MCU as a franchise had on Loki was its establishment of unique time travel rules. Avengers: Endgame forewent the rules of typical time travel movies, stating that changing the past does not alter the narrative at hand, instead creating a branching timeline that includes the consequences of those initial changes; The original narrative remains available to return to. Where Loki is concerned, this allowed the show to explore typical sci-fi tropes without the confusion that tends to be caused by them.

The exploration of timelines, variants, time loops, and other aspects could freely be used in Loki with the knowledge that these would not be confusing. For instance, a lot of shows that use sci-fi tropes can create the question of “what came first?” Time loops and paradoxes are often created as a result of time travel, leading audiences to question what the inception point was. The MCU’s unique time travel rules avoided this, making Loki even more of a science fiction masterpiece.
