For Keeps: 6 Timeless Jewelry Pieces on One Vogue Editor’s Wish List

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Presented by Chanel Fine Jewelry and Watches.

My jewelry box is as intimate and personal as my handbag. It holds memories, receipts (in value, not literally), collected treasures from vacations, and occasionally even keys—these are for lockets, not doors. But while my daily handbag may change, my jewelry is a permanent fixture: worn on repeat, every piece chosen for keeps.

There are aesthetic signatures I look for in jewelry: delicate diamonds (a sprinkle being my favorite), a sculptural shape, and a strong gold glint. These are the qualities that represent my personal style and punctuate my everyday uniform; they’re finishing touches I need to feel “ready” before leaving the house.

There’s little more satisfying than playing and layering with my jewelry in the morning, sometimes forgetting pieces, other times over-stacking them. It’s this spontaneous approach that inspired me to take stock of my collection: What’s missing, what’s staying, and what, wistfully, might be time to part with. Consider this my attempt at building the ultimate jewelry box capsule, anchored by six Chanel fine jewelry and watches icons.

I already mentioned I’m an over-stacker, but I love how the number of rings I wear often reflects how I’m feeling. On Mondays, I might leave the house wearing just one simple gold band—to feel low-key, and ease myself into the week. On a bold day, I love to layer and the Coco Crush family is perfectly fit for my stacking needs. Four is my magic number, to accommodate various sizes and finishes.

Chanel’s Première collection has been on my wish list for a long time—10 years to be precise from when I first saw the Originale at a Chanel boutique in Harrods. (I still have the photo of my wrist from that first try-on.) Its bracelet-like quality is elegant yet functional, though a decade on, I’m equally enamored by the Première Galon and its braided motif. If I had to choose just one, the Originale would be the way to go, purely for the sentimental reasons.

While I champion simplicity in how I dress, it’s rare that I put on a watch and don’t add a companion next to it—either a diamond bracelet, a vintage chain, or a simple bangle. This elegant gold bangle is effortless and will quite literally go with any watch you already own.

A jewelry box curation isn’t complete without a gold chain necklace, I don’t believe anyone who enjoys jewelry can go without one. From dainty delicate chains, to chunkier T-bar clasps and even those that come with a pendant or medallion, the options are endless. I’m settling for something simple, to be styled over a white shirt, or layered over a turtleneck for high contrast. I always want the gold to pop.

A watch should be timeless (excuse the pun), but that doesn't mean it can't feel current. Today, the cocktail watch is trending. Traditionally a very small, jewelry-like watch worn for evening, the timepiece has taken on a new, everyday end-use that can elevate the simplest of outfits. Layer over the sleeves black turtleneck, styled alongside a silk scarf or paired with a diamanté bracelet—the styling opportunities are endless. I especially love designs with contrast like the Chanel Première Ribbon; it feels modern with its sporty-like rubber strap but the black lacquer face and diamond trim give it an ornate quality, reminiscent of the designs of yore.

I have eight ear piercings and counting and love nothing more than a simple but statement-making gold hoop. The quilted motif on the Coco Crush earrings have just the right amount of texture—I’d love to stack them up the side of my ear but that feels ambitious. Once they’re in, I find it hard to imagine wearing any other style.

Balmain Resort 2027

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

“For me, the men’s and the women’s, they are kind of like one entity. I really want the men and women to be able to talk to each other.” Antonin Tron was riffing as we riffled the rails of his first pre-spring collections for Balmain, including his first-ever menswear collection for the house. “I don’t necessarily think of them as a couple,” he qualified: “but I think at Balmain the women are quite sexy and out there. And if I was to do something very different for men, it would be weird.”

Looking at his rails simultaneously, that relationship was manifest in substance as well as spirit: “We have a lot of overcrossing material,” Tron confirmed. The same embroidered surfaces, treated denim, and snakeskin jacquards traveled between the two wardrobes, but in womenswear their expression was differently calibrated. This was a more relaxed continuation of the luxuriously louche and wanton glamour of his last runway collection, which itself had been informed by the Californian desert. “With pre-collection I can explore a bit more the casual side of Balmain,” he said.

That meant injecting a dose of dishevelment into Balmain’s appetite for decoration. Sensuously draped dresses in snakeskin and beautiful, borderline-hallucinogenic florals came with cropped flight jackets, while satin tailoring, sheer lurex blouses, and embellished animalier were countered by washed denim and biker leathers. There were micro cardigans over exposed bras, skirts that succumbed into flowerings of asymmetric ruffle, and lavishly embellished pieces whose surfaces seemed weathered by weeks of sun exposure. What blurred in the haze of Tron’s gaze was much distinction between dressing up and down.

This dissolved tension was more than a styling proposition. Tron has little patience for the idea that luxury dressing must conform either to bourgeois standards of propriety, or fashion’s customary cringe of faux-intellectual snobbishness. “For me, this thing with sexiness is also a class thing,” he said of the physical ideals through which notions of elegance are policed. Balmain, in his view, offers a space to articulate sexuality rather than apologize for it.

He recalled meeting a client in Los Angeles while shooting the collection in Joshua Tree. When he mentioned his last show’s desert-and-hallucinogenics theme, she cheerfully volunteered that she had a house there and used to do exactly that. She was accompanied by her muscular husband. “They both wanted to be hot, and that’s it,” Tron observed. This seemed a pretty good expression of the collection’s carefully direct hedonism. Super-long leather boots and Lucite-heeled wedges provided the strut, while the tasseled Zabriskie bag offered easygoing swing.

His instinct is that luxury should enable freedom rather than impose rules. The filament that connects this new Balmain woman and the new Balmain man, in spirit as much as substance, is Tron himself. “I think slowly, I’m finding that line: I’m finding the woman and the man, basically.”

Slavia Prague 1-2 Arsenal: Slight concerns but another away day win

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Arsenal secured another away win last night, this time in the Champions League beating Slavia Prague 2-1 thanks to goals from Noni Madueke and Viktor Gyokeres.

Mikel Arteta rotated in some areas, with starts for Ben White, Piero Hincapie, Martin Zubimendi, Mikel Merino and Eberechi Eze, but relied too on the foundational stability of Declan Rice with Ezri Konsa and Gabriel behind him. We began in lively fashion, and took an early lead.

David Raya played it long, Gyokeres held it up well and hooked the ball forward, and Madueke’s pace took him beyond the defender and into the box before finishing between the keeper’s legs. Solid play from two players who can sometimes be guilty of fluffing their lines when it comes to those key actions. This time they were spot on.

We had more chances to score. Eze showed nice skills in the box but saw his shot blocked; Gyokeres headed back across goal for Merino but he headed over; the Spaniard saw a shot from distance tipped over, and his smart near post run and a right-footed shot from a Ben White cutback forced the keeper into another decent stop; and there was a brilliant counter and pass from Rice that drifted all the way to the back post without any Arsenal player attacking it for a tap-in.

We did find a second though. Gyokeres combined well with Merino around their box, and when he was played through he opened up his body really well and produced a fine finish from close range and a tight angle to make it 2-0. I don’t think that goal at Fleetwood would have done much for his confidence, this felt much more like the kind of strike he needed to produce – for himself as much as the team.

So, we had a good lead at the break, but this was very much a game of two halves. In the first we had 60% possession, out-shot the opposition by 8 to 2, and passed at nearly 90% success rate. In the second, the tables turned. Slavia Prague took 17 shots to our 4, and our pass success fell to 77%. We were sloppy and untidy, they made a tactical change at half-time which worked well for them, and while we weren’t necessarily under the cosh, the entire dynamic of the game changed and we couldn’t get to grips with it.

We needed David Raya to make one superb save to keep them at bay, before Gabriel had to head one off the line, but in injury time they found the goal I think their second half performance (and ours) probably merited. They whipped in a wicked ball from the left, the striker applied the close range finish, and the clean sheet was gone.

Afterwards, Mikel Arteta was happy with the win, but displeased with the second half, saying:

First of all, you have to value the fact it’s very difficult to win away from home. We’ve done it against a very tough opponent, we expected that toughness. In the first half we played at a really high level in terms of intensity, tempo, quality and clarity in what we had to do, and what the game was demanding. We were very efficient – we should have scored three, four or five.

I already talked about some of the things we didn’t do, and others I’d prefer not to mention. It’s a huge lesson, and we’ll learn from the second half. It’s our third away game in quick succession, and we’re the only team to have played three away games in the Champions League and just one at home – there are a lot of factors – but I don’t like to use that, because in the first half we were at the level we wanted. I take full responsibility for the drop in the second half.

I don’t think the team played well at all in the second half, but I do wonder about how we might have done things differently. For example, I feel like Declan Rice played within himself in that second period, and let me be clear, I understand why. Personally, I think it’s a bit mad he did 90+ minutes in a game like this, even if I accept he and the manager know more about his fitness than any of us. Maybe they need him to be like a steam train, going going going all the time, but when you have options of the quality of Myles Lewis-Skelly and Bruno Guimaraes on the bench, surely using them in his stead allows the best of two worlds: some rest for Rice, and fresher legs in midfield when the game is tilting in the opposition’s favour.

We have this really strong bench, but I don’t think the changes we made worked particularly well. The question, of course, is if they absolutely needed to. We had a two goal lead until late on, and we secured all three points away from home after a difficult and physically demanding game at Anfield three days previously. I just found the Jekyll and Hyde nature of of the two halves a little concerning, but only a little. Ten of our sixteen games this season have been away from home, we’ve won seven of them, so that’s a decent return, added to by this victory.

Ultimately, this is essentially a forgettable Champions League tie that we came away with three points from. Apparently it was a year to the day since we played Slavia Prague last season too, a game I had/have zero recollection of. Which might say something about me more than anything else, but these kinds of fixtures don’t live long in the memory. They’re grist for the mill in this part of the season, we had a job to do, and we got it done, and now all the focus turns to Hull on Saturday and the three points required there before the Interlull.

How confident are you in the Bears and in Caleb Williams moving forward?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Welcome to SB Nation Reacts, a survey of fans across the NFL. Throughout the year, we ask questions of the most plugged-in Bears fans and fans across the country.

The Chicago Bears have won three straight games in the most impressive fashion imaginable. Caleb Williams is the 1st quarterback in Bears history to lead his team to 30+ points in three consecutive games with no turnovers. The Bears also never trailed in any of these three games, and that 3-game streak of scoring 30 points while never trailing is tied for the second-longest streak in franchise history. They went four in a row three times, all in the 1940s (1941, 1942, and 1948). Those teams were led by Hall of Fame quarterback Sid Luckman, who is the only quarterback to win a league MVP for the Bears.

There was some buzz around Williams as an MVP candidate before the season, and that was amplified following the Bears' 59-point explosion in Week 1, but the hamstring injury seemed to derail that because it kept him out of the next three games.

In his weekly NFL MVP Tracker at NFL.com, Kevin Patra wrote about the historical unlikelihood of Williams winning it.

In the post NFL-AFL merger era (since 1970) in non-strike-shortened seasons, only one player has missed three games and still won the NFL MVP award: Joe Montana in 1989. Cool Joe battled through various injuries that season, missing Weeks 6, 8, and 15, and portions of others.

While a long shot, the Bears did just beat last year's Super Bowl participants in back-to-back weeks on primetime. They also have three more stand-alone games on the schedule, including a third straight primetime affair this Sunday.

Patra also explained why Montana's performance in 1989 won it that year and how Caleb could have a similar path.

Despite missing three games, Montana was the best QB on the best team in the NFL, winning the MVP vote in a landslide (62 of 70 votes). The 49ers QB overcame the lack of counting stats by leading the NFL in all the efficiency categories: 70.2 percent completion rate, a 6.7 TD% rate, 53.7% success rate, 9.1 yards per attempt average, 270.8 yards per game, and set the then-record with a 112.4 passer rating — when 100 passer ratings were uncommon.

That's Williams' path to the MVP: Be the most efficient passer on the best team.

Caleb may not hit gaudy passing yard or touchdown numbers, but if all his other numbers are among the best in the NFL, while the Bears keep winning games, how could he not be a finalist?

Matheus Cunha’s late brace secured Manchester United a jaw-dropping 3-2 comeback win against Roma to breathe new life into their season and ease some of the pressure on Michael Carrick.

A nine-match winless run had left the Red Devils head coach’s job hanging by a thread and Tuesday’s wretched start in the Champions League only pushed him closer to the Old Trafford exit.

United were facing a fourth straight loss after Matias Soule’s avoidable opener was followed up by a Donyell Malen penalty in a first half that ended with boos.

Green and gold balloons had cascaded down the Stretford End as fans protested against owners the Glazer family before the match, with the second half offering a reminder of the special spirit that underpins their club.

Bruno Fernandes pulled one back from a retaken, and softly awarded, spot-kick as United played with the kind of purpose and skill lacking this term.

That approach forced a late error that Cunha capitalised on, before the Brazil forward slammed in off the inside of the near post for the winner.

His celebration earned him the first of two quick bookings but the relief was as palpable as the delight as United held on for just their third win of the campaign.

What ended up being a famous European night under the Old Trafford light had started in frustrating, and all too familiar, fashion.

United shot themselves in the foot against the absentee-hit visitors inside 11 minutes as Roma capitalised on a gaping hole left by left-back Diogo Dalot committing forward. United survived a scare shortly after as Dalot’s difficult night continued before tennis balls landed on the pitch as the clock hit 20 minutes and five seconds – a nod to the Glazers’ leveraged buyout in 2005.

Malen forced a save from Senne Lammens and Soule struck narrowly wide in stoppage time, just after Kobbie Mainoo had the home side’s first shot on target, with home fans fuming at the break.

The Clarks Wallabee Goes Gorpcore

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Clarks has been on one lately. Over the past few weeks, Moncler worked up the British footwear brand's signature Wallabee, while A.P.C. took on the Desert Boot and the Weaver. Now, Clarks has called in Italian shoemaker Diemme to drag three of its biggest silhouettes up a mountain.

(In case you don't spend all day thinking about Italian hiking boots, here's a primer: Diemme was founded in Montebelluna, a town in northern Italy with a very serious footwear-making history. The brand is best known for taking the kinda boots originally built for mountains and making them nice enough to wear with your very best trousers. Every pair is still made at its factory in Veneto, and the upcoming Clarks collection is, too.)

The main event is the Wallabee, of course. Diemme reworked the familiar moccasin-shaped shoe and covered it in pale “champagne” suede from Charles F. Stead, the British tannery responsible for some of the nicest fuzzy leather in footwear. So far, so classic. But then you get the thick rope laces and rubber mudguard wrapped around the bottom. Underneath, the OG crepe sole has been swapped out for the chunky, lugged unit found on Diemme's Roccia Vet and Cornaro boots. It makes the whole shoe sit higher and gives it enough tread to deal with more than a slightly sticky pub floor. Your ’Bees are now ready to hit the trails.

There are two more styles in the cross-branded collection. The Desert Boot comes in the classic “cola” brown suede, only now it's been fitted with a heavy-duty sole. Then there's the Desert Trek, which has been transformed into a mid-cut boot and made from black full-grain leather. It keeps the signature center seam and little Trek Man logo, but it's easily the biggest and baddest of the three, with metal D-rings running up the lacing system and a beefed up ankle for extra support.

The Diemme x Clarks collection is set to drop on November 5, purchasable through Diemme, Clarks, and select stockists globally.

Ford’s Frostwarden Bronco Is a One-Off Rescue Vehicle with 1200 HP

The Frostwarden blends Ford’s new Megazilla 2.0 crate engine with an extreme search-and-rescue Bronco build.

A base Ford Bronco makes 300 horsepower. Opt for the V-6 option, and it’ll add 30 horses; if that’s not enough, the Bronco Raptor reaches 418. Not there yet? Try doubling the Raptor’s output and adding a few hundred more horsepower. Enter the Megazilla 2.0.

Ford’s most powerful crate engine ever is now on display for the first time at this year’s SEMA show in Las Vegas, Nevada. The Megazilla 2.0, which builds off the 7.3-liter V-8 crate engine lineup, uses a stronger block, upgraded pistons, larger fuel injectors, a different belt drive and damper, and a supercharger-specific camshaft to keep up with a 3.0-liter Gen 6 Whipple supercharger. All of that combines to make over 1200 horsepower and over 1000 pound-feet of torque.

To show off the engine’s massive output, Ford developed the Frostwarden: a search-and-rescue-inspired Bronco taken to extremes. The outfitted Bronco is lifted with a 74Weld portal lift kit and wears 42-inch-tall BFGoodrich tires mounted on custom 18-inch Weld beadlock wheels. Dana driveline hardware and custom Bilstein dampers round out the undercarriage upgrades.

The Frostwarden’s black-and-yellow livery, ultra-wide stance, and plethora of rescue tools—including off-road lights, a rear-mounted external gas tank, roof-mounted spare tire, and bright yellow front tow winch—make the Bronco hard to miss, even among the ranks of modded SEMA show cars. Inside, yellow-and-black upholstery lines the cabin and a special Megazilla wordmark rests along the bottom of the steering wheel.

The Frostwarden is a one-of-a-kind build, but the 7.3-liter supercharged V-8 that powers it isn’t. Ford says the one-off vehicle highlights the ability to launch extreme builds based around Ford-powered engines, including the massive Megazilla 2.0.
