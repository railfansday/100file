Miley Cyrus Debuts Her Sculptural Wedding Band—Jewelers Explain the Design

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Miley Cyrus did more than give fans a first look at her wedding dress when she shared photos of her nuptials on Sunday. Zoom in and the singer’s thick wedding band is visible, stacked with her gold bombé cushion-cut diamond engagement ring.

She later shared another look at her bridal jewelry, including her band, which appears to be in a black round ring box:

The singer’s band choice is fashion-forward, The Clear Cut’s founder Olivia Landau tells ELLE. “Thick wedding bands have been trending, whether they are larger diamond eternity bands or wider solid gold bands.”

It also really suits her, says Lauren Boc, founder and CEO of Hera Fine Jewelry: “Miley’s wedding set is a beautiful reflection of her personal style—it has some rock and roll edge but still feels upscale.”

Here, jewelers share their first impressions of the piece with ELLE.

Steph Mazuera, who founded her own namesake brand, says Cyrus’s ring has “a slightly curved silhouette” that “appears to follow the contours of her engagement ring.”

“When working with larger, bolder rings, introducing a soft curve or slight bend into the stack can make the overall combination feel more organic,” she adds. “It brings a sense of balance and softness without taking away from the impact of the individual rings. Everything should be designed to flow around the center stone.”

Based on the photos she shared, Cyrus’s engagement ring and wedding band appear to be different metals. Her engagement ring is yellow gold.

Her wedding band, meanwhile, “looks to be a half-round band, around 4 mm, in white gold or platinum,” Boc says. “It appears that there’s a small east-west oval diamond bezel-set on top of the band, similar to our Amir ring. Raising the diamond slightly above the band like this creates dimension.”

Several factors contribute to the value of a ring like Cyrus’s. “A custom band like this one could range quite a bit depending on the maker, gold weight, and craftsmanship,” Mazuera explains. “I would price a similar custom design in my studio in the $3,000–$5,000 range.”

Cyrus’s wedding band is a statement piece that can be worn with or without her engagement ring. “I like that her wedding band feels substantial enough to hold its own alongside her engagement ring without competing with it,” Mazuera says. “Both rings have a strong sculptural presence, but the slight curve of the wedding band helps the two sit together more naturally.”

Worn together, the two pieces have “a really bold look that’s both edgy and luxurious,” Boc says. “It’s maximalist, with a lot of metal on the hand. I love it!”

Cyrus’s wedding band is anything but minimalist—but that’s exactly why it fuels multiple jewelry trends.

“I think we’re seeing a continued move toward more sculptural, substantial wedding jewelry, moving away from the idea that wedding bands need to be delicate or understated,” Mazuera says.

Boc agrees. “Miley’s wedding set definitely reflects the trends we’re seeing at Hera. Chunky, bolder looks with a lot of gold weight have become really popular with our clients. When I first founded Hera in 2021, the big engagement ring trend was really thin, delicate-looking bands. As a jewelry designer who cares a lot about durability, I’m so happy to see people turning toward more substantial designs.”

Less traditional ring designs like Cyrus’s are also becoming more popular. “Our clients are often more concerned with having a ring that feels distinctive and uniquely like them,” Boc says.

Boc notes that while Cyrus’s photos give a limited view of her engagement ring and wedding band together, the apparent mix of metals is right on trend.

“I think there’s something so chic and effortless about a mixed metal stack,” she adds. “Like everything else about Miley’s stack, mixing metals is the kind of design decision that makes your wedding set really feel curated and chosen around a strong understanding of your personal style. She certainly didn’t just pick this up pre-designed at a big box store; a stack like this belongs to someone who’s confident in her personal style and chose something that feels uniquely her.”

The “Soft Wolf” Cut Is the Simple Way to Add Fullness to Mature Hair: How to Recreate It

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

This trendy, ultramodern style is unexpected yet elegant for all ages

A chameleon both on and offscreen, Pamela Anderson proves short hair can be incredibly versatile and has made a strong case for trendy hairstyles that feel grown-up and sophisticated. The celebrated actress and former Bazaar UK cover star has experimented with sharp micro-bangs, gone auburn with a fluffy ’70s mullet, and embraced French glamour with a chic chignon. Her most recent look is understated and fresh, but with plenty of personality: the “soft wolf” cut.

The ’70s-inspired wolf cut is characterized by choppy layers with face-framing pieces that get gradually longer along the bottom, delivering plenty of volume and movement. It’s a cut that makes an impact all on its own and has personality in spades. Anderson’s version takes the wolf cut and gives it a polished, understated twist. A softer take on the trend, this chop feels effortlessly cool. “The benefit of a ‘soft wolf’ cut is that it allows a fashion-led hair shape to be kept feminine and ageless by softening the edges,” says Anita Rice, the cofounder of Buller and Rice salons. “It’s youthful yet chic.”

According to Rice, it’s important to consider the length of hair that sits around the temples. “This is a crucial part of the hairline, and when cut shorter it can give a harder feel, but if you keep it longer and softer, it can create a more versatile, flattering silhouette,” Rice says. It works across all ages, but if your hair tends to be fine, avoid “over-texturizing,” which can make it look thinner and wispier.

Adds Rice: “You still want to create softness, and this is about getting the right type of technique to encourage volume without leaving the hair feeling limp.” To style, wear it à la Anderson by gently pushing it back from the face, or experiment with a deep side part. “Alternatively, adding volume through a bouncy blow-dry with ‘flicky’ ends helps achieve a more ’70s feel.”

Designers Say These 9 Unexpected Bed Styling Tricks Can Wake Up Your Whole Bedroom

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

These pillow ideas make your bed feel intentional, collected, and joyful.

A standard pillow setup—two king pillows against the headboard, with two standard pillows in the front, and a square accent pillow in the center—is a snooze. Designers are treating the bed as an opportunity to showcase a bit of flair. Not to mention awaken the entire bedroom.

What kinds of changes are happening? Throw and accent pillows are now treated as if they are fashion accessories. We’re seeing everything from edgy studs to romantic ruffles in contrasting colors to graphic lines that hypnotize. In fact, mismatched pieces work great and can feel “intentional, collected, and completely joyful,” says designer Betsy Wentz.

Here, nine unconventional ways to play with your bed pillow arrangements.

If the rest of your bedroom is neutral, designers are saying it’s OK to go more bold with your bed pillows. In the above space, it’s impossible not to notice the hypnotizing hue of Yves Klein blue pillows on a striped duvet cover.

“There’s something about that particular blue,” says designer Kate Anlyan, founder of Yasu Home. “It’s so vivid and almost electric, but it also has a depth to it. I love how it cuts through all the other tones without feeling disconnected from the rest of the room.” The hue works as a counterpoint to the wood paneling in the background and provides a balance to the visual weight of the black headboard.

The interconnection between the contrasting stripes on the square pillows and the round velvet ball feels both fun and sophisticated, says designer Beth Diana Smith. The stripes feel especially striking, bringing a sense of energy and definition to the space, when set against the moody purple-brown Benjamin Moore's Cinnamon Slate wall color. To keep the rest of the bedding from competing, Smith tames the room with a muted light gray coverlet.

“Match up the headboard, throws, lamps and artwork with your throw pillows to balance out color throughout your room,” says designer Renée Gaddis. The encapsulated look creates instant cohesion. Starting with a bold hue, like a mustard yellow, will make your job easier because it has personality from the start.

Faux fur is a classic way to add rich texture, but you don’t need to reserve it for a château. Interior designer Mialani Collard uses a neutral linen palette and tactile embellishments that sandwich the fluff to make it less prominent, allowing the bed to work even in tropical climates like Florida.

When you want a home with casual living as the main objective, fill surfaces in a way that doesn't look too pristine or orderly. Interior designer Tyson Ness of Studio Ness mixed different patterns and colors for the bedroom pillows so there’s no “right way” to make the bed after it’s been used.

“The different combinations create a slightly different look each time, keeping the overall feeling light, airy, and totally casual,” Ness adds.

Choosing pillows that match the bedding is easy, but Cate Gutter, owner and principal designer of CWG Design, prefers to add a bit of je ne sais quoi when she styles the bed.

“This bedroom is tucked away in a mountain home here in North Carolina, and we wanted it to have a playful, nostalgic feel reminiscent of summer camp,” Gutter says. “We carried that inspiration into the pillow designs, giving them an almost handmade, craft-like quality that feels whimsical.”

The oversized pillows are made in Imogen Heath's Arty Check, a cheerful yellow check finished with a fuzzy green fringe. In front of it, a smaller bolster is a playful take on a traditional shape, but the muted, multicolored stripe with a contrasting ruffled flange bolsters extra personality. The added texture breaks up the monotony of solid linens.

“Studs are an unexpected detail on bed pillows because they’re so versatile,” says Sean Carter, senior interior designer at Sapphire Pear. “They look just as great on a neutral fabric as they do with bold colors, prints, and patterns.” The metallic element adds a little edge without overpowering the overall design.

Wentz is no stranger when it comes to combining patterns and layering different scales in the same color family. Look for prints with their own personality that won’t compete with each other.

“The smaller confetti and stick stripe pillows give your eye a place to rest against that big graphic headboard, so they feel like friends, not strangers,” Wentz says.

Pillows gone wild can still balance restraint. Creating serious contrast—in pattern direction—will step up your bedscaping game.

“The geometric patterns in the pillows complement the strong linear elements,” says interior designer Nicole Johnson. “The vertical wood slats draw the eye upward, while the horizontal lines of the bed and furnishings provide balance.”

Linear patterns in the pillows at a smaller scale create a picture-in-picture effect that won’t overwhelm the overall design.

Scientists Discover That Male and Female Hearts Age in Surprisingly Different Ways

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

The heart may age very differently from one region to another. A detailed study of mouse hearts found that molecular signs associated with chronic inflammation and tissue scarring were concentrated around the aortic valves and aortic root, rather than being distributed evenly throughout the organ.

Researchers at The Jackson Laboratory (JAX) identified these hotspots by mapping thousands of cells from young and aged mice. Published in Aging Cell, the findings suggest that structures often overlooked in cardiac aging research may play a central role in how the heart changes over time.

Previous studies of cardiac aging have largely concentrated on heart muscle, particularly the left ventricle, which pumps oxygenated blood into the aorta and throughout the body. Because mammalian heart muscle cells have very limited capacity for regeneration, understanding how they deteriorate with age has been a major research priority. The JAX team instead examined the entire heart, using single-cell and spatial genomics technologies to identify both the molecular activity of individual cells and their locations within the organ.

“The valves and the major blood vessels are parts often thought of as plumbing rather than the main engine, but this is a very complicated little machine,” said study author Nadia Rosenthal, scientific director at JAX. “It turns out they’re home to many of the cells we’re interested in, and that’s where some of the most important aging-related signals were concentrated.”

“Some of the markers associated with inflammaging were there from the very beginning in young hearts, but they were focused specifically in areas no one had defined before, in the valves and the aorta rather than in muscle tissue,” Rosenthal said. “We really have to think of aging as something that is not linear, because just like we know that people don’t age at the same rate, what we know now is that organs and tissues don’t age at the same rate either, even within one individual.”

Aging is associated with persistent, low-grade inflammation, sometimes called “inflammaging.” The researchers focused on two cell populations involved in this process: immune cells that help regulate inflammation (macrophages) and structural cells responsible for maintaining connective tissue and producing scar tissue (fibroblasts). In older mouse hearts, macrophages showed increased activity in genes associated with inflammation and tissue remodeling, while fibroblasts exhibited molecular patterns linked to fibrosis (or scarring).

“To keep muscle cells happy, the other cells in the heart have to continue supporting them throughout life,” Rosenthal said. “What this study does, for the first time, is provide a comprehensive picture of how those support cells, particularly fibroblasts and macrophages, change with age and contribute to what we call inflammaging.”

The team also detected increased activity in two genes within the identified hotspots. Ccl8 produces an inflammatory signaling molecule, while Cdkn1a (also known as p21) helps regulate the cell cycle and is commonly associated with cellular senescence. Their activity points to changes in signaling between neighboring cells, not simply isolated changes within individual cell types. “We’re not talking about single genes. We’re talking about an entire conversation between immune cells and fibroblasts. As the heart ages, the way those cells communicate changes, and that shift appears to be linked to the inflammation and tissue remodeling we see in older hearts,” Rosenthal said.

Some molecular markers traditionally associated with senescent cells were already present in young mouse hearts. Cellular senescence occurs when cells permanently stop dividing, often following stress or damage. Senescent cells can contribute to inflammation and tissue deterioration, but the researchers found that the presence of commonly used senescence markers does not necessarily establish that a cell has entered this state.

“What surprised us was that some of these markers that have been associated with aging were already present in young hearts. They weren’t where anyone expected to find them, and that forced us to rethink what those markers are telling us,” Rosenthal said. “That turns the usual interpretation of cardiac aging on its ear because these markers alone don’t mean a cell is senescent. There’s nothing senescent about a six-month-old mouse heart.”

The study also identified differences in how fibroblasts responded to aging in male and female mice. In males, older fibroblasts showed greater involvement in molecular pathways associated with fibrosis. In females, aging was linked more closely to pathways involved in heart development and protein production. These findings describe differences in gene activity, not demonstrated differences in cardiac function or disease risk.

Rosenthal noted that cardiovascular disease patterns also differ between men and women, with disease risk generally increasing after menopause in women. However, the mouse findings do not establish a mechanism for those human differences. Comparisons with previously published human heart data did identify several shared molecular programs associated with aging, including pathways involved in cellular stress responses.

“Our findings change the focus from aging across the entire heart to specific anatomical hotspots, helping us better understand how inflammation and fibrosis are connected and which regions of the heart are involved in the aging process,” said study author Amalia Sintou, a postdoctoral associate at JAX.

Age is the leading risk factor for cardiovascular disease, but separating changes caused by aging itself from those associated with lifestyle, environmental exposures, and disease remains difficult. Although the study did not establish whether the identified cellular changes directly impair heart function, the controlled conditions in which the mice lived helped researchers examine the biological effects of aging.

“Valve replacement is common in older adults, and one of the big questions is how much of the underlying damage is driven by aging itself,” said study author Aleksandra Aljakna Khan, a postdoctoral associate at JAX. “Because all of the mice in our study lived in the same environment and ate the same diet, we were able to isolate the effects of aging and show that aging alone can initiate some of these changes in the valves and aorta.”

The researchers are now investigating how exercise, diet, and disease influence the interactions between immune cells and fibroblasts. Their goal is to distinguish changes that occur during normal aging from those that may increase the heart’s susceptibility to disease. Although the current findings do not immediately suggest new treatments, the cellular map provides a foundation for studying how inflammation and fibrosis develop in specific regions of the aging heart.

Experts on How to Brighten Under Eyes

📅 Nov 14, 2025  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Without fail, the eyes are usually the first place a busy week (or month) shows up. A late night, skipping your skin-care routine, or long flight easily translates to a little puffiness in the morning or some shadowing underneath, and maybe even the dreaded “eye bag.” It happens to everyone, and it usually fades once you’ve caught up on sleep, gotten back on your regimen, and have hydrated enough.

Some of it comes down to how the area is built. The skin around the eyes is thinner than on the rest of the face, so whatever’s going on underneath, like fluid that settled overnight or tiny blood vessels close to the surface, is easier to see. Over time, collagen gradually decreases and the brows can sit a touch lower, which is a completely normal part of getting older. Still, it’s nice to have a few tricks for the days you want to look as awake as you feel.

The good news? There are plenty of ways we can brighten, lift, and hydrate this area of the face. Some are as easy as curling your lashes or keeping a couple of spoons in the freezer. Other methods, like an eye cream with studied ingredients, work slowly over a few months after a little consistency. If you want to go further, in-office treatments can subtly lift the brow or soften dark circles. Below, our experts share their best tips for brightening and lifting the eye area, starting with the simplest.

Makeup artist Lynsey Alexander has transformed many a tired eye in her time, having worked with models backstage at fashion shows and on shoots for years. She always begins with good eye drops. “I then use a beige pencil secretly inside the eyes–it needs to be a skin-toned color, white is too stark,” she says. “My favorite is from Paris Berlin as it’s quite dry and not overly creamy, something you don’t want as it moves too easily.”

“Dark circles are a common complaint in my office,” says aesthetic doctor, Maryam Zamani, MD. “One of the most common reasons for dark circles is loss of volume in the periorbital area. As we age, the eyelid-cheek junction becomes elongated, and where this junction was once confluent in youth, it becomes demarcated, giving the appearance of dark lower eyelids or tear troughs.” It’s a common problem that can begin from as early as your twenties, and is made worse as collagen stores deplete. Dr Zamani often treats it with “meticulously placed micro-droplets of hyaluronic acid” to restore the lost volume and hydrate. You will see brighter, more awake eyes immediately.

Celebrity makeup artist Mary Philips has prepped clients like Hailey Bieber and Kendall Jenner for years, and she knows tired eyes are usually the first thing to give a face away. Her fix starts with a step she has coined called underglazing. “The under-eye area is one of the first places I focus on when prepping the skin because it’s so delicate, and can show dryness and fine lines more easily,” she says. “‘Underglazing’ is really about giving that area the hydration it needs before applying any makeup. I love using lightweight, hydrating skincare and gently pressing it into the skin to help everything absorb. The goal is to make the skin look naturally plump and hydrated, so you need less makeup to begin with.”

The new wave of the best eye creams are more effective than ever before, and come loaded with efficacious active ingredients to illuminate the area and soften fine lines, while maintaining the integrity of the skin barrier. “Tretinoin has the best evidence behind it–with continued use (for about a year), you see increased cell turnover; cue brightening of the skin and a reduction in wrinkles,” explains dermatologist Emma Craythorne, MD. “Azelaic acid is also brilliant at brightening, if used in strength at five per cent.”

If your issue is puffiness, take care to ensure you opt for a detoxifying eye cream instead of a hydrating formula–according to Dr. Zamani, hydrating eye creams (particularly those with hyaluronic acid) may exacerbate it.

Yes, it’s boring, but what you eat and drink does make a big difference to the eye area. Those who have ever woken up the morning after with eyes that feel like lead will know that. “Keeping salt and alcohol low in your diet is key,” says Dr, Zamani. “And sleeping with a pillow and eye mask can also help.”

To stimulate the area and help de-puff, facialist Nichola Joss recommends gently massaging an eye cream or gel around the outer orbital bone. “You can also use cold teaspoons to gently stimulate, de-puff, improve lymph drainage and brighten the area,” she says. For those more serious about texture and pigmentation, she also recommends the Lyma Laser, which can be used at home.

Turns out there are a lot of veins and capillaries underneath delicate, thin skin in the eye area, which can cause the dark hue we recognize as dark circles. “We can use sophisticated lasers to target the vascular plexus in the upper and lower eyelid,” says aesthetic surgeon, Costas Papageorgiou, MD, who works from Harrods Wellness Clinic in London. “This plexus tends to become more prominent with age as a result of accumulative inflammation and loss of elasticity, to name two.” By addressing (and softening) this area, you can reinstate skin clarity and light reflection–plus it works particularly well when combined with under eye filler.

To address cool tones and general darkness in the area, try color correcting. “For blue undertones, opt for a peach-based concealer,” advises Dr. Alexander, who also adds a touch of YSL’s cult Touche Eclat to reflect light in the area. “Then, set the under eyes with a brightening powder, such as Laura Mercier’s Secret Brightening Powder, for added radiance.”

Simple but effective, curling your eyelashes has an immediate refreshing effect. “Creating a bright, lifted eye is about creating space between the brow and lash line, and drawing attention outwards and upwards,” explains Anna Payne, UK Chanel makeup artist. For a little extra oomph, she suggests applying a dot of highlighting shadow on the centre of the eyelid and inner corner of the eye to create an illuminating spotlight effect.

When we analyze drooping or lacklustre eyes, we don’t often consider that part of the problem might be coming from the forehead. “One of the biggest reasons that the eye area looks tired is that on top of the tiny repetitive blinking movements our eyes have to do each day, we also express an array of emotions through them, too,” says Ada Ooi, TCM practitioner and founder of 001 Skincare. “A lot of our eye movements, such as frowning or surprise, come from muscles in the forehead.” She recommends using a gua sha tool to alleviate the tension built in the frontalis muscles (the vertical muscles that lift the brows). Try cross-friction massage (horizontally, in this case) to break them from their usual position, and release fascia stress.

“Freshly threaded brows have transformational powers,” sys Vanita Parti MBE, founder of Blink Brow Bar. “By de-fluffing, removing excess stragglers and creating two neat, symmetrical brows, you open up the eyes immediately.” Lifting the arch also lifts and sculpts the entire face, so post-thread, seek to define them with a brow pencil or pomade.

If curling your lashes is already part of your morning, think of this as the version that lasts. Lash lift technician and founder of beauty studio Studio.Lbb recommends it to anyone who’d rather skip the curler altogether. “A lash lift enhances your natural lashes by lifting, curling and tinting them for a longer, darker and more defined look—without extensions or mascara,” she says. Results usually last six to eight weeks, so it’s a smart one to book before a vacation or a stretch of early mornings.

For a quick lift without needles, celebrity facialist and founder of M.A.S.C. Studio Alessandro Cirino points to microcurrent, which sends low-level electrical currents through the skin to wake up the muscles underneath. “Microcurrent can help tone and firm the eye contour, for a fresher-looking result,” he adds. As for a permanent result, Cirino says the research is still limited. Think of it more as a refresh before a big day unless you keep up with a daily routine.

Most people think of crow’s feet when it comes to injectables around the eyes, but the brow is the less obvious spot for injection. “When strategically placed, neuromodulators can subtly elevate the outer brow by relaxing the muscles responsible for pulling it downward,” says Dr. Casey Goodman, medical director of TIEV. “In the right patient, even a slight elevation can make the eyes appear more open, rested, and refreshed.” Dosing matters more here than almost anywhere else on the face. “Overcorrecting can compromise natural expression or even create heaviness where we intended to create lift,” Goodman says. “The goal isn’t to make someone look treated. It’s to make them look like themselves, without the subtle signs of fatigue that may not reflect how they actually feel.”

Do eye creams actually get rid of dark circles?

Though eye creams can soften the appearance of dark circles, they’re (alas) not a one-swipe magic wand. “Eye creams can improve the quality of the skin around your eyes, but they can’t fix every undereye concern,” says Brauer. “Eye creams mainly help to moisturize and protect the skin around your eyes—one of the most delicate areas of skin on your face. The right eye cream can help to minimize dark circles, but they won’t get rid of them permanently.” So, what to do when those dark circles crop up? Read on.
