AG Blanche, FBI’s Patel dodge questions about Jensen Huang investigation

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel on Tuesday dodged questions about the conclusions of an investigation President Donald Trump cited to push Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO.

Asked by a reporter whether the Department of Justice had investigated Huang and the results of that probe, Blanche said, "What the president said is that he talked to a lot of people going into the decision that he made to encourage the board to reinstate him."

"And so I'm not going to get into communications I have with the president," Blanche said at a news conference in Washington, D.C.

Huang was reinstated as CEO of Nvidia on Monday morning, less than two weeks after resigning amid allegation had had affairs with a purported spy for China and two Nvidia employees, as well as concerns that advance semiconductor chips made by his company had been diverted to China in violation of U.S. law.

In an interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump was asked what led him to personally confirm to Nvidia's board that Huang was not a national security risk.

"We've done a very thorough investigation, and the investigation showed that he did, I mean, he had bad judgment having to do with somewhat unrelated incidents, but he did nothing wrong," Trump replied.

Trump in a Truth Social post on Monday said the U.S. government had no reason to believe Huang's return to the company "poses any risk to National Security," and that Blanche had confirmed the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

Nvidia, in its statement Monday announcing Huang's return, said, "The Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk."

Blanche on Tuesday said, "Nvidia is a good corporate citizen."

"Obviously, if Nvidia was the target, or was the subject of some criminal investigation, or they were obstructing or thwarting our investigation, then that wouldn't be the message that the president is saying to the American people," Blanche said.

Patel did not answer a reporter's question of whether the FBI has spoken to Huang, and whether the FBI had been made aware of an assessment by U.S. intelligence agencies that Huang's reported paramour, Alice Huang, was an asset for China's spy agency.

Jensen Huang is not related to Alice Huang, who was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer of Nvidia's semiconductor chips.

The DOJ and Commerce Department on Nov. 17 said they were investigating Megaspeed for potential diversion of Nvidia chips in violation of U.S. export law.

Asked how the DOJ could rule out Jensen Huang having potential criminal liability given the fact that the investigation remains ongoing, Blanche replied, "So, when you say the export investigation is still ongoing, I don't know what you're talking about."

"We have hundreds, if not more, export investigations going at any time at the Department of Justice," Blanche said.

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Fewer choices available to enrollees on ACA exchanges: RWJF" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The number of regions where patients have limited choices for Affordable Care Act plans is on the rise, according to a new analysis.

The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation report found that for the 2027 plan year, there are more than 200 counties in which there is only one insurer available, and close to 1,000 where there are just two plans available for consumers to choose from.

This makes for an increase of 50% from the 2026 plan year, with more than a third of counties having limited options on the ACA's marketplaces for 2027.

Most states (39) will have at least one county where plan choices are limited for enrollees. For about half of these states, this is the norm and aligns with 2026, but for the rest, this marks a significant shift, the RWJF researchers wrote in the report.

"For the individual market as a whole, the decline in participation is sobering but not a fatal blow," per the report. "The presence of federal and some state subsidies provides an important backstop that will likely prevent a complete meltdown."

"Yet the prospect of a growing number of counties with little insurer participation is grim, and likely will not bode well for premium increases, morbidity or enrollment going forward," according to the report.

The foundation cites a recent analysis from Wakely that found the individual market declined by 16% in the first six months of 2026. The end of the enhanced premium tax credits, cost pressures and growing administrative burdens are likely to contribute to worsening trends in this space.

For instance, ACA marketplace exits from Cigna, CareSource and Medica are having a significant impact in Iowa and Indiana for the coming plan year, the researchers found. In the latter case, most Indiana counties have two or fewer plan choices.

However, there are some bright spots, RWJF researchers wrote. There are certain insurers participating in the exchanges who have beefed up their footprints, and there is a smattering of new entrants. Oscar Health, for example, is planning the largest growth, entering a new state and adding new counties in some of the states it already serves.

Some insurers, like Centene and UnitedHealthcare, are making strategic exits in some markets while entering others, for a neutral impact on their footprints overall.

Insurers like Antidote Health and Peak Health are also entering new regions—even if on a limited basis in some cases—which can help staunch the bleeding.

Blue Cross Blue Shield plans also often play a key role in limited markets, as in close to 80% of counties with a single insurer, that payer is a Blues plan. In addition, in 12% of counties, the single insurer is Elevance Health, which operates plans as Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield.

"This suggests that were it not for the somewhat unique role of Blues plans as a backstop, the threat of a bare county might be higher," per the report. "Single-state Blues, in particular, are under social and political pressure to participate in much if not all of their state, and do not feel as free to exit unprofitable counties as are publicly traded insurers."

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/voters-expect-lawmakers-address-healthcare-affordability-access-poll" hreflang="en">Voters expect lawmakers to address healthcare affordability, access: poll</a>

Voters expect lawmakers to address healthcare affordability, access: poll" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Half of voters said they are paying more this year in premiums and out-of-pocket costs, according to a new national poll.

Lake Research Partners and Bellwether Research & Consulting surveyed 1,000 voters between Oct. 20 and Oct. 28 on behalf of the National Health Council, and 50% said their premiums and out-of-pocket costs are higher this year compared to last year. Among those with commercial insurance, 63% said their premiums have risen and 60% said their out-of-pocket costs have grown year-over-year.

In addition, 25% of those surveyed said they had a claim for a prescription denied, and 24% said they waited more than two weeks for a prior authorization decision. Twenty-one percent said that they struggled to find a specialist.

Healthcare costs and the broader economy were major factors in Democrats' significant victories in the latest midterm election. And the NHC poll finds that voters expect Congress to act in addressing the costs that they face.

Most of the surveyed voters (78%) said they believe that the healthcare system warrants major changes or a complete overhaul, and 84% said they would support a candidate that would make it a priority to reform the healthcare insurance system.

However, 74% of those surveyed rated the effort from elected officials to address affordability and access as either fair or poor.

“Holding onto a plan isn’t the same as being protected by it. Patients are watching their costs rise and their coverage change, and they’re worried about what that means for their families down the road,” said Randall Rutta, CEO of the National Health Council, in an announcement. “The new Congress has a real opportunity to change that, and patients across the country will be watching.”

The survey highlights a number of potential policies that would have broad voter support. For example, 91% of those surveyed said they would support eliminating surprise medical bills and policies to require price transparency.

In addition, 90% said they would back efforts to reduce out-of-pocket costs, while 89% said they favor requiring health plans to cover treatments that were prescribed by doctors. Eighty-eight percent support efforts to increase screening and early detection for medical risks.

"Voters aren't asking lawmakers to talk about health care differently," said Christine Matthews, President of Bellwether Research and Consulting, in the release. "They want to see it work differently. Reform is a priority they expect the new Congress to deliver on."

U.S. is preparing to raise tariffs on China, the E.U., and 14 other nations

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The U.S. Trade Representative is expected to unveil results of its industrial subsidies investigation as soon as Tuesday

Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times via Getty Images

The U.S. is getting ready to hike duties on imports from China, the European Union and 14 other economies in response to their industrial subsidies, according to The Wall Street Journal.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could announce the results of his office's investigation as soon as Tuesday, according to Bloomberg. The timing could change, according to The Wall Street Journal. Tariff rates and an effective date are unclear, according to Bloomberg.

The probe falls under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act, a provision that lets a president act alone in levying tariffs in response to unfair trade conduct, according to Bloomberg. The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative opened the investigation in March, following the high court's invalidation of President Donald Trump's earlier worldwide tariffs.

Greer has said that state backing, subsidies among it, has left the targeted economies with more industrial production capacity than they need. He has said that this lets companies in those economies undercut competitors abroad on price, which hurts American manufacturers. The U.S. Trade Representative's office declined to comment, according to The Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg. The White House did not respond to requests for comment from The Wall Street Journal or Bloomberg.

The new tariffs are expected to apply to the bulk of goods coming from the targeted nations, among them China, Japan, South Korea and the E.U.'s 27 member states, according to The Wall Street Journal. Steel, aluminum and automobiles are expected to be left out because they are already subject to duties under another law tied to national security.

Administration officials have said they plan to set rates close to the ones Trump put in place during 2025, ahead of the Supreme Court's invalidation of his initial round of tariffs in February 2026, according to The Wall Street Journal. Countries with trade agreements, including Japan, the E.U. and South Korea, are expected to face rates of 15% or lower. Vietnam and India could be hit with steeper rates that reflect the agreements they struck with Trump.

China is expected to be charged a steeper tariff than many of its peers because Trump officials have described its subsidies as especially far-reaching, according to The Wall Street Journal. The administration could add wording that would let it increase tariffs if a country's overcapacity in industry grows worse or if the country fails to change its policies during later talks.

The new duties are expected to stack on top of tariffs announced in July over forced labor, according to The Wall Street Journal. Those tariffs, which run from 10% to 12.5% on about 60 economies, are being contested in a lawsuit before the Court of International Trade, according to Bloomberg.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/academic-hospital-heads-petition-congress-2-year-delay-cms-340b-cuts" hreflang="en">Academic hospital heads petition Congress for 2-year delay to CMS' 340B cuts</a>

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Academic hospital heads petition Congress for 2-year delay to CMS' 340B cuts" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Dozens of academic health system and teaching hospital chief executives are turning to Congress in a bid to blunt a controversial subsidy cut coming to Medicare Part B in the new year.

Earlier this month, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services locked in plans to reduce reimbursements for outpatient drugs dispensed by participants in the 340B Drug Pricing Program during the 2027 calendar year.

Specifically, the agency said it will move from the current rate of average sales price (ASP) plus 6% down to ASP minus 28%, with a corresponding 7.5% increase in non-drug outpatient payments to maintain budget neutrality. CMS said the change will reduce Original Medicare drug payments by an estimated $4.35 billion and drug payments from beneficiaries by $1.1 billion during the policy’s first year.

Hospital groups—particularly those that chiefly represent nonprofit or safety-net hospitals—have denounced the plan’s disproportionate effect on hospitals participating in the decades-old subsidy program, as well as a gift to for-profit hospitals that do not participate but will benefit from the budget-neutral offset.

With their formal opposition to the policy’s proposal set aside by CMS, 95 executives whose organizations are members of the Association of American Medical Colleges have now penned a letter to leaders of the House and the Senate pleading for a two-year delay to the rate cut’s implementation.

“This policy will exacerbate already negative Medicare margins at a time when teaching hospitals are already grappling with rising financial challenges resulting from inflation, workforce shortages, manufacturer-driven restrictions on the 340B program and an expected rise in uncompensated care costs,” they wrote.

The executives acknowledged that the final policy’s rate of ASP minus 28% is “slightly smaller” than CMS’ initial proposal of ASP minus 33.4%, but said the change was still a threat to the country’s healthcare safety net. They noted that the cuts represent a nearly $5.5 billion reduction that would be disproportionately shouldered by AAMC-member teaching hospitals, who comprise 17% of all impacted hospitals but will see almost $3 billion of that total.

Those funds are necessary to finance critical care services at academic hospitals including trauma care, inpatient behavioral care, transplant surgery and labor and delivery, the signatories warned lawmakers. They also noted that their institutions are major referral hubs that accept “the most complex transfer cases from lower acuity settings, such as rural and community hospitals,” in a way that for-profit hospitals and others who will benefit from the final rule do not.

“While we understand that Congress has an interest in improving the 340B program, and are broadly supportive of meaningful transparency and accountability for covered entities, we also strongly believe that cutting Medicare payment to 340B hospitals is not the appropriate policy mechanism to achieve these goals,” they wrote. “Therefore, we respectfully request that you support a two-year delay in the [Outpatient Prospective Payment System] rule’s payment cuts for 340B-acquired drugs.”

Among the letter’s 95 signatories were the heads of Cedars-Sinai, Stanford Health Care, Yale New Haven Health, Tampa General Hospital, Rush University System for Health, Mass General Brigham, Nebraska Medicine, RWJBarnabas Health, NewYork-Presbyterian, UNC Health and School of Medicine, OHSU, Jefferson Health, Penn Medicine, Vanderbilt Health and Froedtert ThedaCare.

The academic health systems and hospitals’ turn to lawmakers comes after their rebukes of CMS’ 340B policy have fallen on deaf ears. The agency and broader Trump administration have often sided with critics of the subsidy program on related initiatives like a pilot rebate program, citing 340B’s rapid growth and contribution to increased healthcare spending. A Health and Human Services Office of the Assistant Secretary for Planning and Evaluation policy brief defending the rate cut made those views crystal clear with claims that the program has incentivized provider consolidation while doing little to serve patients’ needs.

Meanwhile, Congress, spurred by public pressure to reduce healthcare costs, has proposed its own legislative fixes to 340B. Though each bill aims to preserve the program and subsidize safety-net care, some are more friendly to the hospital industry than others.

Jensen Huang to face DC grilling the same day Nvidia releases new products at GTC

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Jensen Huang is back as the CEO of Nvidia, and is now tasked with selling the company's artificial intelligence vision while also defending himself in Washington.

As Nvidia employees celebrated the return of their leader to the company's headquarters, Huang told the gathered crowd, "I'll be a better CEO the second time around."

Huang will need to be a better CEO to head off the issues surrounding Nvidia and the broader AI industry exposed by Huang's exit. The challenges include a proliferating roster of investigations and hearings from Congress, regulators and state-level officials, as well as technical and competitive questions about how long Nvidia's currently dominant graphics processing units can remain on top.

"There's nothing prohibiting him from doing what he does on his day job," said Katten chair and former federal prosecutor Gil Soffer. But investigations take significant prep time, Soffer said. "You don't just walk in cold."

At Nvidia's GTC conference in Washington, D.C., next week, the company will attempt to convince customers, partners and suppliers that it is developing the future of AI. At the same time, lawmakers will be pressing its newly reinstated CEO over what he knew and when about Megaspeed executive Alice Huang.

Huang, before his scandals, was scheduled to give the GTC DC keynote speech on Dec. 1 to discuss new Nvidia products and the trajectory of the AI ecosystem. In the past, the two-hour speech was the highlight of the event with hordes of fans full of the Huang-inspired "Jensanity."

"People will want to see that it's business as usual, which hopefully for Nvidia shareholders will mean that Jensen Huang announces some new exciting products," said Babson College associate professor Peter Cohan.

Right now, Nvidia has Ian Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing, filling the slot. A representative for Nvidia declined to answer multiple questions about whether Huang would appear at the company's event.

On the same day, Huang is scheduled to speak with three House committees — Foreign Affairs, Select Committee on the CCP, and the Select Committee on Intelligence — behind closed doors, where his interview will be transcribed.

"The NVIDIA board's brief internal investigation and reinstatement of Jensen Huang do not erase the national security threats and questions that must be addressed," the chairs said in a joint statement. They said that Huang has confirmed he will appear for the transcribed interview on Dec. 1.

Next Tuesday's interviews are just the first of a slew of investigations.

On Dec. 3, just as Nvidia's conference is wrapping up, the Senate Banking Committee will hold a hearing titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls." Sen. Tim Scott, R-S.C., who chairs the committee, said Huang will publicly testify as the sole witness.

California's attorney general is also investigating Huang's remarks, and politicians from the Senate Finance, Intelligence, and Foreign Relations committees have inquired about the scandal. In the House, Energy and Commerce has requested a company briefing from Nvidia, in addition to the three committees mentioned earlier. That's not counting an investigation from the Department of Justice and a reported investigation from the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Plus, Huang still has to cooperate with an investigation from the board of directors, led by the law firm Wachtell Lipton.

Meanwhile, Nvidia needs Huang to reassure investors and the broader AI ecosystem that the company is developing new products that will be in high demand. Investors had said that while Nvidia's products for the next two years have been fully designed, they were worried that the chipmaker's pace of innovation would slow without Huang.

Nvidia is facing new competition from chips from its biggest customers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon and Microsoft. The companies have been developing their own chips to reduce reliance on Nvidia, as well as to control AI costs.

"There was already this worry that we're spending too much on it," said Stephanie Balaouras, vice president of group research at Forrester. "One of those cost management strategies is to match ... the workload requirements to the right infrastructure. You don't need to put everything on a Nvidia GPU."

Many in the AI industry see Huang as one of the staunchest defenders of "open-source" AI that is free to use for commercial purposes, as compared with "frontier models" from companies like Anthropic and OpenAI, which charge users by the token.

Nvidia has also been spending heavily to develop its own open-source models called Nemotron. Currently, Nvidia Vice President Kari Briski is scheduled to present new Nemotron technology at GTC.

On Monday, Nvidia's board said that "senior levels" of the U.S. government provided input on Huang's potential national security risk. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he was central to convincing Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang and said he gave the board assurances.

Trump's help could draw even more scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers, who were already focused on probing Nvidia's chip sales.

"Today's news that Donald Trump engineered the rehiring of Jensen Huang at Nvidia is Exhibit A about the political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration's big tech and AI policy," said Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland in a call with reporters Monday.

Republicans have also criticized Huang's return as the focus on the company's China sales intensifies. Still, the investigations could start to weigh on Nvidia's CEO, experts say.

"How could he possibly do the job?" Jeff Sonnenfeld, senior associate dean at the Yale School of Management, told CNBC, citing Senate investigations, House investigations and other state investigations, even before Democrats take over next year.

And the reaction in Washington to Huang's scandal could start to affect Nvidia's sales.

Last week, the Department of Commerce announced that it will prevent Chinese companies from getting around export controls by renting Nvidia's chips across borders. On Monday, Commerce also added Megaspeed to a trade blacklist.

And it's not just Nvidia that is relying on a strong performance from Huang. The company's investors, customers, partners and shareholders will also look to see if Nvidia still wants to be at the center of the AI ecosystem. Before Huang was removed, he pushed Nvidia to invest in AI startups, while selling them products, and helping finance their infrastructure.

Remarks from Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress last week while she was interim CEO suggested that Nvidia would be taking a "very strong look" at using its balance sheet to support the AI ecosystem, but it's unclear if Huang's second stint as CEO will continue with his prior showmanship or abide by a more measured approach.

Consumers grew significantly more optimistic in November in the wake of the midterm elections, with a growing number of Americans now seeing improvement in the labor market and expecting inflation to cool.

The Conference Board survey released Tuesday showed improvement across the board, with the headline confidence index jumping to 103.3, up 11.4 points from October and much better than the Dow Jones consensus forecast for 92.2.

Confidence among Democrats and independent voters improved sharply, the Conference Board said, while confidence among Republican respondents fell slightly. Still, confidence among Republicans remained the highest among the three groups.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/centralized-platform-submitting-managing-out-network-billing-disputes-launches" hreflang="en">Centralized platform for submitting, managing out-of-network billing disputes launches</a>

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
Centralized platform for submitting, managing out-of-network billing disputes launches" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The Trump administration has launched a new online portal for payers, providers and other stakeholders to manage and monitor disputes under the federal Independent Dispute Resolution process.

The IDR Gateway went online Nov. 13 and was officially unveiled Nov. 20. It replaces the web forms parties previously used to manage disputes, most of which will no longer be available outside of the centralized platform after Jan. 15, 2027, per a government fact sheet.

Payers will eventually be required to register on IDR Gateway so that it is easier for providers to identify the correct party, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services said earlier this year when finalizing an overhaul of No Surprises Act protocols. That registration feature will be added “in the future on a rolling basis,” CMS said last week, with others like in-portal negotiation similarly rolling out over time “to improve accountability and reduce unnecessary filings,” the agency said last spring.

For now, the IDR Gateway allows parties and their representatives to initiate a dispute, respond to proposed selections of IDR entities and submit a payment offer. Its other tools allow users to view, search, set up notifications and broadly analyze open disputes for their organization; an IDR Gateway Dashboard, CMS said, offers a color-coded donut chart breaking down an organization’s different types of open disputes by phase, while another tool can generate detailed reports of dispute line items.

The administration also touted security measures inherent to the new portal. These include a new sign-up process that requires users to verify their identity with an approved credential service provider, and other protocols like a terms and conditions agreement that notes unauthorized users could be subject to criminal prosecution.

Reference-guided generation lets a creator provide more than a prompt. In a conventional exact-label score, confusing hesitation with uncertainty is simply an error. So is confusing hesitation with agreement. Treating those two mistakes alike makes it easier to compare labels consistently, but it can hide whether a model picked up a relevant cue and interpreted it differently from an observer.

Interhuman’s alternative, Evidence-Weighted F1, gives partial credit for related labels and gives more weight to a signal backed by stronger annotation evidence. Each expert-labeled clip is assessed independently by at least three trained raters. Two small details mattered more than I expected. Sorting by id before locking means two overlapping requests always grab locks in the same order, which keeps deadlocks rare. And keeping the transaction short matters. Anything slow inside it, like calling a payment provider, holds those locks the whole time and turns a busy on-sale into a queue.

The trade-off: row locks make busy rows a bottleneck. For a hot event, a lot of requests end up waiting on the same few rows. That's still the right call. A short wait is annoying. Selling one seat twice is a refund, an angry customer, and a bad review.

Caching inventory in a ticketing system is risky. If the cache says 12 seats are left and the database says 3, you've built a fresh way to show people seats that don't exist. You also now have two sources of truth to keep in sync during exactly the moments when everything is under load. So the rule I followed was simple: find out why the query is slow first.

Indexes aren't free. Every insert and update on holds or orders now also updates the index, and ticketing is write-heavy during an on-sale. So I didn't index every column that showed up in a WHERE. Each index had to match a real, frequent query, checked with EXPLAIN ANALYZE before and after. A partial index is a nice middle ground here, since it only pays the write cost for rows that match its condition.

I also used CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY on live tables. It's slower to build, but it doesn't block writes while it runs, and blocking writes on a ticketing table is not something you want to explain.

Generating a PDF ticket with a QR code, sending the confirmation email, expiring unpaid holds: none of this needs to happen while the user stares at a spinner. Doing it inline makes checkout slow, and it means a hiccup at the email provider can fail a purchase that already succeeded.

So the request does the transactional part, commits, and then queues the rest:

Queueing after the commit matters. Queue it inside the transaction and a fast worker can pick up the job before the commit lands, look for the order, and not find it. Or the transaction rolls back and you've emailed a ticket for an order that doesn't exist.

Celery makes retries a one-liner. The harder part is making sure a task that runs twice doesn't do the work twice. And it will run twice eventually. A worker restarts mid-task, a broker redelivers a message, a retry fires after the first attempt actually succeeded.

My rule for every task: re-read the current state from the database and decide from that, never from what the message says.

The confirmation_sent_at check means a duplicate run does nothing. The idempotency key covers the gap where the email went out but the worker crashed before saving that it did. If the provider supports idempotency keys, it drops the repeat on its end. Ticket generation followed the same pattern, with a unique constraint on ticket rows per order and seat, so a second run can't create a second set of tickets.

Hold expiry works the same way. The scheduled task only releases a hold if it's still held and actually past expires_at, checked under a lock. That covers the awkward race where a user pays in the last second while the expiry job is running. Whichever transaction gets the row first wins, and the other one sees the new state and backs off.

acks_late=True is part of this trade. It means a task is only acknowledged after it finishes, so a crash causes a redelivery instead of a lost job. You accept "might run twice" to avoid "might never run," which is only a good deal if your tasks are idempotent.

For the endpoints the frontend depends on, tests check the shape of the response, not just the status code: required fields, types, and error formats. When someone changes a response model, the contract test fails in CI instead of the checkout page failing for users.

It doesn't behave like PostgreSQL around locking, FOR UPDATE, partial indexes, or concurrent transactions, which are exactly the things this post is about. A test that passes on the wrong database doesn't tell you much.

CI runs the same Docker Compose setup: start Postgres and Redis, run migrations, run the suite, build the image, deploy. If it passes there, it behaves the same way in production.

Let PostgreSQL decide who gets the seat. A conditional UPDATE or SELECT ... FOR UPDATE inside a short transaction beats any check you write in application code.

Compute prices on the server, from the database, inside the same transaction as the hold.

Run EXPLAIN ANALYZE before you add a cache. Most of our gains came from a few well-chosen indexes, and caching inventory adds its own consistency bugs.

Queue background jobs after the commit, and write every task assuming it'll run twice.

Test the race and the rollback against the same database you run in production.

The hard part of a ticketing backend isn't the endpoints. It's the few seconds when everyone clicks at once, and the jobs that run after the user has closed the tab. En mars dernier, je pensais surtout ouvrir les archives d’un jeu Macintosh de 1991. Quelques mois plus tard, Hellcats Over The Pacific fonctionne dans Godot et peut se lancer dans un navigateur.

Je n’ai personnellement écrit aucune des fonctions C ou GDScript nécessaires à cette reconstruction. Ouvrir la démonstration dans un nouvel onglet

Cette démonstration charge la scène Native actuelle. C’est un état de travail, pas une réédition officielle ni la promesse que chaque mission se comporte déjà exactement comme sur le Macintosh original.

Le premier article, publié le 30 mars 2026, racontait l’ouverture des archives. À ce moment-là, le projet consistait surtout à retrouver les disquettes, examiner les fichiers avec ResEdit et commencer à comprendre ce qu’un exécutable Macintosh classique contenait encore après trente-cinq ans.

Hellcats a été écrit pour un processeur Motorola 68000, la mémoire et la Toolbox du Macintosh classique, ses fenêtres, ses événements, son affichage et son son. Un Mac de 1991 et un ordinateur de 2026 ne parlent pas le même langage. Il ne suffisait donc pas de copier le programme, ni de refaire ses graphismes. Je voulais retrouver sa logique et reconstruire les services dont il avait besoin.

ResEdit a extrait les menus, dialogues, images, sons et segments de code. Ghidra a ouvert une vue sur l’assembleur 68k. Claude Code a relié ces indices et transformé progressivement l’analyse en code exécutable. Godot est devenu le lieu où ces couches pouvaient se rejoindre.

Le projet contient aujourd’hui trois approches complémentaires. Elles répondent à trois questions différentes : comment retrouver le comportement d’origine, comment sortir peu à peu de l’ancien Macintosh, puis comment adapter le résultat aux appareils actuels ?

Original est la référence historique du projet. Cette version reprend la logique issue du programme de 1991 et reconstruit autour d’elle le Macintosh nécessaire : mémoire 68k, chargement des segments, parties utilisées de la Toolbox, fenêtres, événements, entrées, affichage indexé et son.

Ce n’est pas une image complète de Mac OS 9 lancée dans Godot. Contrairement à un émulateur généraliste comme InfiniteMac, Original reconstruit seulement le matériel et les services effectivement sollicités par Hellcats. Le programme traduit retrouve ainsi son environnement sans que ses règles soient réinventées.

Native conserve les règles d’Original, mais remplace peu à peu ses dépendances historiques par Godot. Les commandes sont remappables, les menus et dialogues peuvent utiliser l’interface du moteur, les sauvegardes ont leur propre stockage et plusieurs éléments de l’écran, du son, de la caméra, de l’IA et de l’état du jeu sont déjà modernisés.

« Native » ne signifie pas simplement « compilé pour macOS ou Linux ». Il décrit une migration encore partielle : la scène hérite d’Original et de nombreux services Macintosh subsistent. Chaque remplacement doit produire le même comportement observable avant que l’ancienne couche disparaisse.

Enhanced repose sur Native et ajoute des améliorations facultatives. À ce stade, le travail porte surtout sur le mobile : un contrôleur tactile en mode paysage, des adaptations d’interface et une gestion correcte de la mise en arrière-plan pour que le vol ne continue pas pendant que l’application est cachée.

L’export Android a été essayé dans un émulateur jusqu’au début d’un vol, avec commandes tactiles. L’export iOS a atteint le cockpit et repris après une mise en arrière-plan dans le simulateur. Ce résultat ne vaut pas validation sur un iPhone physique ; une mission complète au toucher et l’audio iOS restent à vérifier.

Enhanced n’est donc pas, aujourd’hui, un remaster graphique. C’est la couche qui permet d’explorer de nouveaux usages sans modifier silencieusement Original ni Native.

La reconstruction ne repose pas sur un exécutable unique. Trois corpus ont dû être étudiés séparément.

Hellcats 1.0.3 constitue la base principale. Ses disquettes, ressources et segments de code ont fourni la chaîne la plus complète, de ResEdit et Ghidra jusqu’au GDScript exécuté par Original.

Hellcats 1.0.4 est reconstruite à partir de la 1.0.3 et de son updater d’origine, qui apporte notamment un autre pilote sonore et quelques ressources modifiées. Elle peut être sélectionnée au lancement et conserve son propre chemin sonore.

Leyte Gulf est plus qu’un décor supplémentaire. L’extension possède sa propre application, ses ressources, une organisation mémoire différente et un overlay plus important. Une partie du code a été déplacée ou réécrite : il a fallu établir des correspondances plutôt que copier la 1.0.3. Le travail atteint le décollage, le vol, le crash, le replay et le débriefing d’une mission perdue, mais pas encore tous les objectifs ni un parcours de victoire authentique.

Ces trois cas ont rappelé une règle importante : deux versions très proches à l’écran peuvent demander des analyses différentes sous le capot.

Ghidra, Claude Code et Godot ont chacun joué un rôle distinct.

Ghidra lit les instructions Motorola 68000 et produit une représentation proche du C. Il ne ressuscite pas le code source : noms, commentaires, types et intentions ont en partie disparu. Une fonction décompilée montre ce que la machine fait, pas forcément pourquoi.

Claude Code a examiné les appelants, les données et les ressources, proposé des noms compréhensibles, identifié des structures, puis traduit la logique vers Godot. Il pouvait parcourir beaucoup plus de fichiers que je ne l’aurais fait manuellement tout en maintenant les relations avec le binaire.

Je n’ai jamais considéré ses réponses comme des preuves. Les artefacts originaux restent intacts et chaque étape est reliée à la précédente. En cas de divergence, il faut revenir aux octets, aux ressources ou au comportement observable.

Godot fournit la boucle d’exécution, les entrées, l’audio, l’affichage et les exports modernes. Le même projet accueille Original, la migration Native et les adaptations Enhanced. Le build Web permet enfin d’essayer le résultat sans ancien Macintosh.

Cela ne signifie pas que le travail s’est fait tout seul. Il fallait comprendre le système d’origine pour poser les bonnes questions : ce qui appartient au processeur, à la Toolbox ou aux ressources, ce qui doit rester identique et ce qui peut être confié à Godot.

Mon rôle a été celui d’un architecte et d’un pilote technique : définir les objectifs, découper les étapes, choisir les sources de vérité, formuler les demandes, examiner les écarts et corriger la direction lorsqu’elle menait à une imitation plutôt qu’au fonctionnement réel.

La précision des prompts a compté. « Refais le jeu dans Godot » n’aurait produit qu’une approximation. Une demande utile fixe au contraire une frontière : préserver les fichiers d’origine, analyser une seule couche, citer les preuves, garder les incertitudes visibles, obtenir un résultat observable, puis comparer ce résultat avec la référence avant de continuer.

L’IA n’a pas remplacé la compréhension technique. Elle a remplacé une grande partie de la programmation manuelle. Sans connaissances d’architecture, il aurait été difficile de repérer une confusion entre mémoire 68k et état Godot, ou entre décompilation et code source original.

La méthode a fonctionné parce que je n’ai jamais demandé le jeu « en entier » à l’IA. Chaque étape devait livrer un résultat observable et comparable.

Conserver les artefacts. J’ai commencé par réunir les disquettes, les applications, les ressources, les versions et l’updater sans modifier les originaux. Ils restent la référence lorsqu’une interprétation doit être revue.

Comprendre la machine. Avant de reconstruire le logiciel, il fallait identifier le processeur, l’organisation de la mémoire, le format des programmes, les services système, le graphisme, le son et les entrées du Macintosh classique.

Extraire et décompiler. ResEdit a rendu les ressources visibles. Ghidra a transformé l’assembleur 68k en une représentation de type C, plus facile à parcourir mais encore dépourvue des noms et du contexte du code source perdu.

Faire analyser les relations. Claude Code a relié les fonctions à leurs appelants, aux données et aux ressources qu’elles manipulent. Les noms et structures retrouvés restent des conclusions à étayer, pas une vérité créée par le modèle.

Reconstruire par couches. Le travail a commencé par le chargement des ressources et la mémoire, puis le démarrage, les premiers écrans, les interactions, la simulation et les missions. Cette progression évite de masquer dix erreurs derrière un résultat final impossible à diagnostiquer.

Obtenir un résultat observable. Un dialogue qui s’ouvre, un écran qui se dessine ou un avion qui répond aux commandes constitue un point de contrôle. Chaque résultat réduit la zone d’incertitude avant la prochaine étape.

Comparer les comportements. Une image ressemblante ne suffit pas. Il faut vérifier les actions, les transitions, les calculs et les effets produits par rapport au programme historique. Lorsqu’un écart apparaît, la correction doit viser la couche responsable.

Migrer et exporter. Une fois Original suffisamment établi, Native peut remplacer les dépendances historiques une par une. Enhanced peut ensuite adapter cette base au tactile et au mobile. Godot rend enfin possibles le Web, le bureau, Android et iOS, sous réserve de valider réellement chaque plateforme.

Ce découpage rend les prompts plus efficaces : l’IA travaille mieux avec une source vérifiable, une frontière claire et un critère de réussite concret.

Le principe est transposable : retrouver les binaires et les ressources, comprendre l’ordinateur d’origine, désassembler ou décompiler, faire analyser les résultats par une IA, reconstruire l’environnement nécessaire, comparer le comportement, puis migrer vers une plateforme moderne.

Cela ne signifie pas que n’importe quel jeu peut être restauré automatiquement. Un programme protégé, un format inconnu, des données manquantes, un processeur mal documenté ou l’absence d’un exemplaire fonctionnel peuvent rendre le travail beaucoup plus difficile. Les droits de redistribution des ressources historiques posent également une question distincte de la réussite technique.

L’approche exige surtout une personne capable de guider l’enquête. Il faut reconnaître les limites de la décompilation, savoir quand une hypothèse n’est pas prouvée et empêcher l’IA de remplir les blancs avec une solution plausible mais infidèle. La méthode est reproductible dans son principe ; son coût et ses chances de succès dépendent des preuves disponibles.

Native possède déjà des commandes remappables, des couches d’interface Godot, des sauvegardes séparées et plusieurs sous-systèmes modernes, mais dépend encore largement de l’environnement Original. La démonstration Web est donc un jalon de cette migration, pas son aboutissement.

La version 1.0.4 est reconstruite depuis son updater et peut être lancée séparément. Leyte Gulf a beaucoup progressé, mais tous ses scénarios et systèmes ne sont pas encore validés. Enfin, même un grand nombre de comparaisons automatiques ne remplace pas une vérification visuelle et fonctionnelle sur tous les chemins du jeu.

The e-bike industry has a horsepower problem

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

There was a time when 750 watts sounded like a lot of power for an electric bicycle.

These days, apparently those are rookie numbers.

Spend enough time browsing new e-bike launches and you’ll inevitably stumble across bikes advertising 1,000W, 1,500W, 2,000W, or even higher power. Dual-motor models have turned the spec sheet into an arms race, with manufacturers seemingly competing to see who can slap the biggest number onto the product page.

If you ask me, the e-bike industry is developing a horsepower problem.

To be clear, I’m not anti-power. I’ve ridden everything from dainty 250W European commuter bikes to electric motorcycles with enough power to rearrange your chest cavity. Powerful electric two-wheelers are fun, period.

But there’s a difference between building a powerful electric two-wheeler and pretending that every powerful electric two-wheeler is still an electric bicycle.

In the US, the widely adopted three-class e-bike system generally tops out at 20 mph (32 km/h) for Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes and 28 mph (45 km/h) for Class 3, with some minor exceptions, and 750 watts has long been a common benchmark for the motor power limit.

Yet browse online e-bike retailers today and you’ll find plenty of machines advertised with motors well beyond that figure, often capable of speeds far beyond normal electric bicycle classifications.

Sometimes there’s a disclaimer explaining that the bike is intended for “off-road use.” Sometimes there’s a software setting that supposedly limits it for street riding.

And sometimes people just sort of squint at the pedals and agree to keep calling it an electric bicycle.

The problem isn’t that 2,000W electric two-wheelers exist. I love lightweight electric motorcycles and mopeds, or what some are beginning to refer to as ‘e-motos’, despite that terminology having its own issues. So sure, those bikes are great and there is a time and place for such power. The problem is that the industry increasingly seems afraid to call them what they are.

Part of the blame belongs to consumers – to us.

E-bike shopping has become increasingly spec-sheet driven, and wattage provides an incredibly easy number to compare. If ‘Bike A’ has a 750W motor and ‘Bike B’ costs the same but advertises 1,500W, it’s easy to assume ‘Bike B’ is twice as good. This is especially true for younger riders who lack a deeper understanding of how e-bikes work, and just want something powerful and fast to impress their friends after school. They beg their parents for the latest models with the biggest motors, and many parents give in.

The problem is that’s not how electric bikes work – there’s so much more to a bike than the motor wattage rating.

Motor design, controller current, battery voltage, thermal management, gearing, efficiency and software tuning all influence performance. A beautifully tuned 500W or 750W system can be far nicer to ride – and sometimes more useful – than a poorly implemented motor advertising twice the power.

But “excellent controller tuning” doesn’t look nearly as exciting in a giant font on an Amazon listing. So manufacturers respond rationally based on what consumers demand: They make the number bigger.

The result is an e-bike horsepower war that is ultimately hurting the entire industry.

When riders blast through bike paths on motorcycle-performance machines marketed as “e-bikes,” pedestrians don’t care whether the motor technically arrived in a street-legal mode. Police officers aren’t going to inspect controller settings. And lawmakers aren’t famous for approaching emerging transportation technology with surgical precision.

They see a fast machine with pedals, hear complaints about dangerous “e-bikes,” and start writing laws that affect everyone – including riders on perfectly ordinary commuter e-bikes.

In my opinion, that’s the part that should worry anyone who enjoys riding an e-bike.

There’s absolutely a place for high-powered electric two-wheelers. In fact, I want more of them. Lightweight electric mopeds and motorcycles could replace an enormous number of car trips while using a fraction of the energy and space. Those are highly capable machines, but they need to be used appropriately, registered legally, and ridden responsibly.

And we don’t help that cause by pretending they’re bicycles.

Perhaps the e-bike industry’s next great innovation doesn’t need another 500 watts.

Maybe it just needs a little more honesty about what we’re actually building. So let’s build more electric bicycles. And yes, let’s build more e-motos and electric motorcycles. But let’s not confuse the two, either.

At QCon San Francisco 2026, Brian Yang, Member of Technical Staff at OpenAI, described how a small group of engineers used coding agents to build the ChatGPT ads product, reaching $100 million in annualised recurring revenue within six weeks of launch and $1 billion in under 200 days, with zero human-written code.

Yang, previously at Google, Meta and LinkedIn, was a founding member of technical staff on ChatGPT Ads, which was built entirely using Codex. His central claim was that software engineering is alive but reshaped around building the system that builds the product, with the engineer acting more as a manager of agents than a line-by-line contributor. One engineer can now triage an issue, open a pull request, write documentation and unblock CI in parallel, a shift Yang compared to multi-processing.

Every change runs local and unit tests, complex changes get end-to-end user journeys via browser automation, and the most critical changes run inside production-like sandboxes with representative traffic. Sandboxes come in three forms of read-only, write-only with telemetry suppressed, and full-fidelity with telemetry tagged using a shadow tag so it can be filtered out downstream. Yang's team targets around 90% auto-approval of everyday PRs, reviewing the past 300 to 400 PRs weekly and backtesting reviewer thresholds against that rate.

He illustrated the model with a sanitised latency study where candidate PRs targeted time-to-first-ad at P50 and P95 with guardrails for ads returned, timeouts and error rates. Candidates A and B passed; candidate C was rejected because the sandbox returned no ad. Thirty to forty candidates are typically bundled into one PR and run together for a day or two before human review, because changes that pass in isolation can regress when combined.

On failure modes, Yang described codebases filling with "slop" when agent output is unchecked, so maintenance agents sit alongside feature agents to root-cause newly appearing log patterns. Agents over-write tests, so he reviews the 50 slowest tests weekly. He recounted an agent that deleted and re-provisioned slow Kubernetes pods without asking, arguing that teams need big-company controls such as authorised roles for destructive actions and physical confirmation for deployments. Yang encouraged teams to revisit "cached assumptions" such as the belief that four nines of uptime always beats higher revenue, and recommended giving every engineer a monthly AI budget with no leaderboards.

For team adoption, Yang recommended a gradual path from automating one PR, to one feature, to one project, warning that jumping straight to project automation is where systems tend to break. For teams that are resistant to AI, he described rotating an "AI champion" every two weeks. The champion picks the team's most tedious recurring task, such as GPU provisioning at OpenAI which can take hours of babysitting, snapshots and permissions work, teaches an agent to do it end-to-end as a reusable skill, and demos the result at the end of the fortnight. Over a year, that converts 26 pain points into agent-owned workflows and gives sceptical engineers tangible value rather than abstract promises.

Day to day, Yang prefers Codex's bidirectional voice mode over typing for task intake, spending around ten minutes being interviewed by the agent about requirements before agreeing on a spec and handing it to multiple agents. He uses an orchestrator thread that spawns five sub-threads to explore different approaches in parallel and picks the strongest result, rather than copy-pasting the same prompt into several chats. On models, he switches only when a new release makes the workflow feel easier, with fewer clarifying questions and equivalent output quality, rather than chasing benchmark scores. His closing message was that engineers will increasingly do for agents what managers currently do for engineers, requiring an expansion of skills around direction-setting, architecture and verification rather than their replacement.

Review: Cumulus Mountlite Down Jacket

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Cumulus Mountlite is a very warm ultralight winter down jacket designed for winter hiking, ski touring, and climbing. Weighing just 12.9 ounces, it is insulated with 6.3 ounces of highly compressible 900-fill-power goose down, delivering an outstanding warmth-to-weight ratio. An adjustable hood, a #3 YKK zipper with dual sliders, and three zippered pockets round out this impressively warm jacket.

The Mountlite’s key benefit is weight efficiency. The jacket weighs 12.9 ounces in size medium, making it a lightweight option among dedicated winter layers. Of that total weight, 6.35 ounces is 900-fill-power Polish goose-down insulation. This high ratio means that roughly half the garment’s total mass is dedicated directly to trapping body heat. Premium down allows the jacket to compress efficiently and recover its loft quickly after being unpacked in cold environments.

To protect the down insulation from external moisture and wind, Cumulus utilizes 10D Pertex Quantum for the outer shell and liner. This fabric provides a windproof barrier while allowing interior moisture vapor to escape during low-intensity movement. The fabric is thin to minimize total weight but requires careful handling around sharp winter tools.

The hood is shaped to offer close coverage around the face and neck, helping to retain heat during windy stops. The zipper features a standard YKK zipper with 2 sliders that operates reliably in freezing conditions. For draft management, the jacket uses low-profile elastic wrist cuffs and an adjustable hem cinch to seal out cold air from below. The external pockets, one chest and two handwarmer pockets, are positioned to remain accessible while wearing a backpack hip belt or a climbing harness. The jacket is stuffable in its left hand pocket and has an external loop.

The jacket’s insulation is held in sewn-through baffles, which you wouldn’t expect in a jacket this warm. This isn’t the most efficient design because the stitching creates small cold spots with little or no insulation. It may also provide less loft and warmth than a box-baffle jacket. Still, sewn-through construction can be lighter, which is why I suspect Cumulus used it here. I’m honestly amazed at how warm this jacket is regardless of the baffle construction technique, and it is a delight to carry on summit hikes because it packs up so small when compressed.

The Mountlite has an athletic cut, which you should keep in mind when sizing. Its adjustable hood (rear toggle) is helmet-compatible, a useful feature for ski touring, climbing, and mountaineering. Even so, it still insulates very well when used without a helmet. The hood can help reduce heat loss during exposed stops and windy conditions.

The Cumulus Mountlite is an exceptional winter layer for anyone who values serious warmth without accepting unnecessary weight or bulk. Its 900-fill-power goose down, lightweight Pertex Quantum shell, thoughtful hood design, harness-friendly pockets, and compact packed size make it remarkably capable on winter hikes, ski tours, climbs, and summit pushes. Despite its sewn-through construction and delicate shell fabric, the jacket delivers impressive warmth in a package that is easy to carry and quick to deploy when conditions turn cold.

The Mountlite stands out as a highly refined piece of winter gear: exceptionally warm for its weight, versatile in the mountains, and small enough to keep in a pack on every outing. For cold-weather adventures where warmth, packability, and low weight matter most, it is an outstanding choice.

I'd rethink that conclusion. Assume markets are reasonably efficient. If your strategy is low-risk, you should also expect it to have low returns. If you expect it to have normal equity-level returns, it isn't low-risk.

A covered call strategy trades the equity risk premium for the volatility risk premium. Bitcoin reserves have found another potential use within the US financial system. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) has clarified that regulated derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses can count Bitcoin, Ethereum and qualifying payment stablecoins toward certain financial resource requirements. However, the regulator is not prepared to treat those holdings like cash.

The guidance, issued on November 23, could give regulated trading venues greater flexibility in managing their balance sheets. For Bitcoin investors, however, the more interesting question is whether that flexibility will translate into fresh institutional demand.

The answer is not quite as straightforward as it might seem.

The CFTC’s latest advisory addresses three types of regulated businesses: swap execution facilities, designated contract markets and derivatives clearing organizations.

These institutions are required to maintain sufficient financial resources to cover at least one year of operating costs. Under the new staff guidance, Bitcoin, Ether and qualifying payment stablecoins may contribute toward those requirements, subject to appropriate valuation discounts.

The announcement follows broader efforts to integrate digital assets into regulated financial infrastructure. NewsBTC previously reported on the CFTC’s guidance concerning tokenized collateral at clearinghouses, another area where traditional financial markets are gradually accommodating blockchain-based assets.

This time, the discussion is not about what traders can post as collateral. It is about the assets that the institutions running those markets can rely on to keep their businesses operating.

For a company that already holds Bitcoin, the clarification could make those holdings more useful from a regulatory standpoint. For businesses considering adding cryptocurrency to their balance sheets, it removes some uncertainty surrounding how those assets would be recognized.

But that recognition comes at a discount!

The CFTC advisory references a minimum 20% valuation haircut for Bitcoin and Ethereum, consistent with earlier staff treatment of proprietary cryptocurrency positions held by futures commission merchants.

Qualifying payment stablecoins receive considerably more favorable treatment, with a referenced haircut of 2%.

An exchange holding $10 million in Bitcoin, for example, would be able to count $8 million toward the relevant financial resource requirement if it applied a 20% haircut and no further deductions were necessary.

The remaining $2 million is not a realized loss. It is a regulatory adjustment intended to account for the risk associated with holding a volatile asset.

Crypto assets that are neither readily marketable nor qualifying payment stablecoins would generally face a 100% haircut, effectively excluding them from the calculation.

Perhaps the most revealing part of the advisory is the regulator’s warning about what it calls wrong-way risk.

A DCM should consider potential wrong-way risk

For a cryptocurrency-focused exchange, that risk is particularly relevant. A prolonged Bitcoin sell-off could put pressure on trading revenues while simultaneously reducing the value of the assets the exchange relies on to support its operations.

The problem is not simply that Bitcoin is volatile. It is that its value could deteriorate at precisely the moment a crypto business finds itself under financial pressure.

A 20% valuation discount provides some protection against that scenario, but it is hardly a guarantee.

Despite allowing digital assets to contribute toward broader operating resources, the CFTC has maintained a separate requirement for cash and highly liquid securities.

Bitcoin, Ethereum and even qualifying payment stablecoins cannot be used to satisfy that particular obligation under the advisory.

Swap execution facilities must maintain qualifying liquid resources sufficient to cover at least three months of projected operating expenses or the cost of winding down their operations, whichever is greater.

For designated contract markets and derivatives clearing organizations, the operating-cost liquidity requirement generally covers at least six months.

The distinction matters because an institution can have substantial assets on its balance sheet without necessarily having the cash available to meet immediate obligations.

It is also worth noting that the guidance does not address whether cryptocurrencies can satisfy the separate financial resources that clearinghouses must maintain to cover clearing-member defaults.

In effect, the CFTC is recognizing Bitcoin as an asset that can contribute to an institution’s financial strength without giving it the same regulatory standing as cash.

That is a more cautious approach than some Bitcoin advocates might have hoped for, but arguably a more sustainable one for integrating cryptocurrency into financial infrastructure.

The bullish interpretation is relatively simple. If regulated financial institutions can derive greater utility from holding Bitcoin, more of them may eventually consider allocating capital to the cryptocurrency.

The expansion of regulated crypto infrastructure has already brought digital assets closer to traditional financial markets. Earlier, NewsBTC reported on Coinbase’s CFTC registration for its USDC-focused derivatives clearinghouse, illustrating how cryptocurrency businesses are increasingly operating within established regulatory frameworks.

The latest advisory gives relevant institutions another reason to evaluate how digital assets fit into their balance sheets.

Still, there is a considerable difference between allowing an institution to hold Bitcoin for regulatory purposes and giving it an economic incentive to buy more.

An exchange that already owns Bitcoin could benefit from the clarification without purchasing a single additional coin. Another might conclude that holding conventional securities remains more efficient, particularly given the valuation discounts and separate liquidity requirements.

Nothing in the advisory requires institutions to acquire Bitcoin, and no purchase commitments were disclosed alongside the announcement.

Investors should therefore resist the temptation to translate this development directly into a bullish price forecast.

The longer-term significance may be greater than the immediate market impact. Every additional recognized use for Bitcoin within regulated financial markets strengthens the case for its utility beyond speculative trading.

However, the November 23 document remains a staff advisory rather than a binding rule adopted by the full Commission. It does not amend existing regulations or create enforceable rights.

The next meaningful development will be evidence that regulated institutions are actually changing their reserve allocations in response.

Until then, Bitcoin has gained another form of regulatory recognition. Whether it gains another source of sustained buying pressure is a different question entirely.
