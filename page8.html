‘Rivals’ Season 2's Return Teases Its Most Ambitious Shakeup Yet | Review

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

As the saying goes, good things come to those who wait. Well, as Rivals fans, we've done our fair share of waiting. After its first season premiered in October 2024, the sleeper hit series quickly became a favorite, both for longtime fans of Jilly Cooper and new viewers discovering her world for the first time. Now, after a five-month hiatus between Part 1 and Part 2, the raunchy, soapy series is finally paying off the promise it made back in May of this year. Despite some issues with pacing — which can be almost entirely blamed on the bizarre split-season release — Rivals Season 2 proves it can not only embrace the glitz and glamor of Cooper's world, but also forge its own path and create something new.

Following the shocking death of Monica Baddingham (Claire Rushbrook), all of Rutshire is in shambles. Tony (David Tennant) is spiraling deeper into the darkness; Rupert (Alex Hassell) has been thoroughly shaken after it's revealed that Monica spent her dying moments with him, and the O'Haras are on the brink of collapse with the secret of Maud (Victoria Smurfit) and Tony's affair hanging over them like the Sword of Damocles. Although Part 2 has its share of celebratory moments — there's a bonfire night, a debauched toga party, and a wedding celebration for the ages — the ghost of Monica and the fallout from Episode 6 haunt this half of Season 2.

For much of Episodes 7 to 10, the show loses some of that bubbliness that has become signature to the series. There's still big hair and tons of sex with some globe-trotting tossed in, but there's a sense that some light has left Rutshire. Your mileage may vary with how successful this tonal shift is, especially for those who enjoyed the show for its levity; Part 2 is full of fights, tears, threats, and broken hearts. If you're familiar with Cooper's books, this is probably less of a surprise, but even those who know the 1988 novel from back to front will be hit with shocking twists they never expected.

Rivalry, sex, and deceit are the best themes for an addictive TV show.

The person who shines the most in this darkness is Tennant, whose Tony Baddingham is absolutely riveting to watch and tops even his book counterpart. He's darker than he's ever been, but he's not twirling his mustache and cackling maniacally; he's a man who has been utterly torn apart and is at his most dangerous. Dogged and slightly deranged, Tennant gives a masterful performance, showing a man who has nothing left to lose.

Alongside Tennant is Hassell, who is the embodiment of a tortured soul. Part 2 humbles Rupert even more, and Hassell is brilliant at trading in the suave and charismatic bad boy for an emotionally mature reformed rake. As he becomes the platonic safe haven to the women of Rutshire while trying to remain honorable for Taggie (Bella Maclean), his narrative arc is one of the most satisfying in the show.

For those ready for your favorite couples to finally get together, don't expect it to happen immediately. For Freddie (Danny Dyer) and Lizzie (Katherine Parkinson), the two don't waste any time in starting their affair up again. However, the stakes are finally raised as their effervescent love affair is finally weighed down with real-world consequences. With two other spouses and four kids between them, Season 2 properly shows the collateral damage of their affair. Lisa McGrillis' Valerie, who is so often played as comedic relief, is given some meaty scenes, and you might find yourself torn about whether Freddie should abandon his family in pursuit of true love.

Meanwhile, Bas Baddingham (Luca Pasqualino) has firmly wedged himself between Rupert and Taggie. Maclean gives Taggie some teeth in Part 2, which is a delightful development considering how sweet and pure she has been played up until now. These two continue to wistfully and longingly stare at each other, but Taggie is no longer content to just exist on the sidelines. For those worried about the fate of these two, the show might take some liberties with the source material, but it ultimately remains loyal to Cooper's novel. Your patience might be tested, but this farcical will-they-won't-they (let's be honest, we know they will) pays off in the finale, which culminates in a bombshell moment that makes all that yearning worth it in the end.

The other main couples of the series also face a slower pace as the season steadily doles out its angstiest scenes. Aidan Turner and Smurfit deliver their best performances to date as Declan and Maud's marriage crumbles. There's one knock-down, drag-out fight between them that is award-winning for how flawlessly these two play off of each other. Smurfit, in particular, is magnetic as she embodies a guilt-ridden and remorseful Maud. Meanwhile, Gerald (Hubert Burton) is still determined to marry Muffy (Eliot Salt), but he and Charles (Gary Lamont) not only have the fear of being outed, but now must deal with the looming threat and stigma of AIDS. Lamont is fantastic in these episodes — if he broke your heart after the death of Charles' mother in Part 1, he'll completely wreck you in Part 2.

Alongside the fantastic performances, the production of Part 2 is in top form. The massive ball in Episode 10 puts Season 1's New Year's Eve party to shame. The night is complete with musical guests, people chasing a piglet through the halls, and a room called the "Sin Bin" that would look absolutely horrifying under a black light. Bonfire Night lights up the Cotswold sky and is perfectly fitting for the explosive revelations and developments that come with it. A gorgeous party in Argentina puts bright colors and a shining sun on display, making it a welcome change to the wintry English countryside. The way Rivals transforms sets and brings the cast together for these momentous scenes makes it feel like we're truly entrenched in the whimsical and wonderful world of Rutshire.

Beyond that, the show makes a lot of promises. The creators behind the series have already said that they intend to keep Rivals running for as long as it can go, and Season 2 ends where Cooper's second novel wraps up. With Season 3 starting production soon, it's clear that the world will continue expanding, and they'll venture into Polo, the third book in the series. And that's not even mentioning the fact that a character from the fourth book already appears in Episode 7. However, one problem arises. The Rutshire universe is notorious for its massive cast, and in a show already overflowing with storylines, it's hard to imagine room for more.

The show already feels like it is buckling under the weight of its characters, and each episode feels jam-packed. One major reveal that goes completely off-book in Episode 10 will likely blindside viewers, but it never feels properly foreshadowed. What Rivals must contend with in Season 3 is a challenge it has yet to face: how can the show evolve beyond a single book, and can it pare down its cast to make room for new faces? It would be a shame if so many of Cooper's iconic characters were simply blended into the existing ensemble, but at the same time, the cast's cohesiveness is something Rivals has earned through its two seasons. It's an unenviable task, but after Season 2's soaring heights, fans will be eager to return to Rutshire to see just how they manage to pull off the impossible.

We Tracked The World's Longest Airbus A321XLR Flight: Here's The Detour It Takes Every Day

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

On October 25, IndiGo started operating its flights between Mumbai Airport (BOM) and Amsterdam Schiphol Airport(AMS) with the Airbus A321XLR, replacing the leased Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners previously used on the route. At approximately 3,707 nautical miles (6,865 km), this is now the world's longest scheduled route operated by the A321XLR, with a scheduled flight time of around 10 hours from India to the Netherlands.

However, the actual distance flown is considerably longer due to airspace restrictions across the Middle East and South Asia. Using the Simple Flying Flight Tracker, we examined the routing of flight 22 from Amsterdam to Mumbai on November 1, as well as the flight times recorded during the aircraft's first 11 days on the route. Interestingly, the data shows that although the A321XLR is smaller and generally slower than the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner, its average flight time to Amsterdam has been shorter.

On November 1, IndiGo flight 22 departed Amsterdam at 2:31 PM local time, operated by an A321XLR registered VT-NLC. According to our flight tracking data, the aircraft spent approximately nine hours and nine minutes in the air before landing in Mumbai at 4:10 AM local time the following morning. Rather than following the shortest possible route toward India, the aircraft followed a more southern trajectory through Europe and the Middle East.

After departing Amsterdam, the aircraft headed southeast across Central Europe before continuing toward Turkey and Iraq. From there, the route passed through the Gulf region and Oman, avoiding Iranian airspace, before continuing toward India's western coast and eventually Mumbai. The tracking data shows how the aircraft avoids both Pakistan and Iran, despite these countries lying along the more direct route between Mumbai and Amsterdam.

The distance between Mumbai and Amsterdam is about 3,707 nautical miles (6,865 km) along the shortest route. However, according to Aerospace Global News, the necessary detours can increase the actual distance flown to approximately 4,200 nautical miles (7,778 km), or around 13% longer. This puts the flight much closer to the A321XLR's advertised maximum range of 4,700 nautical miles (8,704 km).

IndiGo's 8-hour Bali flights showcase the A321XLR's potential to transform aviation route planning as we know it.

One of the main reasons for IndiGo's detour is the ongoing closure of Pakistani airspace to Indian airlines, in place since April 2025 following rising tensions between the two countries. According to the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), the restrictions apply to aircraft registered in India, as well as those owned, operated, or leased by Indian carriers. Pakistan has since extended the restrictions several times.

At the same time, the ongoing conflict in the Middle East has also made flying through Iran increasingly difficult. Although Iranian airspace is not subject to the same ban for Indian airlines, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) currently advises operators to avoid Iranian airspace at all altitudes.

For IndiGo, Pakistan's restrictions and the security situation in the Middle East considerably reduce available routing options between India and Europe. The airline must therefore follow a longer route, which increases fuel requirements and operating costs. The implications are particularly significant for the A321XLR, as the aircraft has far less flexibility to carry additional fuel and passengers on such a long flight than a widebody such as the Boeing 787-9 previously used on the route.

We tracked a Widerøe Dash 8 through 17 sectors in one day. The shortest flight lasted seven minutes.

Although Airbus advertises the A321XLR with a range of up to 4,700 nautical miles (8,704 km), the actual range depends on several operational factors, including passenger weight, cargo, fuel reserves, and weather conditions. The additional distance flown between Mumbai and Amsterdam consequently puts the aircraft much closer to its operational limits. To ensure that the flight can be completed nonstop, IndiGo reportedly restricts the number of seats it makes available for sale.

According to avicionline, IndiGo is currently selling fewer than 140 seats on the Mumbai-Amsterdam leg, despite its A321XLRs having a total capacity of 195 passengers. The aircraft are configured with 12 IndiGoStretch business class seats and another 183 economy class seats, meaning that over 25% of capacity is reportedly unavailable for sale. However, this restriction is not necessarily identical in both directions, as the aircraft's fuel requirements may differ depending on weather and other operating conditions.

Compared with the 787-9 previously operated on the route, this represents a considerable reduction in available capacity. The Norse Atlantic Airways aircraft offered 338 seats, including 56 premium and 282 economy class seats, according to IndiGo's original route announcement. While the A321XLR already has 143 fewer installed seats, additional restrictions on the Mumbai-Amsterdam leg mean almost 200 fewer seats may be available than on the previous widebody operation.

Interestingly, analysis of Flight Tracker data indicates that in the first 11 days of A321XLR operations, the aircraft spent less time in the air on flights from Mumbai to Amsterdam than the Boeing 787-9. Between October 25 and November 4, flight 6E21 recorded an average airborne time of nine hours and 36 minutes, approximately 20 minutes shorter than the average of nine hours and 56 minutes recorded by the 787-9 during its final nine flights. However, the opposite was true in the other direction: the A321XLR averaged nine hours to fly from Amsterdam to Mumbai, compared with eight hours and 31 minutes for the 787-9.

Looking at the individual flights, the fastest A321XLR service from Mumbai to Amsterdam was recorded on October 28, when VT-NLD completed the journey in nine hours and 19 minutes. The longest was the first flight on October 25, when the same aircraft spent nine hours and 52 minutes in the air. For comparison, the longest 787-9 flight during the period examined took 10 hours and 15 minutes on October 20. On the return journey, the fastest A321XLR service took eight hours and 32 minutes, while the longest required nine hours and 14 minutes, compared with a range of eight hours and 19 minutes to eight hours and 43 minutes for the 787-9.

However, this does not necessarily mean the A321XLR is operating more efficiently or at higher speeds than the 787-9. The two aircraft were operating at different times of day, with the 787-9 previously scheduled to depart Mumbai at 12:25 PM and Amsterdam at 8:35 PM local time, compared with 6:25 AM and 2:15 PM for the A321XLR. The comparison also covers two different periods around the start of the winter schedule, meaning wind conditions, air traffic restrictions, and the actual routings flown may have differed. The 787-9 also generally cruises faster than the A321XLR, making it difficult to attribute differences in airborne time to the aircraft types themselves.

Ultra-long-haul flying keeps pushing further. Singapore Airlines’ nonstop service between Singapore and New York is one of the longest commercial flights in the world, and Qantas’ Project Sunrise aims to link Sydney with London and New York nonstop using specially configured Airbus A350-1000s. Other marathons include Qantas’ Perth to London route and Air New Zealand’s Auckland to New York flights. According to flight tracking data, IndiGo has already used four different Airbus A321XLRs on its Mumbai–Amsterdam service since October 25. In addition to VT-NLD, this includes VT-NLA, VT-NLB, and VT-NLC, with all four operating at least two flights from Mumbai to Amsterdam during the first 11 days of operations. VT-NLC has been the most frequently used aircraft, completing four services in each direction, while VT-NLD operated three flights to Amsterdam and VT-NLA and VT-NLB operated two each.

Although four aircraft appear in the flight-tracking data, the schedule allows IndiGo to operate both directions with the same aircraft within about 24 hours. On November 1, for example, VT-NLC landed in Amsterdam shortly before noon local time, before departing again at 2:31 PM. This left approximately two hours and 33 minutes between landing and takeoff, after which the aircraft arrived back in Mumbai at 4:10 AM local time the following morning.

The relatively short turnaround times allow IndiGo to operate the daily service without dedicating several aircraft exclusively to the route. Meanwhile, the airline has continued rotating its A321XLRs across other international services, including flights to Athens and Istanbul Airport (IST), where the aircraft type is also being deployed. The Mumbai-Amsterdam route nevertheless represents a considerably longer operation than those services, making it particularly dependent on the aircraft's available range and the routing restrictions currently affecting flights between India and Europe.

Sega’s Most Influential Platformer Deserves Its Big Remake Moment Next

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

With The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and even Star Fox recently proving there is still an appetite for remakes done right, other game publishers are likely looking at their backlogs for inspiration. Sega, no stranger to fantastic franchises, suddenly finds itself once more in an enviable position. It has a bunch of great old games ripe for revival, and several of them seem capable of making a mark again.

One franchise that deserves to make its triumphant return celebrates its 25th anniversary this month. After its arcade launch earlier that year, Super Monkey Ball arrived as a GameCube launch game in November 2001. With proper care, that simple but effective game could thrive in 2026, even though recent entries in the series haven’t made quite the same splash.

Super Monkey Ball’s main event lets players choose one out of four monkeys. The simian is then enclosed in a ball that resembles the capsules we used to collect from toy machines at the supermarket as children. From there, they roll toward goal posts on floating platforms, collecting bananas along the way to improve their score. If they fall off the platform or run out of time before reaching their destination, they lose a life.

In the traditional sense of the term, Super Monkey Ball isn’t a platformer. There’s no sign of Mario, for instance, and jumping isn’t a central mechanic. But there are platforms and pits, so the label fits in a more general sense.

When Super Monkey Ball debuted, it reminded me immediately of an old NES favorite of mine, Marble Madness. But that pioneering older game could be completed in five minutes. There were only a few stages, all of them far too short for my liking. I wanted more of that sort of experience, which Super Monkey Ball delivered. There are dozens of stages. Most of them are smaller than the typical Marble Madness level, but they’re similarly compelling, and their quantity makes up for their brevity.

At the start of Super Monkey Ball, rolling through the goal tape is easy enough. Even by the end of that first set of courses, however, the process grows tense. Then there are 30 more courses of more difficult vintage to tackle, and an even larger and more demanding batch after that.

As they advance, players control not the monkeys themselves but the platforms they occupy. Mastery requires the player to tilt each board, which in turn allows the balls to build up enough speed to launch from small bumps in order to clear gaps, or to negotiate rougher terrain, all without moving so quickly that it becomes impossible to proceed along razor-thin beams that span wide gaps.

A full-fledged remake of that original game could add visual polish. It might also double down on the personality of the starring characters, who make irresistible mascots. AiAi even drove a cart in 2012’s Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed, besides starring in various Super Monkey Ball sequels. The included party games and competitive play, which feature attractions such as launching balls toward distant targets, could provide satisfying online play with friends or strangers, especially if they were expanded with additional content.

Sega certainly hasn’t forgotten about Super Monkey Ball. In fact, Super Monkey Ball Triple Pack, a physical-only bundle for Nintendo Switch, will be released on Nov. 10. That compilation is welcome, gathering several recent installments in the series, like Banana Rumble. It also includes Banana Mania, which remastered the stages from the original GameCube releases. But none of those modern installments have managed to have quite the impact that the original one once did, and might again if it were to receive an ambitious remake like Nintendo produced with Ocarina of Time. The main question now is whether Sega has the desire to make it happen.

Those white cameras mounted above traffic lights serve a little-known purpose

Unlike Flock cameras, they're only meant to keep traffic flowing.

Automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) are nothing new, but reported abuses have made drivers understandably wary of the cameras made by Flock and other companies, to the point of wanting to find out where they are so they can be avoided. While that added attention has made intersection cameras seem more visible than ever, not every camera you see mounted above the road serves the same purpose. Most cameras on roadways aren't used to track individual drivers, but rather to monitor traffic conditions.

Aside from ALPRs, cameras meant to track people running red lights are also controversial. They're banned in many states, and they're rare even where they're allowed. Far more common are cameras used to monitor traffic conditions in real time or automatically control traffic lights.

Distinct from cameras used to catch drivers speeding or running red lights, traffic cameras aren't designed to capture evidence for law enforcement. Instead, they're meant to be watched live to monitor the current state of traffic and keep an eye out for accidents. This real-time data can be used by local transportation departments to help keep traffic moving, by overriding automatic light timings in some cases, or to alert first responders when needed. These systems can also be used to track traffic patterns over time, helping traffic engineers determine when changes need to be made to roadways or signals to control congestion.

In many cases, the cameras mounted on traffic lights are tied to automatic signaling systems rather than providing a live video feed. These cameras send data to control systems that determine when lights should cycle. Engineers can designate a section of the video frame that the camera should monitor for the presence of cars. When a car enters that space, it triggers the light to change based on a timing sequence that pre-programmed for each individual roadway. In addition to video cameras, these systems often use radar, infrared cameras and electromagnetic detectors built into roads to detect the presence of vehicles.

Exactly what the cameras on roadways are used for varies widely based on where they are. Local laws determine which types of cameras are allowed to be installed in the first place, and how they're used depends on the needs of a particular intersection. While there's plenty of justified concern over the prevalence of Flock cameras and other ALPRs, chances are when you see a camera mounted above a traffic light, it's just there to keep things moving.

Denis Villeneuve Teases James Bond Progress at LACMA Art + Film Gala: ‘I’m Having a Great Time’

Villeneuve and artist Vija Celmins were feted at the gala, which raised nearly $7 million, setting a new record

When Denis Villeneuve arrived at LACMA on Saturday night, his black Gucci tuxedo made him look more like James Bond than a director who’s spent the last decade in the desert of Arrakis. Perhaps that’s fitting since the filmmaker is currently putting the final touches on the 70mm Imax print of “Dune: Part Three” while simultaneously starting work on his Bond movie.

“It’s a very personal film and a project that is very close to my heart,” Villeneuve told Variety about wrapping up his “Dune” trilogy. “It’s also the project that is the completion of 10 years of work in the deep desert. I can just say that it has been a privilege of a lifetime to spend, and I enjoy every minute of it.”

On the topic of James Bond, news broke Friday that Villeneuve and producers Amy Pascal and David Heyman are inching closer to selecting the new 007, with finalists including Robert Pattinson, Nicholas Hoult, Callum Turner, Josh O’Connor and Jack Lowden.

On the red carpet, Villeneuve politely declined to comment about the casting speculation.

“I should not comment right now because every word I’m going to say is going to be scrutinized,” Villeneuve said. “I would just say that I’m having a great time.”

But inside the gala, he was ready to make some news. “For many years, LACMA has been a temple for me. Tonight feels like home, and to express my gratitude. With the blessing of my new friends at Amazon, I will reveal to you tonight who will be the next James Bond,” Villeneuve began, causing an audible stir in the crowd.

“The actor who will play the next James Bond is…” Villeneuve continued, pausing dramatically.

“And I will stop right now because Amy Pascal is about to have a heart attack,” he said, laughing. (Yes, the Canadian director was only joking.)

Read on for more from Villeneuve’s remarks and a peek inside the high-fashion gala.

Nicole Kidman admitted that she was sweating bullets as she prepared to present to Denis Villeneuve.

“I’m just going to disclose I have sweaty armpits,” Kidman, wearing Chanel, joked. “Let’s keep it real here.”

Then, Kidman revealed that she’d traveled 15 hours from Australia (overnight!) to make the event because she’s such an admirer of Villeneuve — even though they haven’t worked together yet.

“About six years ago, I made a commercial for a little company called AMC Theaters,” Kidman said. “So much of what inspired me to be a part of that campaign and to champion the importance of returning to cinemas was the opportunity to experience films from the likes of tonight’s honoree, Denis Villeneuve.”

Despite the fact that Kidman and Villeneuve had never really met before Saturday night, the Oscar winner said she feels like she’s gotten to know “the deepest parts” of Villeneuve through his films.

“That is what extraordinary filmmakers do. They let you in. They reveal themselves to us through their work,” Kidman said. “They invite you into their imaginations, into their dreams, into their questions, and into their fears.”

That we will never understand.”

Kidman also recalled her experience watching an 11 p.m. screening of “Dune” at her local cinema and plans to return to the theater for “Dune: Part Three” when it hits theaters on Dec. 18.

“I will always be one of your audience,” she said.

Onstage, Denis Villeneuve reflected on a fortuitous visit to LACMA while he was preparing to film 2016’s “Arrival.”

“I was looking for guidance. Then I walk into the James Turrell exhibition … I watched people standing in a room in front of a brilliantly colored light in silence and in awe. And then I thought, ‘OK, I know how to direct the film now.'”

Villeneuve shared that he discovered the power of images early in life. “My first contact with the English language as a boy in Quebec was through cinema,” he explained. “I didn’t understand the words, so I watched the images, and I imagined what the characters were saying.”

In addition to the support from his “Dune” star Timothée Chalamet and Kidman, Villeneuve was also saluted in a clip reel that included filmmakers Steven Spielberg, James Cameron, Chloé Zhao, Alfonso Cuarón, Alejandro González Iñárritu, composer Hans Zimmer and producer Mary Parent.

“When I was 12, all my friends were raving about hockey, and all I cared was about Steven Spielberg,” Villeneuve said. “He became my hero. Steven is the reason I’m standing here today, and I’m lucky enough now to call him a friend.

“Then came James Cameron,” Villeneuve continued. “These are titans. James has always been ahead of his time, and I hope he was wrong when he predicted Skynet, the self-aware artificial intelligence Terminator. These friendships meant the world to me because I’ve always felt isolated as a director in my own bubble.”

He saluted Zhao, Cuarón and Iñárritu as “three phenomenal artists pushing the boundaries of cinematic expression in the most original ways.”

Villeneuve also thanked his frequent collaborators and his crews. “It takes many people to bring cinema to life. I want to honor their contribution in making my dreams come to life on screen,” he said.

At the heart of each of Villeneuve’s films, there’s a question that he seeks to answer. “Dune Part Three,” Villeneuve (seen here with his wife and producing partner Tanya Lapointe) centers on Chalamet’s Paul and Zendaya’s Chani fighting to give their children a better world than the one they were given.

“We are at the crossroads, asking what defines a human being and what human expression is worth,” Villeneuve said. “Every parent on Earth is a little worried about the world our children will enter it.”

“For a filmmaker today, it is very real. I have a choice. I can create something with a prompt,” Villeneuve said, referencing the advent of AI tools. “Or I can go into the desert with actors, with a crew, and go on a real adventure where I learn about life and become better at my craft. Those are two different things.”

Even though one of those options is “more expensive than the other,” Villeneuve cracked, he firmly believes that the process of filmmaking is more important than the result.

“The process is how the human experience acquires its meaning,” Villeneuve said. “In this moment in time, it is more important than ever that human beings keep making art together. That is the heritage I want to leave to my children.”

Each SpaceXAI Bot works on a persistent computer in Cursor's cloud and has access to a browser, a file system and a terminal. You give it an outcome and access to the tools it needs, then the job can keep running even after your own computer is closed.

It still has its limits though. Morgan Wallen’s I’m the Problem leads Billboard‘s Top Country Albums chart (dated Nov. 14) for the 52nd nonconsecutive week. This ups Wallen’s career tally of weeks at No. 1 to 238, allowing him to extend his lead as the artist with the most weeks at No. 1 in the chart’s 62-year history.

Wallen’s achievement is especially impressive because he has amassed this hefty total of weeks at No. 1 in such a short amount of time. He first topped the chart a little more than six years ago — on Aug. 15, 2020.

Top Country Albums originated as Hot Country Albums in the Jan. 11, 1964 issue of Billboard. Johnny Cash’s Ring of Fire (The Best of Johnny Cash) headed the inaugural chart. The compilation was well-timed: “Ring of Fire” had headed Hot Country Songs for seven weeks the previous summer and had crossed over to reach the top 20 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Mammograms May Offer Early Insight Into Cardiovascular Disease

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Mammograms are widely used to help detect breast cancer, but they may also offer insight into heart health.

The routine X-ray screening may identify breast arterial calcification (BAC), which is the presence of calcium deposits within the walls of the small arteries in the breast.

On a mammogram, BAC is commonly identified as thin, white, parallel lines that give a “tram-track” appearance.

While BAC is harmless to the breasts, it is associated with a significantly higher risk of cardiovascular disease. A 2022 study found women with BAC were 51% more likely to develop heart disease or experience a stroke than those without it.

Laxmi Mehta, MD, a cardiologist at The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center, said BAC is associated with cardiovascular risk factors, such as diabetes, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol. Additionally, BAC has been connected to:

“The reasons for BAC resulting in higher risks for cardiovascular disease are not definitely determined; however, there are some potential mechanisms, one being that they share similar risk factors that result in vascular injury or metabolic stress that result in vascular calcification and atherosclerosis,” Mehta told Healthline.

Cheng-Han Chen, MD, interventional cardiologist and medical director of the Structural Heart Program at MemorialCare Saddleback Medical Center, added that because BAC and heart disease share many of the same risk factors, “calcium in breast arteries therefore suggests disease in the heart (coronary) and brain arteries as well,” he told Healthline.

While 1 in 39 females in the United States dies from breast cancer each year, cardiovascular disease causes one out of every three deaths, resulting in 1 death per minute.

“Cardiovascular disease is still the most common cause of death in premenopausal women,” Richard Reitherman, MD, PhD, a radiologist and medical director of breast imaging at MemorialCare Breast Center at Orange Coast Medical Center, told Healthline.

“The presentation of cardiovascular disease in premenopausal women is notoriously different than in postmenopausal women, primarily [because] there are no typical symptoms such as chest pain.”

Reitherman said that a prevalent and common health screening procedure such as a mammogram can lead to the early identification of women with silent cardiovascular disease.

“Cardiovascular outcome studies that have included breast arterial calcifications as a risk factor are increasingly reported in the medical literature,” he said.

“The hope is that identifying younger women with silent cardiovascular risks [like BAC along with] appropriate integrated risk identification, analysis, and interventions can significantly reduce mortality in this age group.”

Mehta said there are several reasons BAC has been overlooked as a risk factor for heart disease.

“Mammograms are completed for assessment of breast cancer, and BAC has been historically an ‘incidental’ finding, which is not clinically relevant from an oncologic standpoint,” she said.

Additionally, there is no standardization on quantification of BAC severity, nor is it a required component of mammogram reports, said Mehta.

However, routine reporting of BAC on mammograms is an urgent goal of the American College of Radiology, Society of Breast Imaging, and Medicare, said Reitherman.

In fact, it is gaining traction in the United States. A new Maryland law, passed on October 1, requires mammography centers to notify women when a BAC is detected on their mammogram.

“Automated identification and reporting of these calcifications detected on screening mammograms will become increasingly prevalent in the coming months [and] years,” said Reitherman.

The next step is to identify this group of women to primary care physicians so that appropriate risk evaluation can take place, Reitherman added.

In addition to doctors routinely screening for conditions related to heart disease, such as diabetes, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol, he hopes BAC will also be routinely considered.

“Under current development are protocols to identify patients needing a cardiologist’s training and expertise to appropriately stratify this group of patients,” said Reitherman.

“The common healthcare behavior [of] having regular mammograms not only reduces risk of morbidity and mortality from breast cancer but will hopefully contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality from cardiovascular disease in younger women.”

He said this type of integrated healthcare approach demonstrates cross-specialty integration and coordination among women, radiologists, and their healthcare providers to improve women’s lives.

“This is an amazing game-changing opportunity,” Reitherman said.

Mehta said it’s reasonable for women to ask if BAC is present on their mammogram report.

“But if it is not uniformly reported by the radiologist, the individual clinician may not have the answer,” she said.

More research is needed before BAC on mammograms can be used routinely to predict heart disease.

Finding out your BAC could provide additional information to help you and your doctors decide how aggressively to address your cardiovascular risk factors, Chen said.

Reitherman agreed. “The goal of reducing morbidity and mortality from any cause such as cardiovascular disease is based on recognition of risk factors, followed by appropriate lifestyle and pharmacologic interventions,” he said.

I Gave My Accounts-Payable Agent Hindsight Memory. It Stopped Asking.

Every month, an invoice from a steel distributor arrives priced 3% above the purchase order, and every month someone in accounts payable stops, opens the contract, finds clause 7.2 (monthly steel-index adjustment, capped at 5%), and approves it. The matching software flags it perfectly. It just has no idea that this exact question was answered last month, and the month before.

That is the gap I built Ledgerwise for: an agent that sits after the 3-way match and resolves invoice exceptions the way your desk resolved them last time — and knows when that history isn't enough.

An AP desk matches three documents: the purchase order (what we agreed to buy), the goods receipt (what actually arrived), and the invoice (what the vendor wants paid). When they disagree you get an exception: a price variance, a short shipment, a GST rate that doesn't match the contract, a surcharge line nobody ordered, a re-sent duplicate, or a vendor letter saying "we've changed our bank account."

Detecting those is a solved problem, and in Ledgerwise it's deliberately boring: matching.py is deterministic Python with no model and no memory. It finds exceptions and measures them — a percentage for price, rupees for a surcharge, units for a short shipment.

The memory layer is Hindsight, an open-source agent memory system from Vectorize. I picked it over rolling my own vector store because AP history isn't a pile of documents to search — it's a set of decisions with actors, amounts and dates, and I wanted something that extracts and organises those facts rather than just embedding paragraphs.

The obvious design is: recall similar past decisions, and if the top result says APPROVE, approve. Run that against the replay data and it looks great for a few weeks — until a logistics vendor sends a ₹6,800 fuel surcharge. Every earlier surcharge from that vendor was approved, but they were ₹1,200–₹2,400, and the agreed cap is ₹2,500. Similarity says "same vendor, same exception, approved." The money says otherwise.

That case reshaped the whole design. The question stopped being "what does memory say?" and became "is memory enough here?"

Each human decision is retained as one string with two parts: a prose sentence that Hindsight's retain pipeline extracts facts and entities from, and a structured line my policy can parse back out.

document_id keeps a re-decision on the same invoice from becoming a duplicate memory, and timestamp uses the invoice date rather than "now", so temporal questions work.

The magnitude matters as much as the decision. "Approved a 2.8% variance" and "approved a 4.2% variance" are different precedents.

1. The reason field is the product. I made the UI refuse to save a decision without a typed reason. Those sentences are what escalations show, what reflect summarises, and what makes the memory auditable. "Approved" is a log line; "approved under clause 7.2, index sheet attached" is knowledge.

2. Store the magnitude, not just the outcome. Similarity search will happily match "same vendor, same exception" and ignore that this one is three times bigger. Every precedent carries its number, and the policy compares numbers.

3. Decide what memory is allowed to override before you write any recall code. Bank changes, unknown POs and anything outside a learned range are human-only by design. Writing those guardrails first made the rest of the policy much easier to reason about.

4. Don't let the agent cite itself. Retaining the agent's own decisions is useful for audit and reflection. Counting them as precedent is how a system drifts.

5. Make memory configurable, not magical. A skeptical, literal bank disposition (skepticism 5, literalism 4) fit an AP desk better than the defaults. One approval of a 3% variance should not become permission for "price variances" in general.

The honest limitation: Ledgerwise is only as good as the reasons people type. A desk that clicks "approve" with "ok" gets an agent that can count but can't explain.

Drummer Chuck Comeau talks TikTok, Good Things Festival, and why a farewell tour is "not in the cards"

Simple Plan have been playing the same songs for 25 years. Lately, they’re playing them to a lot of people hearing them live for the first time.

“Every night we ask people, ‘Hey, how many people are here for the first time?’” drummer Chuck Comeau tells Billboard. “Sometimes it’s like 85% have never seen the band.”

The Montreal pop-punk band is drawing fans who weren’t born when “I’m Just a Kid” arrived in 2002, alongside the original listeners who are now bringing their own kids to shows. “And you’re like, ‘What are you talking about?’” Comeau says.

“There’s nothing cooler than what’s happening to our band right now,” he adds.

Much of that can be traced to “I’m Just a Kid” going viral on TikTok, where it soundtracked a challenge encouraging users to recreate their childhood photos. Comeau calls the trend a “gigantic gift,” but says meeting fans online has always come naturally to the band, which was filming backstage antics and sharing them through its website and DVDs long before TikTok existed.

“It’s the same thing,” he says. “It’s just a new platform.”

The renewed attention builds on a strong early run. The band’s 2004 album Still Not Getting Any… debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard 200, while “Perfect” and “Welcome to My Life” reached Nos. 24 and 40, respectively, on the Billboard Hot 100.

Next up is a return to Australia for Good Things Festival, where Simple Plan join a bill led by Limp Bizkit and featuring Spiritbox, the All-American Rejects, Bad Religion and MxPx. The festival hits Melbourne’s Flemington Racecourse on Dec. 4, Sydney Showground on Dec. 5 and Brisbane Showgrounds on Dec. 6, marking the band’s third trip Down Under in three years.

“We love Australia. What can I say?” Comeau says. “Any excuse, we come back.”

The lineup mixes pop-punk with nu-metal, hardcore and heavier rock, the kind of bill Comeau admits might once have made him nervous. “Being on this lineup would have been a bit more intimidating 25 years ago,” he says.

He credits streaming with breaking down the scene boundaries that once divided fans, making it easier for younger listeners to move between genres. The band has seen it firsthand at European festivals, sharing stages with acts like Slipknot.

“At first, you’re a little terrified,” he says. “You’re like, ‘How’s that gonna go?’ And then you actually realize that people are stoked.”

He has his own Good Things wish list. Bad Religion, whom he calls his favorite band, are near the top, along with Swedish punks Millencolin, who are playing Pennybridge Pioneers in full. He’s also keen to finally see Limp Bizkit live. “I’ve seen a lot of crazy videos right now that are popping up everywhere,” he says.

Meanwhile, Simple Plan have started writing their seventh studio album, with work set to continue into 2027. There’s no release date, but Comeau says the band has a batch of songs it’s excited about, and no intention of rushing.

“We’ve been around for 25 years and we only have six albums,” he says. “So we take our time.”

The record will follow 2022’s Harder Than It Looks, and Comeau says the aim is to refine the band’s sound rather than overhaul it. “We know who we are,” he says. “We’re not going to turn around and make a black metal record.”

The harder challenge is competing with the band’s own past. Songs that soundtracked fans’ teenage years carry a weight that new material has to earn.

“When you get to this stage of your career, you’re almost playing against yourself,” Comeau says. “You’re playing against your history.”

“You’re not the new shiny young band anymore,” he adds. “So you’ve got to work harder to have people pay attention.”

That’s why he won’t put a deadline on it. “There’s never a good time for a bad record, and there’s never a bad time for a good record,” he says.

Next year also marks 25 years since the band’s debut album, No Pads, No Helmets…Just Balls. Comeau says a special-edition vinyl and a few anniversary performances are on the table, but not a full album tour.

“I think we’re bigger than just one album,” he says. “But that album was really consequential for us. It’s the one that started it all.”

Another milestone arrives March 9, 2027, when the band releases its first children’s picture book, I’m Just a Kid: The Story of Simple Plan, illustrated by Jomike Tejido. Comeau says it grew out of watching the band’s original fans grow up and start families of their own.

“We were with them throughout their difficult moments,” he says, recalling how songs about breakups, school and friendships became part of listeners’ teenage years. “Fast-forward 20 years, 25 years, now they’re starting families, they’re having kids. We want to be there with them in this new chapter of their lives.”

The book explores friendship and perseverance and includes nods for longtime fans. “It’s as much for the parents as it is for the kids,” Comeau says.

For all the anniversaries and nostalgia, Simple Plan have never broken up, gone on hiatus or staged a reunion, which is rare among their peers. Comeau believes the years since the pandemic have been some of the band’s best, with bigger crowds and a sharper focus on the live show.

“When we were young, you were going out and you were partying,” he says. “Now it’s like, no, we don’t do that anymore. We’re all doing yoga and stretching.”

“We’ve always just kept going,” he says. “I feel like there’s a lot of work to be done.”

“There’s no talk about a farewell tour,” Comeau says. “Not in the cards for now.”

Before Captain America, Chris Evans Starred In An Underrated Non-Marvel Superhero Movie

Chris Evans is one of the most recognizable faces in superhero cinema, thanks largely to his portrayal of Steve Rogers in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. For nearly a decade, Evans helped bring the First Avenger to life, becoming a central figure in one of the biggest movie franchises of all time. However, his journey into the world of comic book-inspired adventures began long before he picked up Captain America's shield.

In 2005, Evans played Johnny Storm / The Human Torch, in Tim Story's Fantastic Four. He reprised the role in 2007's Fantastic Four: Rise of the Silver Surfer, bringing plenty of charisma and humor to the hot-headed member of Marvel's First Family. Nearly 20 years later, Evans stepped into the shoes of Johnny Storm again with a memorable cameo in Deadpool & Wolverine.

Between his appearances as the Human Torch and his eventual transformation into Captain America, however, Evans headlined another superhero adventure that deserves considerably more attention than it reserves. Released at a time when Hollywood was becoming increasingly fascinated with comic book stories about people with extraordinary abilities, this ambitious science-fiction thriller offered a decidedly different take on the genre.

It featured an intriguing mythology, an international setting, and a collection of characters whose powers made them anything but traditional superheroes. Unfortunately, the film never became the franchise starter its creators might have hoped for.

Push is a 2009 science-fiction action thriller directed by Paul McGuigan and written by David Bourla. Released in theaters on February 6, 2009, the film stars Evans alongside Dakota Fanning, Camilla Belle, and Djimon Hounsou. Rather than adapting an established comic book, Push creates an entirely original world populated by people with extraordinary psychic abilities. Its premise revolves around a secret government organization called Division, which captures and experiments on individuals with special powers in an effort to turn them into living weapons.

Evans plays Nick Gant, a young man with telekinetic abilities who has spent much of his life hiding from Division. His circumstances change when he encounters Cassie Holmes (Fanning), a teenage clairvoyant who can see glimpses of the future. Together, they search for Kira (Belle), a woman with the ability to manipulate people's thoughts and memories, while attempting to stay one step ahead of Division and its dangerous agents. As their mission develops, the characters must use their individual abilities to outsmart an organization that knows almost everything about their capabilities.

What makes Push particularly interesting is how it approaches the superhero genre. Instead of relying on familiar costumes, secret identities, and spectacular displays of power, the movie builds its mythology around different categories of psychic abilities. Telekinetics, clairvoyants, and mind controllers operate within a shared world, and their powers come with limitations that make confrontations more about strategy than brute strength.

The film’s Hong Kong setting also gives it a distinctive visual identity, combining neon-lit streets, cramped apartments, and elaborate action sequences. The result is closer to a supernatural espionage thriller than a conventional superhero blockbuster.

Despite its ambitious concept and talented cast, Push struggled to win over critics when it arrived in theaters. Its complicated mythology and purportedly convoluted storytelling became major points of criticism, with some reviewers arguing that the movie introduced too many ideas without giving them enough room to develop. Rotten Tomatoes currently lists the film with a 23% critics' score, reflecting the largely unfavorable response it received.

Nevertheless, dismissing Push entirely overlooks some of its most appealing qualities. Evans delivers a charismatic leading performance, while Fanning brings an intriguing combination of confidence and vulnerability to Cassie. The film's visual style, creative approach to psychic abilities, and willingness to experiment with familiar superhero conventions give it a personality that separates it from many other genre releases of its era. It also arrived during a particularly interesting period for superhero entertainment, when films like Iron Man and The Dark Knight were helping reshape audience expectations.

Push might not be a masterpiece, but it remains a fascinating chapter in Evans' expansive superhero career. Its original mythology and unconventional approach make it worth revisiting, particularly for viewers interested in seeing the actor outside his familiar Marvel roles. Before he became the face of Captain America, Chris Evans was already leading an ambitious superhero adventure that deserved a little more recognition.

Prime Video has been amassing a collection of adaptations over the years, but one of it's most popular series thrived in part because of how much it went off script. When adapting a narrative, it's important to either try to stick as closely to the source material as possible, or to veer away from the foundations in meaningful ways while also keeping the spirit and heart of the story and characters.

If an adaptation attempts to adapt a story faithfully but makes a handful of changes, it will often be torn apart by the fanbase as they pick apart how it lost sight of what made the series great in the first place. Alternatively, if the story feels so drastically different that it fails to even evoke a sense of what the original story inspired, it begs the question of why it was considered an adaptation in the first place.

So, when Prime Video's The Boys franchise took its lead from the comics as imagined by Garth Ennis and Darick Robertson, it was exciting and refreshing to see where the show changed course to make a story that would be engaging and surprising for longstanding fans of the franchise and newcomers to the series.

While The Boys comics serve as the foundation for the TV show and its spin-offs, Gen V and soon the prequel series Vought Rising, they are fundamentally different to the Prime Video series. Just on the surface level, the show makes a major narrative arc around how members of The Boys are people without powers, and they generally despise supes, but in the comics, all of the members of the team take Compound V to gain permanent powers and fight against the corrupt supes.

Additionally, there is a pivotal difference in the fact that the show positions the story as this group of underdogs fighting against a massive corporation, Vought International, as they expose corporate greed, manipulation, and coercion. In the comics, The Boys are more focused on challenging three core teams, The Seven, Payback, and the G-Men.

While discussing the G-Men, these characters also serve as the foundation and inspiration for Gen V, which expands the story to include Godolkin University and explores how young supes are trained in the corporate machine to become a part of the super cycle for Vought. In the comics, The Boys simply fight and kill the G-Men, which serves as a small side plot in the wider narrative.

Evidently, the show, and showrunner Eric Kripke, had a lot of love for the comics and the ideas they introduced in a landscape dominated by more traditional superhero stories, but they were able to make it their own and create something that ultimately feels much more prescient and relevant for modern audiences.

But, if The Boys had truly departed from the source material completely, it would lead to a subpar series that failed to capture what the original creators set out to create. Instead, The Boys took the core of the story, an examination of what flawed people would be like if given incredible and veritable powers, and the core cast, and made something that felt fresh while honoring the source material.

Many of the main characters are carried over from the comics to the screen, and in many ways, they thoroughly resemble their drawn counterparts. From the abrasive and gruff Billy Butcher, to the arrogant supe with a god complex, Homelander, these characters feel largely the same.

As a result, the series benefits massively as it draws in the established fanbase alongside fresh eyes, and delivers something that genuinely feels like it belongs in the world of the comics, but does not remain constrained by what came before. With that, The Boys and its wider franchise have become one of Prime Video's biggest success stories.

History Says Energy Led All Sectors in the Last Bear Market. Here's What's Different Now.

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Energy stocks have delivered exceptional gains in 2026, but a surprising shift in the oil market could change the outlook for investors.

Energy stocks were the clear winners during the 2022 bear market. The S&P 500 energy stocks delivered a total return of 65.7%, while the broader S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.31%) lost 18.1%, including dividends.

Energy stocks are leading again in 2026, but without a broader bear market. S&P 500 energy stocks have gained 42% so far this year, compared with a 12% gain for the broader S&P 500 index, excluding dividends.

In 2022, rising oil prices boosted energy companies' earnings, while inflation and aggressive interest rate hikes weighed on the broader market. The 2026 rally has been supported by oil supply disruptions in the Middle East, with unusually high refining margins. But a recovery in global oil supplies could put pressure on energy profits and stock valuations.

ExxonMobil (XOM +0.34%) reported earnings of $55.7 billion in 2022, compared with $23 billion in 2021. Chevron's (CVX -0.15%) earnings also more than doubled to $35.5 billion in 2022. Both companies returned billions to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.

However, April 2025 offered a different picture. President Donald Trump's tariff announcements triggered a sharp market sell-off. The S&P 500 fell 21.3% from its February 2025 peak during intraday trading, but did not officially enter a bear market based on closing prices. But S&P 500 energy stocks fell 13.7% in April 2025, making energy the worst-performing sector.

Rising concerns about slowing economic growth and weaker oil demand negatively impacted energy stocks in April 2025. This also demonstrated that energy stocks do not outperform during every market downturn.

The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) estimated that 4.8 million barrels per day of Middle East crude oil production remained shut down in September 2026, compared with a peak of 10.9 million barrels per day in May. But damaged pipelines and risks to oil tankers continued to affect shipments.

However, EIA expects global liquid-fuels production to fall short of consumption by around 1.3 million barrels per day in 2026. The production is then projected to exceed consumption by approximately 5 million barrels per day in 2027. This represents a potential swing of nearly 6.3 million barrels per day in the global liquid-fuel supply-demand balance.

This forecast assumes that supply routes will reopen and production will also recover. If that happens, rising inventories could reduce oil prices even without a recession in the U.S. The EIA forecasts annual average Brent to decline from $96 per barrel in 2026 to $84 per barrel in 2027. However, renewed geopolitical tensions or slower infrastructure repairs could delay the recovery.

Oil markets are already responding to expectations of easing supply disruptions. Brent crude prices dropped below $100 this week. Hopes of peace agreements involving Iran and Ukraine contributed to the decline, even though the underlying supply disruptions had not been fully resolved.

In the third quarter of 2026, ExxonMobil's Energy Products segment, which includes refining and fuels, reported earnings close to $7.2 billion. Chevron's downstream business earnings also more than quadrupled to $4.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

However, these strong downstream earnings may not persist. Disruptions to refineries in the Middle East and Russia have constrained gasoline and diesel supplies. This has pushed their selling prices higher relative to crude oil. However, as damaged refineries resume production and fuel supplies recover, the difference between refined product and crude oil prices could narrow.

Lower crude prices could also hurt earnings from oil production. ExxonMobil estimates that every $1 per barrel change in Brent crude prices would affect its annual upstream after-tax earnings by approximately $700 million, based on expected 2026 operations. Applying this sensitivity to the EIA's projected $12-per-barrel decline in annual average Brent crude oil prices suggests a potential $8.4 billion annual headwind for Exxon's upstream earnings. This estimate assumes that Exxon's production volumes, costs, and other operating conditions remain largely unchanged.

According to FactSet Research Systems' Oct. 30 report, S&P 500 energy stocks traded at 13.2 times expected earnings over the next 12 months, significantly lower than S&P 500's forward 12-month price-to-earnings ratio of 18.7x. However, the valuation of S&P 500 energy stocks was above its own five-year average forward 12-month PE of 12.6.

According to analyst estimates compiled by FactSet, S&P 500 energy sector earnings are expected to decline 7.8% in 2027, compared with projected earnings growth of 16.3% for the broader S&P 500. This suggests that energy's lower valuation relative to the broader market may reflect expectations of weaker future earnings, rather than necessarily making the sector a bargain.

I would avoid aggressively buying energy stocks after their substantial gains in 2026. A recovery in oil supplies could weaken earnings, even if the broader stock market remains strong.

TL;DR: Coinbase’s November 5 product update describes an integrated set of services for token issuers, from sales and distribution through listings, custody and liquidity. It cites Monad, Ethena and Hyperliquid as examples, but the examples are company-selected and do not eliminate the risks or regulatory limits facing token projects.

For a blockchain project, issuing a token may be the relatively easy part. Finding real users, managing custody, listing on exchanges and supporting institutional trading each introduce complicated operational decisions. In a November 5 announcement, Coinbase presented those tasks as parts of one integrated platform for token issuers.

The pitch is straightforward: use fewer separate service providers and reduce the number of operational handoffs. For developers and investors, the interesting question is how much of that convenience comes from genuine technical integration and how much reflects the commercial advantage of a large incumbent bundling its own services.

Coinbase pointed to a public MON token sale involving an affiliate of Monad Foundation, describing more than 85,000 purchasers in over 70 countries. It said the token now has more than 150,000 unique traders and $7.2 billion in spot volume on its platform. Those are company-reported measures from a chosen example, not a representative result for every issuer that uses similar services.

With Ethena, Coinbase described a relationship that expanded from token-management tools into institutional custody, wallets and trading. It also cited a USDC-related product and hundreds of millions of dollars in new deposits. For Hyperliquid, the company emphasised its role in deploying USDC treasury infrastructure and supporting large transfers.

The three examples involve different business models, market structures and needs. They demonstrate possible uses for the platform, but readers should not assume that success in one project translates directly into another token’s demand or risk profile.

Bundling services can remove duplicate work for a project team. One integration may simplify onboarding, compliance coordination and support. It can also make changing suppliers harder if critical functions become dependent on the same provider.

A token issuer should examine who legally controls assets at every stage, what happens if a service is paused, how withdrawal requests are handled and whether exchanges and custodians have the right safeguards for each market. Convenience is not a substitute for operational resilience.

That issue connects to NewsBTC’s earlier coverage of Coinbase’s restrictions on certain stablecoins for EEA customers. An exchange service can be available in one region but restricted in another without changing the underlying token network. Eligibility and local regulation remain part of any credible launch plan.

Coinbase also said it is broadening work around tokenised equities and access to private markets. Those plans should be distinguished from products already live and from securities that may require additional identity or jurisdictional checks.

The practical questions go beyond transaction volume: what are the total fees, the terms of custody, the geographic eligibility limits, support commitments and data-portability arrangements? What functions can an issuer replace if its needs change? Where do responsibilities sit when an incident affects users across several services?

NewsBTC recently examined the operational requirements of tokenised securities venues, where ownership records and corporate actions remain essential despite the appeal of blockchain settlement.

Coinbase’s new presentation shows how major crypto platforms are positioning themselves as end-to-end service providers rather than standalone exchanges. Whether that gives issuers better outcomes will depend on transparency, competition and the performance of the services after the marketing presentation ends.

“Patients should not have to wonder in December whether their January appointments will happen—or whether they will lose access to medications they rely on,” said Chris Adamec, Alliance for Connected Care executive director, in a statement. “With 59 days remaining and no published final rule, DEA can give providers and patients the stability they need by issuing a fifth extension as soon as possible.”

Advocates are calling for an extension through at least Dec. 31, 2027.

Hims & Hers inks deal with Eli Lilly to offer branded weight loss drugs" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Hims & Hers struck a deal with Eli Lilly to offer the drugmaker’s branded obesity treatments through its online health and wellness platform.

Under the agreement, Eli Lilly will give Hims & Hers pharmacies direct access to its U.S. Food and Drug Administration-approved GLP-1 medications at self-pay prices. Eligible patients on the Hims & Hers platform can be prescribed and order Eli Lilly's injectable Zepbound (tirzepatide) and daily oral medication Foundayo (orforglipron) without leaving the company's platform.

Prior to this agreement, Lilly drugs were available to patients on Hims & Hers only if they left the site and went to Lilly's direct-to-consumer platform, LillyDirect.

Shares of Hims & Hers jumped 8% Friday following news of the Eli Lilly partnership.

The agreement brings together Lilly’s obesity medicines and Hims & Hers’ consumer platform to expand consumers' access to FDA-approved weight loss drugs, the companies said.

"Patients can complete a consultation, receive a prescription if their provider determines it is appropriate and have an FDA-approved Lilly medicine shipped directly to their home, all in one seamless experience," the companies said in a press release.

Self-pay pricing starts at $149 per month for Foundayo and $299 per month for Zepbound.

"At Lilly, we are always looking for new ways to help more people get the medicines they need, and that means meeting patients where they already are," said Ilya Yuffa, executive vice president and president, Lilly USA and global customer capabilities, Eli Lilly and Company, in a statement. "Millions of people turn to Hims & Hers for their care, and through this agreement, those patients now have a simple, more direct way to get FDA-approved Lilly medicine at an affordable price if prescribed."

Hims & Hers' technology platform supports provider outreach, follow-up care and treatment planning. Weight loss patients on Hims & Hers receive a membership experience that includes 24/7 access to a care team, nutrition guidance, regular clinical check-ins and access to the Hims & Hers Weight Loss community, according to the company.

The agreement with Eli Lilly mirrors Hims & Hers' partnership with Novo Nordisk, which has been selling its branded weight loss drugs Wegovy and Ozempic through Hims & Hers since March. Novo had previously sued the telehealth company for offering compounded versions of its medications. Hims & Hers' agreement with Novo marked a shift in its obesity strategy toward FDA-approved branded GLP-1 treatments and away from cheaper compounded GLP-1 drugs.

Earlier this year, Hims & Hers took heat from the pharmaceutical manufacturer and federal regulators after announcing plans to launch a knockoff of Novo's oral pill. It backed down from those plans under pressure.

Hims & Hers stated that it never offered access to compounded copies of Lilly’s GLP-1 drugs and has committed not to do so in the future.

As part of the agreement, the companies have committed to protecting independent clinical decision-making, so that providers on the Hims & Hers platform continue to prescribe the treatment they determine is right for each patient.

“Lilly is one of the most innovative pharmaceutical companies in history, and I’m incredibly proud that Hims & Hers is a part of making sure more people can access the medicines they’ve created,” said Andrew Dudum, co-founder and CEO of Hims & Hers, in a statement. “By working together, we’re not just reaching more people with obesity with seamless, personal access to FDA-approved Lilly medicine. We’re showing the industry what innovators can achieve for customers everywhere when we work together to make the world well.”

Hims & Hers has faced financial pressure as it shifts from high-margin compounded GLP-1s to lower-margin branded weight-loss drugs. The company posted a $92 million loss in Q1, including $33 million in restructuring costs, largely from write-downs of its compounded GLP-1 supply chain, which executives said faced “risk of obsolescence.”

In Q2, Hims & Hers reported an $86 million loss, compared with a $43 million profit a year earlier. The quarter included $81 million in one-time costs, including expenses tied to its exit from compounded GLP-1s.
