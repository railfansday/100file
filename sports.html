49ers insider says Raiders owner is main holdup to Maxx Crosby blockbuster trade

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

With the NFL trade deadline just hours away, one San Francisco 49ers insider said he still believes the 49ers' top target is still Maxx Crosby - and revealed what the holdup for a deal likely is.

San Francisco Standard columnist Tim Kawakami has been adamant for months that the 49ers would be most interested in trading for the star defensive end from the Las Vegas Raiders. He first wrote about the possibility after the 49ers were knocked out of last season's playoffs and even made a bold prediction of an offseason trade for Crosby all the way back in February.

The Raiders were clearly willing to move Crosby and agreed to trade him to the Baltimore Ravens in exchange for two first-round picks. But the Ravens ultimately killed the deal when the Ravens said Crosby failed his physical. Other teams reportedly expressed interest in a trade after that move, with ESPN reporting the Rams led the pack, but Crosby didn't want a trade in the offseason. Things could be different now, though. The Raiders started the season 3-0 but have fallen to 4-5 since and, while clearly improved, don't look like a Super Bowl contender.

That's where the 49ers fit in. They were among the teams interested in a Crosby trade after the Baltimore deal fell apart, and Kawakami continues to say the interest from the 49ers remains very high in a deal. On the "49ers Plus Minus" podcast he co-hosts with the Athletic reporter Matt Barrows, Kawakami discussed the 49ers' trade plans at length.

"If [the 49ers] are going to trade a first- or second-round pick, it's going to be for a defensive end - I don't think it's going to be receiver, I definitely don't think it's going to be for a cornerback," Kawakami said. "... I think, if you're going to go get a starter who's going to impact games, you know, right away, it's going to be a defensive end."

Barrows pointed out the struggles the 49ers have had to rush the opposing quarterbacks - even star Nick Bosa doesn't have a sack in the three games he's been healthy for so far this year. He also noticed how many 49ers players and staffers showed Crosby love after the game and noted the connection between Crosby and 49ers defensive line coach Kris Kocurek.

But Kawakami, the insider who has been pushing the "Crosby to the 49ers" trade harder than anyone, is now saying he doesn't expect a trade to happen in time for this season's Super Bowl run. His change of tune even caught Barrows off-guard, leading Barrows to directly ask why Kawakami backed off on a deal.

"I don't think [Raiders owner] Mark Davis is trading Maxx Crosby to the 49ers," Kawakami said. "Most of what I've been saying is that's who the 49ers want. I am not coming off of that. My second [point] has been that I think Crosby would certainly be happy with a trade to the 49ers. I don't think there's anything denying that, and we saw it on the field."

Kawakami said those points and the fact that the Raiders already made one trade agreement to deal Crosby are why he has been so adamant about the possibility. He even said the 49ers would be willing to part with a top draft pick to land Crosby. But in his estimation, the Raiders owner (and perhaps the longstanding rivalry between the former Bay Area rivals) is the biggest reason why any deal isn't happening.

"This [49ers] locker room really has circled Maxx Crosby as someone they think would fit," Kawakami said. "The same way they did Christian McCaffrey, the same way they did getting [DeForest] Buckner back - obviously that's a no-brainer - but they do that sometimes. Like, 'This is a guy that would really fit,' and it's Maxx Crosby.

"That's why I'm always thinking about him, but that does not mean that Mark Davis has to agree with it. And I don't think Mark Davis is trading Crosby to the 49ers."

The NFL trade deadline is 1 p.m. Pacific on Tuesday.

Longtime St. Henry volleyball coach Maureen Kaiser is retiring from coaching after leading the Crusaders for 36 seasons, the school announced.

She is among the winningest coaches in state history, claiming 914 victories (889 at St. Henry). Under her watch, the Crusaders won eight All "A" state championships, most recently in 2024, claimed the 2021 state title and were state runners-up in 2005 and 2006. Her teams won 30 matches in a season eight times and claimed 25 district titles and nine Ninth Region titles.

"Coach Kaiser has built a program at St. Henry that represents faith, excellence, dedication and tradition. Her legacy goes far beyond wins and championships, as she has impacted generations of student-athletes and helped establish a standard of success that will continue for years to come," principal Grant Brannen said.

Kaiser is a member of the school's hall of fame, the Northern Kentucky Athletic Director's Hall of Fame and the Buddy LaRosa's High School Sports Hall of Fame. Hers is one of two names on the court in the Crusaders' gym alongside longtime boys basketball coach Dave Faust, who retired in 2025. She was a member of St. Henry's first state championship team in 1981.

"Coach Kaiser wasn't just one of the greatest coaches in Kentucky history — she was a builder of character, a mentor who shaped generations of student-athletes with integrity, passion, faith and heart. She built the St. Henry Volleyball program into a consistent state power year in and year out. We are so thankful for coach and what she has given to the SHDHS community. Her records and coaching accolades speak for themselves," athletic director Jim Demler said.

St. Henry is well-positioned to compete for the Ninth Region title in 2027. Only two seniors graduate in Kali McCane and Kayleigh McCormack. The returning cast is led by all-state honorees Violet Main and Kenadi Sieg.

Kaiser will continue to teach health at the school.

Winifred Notty is the sanest person she knows, or so the heroine (or anti-heroine, depending on how you might see it) of Zachary Wigon’s “Victorian Psycho” is quick to tell anyone who will listen. Or not, as star Maika Monroe delivers that damning and darkly funny insight into Winifred’s soul via a prim voiceover that seems to exist for her own benefit as much as the audience she seems to imagine.

That’s tangled enough, but there’s also this: Winifred doesn’t really know that many people, mostly because just about everyone she’s come across in her life is dead. Still, sane! Ish. When we first meet Winifred, the Victorian-era governess is bound for her next assignment to teach the children of Mr. and Mrs. Pounds (Jason Isaacs and a sneaky-great Ruth Wilson, respectively) at their sprawling Yorkshire estate. “This won’t be like the other times,” she tells herself, winking mightily at how all those other times turned out. Our Winifred sports dead eyes and a rictus grin she works so very hard to fix in place. It’s even better when she attempts to imitate those around her, all the better to approximate “being human.”

No question, there’s something very wrong with Winifred. But what? You’ll find out soon enough. Possessed of “a darkness” she cannot shake, Monroe’s Winifred practically vibrates with the need to hold things together. It does not always work for her, but it is nearly always funny, deliciously and dementedly so.

Wigon and Freito don’t balk at the “psycho” promise of their story, as Winifred happily whacks away (often literally) at everyone icky, from gardeners to nurses (Thomasin McKenzie) and, yes, at least one baby. Killing scenes are elegantly, amusingly cut by editors Dustin Chow and Lance Edmands and designed for maximum gasps. Blood flows, then spurts, then pools. Sometimes it gets on Winifred’s face or her collar. She moves on, grimly and happily.

Cinematographer Nico Aguilar brings a similar energy to his work, shooting in and through keyholes, swinging his camera like a clock pendulum, even briefly adapting the perspective of a swallowed (redacted, for spoilers) as it travels into Winifred’s belly. It’s playful and gross, wholly fitting the material. Mostly, it’s so dark and fucked up you can’t help but laugh. OH! Did they do that? Yes, yes they did.

Possessed by the notion that she’s meant to be a part of the Pounds family (who would want to be? these aristocrats are monstrous), Winifred continues to fray. Bloodlusty audiences will be charmed by her go-for-broke attitude (more like go-for-the-throat), plus a steady stream of visual cues as to her current state. Dreams and nightmares merge, she’s haunted by the Pounds’ family mascot of a wild boar (particularly in an ever-morphing tapestry), and she simply can’t stop sneaking into places to stare at people while they sleep.

Clocking in at a tight 90 minutes, some changes to the also-slim book are smart, while others dilute its darkest impulses. Gone is the countdown to a Christmas massacre, which frames the novel; hell, gone is the whole Christmas massacre. Freito’s script leads up to a different sort of celebration, doing away with many of the most colorful supporting characters in the process (Mrs. Fancey, we hardly knew ye!).

That choice isn’t disastrous, although the execution of the big! final! event! is oddly watered down. Here it’s imagined as a battle of Winifred’s wills… but, erm, what wills? That’s such a shame because for much of the film, she’s even better. By tempering her impulses and inner monologue, the film ends with less of the giddy gore that makes the rest of it such a dark treat. Dare we say it? We would have loved for this one to go full, ahem, boar.

Diamonds are forever - phoenix club seek FA Cup upset

The notion of your football club ceasing to exist is unthinkable for many fans, but for supporters of Rushden & Diamonds it became a sad reality in 2011.

The Northamptonshire-based side, backed by the late Max Griggs, rocketed through non-league in the 1990s and in 2003 reached the third tier of English football before financial issues after he stepped away ultimately saw them fold.

A community lost its focal point, and within years their Nene Park stadium had been razed to the ground.

"It was heartbreaking - we knew we'd been robbed." reflected Rob Usher, the chairman of phoenix side AFC Rushden & Diamonds, who will make their FA Cup first-round debut this weekend.

"I think it was that anger that probably fuelled the fans saying 'no, we don't accept it. We're not gone, we're not done and we want our club back."

Some 2,000 travelling fans will make their way to see the now eighth-tier Diamonds take on League One side Burton Albion on Saturday - a bittersweet reminder of days the supporters of the original club could have once taken for granted.

Usher, like many of those involved with the Northern Premier League Division One Midlands side, has followed both incarnations of the club - firstly as a fan, but also as a groundsman and turnstile operator before becoming chairman in 2022.

"My first game was an FA Cup tie against Shrewsbury Town in 1998. It was the year we ended up playing Leeds United in the third round." he said.

"Rushden & Diamonds gave us some great times. Winning leagues, the Duane Darby's, the Onandi Lowe's of our past. But I tell everyone I've never traded for what we have with AFC Rushden & Diamonds.

"This is a community, this is an army of people who turn up and give their time and their money, their care, their devotion... not because they want the club to exist, but because they want the club to thrive. That's why it's so special".

Diamonds chairman Rob Usher said the club was at the heart of its community [James Burridge/BBC]

Diamonds, who now play their home games at Hayden Road, started their journey to the first round proper with a 5-0 win at Kimberley Miners Welfare in the extra preliminary round on 8 August.

They have also beaten Long Eaton United, Desborough Town, Peterborough Sports, Braintree Town and Morecambe to reach this stage.

Along with Stafford Rangers, who host Grimsby Town on Sunday, they are the only side left in the competition to have played from the earliest round and have so far earned £23,194 from the FA's prize fund.

The winners of the tie at Burton's Pirelli Stadium will collect £47,750, with the losers taking £15,800.

Joining AFC Rushden & Diamonds' attempt to make more history will be Billy Sharp, the Championship's all-time record goalscorer, who has reversed his recent decision to retire.

The 40-year-old, best known for his spells with Sheffield United and Doncaster Rovers, scored his first nine professional goals while on loan from the Blades to the original Diamonds in 2005.

"They [Rushden & Diamonds] made me as a player," said Sharp, who will be targeting a landmark 300th goal while filling in for forward James Clifton who is serving a suspension for accumulated yellow cards.

The club reached the Conference, now known as the National League, in 1996 and realised Griggs' dream of EFL football in 2001 under the management of Brian Talbot.

In 2003, Diamonds were promoted as champions to Division Two, now known as League One, but Griggs' retirement as chairman signalled the beginning of their ultimate demise.

By 2006 they had been relegated back to non-league and mounting financial pressures saw them kicked out the Conference in 2011 before ultimately folding.

Rivals Kettering Town moved into the club's Nene Park stadium at Irthlingborough for a brief and ill-fated period between 2011 and 2012, before the ground was demolished in 2017.

‘I hate the man’: Recalling Alex Ferguson’s first fight at Man Utd with their City-supporting bus driver

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Sir Alex Ferguson took only two days to pick his first fight as Manchester United manager – with their over-familiar, City-supporting bus driver.

Ferguson was appointed 40 years ago when he moved to Old Trafford from Aberdeen to take over from the sacked Ron Atkinson and join “the biggest club in football”.

When he moved south on November 6, 1986, the Scot encountered a sleeping giant, positioned then in 20th place in Division One, and immediately identified a culture that needed to change if United were ever to reach the top again.

“Look, I’ll tell you right now, I won’t change,” Ferguson told the squad upon gathering them in the gymnasium at The Cliff training ground on his first day.

“I won’t change my beliefs in what a football club should be about… I’m not going to change. You’ll have to change.”

Ferguson’s first target was unexpectedly identified during his first match on his third of 9,631 days in charge at Old Trafford.

Derek Sutton had driven Ferguson and the United squad to Oxford United for the new manager’s first game on Saturday November 8.

Fergie was first off the Finglands coach and swallowed up by a media scrum on his way into the Manor Ground.

With his cargo unloaded, Sutton then did what he always did: parked his bus, then parked his butt on the United bench.

Having the bus driver in the dug-out was as standard as a Sunday piss-up for United then. Even if Sutton was a Manchester City fan.

Just a fortnight earlier, in Atkinson’s penultimate match as United boss, Sutton was in the City dug-out at Maine Road after driving United to the derby.

“The thing that always makes me laugh when I remember these games is that our coach driver Derek – or Del Boy as we used to call him – would drive us to Maine Road and then go and sit on the City bench,” Atkinson recalled for the BBC.

“He was a funny man and a great laugh. He normally sat in the dug-out with me and he used to make the teas for us at half-time but he was a City fan, and was their driver too.”

Ferguson did not know that ‘Del Boy’ was a City fan. He barely knew him at all. But Ferguson quickly decided his bus driver was overstepping when the new manager left his seat in the main stand with United 1-0 down towards the end of his first half in charge.

“I went down just before half time and in the dug-out, the bus driver was sitting there,” Ferguson later recalled to ITV.

“I didn’t say anything, I just thought ‘this is interesting’.

“So we went in at half-time and the bus driver is in the dressing room pouring the tea for the players.

“I said ‘excuse me, Derek. I don’t need you. Don’t come in the dressing room, don’t come in the dug-out again’.”

United physio Jim McGregor – far left on the bench in the photo above, with substitute Jesper Olsen, reserve-team manager Brian Whitehouse and kit-man Norman Davies between him and Ferguson, while Sutton sits next to the manager for the first and last time – and goalkeeper Chris Turner have backed up Fergie’s version of events.

But Sutton later disputed it. And, like most time-served City fans, he holds a sizeable grudge towards the United legend.

“Ferguson never told me to my face that he didn’t want me in the dressing room, he did that behind my back to my bosses at the coach firm,” Sutton said in 2004.

“I hate the man – he is a very hateful person.

“I was always in the dug-out until Ferguson came along. I always served the tea at half-time.

“Atkinson brought me in and he was always sound with me. I’d have passes to walk anywhere around Old Trafford and sat on the bench for the 1985 FA Cup final at Wembley when United beat Everton.

“When Ferguson came in, he wanted to stamp out all the old regime.”
