Pochettino has emerged as a surprise frontrunner for the job, with United said to be weighing up whether to activate the United States Men's National Team's $10 million release clause (via Ben Jacobs).

Should they do so, and should the Argentine accept the proposal on the table, it'll be his first managerial role at club level since he parted ways with Chelsea at the end of the 2023/24 season.

Inheriting a mess with the USMNT, Pochettino slowly rebuilt the feeling around the nation's set-up, before unleashing a highly competent and respectable outfit at the World Cup in the summer.

The scale of the task at United is a lot bigger, but the premise is the same. Rebuild and revitalise a broken and fractured team, culturally and tactically.

Excited United supporters are unsurprisingly focusing on the latter aspect of Pochettino's potential job, amid reports that he is now the leading candidate. Preferred starting elevens are already circulating in Red Devils circles, with fans theorising what their team might look like under the ex-Paris Saint-Germain coach.

United content creator, AliceTalksFooty, published a mock-up of what she believes Pochettino could opt for. Several big names miss out, including the ever-present Harry Maguire and new signing Youri Tielemans.

Senne Lammens is predicted to retain his position in between the sticks, despite his dubious recent form. Like the Belgian, Diogo Dalot's performances in recent weeks have been heavily scrutinised, but the Portuguese is still tipped to start at right-back, with Patrick Dorgu holding down the other flank as the other 'athletic' full-back.

Interestingly, Matthijs de Ligt is favoured over Maguire in this concept eleven. This is despite the Dutchman hardly featuring over the last 12 months — he hasn't played a minute of Premier League football since November 2025 due to a back injury.

Elsewhere, Tielemans' potential absence in the middle of the park will come as a surprise to many. United forked out £35 million for the 29-year-old in the summer, yet the blend of attributes in midfield with himself and Bruno Fernandes doesn't feel right.

Many feel that having both start leaves United vulnerable defensively, particularly in transition, and this has certainly been reflected in the Red Devils' dire defensive record thus far this season.

Instead, Kobbie Mainoo and Carlos Baleba are favoured, providing Fernandes with a platform to express himself further up the pitch.

Antonio Conte is believed to be a top target for Man Utd to replace Michael Carrick, and this is how he would likely line up his side if he took over.

While many believe Marcus Rashford could be a favourite of Pochettino's, Matheus Cunha is still tipped to hold down the left-wing spot. Benjamin Sesko, who is currently struggling with an injury, is backed to be the 2019 Champions League finalists' first-choice striker, ousting Bryan Mbeumo out to the right wing.

It remains to be seen whether this set-up resembles what a Pochettino United team might actually look like, although the core feeling that he might ruffle a few feathers in a divided dressing room with several big egos is almost certainly accurate.

Manchester United make Michael Carrick decision ahead of Liverpool clash

Manchester United have made a habit of turning turbulence into strategy, and that usually ends badly. According to The Athletic, Michael Carrick is now expected to lose his job before the club return from the international break, with United 18th in the Premier League and the numbers now impossible to dress up.

One league win, three victories in all competitions, a place in the relegation zone, and an Old Trafford crowd watching a chaotic 4-4 draw with Aston Villa. That is not a blip, it is a trend. Carrick had already been hanging by a thread after the 3-0 defeat at home to Bournemouth, and the decision to hold fire then looks more like hesitation than conviction.

What makes this especially awkward is how quickly the mood has flipped. Carrick earned the permanent role in May after a strong interim spell, taking United from seventh to third with 39 points from 17 league matches. That run included statement wins against Manchester City, Arsenal, Liverpool and Chelsea. Over that period, nobody in the division collected more points. Fair enough, he gave them a bounce. The problem is that football does not hand out credit indefinitely.

This season, the drop-off has been severe. United have looked open, anxious and oddly incoherent for a squad built at huge cost. Their Carabao Cup exit to Brighton & Hove Albion added to the sense that early-season damage was becoming structural. Once a club of that size slips into the bottom three in November, sentiment goes out the window.

The report suggests Mauricio Pochettino is expected to be a leading contender, while Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann also have admirers. That tells you United are already beyond internal review and into succession planning. Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family will still need to approve the final call, but the direction of travel looks obvious.

At a serious club, last season's rescue act buys respect, not immunity. Carrick may feel unfortunate, but United's table position leaves no room for nostalgia.

There is a certain grim fascination in watching Manchester United wrestle with the same problem again and again. They chase the uplift, enjoy the headlines, then discover that momentum and structure are two different things. Carrick clearly did a very good job in the short term last season, and those results were real, but short-term surges can fool decision-makers into believing deeper issues have gone away.

For Liverpool, the lesson has always been clear. Big clubs need clarity, ruthless standards and a football plan that survives a bad month. If United now move for another high-profile name without fixing recruitment, dressing-room hierarchy and the basic identity of the side, then the next coach will be walking into the same trap.

There is also the small matter of timing. Sack a manager in November when you are 18th, and every game starts to feel like a cup final. Rivals will smell panic. Supporters will demand certainty. Players will hear every rumour. That is how pressure multiplies.

And, truthfully, there is no sympathy for a club that keeps making expensive decisions twice. If this report is accurate, United are paying for a summer of misplaced confidence. The rest of the league will simply move on and punish any weakness they see.

Why Chelsea aren't European contenders, Tottenham aren't 'back'... and Brighton and Bournemouth are in a title race: What you should believe and what's just a mirage after 10 Premier League games - and the proof behind it

We are ten games into the Premier League season and another international break provides the ideal opportunity to take stock on what has been a bewildering start to another campaign.

Michael Carrick heads off for a couple of weeks with Manchester United still in the bottom three. Brighton perch second in the Premier League behind Arsenal, who are threatening to run away with it. Tottenham are only a point outside the top half, which is enough on its own to make you squint and rub your eyes. That can’t be right.

We are more than a quarter of the way through now, enough time for patterns to emerge and directions of travel to settle but also not long enough for us to be entirely sure who’s still benefitting from an early hot streak or suffering from unfair misfortune.

Who is where they belong? What can we believe? Daily Mail Sport digs into the numbers to figure out what’s real and what is fake about this Premier League season so far...

REAL: Defences CAN win you titles, just ask Arsenal

Sir Alex Ferguson was wrong, of course, when he declared that ‘attack wins you games, defence wins you titles’. Over the 31 completed 20-team Premier League seasons, fewer than half of them were won by the team that conceded the fewest goals. More than two thirds of them, however, were won by the side that scored the most.

Sometimes, as was often the case during the Pep Guardiola era, teams can be the best at both. Of the 15 times since 1995-96 that the best defence triumphed, they were also the most prolific attack on nine occasions.

Arsenal's goals, xG, shots and shots on target are all at their lowest per game since Mikel Arteta’s first season in charge. But thanks to their defence, they are five points clear at the top

Arsenal, however, are certainly not that. Their title win last season was only the sixth time in the previous 30 years that the best defence alone was crowned champions. They are doing it again this season, five points clear and proving that the best defence can still win you a title on its own.

And it’s a good job, too, because while their defence is as rock-solid as ever, their attack is getting worse. Last season, the Gunners ranked second for goals but fourth for expected goals (xG) and shots. This term, they are sixth for goals, fifth for xG and 10th for shots.

Their goals, xG, shots and shots on target are all at their lowest per game since Arteta’s first season in charge. Their win over Hull was their fourth 1-0 victory of the campaign. Not that Arteta will care. His defence means they still have the best xG difference in the division and it’s going to win him another league title.

That euphoric 3-1 victory over Manchester City was coming. There had been a fair amount of noise building around Oliver Glasner’s start to life on the Trent as Nottingham Forest went into the weekend second from bottom in the Premier League.

One defeat in nine. Was it Glasner’s new system taking time to bed in like it did at Crystal Palace? How quickly would ‘Mr Marinakis’ lose his patience this time around and fire another head coach?

This time last year, Forest were already on to their third manager of the season. Patience, for once, will reap its rewards. Forest didn’t deserve to be 19th. They have the fifth-best expected goal difference in the division. When you dig into the numbers, Forest have the best defence of any team not named Arsenal.

Fewest shots faced, second-fewest shots on target faced after the Gunners, second lowest xG conceded after, yep, Arsenal. They have made the fewest errors leading to shots all season with just two. Bournemouth are next on five.

Their problem is they, somehow, have not been able to score. Only Sunderland – another side doing worse than their expected numbers would suggest – have underperformed their xG in front of goal more than Forest. I was at Crystal Palace for Glasner’s return to Selhurst Park and, quite simply, Forest should have been 4-0 up by half-time.

They have the lowest shot conversion rate of any side in the league at just 5.5 per cent. That’s unsustainably low. Only one team since 2014-15 has maintained one as low as that across an entire season and that was Huddersfield in 2018-19, when they finished bottom with 16 points. Forest are nowhere near that bad.

Their need for a striker has long been obvious and if Liam Delap’s brace against City can spark some confidence and form, they might have the striker they need to start climbing up the table and, who knows, into European contention.

Nottingham Forest will hope Liam Delap's brilliant brace against Manchester City is the start of them getting past their woes in front of goal

Listen to Jamie O’Hara on talkSPORT after a run of three wins on the trot, and he might have you believing that Spurs are back.

Along with another defeat, this time to Leeds, to bring Spurs hurtling to earth, their numbers simply are not good enough – and certainly not in attack – to be deemed as such.

They may have attempted the fifth-most shots in the division but in doing so have still put up the fourth-worst xG, as their xG per shot is the worst in the division. In short: they shoot a lot but hardly ever from anywhere dangerous. That 40 per cent of their goals have come from outside the box, the highest in the league, is unsustainable.

They just don’t create enough. Left back Andy Robertson has created the most chances for Spurs this season with 14. Twenty-seven players have created more than him so far, with 15 other clubs boasting at least one star higher than Tottenham’s top man. Sunderland have two above him and Arsenal, Brighton and Manchester United have three.

Tottenham have the worst xG per shot in the league - meaning they rarely get into good positions to score

REAL: Brighton (and maybe Bournemouth) are in a title race

We have got used to seeing these modest but exceptionally run clubs punching above their weight in the Premier League but even the most appreciative of us could not have expected to see at least one of them in a title race.

If, by title race, we mean the team that might get closest to Arsenal. Looking at the underlying numbers, it could even be both of them.

When you look at the expected goal difference of all clubs in the division – those you should have scored from the quality of the chances you create, against those you should have conceded from the quality of the ones you allow your opponents to create – Bournemouth and Brighton sit behind only Arsenal and Man City.

Brighton are the division’s top scorers by a distance, five more than anyone else, but even though they are outperforming their expected numbers (their players can’t keep scoring one in every 10 shots they take from outside the box – looking at you, Diego Gomez) the Seagulls are still putting up the second biggest xG behind City.

Of the 11 players to create more than 20 chances so far, Brighton are the only side to have two names in there in Pascal Gross and Maxim De Cuyper. They also, of course, beat Arsenal and City without conceding to either team.

They do need to get tighter at the back if they wish to stay near the top, mind. Brighton should have conceded about seven more goals than they have done. No side has overperformed their defensive data quite so much.

Bournemouth are the ones who should be doing better, which sounds mad for a team that’s just won eight games on the bounce in less than a month in all competitions. The Cherries sit seventh but have the third-best xG, and the third-best xG conceded. That, no matter how you look at it, is a recipe for prolonged success.

Speaking of overperforming, few teams are doing quite so well to hide their abundant flaws as Chelsea. Xabi Alonso’s side sit fifth in the Premier League and the Spaniard can be thankful to the football gods for it. They should not be fifth.

Chelsea sit in the bottom half for both xG for and against. Their expected goal difference puts them in 14th, below Fulham, Manchester United and Sunderland.

Only Brighton have scored more goals than they should have done based on their chances but, unlike the Seagulls, Chelsea’s baseline is far more mediocre. Opta’s expected points table has them 12th.

Some 2,000 travelling fans will make their way to see the now eighth-tier Diamonds take on League One side Burton Albion on Saturday - a bittersweet reminder of days the supporters of the original club could have once taken for granted.

Usher, like many of those involved with the Northern Premier League Division One Midlands side, has followed both incarnations of the club - firstly as a fan, but also as a groundsman and turnstile operator before becoming chairman in 2022.

"My first game was an FA Cup tie against Shrewsbury Town in 1998. It was the year we ended up playing Leeds United in the third round." he said.

"Rushden & Diamonds gave us some great times. Winning leagues, the Duane Darby's, the Onandi Lowe's of our past. But I tell everyone I've never traded for what we have with AFC Rushden & Diamonds.

Sir Alex Ferguson took only two days to pick his first fight as Manchester United manager – with their over-familiar, City-supporting bus driver.

Ferguson was appointed 40 years ago when he moved to Old Trafford from Aberdeen to take over from the sacked Ron Atkinson and join “the biggest club in football”.

When he moved south on November 6, 1986, the Scot encountered a sleeping giant, positioned then in 20th place in Division One, and immediately identified a culture that needed to change if United were ever to reach the top again.

“Look, I’ll tell you right now, I won’t change,” Ferguson told the squad upon gathering them in the gymnasium at The Cliff training ground on his first day.

“I won’t change my beliefs in what a football club should be about… I’m not going to change. You’ll have to change.”

Ferguson’s first target was unexpectedly identified during his first match on his third of 9,631 days in charge at Old Trafford.

Derek Sutton had driven Ferguson and the United squad to Oxford United for the new manager’s first game on Saturday November 8.

Fergie was first off the Finglands coach and swallowed up by a media scrum on his way into the Manor Ground.

With his cargo unloaded, Sutton then did what he always did: parked his bus, then parked his butt on the United bench.

Having the bus driver in the dug-out was as standard as a Sunday piss-up for United then. Even if Sutton was a Manchester City fan.

Just a fortnight earlier, in Atkinson’s penultimate match as United boss, Sutton was in the City dug-out at Maine Road after driving United to the derby.

“The thing that always makes me laugh when I remember these games is that our coach driver Derek – or Del Boy as we used to call him – would drive us to Maine Road and then go and sit on the City bench,” Atkinson recalled for the BBC.

“He was a funny man and a great laugh. He normally sat in the dug-out with me and he used to make the teas for us at half-time but he was a City fan, and was their driver too.”

Ferguson did not know that ‘Del Boy’ was a City fan. He barely knew him at all. But Ferguson quickly decided his bus driver was overstepping when the new manager left his seat in the main stand with United 1-0 down towards the end of his first half in charge.

“I went down just before half time and in the dug-out, the bus driver was sitting there,” Ferguson later recalled to ITV.

“I didn’t say anything, I just thought ‘this is interesting’.

“So we went in at half-time and the bus driver is in the dressing room pouring the tea for the players.

“I said ‘excuse me, Derek. I don’t need you. Don’t come in the dressing room, don’t come in the dug-out again’.”

United physio Jim McGregor – far left on the bench in the photo above, with substitute Jesper Olsen, reserve-team manager Brian Whitehouse and kit-man Norman Davies between him and Ferguson, while Sutton sits next to the manager for the first and last time – and goalkeeper Chris Turner have backed up Fergie’s version of events.

But Sutton later disputed it. And, like most time-served City fans, he holds a sizeable grudge towards the United legend.

“Ferguson never told me to my face that he didn’t want me in the dressing room, he did that behind my back to my bosses at the coach firm,” Sutton said in 2004.

“I hate the man – he is a very hateful person.

“I was always in the dug-out until Ferguson came along. I always served the tea at half-time.

“Atkinson brought me in and he was always sound with me. I’d have passes to walk anywhere around Old Trafford and sat on the bench for the 1985 FA Cup final at Wembley when United beat Everton.

“When Ferguson came in, he wanted to stamp out all the old regime.”

Of ‘Black Sea Waves’ and ‘gummy bears’: Freiburg’s Julian Schuster explains Conference League triumph

SC Freiburg head coach Julian Schuster has offered an unusual explanation for his team’s impressive 1-0 Conference League victory over Trabzonspor on Thursday evening. The 41-year-old trainer compared the tactical discipline required to overcome the Turkish side to children being told not to touch a tempting supply of Haribo gummy bears.

Freiburg secured their second Conference League victory through a 14th-minute goal from Lukas Kübler, successfully protecting their narrow advantage despite increasing pressure from the hosts after halftime.

The victory represented an important response to Freiburg’s recent difficulties against stronger opposition. Defeats against Bayer Leverkusen and Bayern Munich in the Bundesliga, along with a disappointing 0-3 loss to AS Monaco in the Conference League, had raised questions about the Schwarzwaldverein’s ability to compete consistently against higher-calibre opponents.

Thursday’s performance provided an encouraging answer.

Trabzonspor entered the encounter with considerable momentum, having recorded seven consecutive victories under German head coach Thomas Reis. The Turkish outfit’s aggressive pressing and dangerous counterattacking play presented a significant challenge for Freiburg.

Schuster’s side nevertheless took the initiative early on, repeatedly forcing mistakes in Trabzonspor’s buildup through determined pressing of their own. As detailed in Get German Football News’ firsthand match report and player ratings, Freiburg created several opportunities during an impressive opening period before Kübler capitalized on André Onana’s spilled save to put the visitors ahead.

The Bundesliga representatives subsequently demonstrated an increasingly mature understanding of when to attack and when to exercise restraint. Speaking after the match, Schuster praised the precision with which his players executed their instructions.

Schuster was particularly impressed by the team’s ability to resist tempting attacking opportunities when a loss of possession could have left them vulnerable to Trabzonspor’s dangerous transitions.

“The lads really carried out all the assignments we gave them to perfection,” the Freiburg coach explained. “Whenever there’s something tempting, you want to reach for it, but you mustn’t give in. I’m thinking of an example involving children and Haribo gummy bears. They’re sitting alone in a room and are told not to touch them.”

The Freiburg trainer emphasized that resisting such opportunities was particularly demanding from a psychological perspective.

Schuster’s observations also reflected the broader development of a team that has experienced considerable success during his tenure without always managing to impose itself against elite opposition. The difficulties in Monaco provided a particularly useful comparison. Freiburg had struggled to establish themselves in that contest, eventually returning from the principality with a three-goal defeat.

The circumstances in Trabzon could hardly have been more different.

In addition to the hosts’ aggressive approach, Freiburg were confronted by a particularly hostile atmosphere. Rather than allowing the conditions to unsettle them, Freiburg used the atmosphere as additional motivation.

The visitors also demonstrated considerable defensive resolve after Trabzonspor increased the pressure during the second half. Jordy Makengo, Philipp Lienhart, Matthias Ginter, and Lukas Kübler worked effectively as a defensive unit, while goalkeeper Mio Backhaus produced a crucial intervention to deny Mohamed Salah an equalizer.

Schuster drew a colourful comparison between the Turkish coastline and the waves of pressure his side encountered after halftime. During a team meeting, the Freiburg coach had looked out at the Black Sea and noticed that the waves differed considerably from those he had observed along the Mediterranean coast.

“Perhaps that was symbolic of some of the waves that came at us in the second half,” Schuster explained. “The lads stood firm against them and let everything bounce off. That’s quite remarkable.”

The Freiburg coach ultimately described the performance as his team’s best of the season. The result leaves Freiburg with six points from their opening three Conference League fixtures and strengthens their prospects of advancing directly to the Round-of-16.

More importantly, it offered another indication of the progress Schuster continues to demand from his squad. As previously demonstrated during Freiburg’s ten-man Bundesliga victory over Schalke, the Sport-Club have increasingly shown an ability to adjust to difficult circumstances without abandoning their collective principles.

In Trabzon, that maturity meant recognizing that not every promising opportunity was worth pursuing.

Sometimes, as Schuster’s unusual comparison suggested, the smartest decision is simply to leave the gummy bears alone.

Why Unai Emery is right to stay at Aston Villa – and reject Man United

Emery is on the verge of signing a new contract with Aston Villa beyond 2029, which feels a pointed message ahead of Sunday’s trip to Old Trafford and Manchester United

It was the kind of thinking that used to govern most of football. A few years ago – although it could really have been many examples from the last 50 – a promising manager was being pursued by one of the game’s wealthiest clubs. He had built up a fine team with an upwardly mobile club, and it looked like they could be on the brink of something special… but that one thought wouldn’t go away.

“These opportunities don’t always come along, so you have to seize them.”

The manager ended up taking it, but not quite seizing it. He didn’t perform to the level required, albeit amid a lot of debate around the circumstances he walked into. His career has never reached the same heights, but the key point is that its trajectory was, in that moment of acceptance, drastically changed.

The old thinking was wrong. That job just wasn’t right for him.

He’s not the only one. There are a few examples, from Graham Potter to Julen Lopetegui, and there’s much more to it than just “backing yourself”.

Unai Emery is at a very different point of his own career, having experienced historic Europa League success  – with three different clubs – over 22 years, but it’s impossible not to wonder whether any of this weighed on his own thinking as he now discusses a new deal with Aston Villa.

The Basque will be all too aware of the noise around Manchester United’s interest, and the latest developments – including Villa’s very ‘LinkedIn’ post about "long-term strategic direction" – all felt very pointed ahead of Sunday’s trip to Old Trafford. Many in football even believe that one reason his club resorted to the absurdity of last week’s legal threat against Jamie Carragher was aggravation about their manager consistently being linked to “a bigger club”.

This, it should be acknowledged, is still Manchester United. It’s Sir Alex Ferguson, Sir Matt Busby and his babes, and so much glory and legacy. Despite all the current problems at Old Trafford, there is a history and consequently inspiration for the future; of what you might be able to do with that.

Others in football believe Emery might previously have been open to joining such a club, primarily because of the will to finally win a major league and the Champions League.

That isn’t to say either are indefinitely impossible at Villa given the club’s rapid recent growth, but their own fans have been frustrated over regulatory constraints.

And yet such strands lead into the nub of this, where this new thinking is warranted.

Emery’s record above Europa League level might raise considerable questions over whether he would suit a club of United’s scale, and those at Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain have long insisted his outlook didn’t fit.

Far more relevant, however, might be that United just don’t suit Emery.

There are numerous persuasive arguments as to why he should absolutely sign that contract and stay at Villa.

None of them are to deepen these debates about club size, or even Emery’s own career.

It’s more that the 55-year-old is in the very rare modern position of having earned an almost ideal job.

At Villa, everything is built around him, in a way you just don’t really see at this level any more. The only comparison in the Premier League or any of Europe’s wealthiest clubs is at Arsenal, where Mikel Arteta was only given such authority after initially proving himself. Emery has almost complete control, and those circumstances have ensured everything is in tune. The work of a very good manager is consequently greatly amplified, so that Villa consistently over-perform.

That can even be seen in how they’ve responded to the great frustration of the summer. They’re already in far better shape than would have been expected. Emery might have had his Europa League winners ripped up, but it is already impressive how he has built a coherent unit. Sure, there are still issues and gaps that are difficult to fill amid constraints, but there’s a bite there. This is personified in Nicolas Jackson.

And, from that, and a relatively volatile Premier League, there is a real opportunity for top five again. That can bring further growth… but maybe more? He’s already had more at Villa than most managers will ever enjoy, since he is an adored club legend.

By contrast, links to United have been met with scepticism by tranches of the Old Trafford support. They are also fully aware that the actual problems are way above the manager. United are unlikely to give Emery the keys to the club, in the way Villa have done. Sir Jim Ratcliffe has already been insistent on building a modern sporting director culture, even if this iteration has so far failed.

Worse, a U-turn on that, to make someone like Emery manager, would actually just look even more indicative of dysfunction; that they’re going from extreme to extreme again. And that’s even before you get to the amount of rot that still needs to be cleared at Old Trafford. United are not yet close to being a club who just need a coach coming in and imposing his influence to get them winning. They require a patient rebuild, but the wait can derail coaches.

Put bluntly, if this comes down to a chance of winning the league or Champions League, there’s an argument that Emery specifically has a better opportunity at Villa, certainly in the medium-term. There would be so much more work to do at Old Trafford, with a lot more risk.

Maybe most pertinently, he would be giving up a very good thing, a very rare thing, that has the potential for so much more. And it’s that context that is also so key to such debates. It’s not just that the thinking has started to change. So has the reality. With more and more clubs looking to head coaches in wider infrastructures, the jobs are much more transitory. Such opportunities actually come along more frequently, but they’re far more contoured than before.

Better-run clubs than United are now geared in such a way that they only suit certain profiles, or certain ideologies. Tactical shifts can take time, and be painful, as currently being seen at Newcastle United. On Premier League pitches, the game is so systemised and optimised that the margins have become very thin. Hence wild swings in results when clubs aren’t in tune.

At Villa, it’s almost note-perfect for Emery. That isn’t something to be given up easily.
