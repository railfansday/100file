Alejandro Garnacho's time at Aston Villa has been brief - and it already looks to be over.

The latest snub by Villa boss Unai Emery was the most damming of all, with the on-loan Chelsea winger left out of Tuesday's Champions League defeat at Galatasaray.

"The players who were here I think are the best players to be here with us," said Emery.

Villa could have had 12 substitutes in Istanbul but Emery chose just 11 - including two goalkeepers - as Garnacho stayed at home.

But now there is doubt he will ever play for them again.

The post Garnacho wrote on social media after a pre-season game in July has - along with anything else related to Villa - been deleted from his account.

He has played just 34 minutes in the Premier League this season, and seven of Villa's 11 games ended without him getting on the pitch.

His last appearance - just a three-minute cameo - came in the 1-1 draw against Fulham on 31 October.

Including Saturday's 0-0 draw with Sunderland, Garnacho has been omitted from the past three matchday squads - a strong sign Emery has lost faith in him.

But he may have been fighting a losing battle from the off, having arrived at Villa unfit.

Sources have acknowledged the 22-year-old was not at the level required and were surprised how off the pace he was when he signed in July.

He initially trained on his own, and questions over the signing were already being asked, with eyebrows raised by some internally given his inconsistent form for both Chelsea and Manchester United.

Yet he had been on Villa's list of targets - albeit a long list - while he was at Stamford Bridge as Emery was keen to sign a pacy winger in the summer.

Garnacho's problems were compounded by a head injury suffered in Thailand in pre-season, and even a boot camp during the international break in September and October, and scoring in friendlies against Sevilla and Southampton have not helped.

One of the issues Villa have is there is no exit clause, and both teams would have to agree to end the season-long loan deal early.

This time last year, Emery had already decided not to sign Harvey Elliott permanently in a Â£30m deal, leaving the on-loan Liverpool midfielder in limbo for the rest of the season.

And it appears Emery has already come to the same conclusion with Garnacho.

With Villa unable to find a solution with Liverpool, Elliott wasted a precious year of his career. Those close to him recognise the damage last season did, playing just nine times after being named Player of the Tournament at the European Under-21 Championship in 2025.

He is now at Valencia and is yet to start in La Liga this season.

The risk of going on loan to Villa became a topic of conversation in the game because of Elliott's plight, with sources suggesting players would think twice about joining the club. Garnacho's situation only increases those concerns.

Three times in two seasons, Emery and Villa have signed a high-profile player on loan only for it to fail.

Jadon Sancho's expensive loan from Manchester United last season - Villa paying a guaranteed 80% of Sancho's wages of more than Â£200,000 a week - ended with him having made just nine Premier League starts and scoring once in 39 appearances - 21 of those as a substitute.

Sancho may have played more than Elliott and Garnacho - and came off the bench in their Europa League win - but what value did Villa really get?

Of course, Marcus Rashford and Marco Asensio enjoyed positive loan spells previously - Rashford returning to the England squad while at Villa - but they are exceptions to the rule.

Nicolo Zaniolo, Clement Lenglet and Axel Disasi have joined in recent years and barely merit footnotes, while Douglas Luiz's loan return last season did not prompt a return to the heights he had reached previously at Villa.

The club have constantly complained about financial restrictions with some justification, but questions can certainly be asked about the money spent on many of their signings.

The blunt truth is there is no clear path.

Teenager George Hemmings has just signed a new deal and has earned praise from Emery for understanding the way he wants his players to work.

Ibrahim Mbaye and Alysson are also ahead of Garnacho, leaving him well behind even before regulars John McGinn, Johan Manzambi and Emi Buendia are factored in.

There was interest from abroad in the summer, with Roma keen, and it seems increasingly unlikely we will see Garnacho at another Premier League club soon.

Fifa rules allow players to play for two clubs in a season - or three if they move to a team whose season overlaps like the MLS - meaning Garancho could feature for another, unlike Elliott as he had already played for Liverpool and Villa.

Now nothing, it seems, will persuade Emery that Garnacho is worth persevering with - leaving the winger's career continuing to drift.

"I watched the Brighton game yesterday, and I still can't quite work out [how they didn’t score]," he said.

Regardless, Lineker said he ‘definitely can see’ Brighton in the Champions League next season.

"I know it's ridiculous – and we're talking about jumping on the bandwagon and being reactionary – but I think for a real push, they had to win that game," he said.

Shearer, 56, also felt the result should not overshadow the quality of Brighton's display.

Brighton were not the only team to receive praise, with Richards also highlighting Bournemouth's recent formidable form.

The podcast trio agreed that the quality across the Premier League is improving, with Richards arguing that teams outside the traditional 'big six' are becoming increasingly difficult opponents.

For Brighton, however, Lineker's message was clear. He believes Hürzeler has a side capable of playing some of the best football in the country, but adding a striker with the instincts and finishing ability of Vardy could make them a much more dangerous proposition.

“RIZE Up Day” aims to empower Western PA middle schoolers

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Approximately 200 middle school students from the Aliquippa and Shaler Area school districts gathered at the RIZE Sports Complex in Leetsdale on Tuesday for "RIZE Up Day."

The four-hour program engaged sixth-, seventh- and eighth-grade students in sports training, health workshops and career exploration.

Organized by the Boys & Girls Clubs of Western Pennsylvania and hosted by RIZE Sports, the event took place at 1201 Brickworks Dr. Program organizers structured the day to help young people build confidence, maintain active lifestyles and learn about career opportunities in the sports and wellness industries.

Chuck Cooper III addressed the students as the keynote speaker following opening remarks. Cooper is the president and chief executive officer of the Chuck Cooper Foundation. He is the son of Charles "Chuck" Cooper, the Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame member who became the first African American drafted into the NBA.

Participants took part in physical training spread across three activity rotations. Community coaches from the Cleveland Cavaliers directed basketball skills sessions, while players from the University of Pittsburgh women's lacrosse team led agility and conditioning drills.

Beyond athletics, the morning incorporated wellness and emergency response education. Representatives from the Children's Institute of Pittsburgh guided students through mindfulness and yoga rotations. In a separate hands-on session, instructors from the American Heart Association provided students with foundational CPR training to teach basic life-saving skills.

During lunch sessions, students attended a panel discussion focused on professional careers in sports. Jim Britt, executive director of SportsPITTSBURGH, served as moderator. The panel featured Rachel Brecht, chief people officer of the Pittsburgh Penguins; Ethan Lounsbury, senior director of marketing for the Pittsburgh Riverhounds; and Benjamin Moore, senior sports marketing manager at F.N.B.

Germany's Freigang to miss women's World Cup due do doping ban

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Germany national team player Laura Freigang will miss next year's women's World Cup in Brazil after she's been suspended for missing doping tests, the German Football Federation (DFB) said in a statement on Thursday.

Freigang will be suspended until October 7, 2027 after she reached a settlement with the DFB, the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) and Germany's National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA).

Proceedings against Freigang had been ongoing for months. The 28-year-old was accused of not being at the location she specified for a doping control on three occasions between March 2025 and February 2026.

In June, the Eintracht Frankfurt captain explained that the missed tests "were not deliberately avoided appointments but inconsistencies or misunderstandings in the comprehensive details that have to be updated daily and which we as national team players have to update in the system on a daily and hourly basis."

Freigang has 45 caps for Germany and has scored 17 goals, but hasn't been a regular starter recently.

Chris Falkenstein (left) and Don Reid (right) were long-time climbing partners and guidebook co-authors. (Photo: Courtesy Falkenstein Family Collection)

Chris Falkenstein passed away peacefully on August 9, 2026 after a brave and exhaustive battle with cancer. Better known by many as Falky, Finkelstein, Steiny, Schwackenstein, or Commando, Chris wore many hats throughout his life: devoted husband and father, loyal friend, accomplished climber, photographer, skier, whitewater kayaker, windsurfer, mountain biker, self-proclaimed computer nerd, and passionate lover of Yosemite and the outdoors.

I grew up climbing with my Dad, but it was always his sport. I enjoyed it, but pursued other sports. In a way, I don’t think I’ve found a climbing partner that was ever as dialed as he was. My Dad was so quiet and humble about his achievements; there were so many things he never mentioned. I remember being out climbing with him, when he would tell some story like, “We were out here in the ‘80s, blasting a boom box and climbing.” It turns out, he would be talking about a first ascent.

Those who knew him will remember his adventurous spirit, his enthusiasm for life, and the joy he found in exploring the world around him. His love for the outdoors was infectious. And the memories he created along the way will live on in those fortunate enough to have shared in his adventures.

When Chris arrived in Yosemite in 1972, he brought a foundation of climbing experience from the Gunks of New York. His natural athleticism, determination, and enthusiasm quickly drew him into the dynamic and evolving climbing scene of Yosemite Valley. What began as an introduction to the Yosemite climbing community grew into a lifelong passion and a lasting legacy.

He once remarked, “So off I went at 18 to explore climbing in Yosemite. Best decision of my life, and a true highlight… I consider my life beginning after 18. Stay young at heart forever if you can.”

Chris spent the remainder of his life in and around Yosemite, calling many places home over the years, including Camp 4, the Annex, Badger Pass, Sonora, Tuolumne Meadows, and El Portal. Eventually, he settled in Foresta, where he and his wife, Doe, retired to the home they built together.

Throughout his years in Yosemite, Chris became a respected presence in the climbing community, known for being a quiet and humble force. He established multiple first ascents in Yosemite Valley and Tuolumne Meadows. Among his notable first ascents were Do or Fly (5.11c), The Speed of Life (5.11b), Black Angel (5.11a), and Highway Star (5.10a). Along with Don Reid, he co-authored Rock Climbs of Tuolumne Meadows, first published in 1983. Four additional editions later followed. He also authored Yosemite Sport Climbs in 1992. Through his climbs, guidebooks, and contributions to the community, Chris helped shape and document Yosemite’s rich climbing history.

Beyond his physical accomplishments in climbing, he inspired others and expanded the appreciation for the outdoors through his photography, videography, and online content. He captured the many aspects of climbing, mountaineering, and skiing, while sharing the extraordinary beauty of Yosemite.

Chris’s work appeared in a wide range of publications, climbing guides, gear catalogs, climbing films, commercials, Hollywood productions, and websites. Chris’s photography became a familiar part of the Yosemite landscape itself. As Don Reid once remarked with a teasing smirk, “You know you are made with Yosemite photography when your photos appear on the Coke machines, shuttle buses, and climbing guide covers of the place.”

When it was too cold to climb, Chris also had a deep love for winter sports. He played hockey throughout his life and contributed to the local pick up hockey club in Yosemite. Alongside Bruce Brossman and Loyd Price, Chris completed the first single-day trans-Sierra ski trip via Tioga Pass. His name also lives on through several first descents in the Sierras, including the Solstice Couloir and Powerhouse Chute.

At Badger Pass Ski Resort, Chris spent many years serving as ski patrol manager. He brought his knowledge of climbing techniques into ski patrol training, developing methods for chairlift rescues and assisting with backcountry rescue efforts. His impact was perhaps best captured by a skier he helped, who later described him as “an angel emerging from the woods.”

Early in his career, Chris worked for the Yosemite Climbing School, teaching climbing during the summer months and cross-country skiing in winter. He enjoyed summers as the employee housing manager in Tuolumne Meadows. In retirement, Chris continued to explore and enjoy the Sierras, while taking great pride in perfecting the art of being a grandfather. Chris also became an unofficial voice of Yosemite locals, keeping friends and former park residents connected to the place they loved by sharing updates and insights into the ever-changing landscape of Yosemite.

Chris loved everything Yosemite. He devoted much of his time to documenting not only the climbing, skiing, and athletic pursuits that made the park famous, but also its immense beauty, extensive history, and the development and change over time. With an eye for the extraordinary as well as the everyday, he captured the moments, places, and details that might otherwise go unnoticed. Through his lens and storytelling, Chris shared his deep appreciation for Yosemite. In so doing, he preserved a piece of its story for others.

Chris is survived by his wife Doe, his son Alex, his daughter-in-law Danielle, and his two-and-a-half-year-old grandson Finn, who loves trying to climb up anything in his path.

The 10 Most Overrated Rock Songs of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

There will never be a better genre of music than rock and roll. Rock is the basics, with a band of guitar players and a drummer singing songs they wrote themselves, free of anything synthetic. Rock is real, a genre free of restraints. The listener feels it all, from the energy of the beat, to the emotion behind the lyrics. Today, even as newer rock songs lose their hold on mainstream popularity, classics from the past continue to reign. Still, some rock classics get more credit than they deserve. While none of them are bad by any means, being overplayed, or simply being unworthy of their all-time greatness status, has made these 10 rock songs overrated.

Putting The Beatles on such a list is just wishing to be hated. Let's get one thing clear right up front. The Beatles are the best band of all-time. But this status has also made it so that every song they ever released is put on a pedestal, and not all of them deserve it. Among them is "Come Together." The first song from their iconic Abbey Road album, "Come Together" is written and sung by John Lennon. While it has a cool opening riff and hook, the song repeats itself with lyrics which make absolutely no sense. Gone is any emotion, replaced instead with lines like "He wear no shoeshine, he got toe-jam football." What?

This song refuses to die. Being overplayed over the decades has caused listeners to cringe any time Journey's "Don't Stop Believin'" comes on. The piano intro is pretty standard, and there's nothing all that impressive with basic lyrics like, "Just a small-town girl livin' in a lonely world." The beat feels like a throwaway song without much effort put into it, and the theme is so spot-on that it's more cheesy than anything else. Throw in how it now will forever make us immediately think about the unsatisfactory ending to The Sopranos, and this song is one that can please stop.

Cheap Trick's "I Want You to Want Me" has two versions. The studio version is completely lifeless and forgettable. The entire band sounds bored, and Robin Zander is twisting his voice in ways that don't work. The song only became a classic because of the live version. There, Cheap Trick comes to life, a band set free. Still, there's only so much you can do with such a dull original song. Zander's voice is much better here, and we won't deny how catchy it is, but a live version of a song no one wanted isn't worth the hype.

The Police are one of the greatest bands of the 1980s, and it can't be understated what Sting means to rock history. The band's final album, Synchronicity, is one of the best of the decade, and it's carried by the second single, "Every Breath You Take." The song lacks any real energy that The Police had on other big hits. What really makes it annoying is how the song was treated by the public. Despite being a song about a man's obsession with the woman who got away, so many listeners refuse to look at the deeper meaning, instead treating it like a love song worth getting married to. Come on, people!

Folk rocker Bob Dylan was the voice of America and the forgotten man and woman in the 1960s. What he means to music deserves to be celebrated. This doesn't mean that every song of his was some genius recording worthy of his reputation. "Lay, Lady, Lay" is an unusual one. Gone are the powerful political protest songs Dylan was known for. In its place is lyrical simplicity about either true love or an affair. And missing from it all is Dylan's well-known nasaly whine.

Many lists will recognize "Hotel California" by the Eagles as one of the greatest rock songs of all-time. And that's why it makes this list. This is an American classic, but it's also hyped beyond belief and has been overplayed to death. At six plus minutes, it's much too long. The lyrics, about the excess of fame and living it up in southern California, are a little too vague. As for the music itself, outside of the usual guitar solos, "Hotel California" seems stuck in first gear. It doesn't deserve the hate it gets, but this is not one of the best songs ever made.

Thanks to albums like The Joshua Tree and Achtung Baby, the Irish band U2 were one of the biggest and best bands of the late 1980s and early 1990s. Then they got experimental, and in the late 90s, as rock changed, U2 was left behind. The 2000 album All That You Can't Leave Behind was seen as a comeback return to form. It's carried by the first single, "Beautiful Day," a song that was everywhere to begin the 21st century. Although it's a nice song, it can't live up to their classics. Rather than being a challenging song, "Beautiful Day" is overly cute, the lyrics a far cry from what the band once was.

Green Day are one of the better modern bands still out there and going strong, but oh man, if you grew up in the late 90s like this writer, did it get old hearing "Good Riddance (Time of Your Life)." The punk pop power the group is known for is stripped away in favor of an overly sappy acoustic number. At just two-and-a-half minutes, the song doesn't overstay its welcome, but the millions who heard it weren't paying attention. Billie Joe Armstrong is saying goodbye to an ex, trying to be heartfelt while also saying good riddance. Yet, for years after, it was part of every damn high school and college graduation in the country.

"In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida" by Iron Butterfly is one of the most well-known rock one hit wonders. It's also a song that doesn't hit as hard the more you listen to it. Just the title itself, a mispronunciation of "In the garden of Eden" is annoying. That opening hook, with an organ leading into a sick guitar riff and drum beat, is admittedly badass, and lead singer Doug Ingle sounds a bit like Jim Morrison. But once you get past the first minute, this one starts to fall apart. It's the same riff and basic structure repeated over and over. If you have the patience, check out the whopping 17-minute album version.

In the late 1990s, as rock was trying to figure out what came next in the post-grunge era, along came Semisonic and their song "Closing Time." It's a catchy song from a decent band, but it's basic verse-chorus-verse style isn't anything original. If it stood on its own as a minor hit, this one might not be on the list. It's how it was received that makes it so overrated. Not only was it played on every Top 40 and rock radio channel non-stop, but good luck being at a bar at closing time in 1998 and the years after. Drunken sing-a-longs turned a fun little song into something rather annoying.

Few video game franchises wear their cinematic influences on their sleeve as proudly as the Grand Theft Auto franchise. As the most recent entry in the series, Grand Theft Auto 6, continues to break records, already pulling in billions of dollars in sales and receiving near-universal praise, many have begun to notice that the game's story shares plenty of similar tropes and narrative threads with some of Hollywood's biggest and best films. From the world of organized crime depicted in Goodfellas, the elaborate heists of Heat, or Scarface's violent confrontations, the games of Grand Theft Auto have always been influenced by movies, leading to some of the franchise's best moments. Scarface may be most closely associated with the franchise, inspiring much of Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, right up to the movie's climactic showdown in a drug dealer's mansion. But with Grand Theft Auto 6 returning to a new interpretation of Rockstar Games' fictional Miami, it's another crime thriller that feels particularly relevant.

Directed by Tony Scott and written by Quentin Tarantino, 1993's True Romance is a wildly entertaining combination of crime drama, action thriller, and unconventional love story. Starring Christian Slater and Patricia Arquette, the film follows the two young lovers as they find themselves caught up in the world of organized crime, after their relationship sets them on a collision course with gangsters and drug dealers. Given that Grand Theft Auto 6's story is also centered on a couple attempting to navigate the criminal underworld while also becoming criminals themselves, it makes True Romance a perfect companion piece for any fans of the game.

Clarence Worley (Slater), a comic book store employee in Detroit, and Alabama Whitman (Arquette), a call girl hired by his boss, quickly fall in love. Both of their lives forever change when Clarence stands up to Alabama's former pimp, a confrontation in which he unexpectedly acquires a suitcase filled with drugs. As they head to Los Angeles to start a new life after selling the drugs, embarking on a chaotic adventure involving mobsters, Hollywood filmmakers, and police, the film follows the sort of storyline that would feel right at home in the world of Grand Theft Auto.

Christian Slater and Patricia Arquette starred in the cult classic as Clarence and Alabama, respectively.

Similar to True Romance, Grand Theft Auto 6 has a similar plot as it follows Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal couple caught up in a world that is as ruthless as it is rewarding. It's easy to see how True Romance makes for such an appropriate comparison to the game, as Jason and Lucia share many of the same motivations as Clarence and Alabama: They want a better life together, and their willingness to break the law in the pursuit of that dream makes them all particularly compelling, albeit reckless, protagonists.

One of the defining characteristics of the Grand Theft Auto series is its ability to balance dramatic storytelling with humor and outrageous, over-the-top violence. Rockstar's fictional worlds are populated by eccentric personalities, dangerous foes, and people whose egos ultimately lead to their demise. "Pride comes before the fall" is a recurring theme of the Grand Theft Auto franchise, and it's a trope that few writers understand better than Tarantino. So it's only natural that a movie written by him would share such similarities with the GTA series.

While Tarantino didn't direct True Romance, his fingerprints are all over the movie, which is full of unorthodox dialogue, colorful characters, and sudden violence that make it remarkably compatible with Rockstar's style of storytelling. His writing finds a perfect balance with the direction of Tony Scott, as it gives the film the momentum of a blockbuster action thriller similar to the director's classic Top Gun. The movie has the ability to transition from heartfelt dialogue into dark comedy and explosive violence without losing its identity as an atypical love story. It's a tonal balancing act that requires all the strengths of the director and the writer, a similar problem that Grand Theft Auto deals with masterfully.

More than three decades after its release, True Romance remains one of the best crime thrillers of the 1990s. Its combination of memorable characters, dark humor, action, and romance makes it one of the best entries in Tarantino's screenwriting filmography. For fans of Grand Theft Auto 6 fans, it's also a reminder that the most compelling crime stories aren't always about money or power. Sometimes they're about finding the right person who you're willing to risk everything for.

3 Bengals legends still alive in Pro Football Hall of Fame race for Class of 2027

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

It's that time of year again, Cincinnati Bengals fans. The turkey's thawing, the Bengals are in the thick of the stretch run, and we're once again refreshing our feeds to see if Canton is finally ready to give Cincinnati its due.

On Tuesday, the Pro Football Hall of Fame trimmed its Class of 2027 player pool down to 52 names, and three familiar stripes are still on the board: quarterback Ken Anderson, offensive tackle Willie Anderson, and defensive tackle Geno Atkins.

If you're wondering why Ken Anderson's name is showing up on the same list as Ben Roethlisberger and Rob Gronkowski, it's because this is a whole new ballgame. Back in September, the Hall announced the biggest overhaul of its selection process in its history.

The separate seniors category is gone, so every eligible player, no matter when he retired, is now in one pool. The selection committee was cut roughly in half, to 25 voters plus three non-voting participants. And the final ballot now has room for only 13 players, joining one coach and one contributor. Those two spots already belong to Bill Belichick and Robert Kraft, by the way.

The 52 will be cut to 25 in about two weeks. The final 13 will then go into the room ahead of Super Bowl LXI, with the class revealed at NFL Honors in February.

For the Bengals, the stakes are pretty simple. This franchise still has just two career players enshrined in Canton: Anthony Muñoz and Ken Riley. That's the fewest in the league. Let's look at the three men trying to change that.

The résumé hasn't changed, and it never needed to. Anderson spent all 16 of his NFL seasons in Cincinnati. He won four NFL passing titles, was the 1975 NFL Man of the Year, and took home league MVP honors in 1981 while leading the Bengals to their first Super Bowl. He's still the franchise's all-time leader in passing yards, and he remains the only eligible quarterback with four passing titles who isn't in the Hall.

The path to Canton has been a long, winding, and frankly maddening one. Anderson was a finalist back in 1996 and 1998, then fell into the seniors pool, where he reached the semifinals four straight years. Last cycle, he finally broke through as one of three senior finalists for the Class of 2026, only to come up short in a year when voters were also weighing Belichick and Kraft.

Now, with the seniors category scrapped, Anderson doesn't have to wait for a small committee to pick him. But he also has to beat out every modern-era candidate on the list for one of those 13 spots. There's real momentum behind him, though. Even Joe Montana, the guy who beat him in Super Bowl XVI, has publicly said Anderson's call is long overdue.

"Big Willie" was the anchor of the Bengals' offensive line for 12 seasons (1996-2007) before a final year in Baltimore. He made 184 career starts and earned three first-team All-Pro nods, and he did it at right tackle, at a time when that position rarely got that kind of recognition. He was one of the most dominant run-blockers of his era, and he did it for a lot of bad Bengals teams.

The frustrating part is that Willie has been about as close as you can get without walking through the door. He's been a finalist five years in a row and made the final seven for the Class of 2026. Under the old bylaws, that would have made him an automatic finalist for 2027. The new rules wiped that safety net away, so he has to earn his way back to the final 13 from scratch, alongside a loaded crop of first-time eligibles.

If there's any silver lining, it's that voters clearly already know his case. Getting back into the room is the hurdle. Getting the votes once he's there feels like it's been a matter of when, not if.

This is the one that should get Bengals fans excited, because it's new.

Atkins was a fourth-round pick (No. 120 overall) out of Georgia in the 2010 NFL Draft, and he turned into one of the best interior pass rushers of his generation. Over 11 seasons, all in Cincinnati, he piled up 75.5 sacks from the defensive tackle spot. He made eight Pro Bowls, earned two first-team All-Pro selections, and was named to the Hall of Fame's All-Decade Team of the 2010s. That last one matters, because All-Decade nods tend to carry real weight with voters.

By reducing the personal information exposed through data brokers and people-search sites, Cloaked will help protect the Cowboys against social engineering and other targeted attacks.

Fans will see Cloaked throughout AT&T Stadium starting with the Cowboys' Thanksgiving Day home game on Nov. 26. The partnership will also engage the Cowboys' community promoting data privacy, safety, and protection.

"The Dallas Cowboys trusting Cloaked to protect the people behind America's Team means a great deal to us," said Arjun Bhatnagar, co-founder and CEO of Cloaked. "The visibility that makes the Cowboys one of the most recognized organizations in sports also puts the personal information of the people behind the team at risk. But, you don't need to be famous to have your home address, phone number and family details for sale online. If privacy protection matters for America's Team, it matters for everyone. This partnership lets us show millions of Cowboys fans how simple it can be to take back control of their personal information."

Cloaked will also appear across The Star, the Cowboys' headquarters in Frisco, Texas, and on Cowboys preseason television broadcasts and the Dallas Cowboys Radio Network. Fans will also find Cloaked across the Cowboys website, mobile app and social channels, including a recurring game-day series showing how Cloaked helps protect America's Team.

"Visibility comes with being the Dallas Cowboys, and so does the responsibility to protect the people who make this organization what it is," said Matt Messick, Chief Information Officer, Dallas Cowboys. "Personal information available online is exactly what bad actors use to target an organization like ours. Cloaked Enterprise protects people across the Cowboys by removing that information from data brokers and people-search sites, making them harder to find, harder to impersonate and harder to target."

Learn more about Cloaked by visiting www.cloaked.com.

"Cloaked is a consumer-first privacy and security company that helps people take control of their personal information. Cloaked has removed more than 3.5 billion records from data brokers and people-search sites and screened more than 100 million spam and scam calls. Its platform also creates private phone numbers and email addresses to use in place of real ones, and Cloaked Enterprise extends that protection to organizations and their people. Founded by brothers Arjun and Abhijay Bhatnagar, Cloaked has raised more than $400 million from investors including General Catalyst, Liberty City Ventures and Lux Capital. Learn more at www.cloaked.com."
