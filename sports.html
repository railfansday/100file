Freiburg’s Derry Scherhant celebrates dream Berlin homecoming

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

SC Freiburg attacker Derry Scherhant enjoyed an unforgettable evening in his hometown of Berlin on Monday, scoring Germany’s opening goal in a commanding 5-0 Nations League victory over the Netherlands. The 24-year-old subsequently reflected on his unlikely journey to the national team and took particular satisfaction in proving those who had once questioned his ability to become a professional footballer wrong.

Scherhant had arranged approximately 50 tickets for family members and friends ahead of the encounter at the Olympiastadion. The former Hertha BSC youngster, who grew up just five minutes from the venue, marked the occasion with an outstanding individual performance and a spectacular first-half goal.

Scherhant opened the scoring in the 41st minute after receiving a pass from Joshua Kimmich, cutting inside and dispatching a powerful effort into the upper-right corner. The Freiburg professional had already struck the outside of the post earlier in the half and subsequently played a decisive role in Germany’s fifth goal.

The performance represented another significant milestone in a remarkable international breakthrough that has gathered momentum since Jürgen Klopp took charge of the German national team.

As Get German Football News detailed in its comprehensive October feature on Scherhant’s unusual career trajectory, the winger’s development was anything but straightforward. Scherhant struggled to establish himself at Viktoria Berlin and Tennis Borussia before eventually finding his way into Hertha BSC’s academy in 2020. His early difficulties included repeated questions about his physical development and whether he possessed the necessary qualities to succeed at the professional level.

He ultimately earned a Bundesliga debut with Hertha, although regular opportunities remained elusive during the club’s turbulent relegation campaign in 2022/23. The subsequent spell in the 2. Bundesliga proved important to his development, and SC Freiburg eventually secured his services in the summer of 2025. The move to Breisgau provided Scherhant with a more stable environment, even if initially displacing established attacker Vincenzo Grifo presented another considerable challenge.

GGFN‘s 2026/27 tactical preview of SC Freiburg identified Scherhant as one of the club’s most impressive performers during preseason preparations. The former Hertha attacker subsequently consolidated his place in Julian Schuster’s starting XI.

A particularly impressive Bundesliga performance against Eintracht Frankfurt followed his first senior Germany nomination in September. Scherhant contributed a goal and an assist in the 2-2 draw, providing further evidence of his rapid development.

The remarkable progression continued at international level. Scherhant earned his senior Germany debut during Klopp’s first international window and impressed sufficiently to retain his place when the Bundestrainer reduced his squad ahead of November’s fixtures. Get German Football News subsequently reported on Scherhant and Freiburg teammate Philipp Treu’s appreciation of the supportive environment at their Bundesliga club. Both players credited Freiburg’s collective mentality and stable working conditions with helping them establish themselves at international level.

Scherhant also spoke enthusiastically about the prospect of representing Germany in Berlin. The winger celebrated his 24th birthday during the November international break and regarded the opportunity to return to the Olympiastadion as an extraordinary personal occasion. His performance against the Netherlands ultimately exceeded even those expectations.

The Freiburg attacker became only the third Berlin-born player to score for Germany in an international match staged in the capital, following Thomas Häßler against Bulgaria in November 1995 and Hans Ruch against Scotland in June 1929.

Klopp was particularly impressed by the contributions of Scherhant and fellow attacker Brajan Gruda. The Bundestrainer praised not only their technical abilities but also the considerable defensive work and intensity they contributed to Germany’s convincing victory.

Speaking after Monday’s victory, Scherhant recalled how frequently his chances of reaching professional football had been dismissed during his youth.

“A few years ago, people told me that I wouldn’t make it as a professional,” Scherhant explained. “Then I became a professional. And even then, some people were still saying that it wasn’t enough, that the Regionalliga was the highest level I’d ever reach. It’s simply unbelievable that I’m now standing here, with the satisfaction of having proved those people wrong. 99 percent would have said never. One percent might have said perhaps, if he works really hard.”

Klopp offered his own assessment of Scherhant’s contribution alongside Gruda.

“Derry Scherhant and Brajan Gruda, the way those two play football — my goodness,” Klopp remarked afterwards. “But the amount of work they put in and had to put in today was extraordinary. That’s what ultimately makes a team. Because there are other players who can play football too. But it’s the commitment and passion you bring to it that ultimately matter.”

Scherhant himself struggled to contain his delight after scoring in the stadium he once called home.

“What can I say?” Scherhant said. “It couldn’t have gone any better, scoring the opening goal here in my own city. You couldn’t have dreamed it up any better. It’s truly unbelievable. I had a tremendous amount of fun and I’m simply overjoyed.”

With his postponed birthday celebrations still awaiting him, Scherhant could finally look forward to an evening with family and friends after a homecoming that will remain among the most memorable moments of his young career.

Thomas Frank is facing a difficult start to life as the new Crystal Palace manager as he prepared to take charge in South London.

The 53-year-old has been chosen as the man to replace Pierre Sage at after an agreement was reached at Selhurst Park, with a formal announcement expected soon.

The former Brentford boss has long been admired by members of the Palace board, who quickly identified him as their first choice to step into the dugout.

However, former Man United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider that Frank may have been set up to fail at Crystal Palace amid concerns about his appointment.

The Eagles had initially planned to hire their next manager as quickly as possible during the international break to prepare for a crucial run of fixtures on the horizon.

However, there are just four days until they visit Coventry City, and the boss is not expected to lead his first training session until later this week, raising red flags.

Brown told Football Insider on 16 November that Frank's appointment was delayed as the Palace board were surprised by his financial demands after his exit from Tottenham.

Meanwhile, negotiations over the manager's backroom staff are ongoing, despite the agreement with Frank, as they aim to back him to bring in his trusted allies.

Those delays, however, could cause further problems for Frank and Crystal Palace as they prepare for a battle to move away from the relegation zone.

"There is one big problem with all of these delays, though," Brown told Football Insider.

"It looks like Frank is going to be the next Crystal Palace manager, I wouldn't worry about that, but I would be concerned about how long he will have to get his plans in place.

"They'd hoped to do it quickly, it should've been done as soon as they decided to sack Pierre Sage, he could've come in with plenty of time to get to work.

"Now, it's only a few days until they face Coventry, and he's going to have very little time to start getting his ideas across before the game.

"That's not an ideal situation for any manager going into a new club, you need time and space to do your work, so he's been set up to fail a bit in that respect.

"It hasn't gone as smoothly as Palace had hoped, but that's the way things happen sometimes, so it's up to them and up to Frank to do the best with what they've got.

"Palace don't want to get sucked into a relegation battle, although it may be too late for that, but Frank must be given the right support to get things going in the right direction."

Frank quickly emerged as the front-runner to land the Crystal Palace job after their decision to sack Sage, having also been in the running for the job in the summer.

A number of other names have been assessed by the Eagles, including both Sean Dyche and Vitor Pereira, as they were keen to consider all potential targets.

Football Insider revealed on 15 November that Frank's Premier League experience and remarkable work with Brentford have made him the top choice for Palace.

The boss is also viewed as a good motivator, which is something Palace are searching for to improve their morale having dropped to the bottom of the Premier League table.

Crystal Palace hope Frank's reign will reflect the work he did with Brentford rather than with Tottenham, where he struggled, and believe he is the right man for the job.

As a reminder, players must receive votes on at least 75% of the ballots cast to be inducted into the Hall of Fame with the 2027 class. Those who get at least 5% of the vote can remain on the ballot the following year, and so on, for a maximum of 10 voting cycles. Players who are on the ballot for 10 years without getting 75% of the vote, in addition to players not getting 5% of the vote, fall off the ballot in ensuing years, though that doesn't mean the doors to the Hall are permanently shut.

The results will be announced on Jan. 19, with the induction set for Cooperstown over the summer.

Here's everything else you need to know about the Baseball Hall of Fame this offseason.

As alluded to earlier, there are two different votes each offseason. The BBWAA vote is for former players who have been retired from Major League Baseball for at least five years. It's the most common method in mainstream Hall of Fame ballot discussions. When you hear stuff like "first-ballot Hall of Famer," it means via the BBWAA vote.

Players who don't make it via the BBWAA vote can get extra chances via committees. That's how, for example, Jeff Kent made it last year. Formerly called the Veterans Committee, the Hall of Fame now uses Era Committees, which are divided into two categories: contemporary baseball (1980 to present) and classic (prior to 1980). The contemporary ballot is then split into two, one for players and one for non-players like umpires, executives, pioneers and managers.

Jake O'Brien remains committed to Everton despite ongoing speculation about his long-term future at the club.

The 25-year-old has dropped out of the starting XI since the season began, and has made just six Premier League appearances so far, but is showing no sign of discontent.

While away on international duty with the Republic of Ireland, the centre-back explained that, despite the speculation, he is prepared to work hard to regain his place.

Former West Ham scout Mick Brown, who worked closely alongside David Moyes at the London Stadium, told Football Insider that the boss will love what he has heard from O'Brien.

Despite being a natural centre-back, the defender has often been asked to fill in on the right-hand side, and while he acknowledges it is not his natural position, he is happy to fill in.

During the summer, it had been suggested that O'Brien was close to leaving Everton, but he has insisted an exit was never on the cards and he remains fully committed.

Brown told Football Insider back in September that Everton were prepared to let O'Brien leave during the summer and were unlikely to change their stance in January.

After he was pushed down the pecking order at the Hill Dickinson Stadium, leading to questions about his long-term role in Moyes' side, doubts have emerged over his future.

However, Moyes is believed to be a strong admirer of O'Brien, and after what he said about his role in the side, plans could be made to keep him at the club.

"Jake O'Brien's comments about his role at Everton will be music to the ears of David Moyes," Brown told Football Insider.

"That's exactly what he wants from his players, that willingness, that commitment and that desire to play a role for the team rather than just for the individual.

"O'Brien is right, he's not a right-back, but if it's where he's being asked to play then he'll get his head down and do it.

"There's obviously been a lot of talk about his future at Everton, but David Moyes loves players like that who are willing to commit to their job, and he's just proven that.

"So I think any talk about his future can probably be dismissed for now, because he's certainly got a role to play under Moyes."

Speculation about O'Brien's future at Everton had placed a number of Premier League clubs on alert, and they are believed to be keeping a close eye on his situation.

Should he be allowed to leave the Hill Dickinson Stadium in the January transfer window, there will be no shortage of suitors, with much depending on Everton's stance.

Brown told Football Insider last month that Crystal Palace could make a move to bring O'Brien back to South London, three years after he left the club to join Lyon.

After struggling to replace Maxence Lacroix in the summer, Crystal Palace could look to bring in another centre-back, and O'Brien remains highly regarded at Selhurst Park.

Thomas Frank has been unveiled as Crystal Palace’s new manager on a bumper three-and-a-half-year deal.

Frank has been out of work since being sacked by Tottenham Hotspur in February and has taken a significant pay cut to take over at Palace, who languish at the bottom of the Premier League.

However, he will earn more than his predecessor, Pierre Sage, who was sacked last week after winning just two of his ten league matches in charge.

New Palace boss Frank said: ‘It's an honour to sit here and be in charge of this fantastic Football Club. I'm really, really looking forward to it.

‘Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League. I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I'm really looking forward to meeting them.’

Across his spells at Brentford and Tottenham, Frank has managed 178 Premier League matches with a 34 per cent win rate.

Palace are set to approach Chelsea about Bernardo Cueva, who worked with Frank at Brentford before being poached to become the Blues' set-piece specialist.

Luis de la Fuente insists he has "nothing but words of pride and gratitude" for his players as Spain's incredible 43-match unbeaten run came to an end on Sunday.

La Roja fought back after that, though, with Elliot Anderson given his marching orders for a desperate foul on Lamine Yamal, and Mikel Oyarzabal calmly converted the resulting penalty.

Nico Williams pulled another back in the 75th minute, but Spain were unable to salvage a draw, though De la Fuente could not fault his players.

Week 10 Booms and Busts: Patrick Mahomes, Chiefs showcase best fantasy football outing — without Kenneth Walker III

Although the Chiefs have been contenders all year, the shape of this offense has always felt different. New running back Kenneth Walker III has been the star. Signature QB Patrick Mahomes has played well, but in a secondary role. The offense has been missing splash plays downfield. More often than not, this KC team wore you down and beat you up, but other than the Walker highlights, it hasn't been especially pretty.

And then came Week 10. With Walker unavailable, the Chiefs ironically showed their best offensive form in two months.

Mahomes was just about perfect in a 35-13 romp at Atlanta, completing 22-of-35 passes for 348 yards and five touchdowns. He was only sacked once — on several plays he had ridiculous time to throw — and didn't turn the ball over.

The Chiefs kept hitting haymakers downfield. Seven different players had a catch of 20 yards or more, and Mahomes spread his touchdowns around to four teammates. Josh Downs (4-53-1) produced in his first KC start. Cyrus Allen (2-44-1) got behind the Atlanta defense for a 39-yard score. Andrew Armstrong made the most of a late target, turning it into a circus catch for a 43-yard touchdown. Rashee Rice (5-82-0) didn't score, but he had a rare downfield chunk play, a nifty 50-yard grab on a contested route. Some days, it feels like everything works.

And then there's rookie Emmett Johnson, the replacement for Walker (groin). Johnson is obviously one of the biggest Week 10 winners. The rookie racked up 171 total yards (101 yards rushing, 70 receiving) and caught a couple of touchdown passes. The first was a win for play design, the second a classic case of Mahomes making something when the original options aren't there. Everyone knows how special Walker has been this year, but Johnson's a league-tilter if Walker isn't available. Both players are fun to watch.

So now we wonder when Walker might be back, and if Johnson can maintain some kind of role when Walker plays. Johnson now has four touchdowns in three weeks, and he had six touches in both Weeks 8 and 9. Walker's job is obviously secure, but Johnson might be too good to ignore moving forward. Perhaps the rookie can hold deeper flex value in the final third of the year.

For as muted as Mahomes has looked at times this year, he hasn't been bad. He was the QB7 before this week started. He rarely turns the ball over. The Cardinals are a favorable draw next week, and the Buffalo matchup in Week 12 could turn into a pinball game.

The one big name from the Chiefs who missed this party was Travis Kelce. The Hall of Fame-bound tight end had an early 23-yard catch but dinged his ankle on the play and missed some time in the game. He eventually finished with two catches for 30 yards. He's now gone six straight weeks without a touchdown catch, though he did have a scoring run last week. He's on the starting fringe for the rest of the fantasy season.

The Rams were another offense that had everything working in Week 10, despite Puka Nacua and Davante Adams not being available. Matthew Stafford threw for three touchdowns and the offense racked up 202 rushing yards. The 34-6 win over the Cardinals was never competitive.

Okay, maybe everything wasn't working. While Blake Corum (17-100-1) was excellent throughout and Ronnie Rivers (15-81-0) cleaned up in garbage time, Kyren Williams (12-23-0 rushing, 2-9-0 receiving) could never get started. Williams is still a good player, of course, but it's possible Corum is taking over this backfield. The Rams will have a lot of other things to think about as they enter their Week 11 bye.

The Carolina offensive line has been hurt at times this year, but nothing has stopped Hubbard. He'll do his thing at home against Baltimore next week.

Why do birds flap before landing? It may be about more than slowing down

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

New calculations suggest birds flap during landing not just to control speed, but to stop within limited space.

Most birds flap their wings as they come in to land, but those final wingbeats may do more than slow them down. New calculations suggest they also help birds stop within a tight space.

A bird the size of a pigeon could glide to a complete stop with its wings held still.

To manage it, though, the bird would have to begin its landing maneuver at about 80 mph (36 meters per second). It would also have to climb roughly 65 feet (20 meters), the height of a six-story building.

Flapping, the researchers propose, lets a bird stop inside the space it has.

Tom Rottier carried out the study at the University of Manchester and the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies. He worked with Philippe Lavoie of Toronto and Ben Parslew of Manchester.

No birds were flown for the work. Instead, equations were written for a bird gliding on fixed wings.

Only gravity, lift, and air resistance acted on this model bird. The team then solved the equations for the slowest possible touchdown.

The forces on a wing depend on how fast the wing moves through the air. Those forces shrink as a bird slows down. Its control over where it goes shrinks with them.

Arriving fast is no better. Whatever speed is left at touchdown is usually absorbed by the legs. A bad landing can damage muscle and bone, or it can kill the bird.

The difficulty, said Parslew, an aerospace engineering lecturer at Manchester, is that “landing isn’t as simple as just slowing down.”

A wing also has a minimum working speed. In level flight, the bird’s whole weight has to be carried by lift. A wing that moves too slowly cannot make enough of it. Engineers call that minimum the stall speed.

The stall speed is not fixed, however, as the team’s equations show. A bird that climbs more steeply puts less of its weight on its wings. The stall speed drops as a result. In a climb straight up, it is zero.

A climb that ends straight up is how a gliding bird could stop without flapping. The bird comes in fast and pulls up. It then climbs more and more steeply while gravity and air resistance slow it down.

At the top, the bird is pointed straight up and has no speed left.

More lift also means more drag, and drag takes away the speed the climb depends on. For that reason, the tilt of the wings has to keep changing on the way up. By the end, the wings are turned broadside to the air.

The team calculated what such a landing would take for a rock pigeon weighing 14 ounces (400 grams).

Starting from level flight, the bird would need that 80 mph speed. This is more than double a pigeon’s typical cruising speed.

The bird would travel about 200 feet (60 meters) horizontally, roughly two-thirds of a football field, while climbing 65 feet.

City pigeons fly between buildings. A landing of that size is not feasible in many of those places, the team wrote. Bigger birds would need even more room.

The researchers therefore propose that flapping is not simply a way to slow down. A glide can do that. Flapping does it in a small space.

A perch can be approached from below, but the ground cannot. That changes the answer.

The researchers then tested landings in which the model bird could not climb at any point. No path brought it to a stop.

The slowest landing allowed by the equations came in at an angle of about 34 degrees below level.

A bird that glides down to the ground therefore arrives with some speed left. That speed has to be removed by flapping, by braking with the legs, or by both.

The legs need enough muscle and bone to absorb the impact. But that adds weight the bird must carry in flight.

The researchers suggest this trade-off may have shaped how birds evolved. The need to withstand landing impacts may have set a minimum strength for their legs.

Leftover speed may also help explain why ducks, geese, and swans land differently on water than on solid ground.

More of a bird’s energy is absorbed by water than by the ground. The bird can therefore arrive faster.

Some birds of prey do finish a landing without flapping.

A 2022 study observed Harris’s hawks flying between two perches. They learned to dive low and then glide up to the perch.

A wing that is tilted very steeply is stalled, meaning air no longer flows smoothly over it.

The authors of that study proposed an explanation for the swoop. In their view, it keeps the distance a hawk flies with stalled wings as short as possible.

Rottier’s team tested the idea by giving their model bird one goal at a time. For this test, the model bird was allowed to flap during the dive, as the hawks did.

First, it was told only to spend as little time stalled as possible. The model bird climbed and then came down onto the perch. It arrived at speeds that would harm a real hawk, the team wrote.

Next, the only goal was to land as slowly as possible. This time the model bird swooped deep.

A mix of the two goals generally matched the real hawks’ paths best. Touchdown speed did more than stall time to produce the swoop.

Diving builds speed, and speed allows a steeper climb. A steeper climb ends in a slower arrival.

The pigeon figures come from equations, not from a bird in the air. In its main form, the model has no wind, no body or tail, and no flapping.

A wing gets a brief boost in lift and drag when it tilts up quickly. The wings of landing birds do this, yet the boost is also left out of the model. To see how much it could matter, the team doubled the model’s maximum lift.

For a slow touchdown, the starting speed the bird needed fell by 44%. The forward distance fell by 65% and the climb by 55%. When the maximum drag was doubled instead, the speed needed went up.

A living bird may therefore be able to glide to a stop in less room than the pigeon numbers suggest. How much less cannot be said from the model.

The equations were written so that wind can be added later. The team also described the model as a base for studies of single species.

The same physics may apply to animals that never flap, such as flying squirrels and flying snakes. It may also apply to drones built to land on perches.

The full study was published in the journal Proceedings of the Royal Society A.

Being in the NBA has afforded Wagler opportunities early in the season to play against some of his idols with the Clippers having played the Thunder twice this season.
