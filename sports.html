Ankeny Christian returns to state volleyball tournament with a victory

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

CORALVILLE ― Coach Michele Quick and Ankeny Christian started the 2026 state volleyball tournament the way they left it in 2024.

The Eagles (38-2) beat West Monona 25-16, 25-19, 25-19 on Nov. 3 to advance to the Class 1A semifinals.

Ankeny Christian missed the state tournament a season ago, and 1A teams got a break from seeing Eagles consistently dominate the field.

"It's pretty exciting to be back," Quick said. "We knew we had a lot of potential this year, and we had a lot of pieces that we needed as far as our offense, and we have some great defense behind our offense as well. So it's pretty exciting. We have some new girls that haven't been here before, but we definitely have some experienced girls too."

After a season of regrouping, a school-record 35 regular-season wins, a 7-0 conference mark and five regular-season tournament wins, the Eagles are primed to get a fourth state title in five seasons.

The Eagles put together an impressive regular-season resume with wins over North Polk, Aplington-Parkersburg, Denver and Grundy Center. The only losses all season were against 5A Ankeny and 3A Roland-Story, two teams that also qualified for the state tournament.

West Monona battled in the early parts of each set Tuesday night. In the first set, Breeze Bentz kept Ankeny Christian afloat. Her kill from the right side made the score 14-10. A play later another kill from Bentz gave the Eagles a five-point cushion.

The second set was an even stronger fight from the Spartans, and Ankeny Christian didn't pull away until late in the game. Havanna Dolbeare added to her kill total and gave the Eagles the 25-19 win.

In the third set, West Monona got within two, 15-13, but the more disciplined Ankeny Christian team used its service game and attacking to win and end the night.

"I feel like we just focus on the next point," Dolbeare said. "We don't think about all the mistakes we made in the past. We let those go, and we just focus on the next point, and we lock in from there."

Dolbeare and Bentz combined for 32 kills, and setter Gabbie Quick assisted 30 times in the win.

Dani Craighead and Gabbie Quick are freshman starters. Dolbeare and Karmen Lamberts are sophomore starters. Bentz is a junior, as is fellow starter Liv Nehring. Libero Katelyn Weathers is the only senior starter.

"We are just such an exciting group," Weathers said. "We have fun, and right now we are just taking everything one point at a time. It's really, really important that we come out strong, and even if we don't and we are stuck in a rut, we want to come out and stay high."

Michele Quick is now 10-0 in state tournament competition since 2022, and with two more wins she will have her fourth 1A state title since arriving at the school in 2018.

"I think the newness and the youngness is what's different," Michele Quick said. "Putting the pieces together in the past, we had those girls together since freshman year. Whenever you kind of combine a lot of different varieties, you never know what it's going to look like."

NEC Nijmegen and Omonia face off at Goffertstadion in Thursday's Europa League matchday-four action, with the Dutch side looking to leapfrog the 11th-placed visitors.

Dick Schreuder's men have struggled domestically amid their European commitments, but they enter this week's fixture buoyed by a 4-2 win over Dinamo Zagreb on matchday three and aim to secure consecutive continental victories at the expense of the Cypriot champions.

All things considered, NEC's campaign has been hugely disappointing so far, given that Schreuder's team finished third in the Eredivisie last season.

That placed the Nijmegen outfit on the brink of a Champions League debut, and although defeat to Bodo/Glimt denied them a place in Europe's premier club competition, Europa League football was no less than they deserved.

However, Europe's second-tier club competition has brought mixed results for the Eredivisie outfit: they have lost one, drawn one and won one, with the 4-2 comeback victory over Dinamo last time out providing timely encouragement.

That success in Zagreb marked NEC's first victory in any competition since late August's 3-1 win at PEC Zwolle, highlighting their broader struggles both domestically and on the continent.

Schreuder's men sit 13th in the Dutch top flight, two points clear of the relegation playoff spot, and 20th in the 36-team Europa League standings, although maximum points on Thursday could propel them up the table.

As such, it will be fascinating to see how their Cypriot opponents approach matchday four against a side that typically play all-or-nothing football.

Henning Berg's side enter the fixture having ended a three-match winless run that included consecutive defeats to Benfica in Europe and AEK Larnaca, the latter in their penultimate October fixture.

Indeed, it was no surprise that the Queen's first victory in four coincided with their first clean sheet since mid-October's 3-0 success over Celje, which had marked the Shamrock's fourth shutout in five matches across all competitions.

That sequence was followed by eight goals conceded across three matches, giving NEC reason to believe that their Cypriot opponents can be exploited in certain game situations.

Nonetheless, Omonia travel to Nijmegen aware of their promising European position as the league phase reaches its halfway point; another victory could lift the 11th-placed Shamrock into the coveted top eight, with the three sides occupying sixth to eighth all just one point above Berg's troops.

Dusan Tadic is NEC's most notable absentee due to a calf injury, having not played since mid-October, while Philippe Sandler, Bram Nuytinck, Emre Mor and Dennis Geiger are also sidelined.

Tjaronn Chery has been the Nijmegen club's standout performer in Europe, scoring three of their five goals to share the lead in the competition's scoring charts with six other players.

Unlike the Dutch outfit, Omonia have shared the goals around, with Jaden Montnor, Senou Coulibaly, Jure Balkovec, Muamer Tankovic and Andronikos Kakoullis all finding the net for the Shamrock.

While the visitors have a relatively healthy squad, Berg must manage without Loic Nego (muscle) and Carel Eiting (knee).

Chivu: ‘Inter were the best version of ourselves’ in 3-0 victory at Feyenoord

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Cristian Chivu feels Inter were ‘the best version of ourselves’ in tonight’s 3-0 Champions League victory away to Feyenoord, but ‘consistency is what brings results.’

The Nerazzurri were under pressure in Rotterdam and needed to send a response after their 3-1 defeat to Milan in the Derby della Madonnina on Saturday.

It was certainly that, as they dominated against a Feyenoord side that beat Como here 3-2 and has a notoriously strong home record.

Yann Bisseck, Francesco Pio Esposito and Piotr Zielinski were all on target in a pretty comfortable encounter, and Esposito had another goal ruled inches offside on a Federico Dimarco pass.

Notably, Inter have been criticised for their bad starts to games and it was not since August that they had scored in the opening eight minutes.

“We have certain values, we understood certain things and put them on the pitch,” Chivu told Sky Sport Italia.

“That doesn’t mean we haven’t done well so far this season. We tried to be the best version of ourselves, it worked today with organisation, intensity and everything we needed for a strong performance in a stadium that is not easy to play in.”

What did the coach mean when saying the team understood certain things?

“We always have discussions after every game, whether it goes well or badly, we try to correct errors and build on our strengths. Sometimes it goes better than others, perhaps we understood what wasn’t going as well,” said Chivu.

“The Inter world, the club owners, the fans, we are all aware of the values of these players who want to fly the flag high, that has never been in doubt.”

Another recurring problem so far this season has been a lack of consistency throughout the 90 minutes, but this evening they always seemed to be in control.

“Consistency is what brings results, it gives you self-confidence and belief in what you are doing, it is what we are looking for at this moment. This game gives us a big boost of confidence in what we are building, so we know football can take you down some strange paths, but you must be able to react and get back onto the right path,” concluded the Inter coach.

This result allows Inter to climb up the Champions League table, but they still have only six points from four games, so it will be a fight to the end in order to scrape into the next round.

It's not Grumors. Jon Gruden will be in Knoxville for Tennessee football vs Kentucky

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Jon Gruden still has a decades-old binder of notes from his time as a graduate assistant for Tennessee football. The Super Bowl-winning coach got his start in coaching at UT on Johnny Majors' staff in 1986-87.

One of the pages in that binder includes Majors' points of focus before the Vols played Kentucky in 1986.

The first line reads: Stakes are rather high. No place for the weak, faint-hearted.

Gruden, who now works for Barstool Sports, announced that he'll be in Knoxville to see the No. 16 Vols (7-1, 3-2 SEC) play No. 7 Kentucky (7-1, 5-1) on Nov. 7 (6:15 p.m. ET, ESPN). It will be the 123rd matchup between the two programs in a series dating back to 1893.

In his announcement video, Gruden skimmed through a few typed-out pages of notes from the Thursday before the 1986 matchup. They included simple tasks, like reviewing a field position chart, and motivational notes such as "we lost because we reached their level" and "we could be the best turnaround of the year."

"There's nothing like Tennessee football," Gruden said. "This is the good days right here."

Gruden's current job has him making football-related content online, including videos highlighting visits to college football programs like Oklahoma, Auburn, Navy and Iowa. This weekend he'll be back in Knoxville.

Gruden, who won Super Bowl 37 coaching the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and also had multiple stints leading the Raiders, was rumored as a candidate for the Tennessee job multiple times. His name was floated in 2012 when UT hired Butch Jones and again in 2017 before the Vols hired Jeremy Pruitt.

Gruden has said that he was interested in coaching UT in 2012 and even spoke with then-athletics director Dave Hart about the position. But he decided he was content continuing to call "Monday Night Football" games as an analyst for ESPN.

"I was very interested in doing it but my job was too good at that time," Gruden said at the Knoxville Quarterback Club in October 2025.

Bayern skeleton crew sink Atletico in Champions League

Bayern Munich overcame a horror list of absences to cruise to a 2-0 win away at Atletico Madrid in the Champions League on Tuesday.

Teenage Germany forward Lennart Karl opened the scoring and Konrad Laimer added another as Bayern triumphed despite a bulging casualty ward.

Jamal Musiala, Luis Diaz, Serge Gnabry and Ismael Saibari were on Bayern's injury list but it meant little as freshly-minted Ballon d'Or winner Harry Kane and captain Joshua Kimmich marshalled his troops to a memorable victory.

The German champions also arrived in Madrid without coach Vincent Kompany, sick with a meningitis-related condition.

"There was a lot of noise around us heading in but we're very convinced of the squad we have, of the players we've got at our disposal and I think we showed over 90 minutes the type of football we can play," Laimer, who was named man of the match by UEFA, told Amazon Prime.

"We could've scored another goal or two. It was a very deserved win against a good team and an important three points."

Despite laying on an assist for Laimer, Kane failed to score meaning he missed out on equalling Cristiano Ronaldo's record for scoring in 11 straight Champions League games.

"We are standing together despite the difficult times at Bayern in the last weeks and are delighted we could experience this team victory together," Bayern captain Manuel Neuer said.

"We heard that people were doubting how well we could perform today against a top team, just two points behind Barcelona in La Liga, but to give an answer in the way we did is sensational."

Atletico's Marcos Llorente told Movistar+ the hosts were stunned by Bayern's opener.

"They're a strong side but we were holding our own. It's the little details that decide a game, and we weren't expecting that first goal.

"We were covering well, so it hit us like a bucket of cold water."

With two wins and two draws, Bayern climbed to eighth spot on the table while Atletico finish Tuesday night in 22nd spot on the 36-team table.

Undermanned Bayern were handed a boost pre-match when Michael Olise was given the all clear to start after being subbed out late with a knee injury against Borussia Dortmund on Saturday.

Olise joined Bayern's two remaining senior forwards Kane and Karl in the starting line-up, with wingback Alphonso Davies pushed up onto the left wing.

Atletico were received their own late injury withdrawal when Christian Romero was ruled out on matchday.

Julian Alvarez came close to giving the hosts an early lead, flashing inches wide of the post three minutes in.

Kane had his own chance to break the deadlock midway through the opening half but blasted wide of Jan Oblak's goal on the turn.

Atletico had repelled the visitors despite ceding possession and field position but it was a simple mistake from the hosts which allowed Bayern to take the lead.

Robin Le Normand took too long on the ball allowing the energetic Karl to pick his pocket and loft a shot over the advancing Oblak.

Chasing the game, Diego Simeone made three changes but Bayern scored next when Kane drifted a clever pass into Laimer's path, allowing the Austrian to blast in off the crossbar.

Bayern could have had a third with 10 minutes remaining when Dayot Upamecano outmuscled his marker and headed in, but the big Frenchman was penalised for an offensive foul.

Despite having few remaining bench options, Bayern looked the team most likely to score in the dying moments as they easily kept Atletico at bay in a comfortable victory.

Villarreal 0-6 Paris Saint-Germain: Fabian scores twice as clinical champions make history

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Fabian Ruiz scored twice as Paris Saint-Germain made history in their 6-0 battering of Villarreal that saw them stay perfect through four matches of the Champions League.

The defending champions were four goals to the good inside just 17 first-half minutes at Estadio de la Ceramica on Tuesday. Indeed, the four goals they scored after 16 minutes and two seconds is the fastest a team has ever scored four goals in the competition's history.

Joao Neves' clever flick got the ball rolling, before Desire Doue and Senny Mayulu benefited from two poor Peter Gulacsi clearances, and Fabian hammered in a fourth.

Fabian was on target again five minutes after the restart and teenage defender David Boly bagged off the bench as Luis Enrique's side made it 12 points from a possible 12.

Neves got PSG up and running, ingeniously picking out the top corner inside the six-yard box when Villarreal failed to clear their lines at a ninth-minute corner.

Gulacsi made a hash of a routine clearance that led to Doue rolling into the bottom-left corner after being teed up by Maghnes Akliouche, and another poor moment from the Villarreal goalkeeper eventually led to Mayulu pouncing with an effort he drilled into the floor after Neves' effort was blocked.

By the time Fabian slashed a bouncing ball into the bottom-left corner in the 17th minute, it was over as a contest, though the Spaniard and Doue almost stretched the lead before half-time.

Fabian had a wonderful second early in the second half, brilliantly taking down Vitinha's long pass, cutting inside Renato Veiga and flicking past Gulacsi.

There was time for another, as Neves picked out Boly on the right of the area, and the 17-year-old smashed his effort high past Gulacsi via a deflection off Veiga before wheeling away in delight at his first Champions League goal.

In truth, this game was over by the 17th minute, and PSG are now four wins from four to sit top of the league-phase standings.

PSG are now unbeaten in 14 games in the Champions League (W10 D4), equalling the longest unbeaten run by a French side in European Cup/Champions League history after Lyon did so between December 2004 and March 2006.

The Parisiens are unbeaten in five Champions League away games against Spanish opposition since Luis Enrique took charge (W4 D1), having only two of 12 such contests prior to his arrival (D3 L7).

There often comes a point in a manager’s reign when the blame for defeat shifts from the dugout to the dressing room, and it is invariably the point of no return. Rarely does it arrive after just nine matches.

So when Matthias Jaissle took aim at his players in the wake of Newcastle’s 1-0 defeat by Everton on Monday, it was arguably a more concerning display than the 90 minutes which had preceded it, as alarming as those had been.

Jaissle’s post-match reflections were not inaccurate. This was not a manager talking nonsense. He fronted up and gave what felt like honest answers.

The 38-year-old is bright and articulate and, with each defeat, has shown an increasing humility that was less visible during the optimism of his introductory days.

To show the players the underside of the bus so soon, however, is as baffling a tactic as the strategy enacted on the pitch, even if Jaissle insists that performance had no resemblance to what he had asked of his team.

Rather, says the German, he had wanted his players to keep the ball on the floor and get the wingers in one-on-one situations high up the pitch. What he saw was a collection of long balls and a heading-practice session for Everton’s centre backs. The wingers, meanwhile, were spectators.

When a manager blames the players for defeat - as Matthias Jaissle did after Newcastle lost to Everton on Monday night - it is often the beginning of the end for the man in the dugout

‘The plan was not to shoot the long balls,’ he said.

Nor was the plan to shoot at all, it seemed. It took until the 85th minute for Newcastle to trouble Jordan Pickford, and even then the goalkeeper wasn’t overly concerned.

Short ball? Long ball? Whatever pass Newcastle pick, it is invariably the wrong ball. They are a team without identity – no patterns, no partnerships and, certainly, no sign of progress. I once wrote of Everton boss David Moyes, when in charge of Sunderland, that he had inherited a sinking ship, only to drill another hole. Moyes, for the record, is a very good manager, he just wasn’t at Sunderland.

And that is the danger with Jaissle. He, too, walked into a difficult situation post Eddie Howe and the loss of several star players. Their replacements have been bench-warners or cold starters.

But Jaissle has to be the solution, not another problem. At present, the fear is that a young manager with no major-league experience is held hostage by his ideals and refuses to bend to what his players want and need.

His continued use of a 4-2-3-1 system, with two midfielders who cannot really run and a revolving cast of No 10s who are not No 10s, feels with each passing week and each passing defeat like a blueprint for relegation.

Maybe the team ignoring their manager’s wishes on Monday night was a cry for change. A protest, even? Do they not believe in his methods or do they not understand them?
