Jerry Jones defends decision to not pay top dollar for an NFL head coach: 'There's no correlation'

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Dallas Cowboys collapsed in embarrassing fashion in Week 8. Despite leading 17-3 in the third quarter, Dallas blew Sunday's game against the Arizona Cardinals after the team benched its starting quarterback for Gardner Minshew.

It was a failure on all ends for Dallas. The team's defense suddenly couldn't get a stop despite a backup quarterback entering the game, and its offense couldn't put enough points on the board to put the Cardinals away for good.

When a team fails in such a spectacular way, the focus tends to shift to the head coach. Brian Schottenheimer wasn't a big-name hire when the Cowboys chose him to succeed Mike McCarthy. Instead, the team went with who they knew, as Schottenheimer previously served as the Cowboys' offensive coordinator.

That decision hasn't paid off just yet. While the Cowboys remain a strong offense, Schottenheimer is just 10-14-1 in a season-and-a-half at the helm. That record, combined with Sunday's result, have some wondering whether the Cowboys should have made a more splashy hire following the 2024 season.

Doing so would have cost money, something team owner Jerry Jones has not been willing to do at head coach. Jones was asked about that strategy during his weekly radio appearance Tuesday. He defended the practice, saying expensive coaches don't necessarily produce wins, per Jon Machota of The Athletic.

"There is zero correlation (in the NFL) between how much you pay a coach or how many coaches you have as to winning. There is no correlation. And that's a random sample of years and all 32 teams involved. So put that in your hopper and smoke it."

Jones is, seemingly, true to his word. Schottenheimer doesn't rank among the top-20 coaches in salary for the 2026 season, per Front Office Sports.

Whether that's a smart strategy remains to be seen. Schottenheimer has yet to prove himself as a strong hire, and his team's collapse in Week 8 doesn't speak highly of his ability keep a team in line in crunch time.

Jones is probably correct that having the right coach is far more important than having the most expensive coach. Unfortunately for Jones, it's his responsibility to make that decision.

Big Mistake: Newcastle Must Replace Jaissle With Nagelsmann ASAP

Newcastle United's poor start to the Premier League season continued on Monday night as the team fell to a 1-0 defeat at home to Everton.

Thierno Barry's second half goal was the difference between the sides, as the Magpies suffered a fourth defeat in nine games so far this season, and slipped down to 13th in the table, just four points above the relegation zone.

The defeat means Newcastle have now lost four of their last five games in all competitions and five of their last seven, and the level of performance has seen many fans and members of the media question whether the decision to appoint Matthias Jaissle as Eddie Howe's replacement was the right one.

When Howe surprisingly resigned just two weeks before the season began, the Saudi owners moved quickly to bring the German into the job because of his attacking style of football.

The season started well, too, with a draw against Liverpool at Anfield and a win over Tottenham, but since then the style of football has looked unsustainable with this squad. Jaissle has struggled to make the team pick up their tasks properly; the new signings are largely struggling to make an immediate impact, and he doesn't seem to trust his entire squad.

After the defeat to Everton, Jaissle publicly switched the blame to his players too, accusing them of going against the game plan by playing long balls despite him wanting the exact opposite.

"There is big frustration & disappointment. We could not not transfer what we saw in the week, training in a high level. Since the start of the game, not much was seen. That is difficult to understand.

"We were not brave enough to break the line and play on the ground. We saw it in training, but there was almost nothing to see from these aspects in the game. In possession, this was not the level we wanted to see.

"If you just play long balls, it’s not the style Osula prefers & that was not the plan. We need to analyse this in depth, why we could not start the game how we planned. I’m responsible for that, to make it work.

"We have a clear identity of how we want to play. To bring the opposition in stressful situations high in the pitch. We spoke about creating 1-on-1 situations. The plan was not to shoot the long balls."

When the management and players begin to go head-to-head, things usually only end in one way, and that is with a change in the dugout.

But Newcastle don't need to wait for results to worsen, or for the situation to get to a point of no return to make a change. They can get ahead of the curve, admit that his appointment was a mistake, and make an ambitious move to replace him with someone who is undoubtedly better.

With Jaissle seemingly on his way out at St James' Park sooner rather than later, barring any big turnarounds, Newcastle should be assessing their options to replace him.

Eddie Howe is obviously not going to return having just left, but Julian Nagelsmann is a manager who is available immediately and could make a huge difference to the squad. He and Jaissle play a similar style of football having both come through the ranks in Germany, but Nagelsmann has got experience at the highest level.

Tenerife striker Jesus de Miguel, 30, died this morning just hours after receiving a flying knee to the chest from Andorra FC goalkeeper Jesus Owono, who was sent off before the match in Spain's second division was abandoned.

Owono, 25, is reportedly planning to apologise to de Miguel's family. He is said to be 'deeply upset' and has insisted there was 'no intention to cause harm'.

The Andorra goalkeeper was subjected to chants of 'murderer' by the Tenerife fans, who clashed with him as he left the pitch.

And the Homicide Group of the National Police in Santa Cruz de Tenerife are now investigating what happened during the match.

But other reports suggest Owono is expected to avoid criminal sanctions over the horrifying on-pitch collision, with his challenge likely to only result in sporting penalties.

In Spain, investigations are typically opened for any deaths not linked to natural causes.

Spanish sports lawyer Miguel Galan said today: 'The Supreme Court's doctrine states that excesses during a play are subject to sporting, not criminal, sanctions.'

Tenerife, from Spain's second tier of football, confirmed Jesus de ​Miguel's death in a statement this morning

Goalkeeper ‌Jesus Owono has reportedly insisted there was 'no intention to cause harm' after his challenge led to the death of the Tenerife striker

De Miguel was admitted to intensive care after the tackle. However, Tenerife announced today that his condition had worsened overnight and he passed away this morning after entering cardiac arrest.

The striker was treated for over five minutes while his team-mates were visibly distressed, with some appearing to be in tears.

Journalist Iván Alvarez, speaking on COPE radio before De Miguel's death was announced, reported that the goalkeeper intended to apologise to De Miguel's family in person.

Alvarez added that Owono and Andorra FC had insisted there had been 'no intention to cause harm' and that the goalkeeper had closed his Instagram account after being subjected to 'murderer' chants and messages.

'Some of the people who were at the stadium and some messages I exchanged with players told me it's the hardest thing they've ever experienced on a football pitch,' the journalist said.

He added that Owono is 'very upset' and that 'no one would ever think of someone wanting to harm a fellow professional in that way'.

Alvarez continued: 'Andorra emphasises that there was no ill intent at any point, acknowledging that it was a mistimed outburst, an action that went terribly wrong and, I would say, excessive and violent, but they insist there was no intention to cause harm.

'Owono, who doesn't appreciate hearing an entire stadium like the Heliodoro chanting "murderer, murderer", has even closed his Instagram account after all the criticism he's received this afternoon.

'His intention is to contact Jesus de Miguel's family or someone close to him personally, when the time is right. He wants to do so privately first, before making any public statement.'

Footage taken from the stands had shown Owono clashing with supporters as he left the pitch.

The AS newspaper reported that a challenge judged to be an act of 'violent conduct toward an opponent during a game that causes serious injury or harm' is punishable by a suspension of four to 12 games.

Owono is a current Equatorial Guinea international and has represented his nation 44 times, including playing at the Africa Cup of Nations twice.

He grew up in the Basque Country after moving to San Sebastian in northern Spain at the age of eight.

Owono came through Real Sociedad's youth academy and joined Alaves at 16.

He became the first player born in Equatorial Guinea to play in the Spanish top flight.

Owono played 11 LaLiga matches before joining Andorra on loan last season and signed permanently for the club this summer.

The goalkeeper and his team-mates had reportedly returned to Barcelona this morning.

De Miguel is believed to have suffered a cardiac arrest while being transported to hospital and had to be revived inside the ambulance, according to Spanish media outlet Marca.

Rayco Garcia, Tenerife's majority shareholder, and the club's president Felipe Minambres spoke of their 'unbearable pain' after de Miguel's death.

The club confirmed plans for fans to pay tribute to the striker this Saturday at their stadium, while outlining that his team-mates will receive support following the tragedy.

Rayco said on Radio Marca Tenerife: 'We are facing an unprecedented situation. We are very hurt, Tenerife fans are in mourning. It is very difficult to accept. Tomorrow, or the day after tomorrow, we will have the definitive "ok" to transfer the player's body, at the express wish of his family, to Navahermosa, in Toledo.

'There we will pay our last farewell to Jesús de Miguel. On Saturday we summoned all the fans of the island, at 18:00, at the Heliodoro, to say our last goodbye together, and we will join forces to move forward. Life has hit us all, but we are a big family and we are going to move forward.'

Minambres added: 'It is a very sad day... It is an unlived situation, that a player dies like this on a pitch. We are in moments of weakness, of sadness, of not believing what has happened.

'We will go through the mourning and this will make us more united. We have to spend a week in mourning, we are going to need professional help, us, the players and their families. Also that of our followers.

'We have to pay the tribute that "Demi" deserves. We will always carry it in our hearts. It is a great misfortune that we have suffered.'

De Miguel was rushed to hospital following the incident after appearing motionless on the pitch

In a statement this morning, CD Tenerife had confirmed the striker's death, saying: 'Broken with pain, with a lump in our throat and unable to believe that this is true, from CD Tenerife we communicate the tragic death, this Tuesday, November 3, 2026, at 8:00 a.m., of Jesús de Miguel Alameda.

'There are no words of comfort. There is absolutely nothing to mitigate this feeling full of sadness.

'We are with his devastated family, with his loved ones, with all the people of Tenerife who today mourn this cruel outcome.

'The family of Jesus de Miguel is grateful for each and every one of the expressions of support received, and also requests, from the bottom of its heart, maximum respect for his privacy, in these terrible moments.'

De Miguel had become a father just last year and, according to El Espanol, his wife and baby daughter were watching in the stands at the time of the fatal collision.

Tributes have been paid to the striker from across the football world following the tragic incident.

European football body Uefa has confirmed that a minute of silence will be held at all their matches this week in tribute to De Miguel. Arsenal held a minute of silence at training this morning.

Uefa president Aleksander Čeferin said: 'We are heartbroken by the death of Jesús de Miguel Alameda.

'We have searched for words and found only silence. In that silence, our hearts and thoughts are with his family, his teammates, everyone at CD Tenerife, and all those who mourn this tragic loss.'

The Royal Spanish Football Federation have meanwhile cancelled all domestic matches today.

Tenerife's opponents Andorra offered their condolences following news of De Miguel's death, writing: 'With broken hearts and devastated, we want to send our most sincere condolences to the family of Jesus de Miguel, to all his teammates, friends, and to CD Tenerife. Rest in peace.'

Football clubs across Spain, including Barcelona and Real Madrid, have led tributes following the news of the death.

'Our thoughts and support are with his family, friends and loved ones during these difficult times,' Barcelona said in a statement.

Real Madrid also expressed their condolences, stating: 'Real Madrid wishes to express its condolences and affection to his family, his teammates, his club, and all his loved ones.'

The Spanish Footballers Association also released a statement, expressing its 'deepest sorrow' at the footballer's death.

It read: 'His loss leaves a huge void among those of us who shared his passion for football, his commitment to the footballers' community, and his work defending the rights of our fellow professionals.

'The Board of Directors, all the staff of AFE, and the footballers' community wish to extend our deepest condolences to his family, friends, and loved ones, as well as to his teammates at Club Deportivo Tenerife. In these moments of immense sadness, we send them all our love, support, and solidarity. You will always be part of our family.'

De Miguel joined Tenerife in 2025 and scored 11 goals in 30 appearances to help secure the team promotion from the Spanish third division as champions.

The forward had previously helped to secure Internacional de Madrid and CD Castellón promotions from the fourth and third divisions, respectively.

CD Castellon also released a statement on Tuesday morning in tribute to their former player, saying: 'With broken hearts and inconsolable grief, Club Deportivo Castellón bids farewell to Jesus De Miguel.

'Words fail to express the profound injustice of this loss. He wore this shirt with pride, celebrated goals with our fans at the SkyFi Castalia stadium, and was an integral part of a story that this fanbase will never forget.

'We extend our deepest condolences to his family and loved ones, offering them our heartfelt support during this difficult time. We also join CD Tenerife and its fans in mourning, sharing this sorrow with them. You will always be one of us, Demi. Rest in peace.'

Matthias throwing his players under the bus is a dangerous game. Newcastle have no identity, no direction and no patterns - and that's on the manager, writes CRAIG HOPE

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 05:50 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 11:58 EDT, 3 November 2026

There often comes a point in a manager’s reign when the blame for defeat shifts from the dugout to the dressing room, and it is invariably the point of no return. Rarely does it arrive after just nine matches.

So when Matthias Jaissle took aim at his players in the wake of Newcastle’s 1-0 defeat by Everton on Monday, it was arguably a more concerning display than the 90 minutes which had preceded it, as alarming as those had been.

Jaissle’s post-match reflections were not inaccurate. This was not a manager talking nonsense. He fronted up and gave what felt like honest answers.

The 38-year-old is bright and articulate and, with each defeat, has shown an increasing humility that was less visible during the optimism of his introductory days.

To show the players the underside of the bus so soon, however, is as baffling a tactic as the strategy enacted on the pitch, even if Jaissle insists that performance had no resemblance to what he had asked of his team.

Rather, says the German, he had wanted his players to keep the ball on the floor and get the wingers in one-on-one situations high up the pitch. What he saw was a collection of long balls and a heading-practice session for Everton’s centre backs. The wingers, meanwhile, were spectators.

When a manager blames the players for defeat - as Matthias Jaissle did after Newcastle lost to Everton on Monday night - it is often the beginning of the end for the man in the dugout

Newcastle winger Harvey Barnes struggled to get on the ball during the defeat by Everton

‘The plan was not to shoot the long balls,’ he said.

Nor was the plan to shoot at all, it seemed. It took until the 85th minute for Newcastle to trouble Jordan Pickford, and even then the goalkeeper wasn’t overly concerned.

Short ball? Long ball? Whatever pass Newcastle pick, it is invariably the wrong ball. They are a team without identity – no patterns, no partnerships and, certainly, no sign of progress. So while there is some sympathy with Jaissle if players are failing to follow his instructions, it always comes back on any manager to make sure they do.

I once wrote of Everton boss David Moyes, when in charge of Sunderland, that he had inherited a sinking ship, only to drill another hole. Moyes, for the record, is a very good manager, he just wasn’t at Sunderland.

And that is the danger with Jaissle. He, too, walked into a difficult situation post Eddie Howe and the loss of several star players. Their replacements have been bench-warners or cold starters.

But Jaissle has to be the solution, not another problem. At present, the fear is that a young manager with no major-league experience is held hostage by his ideals and refuses to bend to what his players want and need.

His continued use of a 4-2-3-1 system, with two midfielders who cannot really run and a revolving cast of No 10s who are not No 10s, feels with each passing week and each passing defeat like a blueprint for relegation.

Maybe the team ignoring their manager’s wishes on Monday night was a cry for change. A protest, even? Do they not believe in his methods or do they not understand them?

Either way, Newcastle must try something different. The Everton loss, and the nature of a performance absent of direction or conviction, was not a surprise. This run of form – four defeats in five in all competitions – has been coming since the early weeks of the season, when results flattered performances and the underlying numbers told a more troubling story.

It is not the time to change manager, and nor do the club’s hierarchy think so. The change has to come from within – approach, attitude, execution. That is on Jaissle and the players, not one or the other.

That is why before the head coach addresses what happened on the pitch, he must clarify to his players what he said off it. To lose matches is one thing, but to risk losing your dressing room is quite another.

That would leave Jaissle facing the one situation from which his ‘vertical football’ could offer no way forward.
