Messi's defending champions Miami knocked out of MLS Cup playoffs

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Columbus Crew, with an injury-time goal from Santiago Rodriguez, beat Lionel Messi's Inter Miami 1-0 to knock the defending champions out of the first round of the MLS Cup playoffs on Friday.

Rodriguez's goal in the second minute of stoppage time gave Columbus a 2-0 triumph in the best-of-three first-round series, after their stunning 3-0 victory in game one in Florida.

Defending the MLS Cup was the last chance the ambitious franchise co-owned by David Beckham had to salvage its season after falling in the round of 16 of the CONCACAF Champions Cup and in the group stage of the Leagues Cup.

The only trophy that made it into Miami's cabinet this year was the Campeones Cup, the annual matchup between the champions of MLS and the Mexican league.

For Argentine superstar Messi, MLS's top scorer in the regular season and the favorite to win an unprecedented third straight Most Valuable Player award, it was a second first-round exit in the three MLS Cup playoffs he has contested.

In 2024 Miami stumbled against Atlanta United before earning their first MLS Cup crown last year.

The star-studded side featuring Luis Suarez, Casemiro and Rodrigo De Paul as well as Messi, had chances to score on a cold, rainy night in Ohio.

German Berterame had a golden opportunity six minutes from full time, but his header from point-blank range went over the crossbar.

"For me, the defeat is totally unfair because I think the team rose to the occasion, we deserved to win it throughout the whole match," said Miami coach Cristian "Kily" Gonzalez. "The clearest chances were ours, but that's football.

"The opponents defended well, too. Even though Leo didn't have a shot on target, we created a lot of chances."

The match appeared headed for a penalty shootout with injury time ticking away. But Inter's defense allowed Rodriguez to bring down a cross in the box from Moroccan Anass Zaroury and blast it past Canadian goalkeeper Dayne St. Clair.

Miami's early exit extends the champion's curse in MLS. No team has retained the title since the Los Angeles Galaxy in 2012.

Gonzalez shook up his starting lineup, leaving Suarez on the bench in favor of young Brazilian Riquelme Fillipi in a change that gave Miami's attack a spark in the first half.

But Messi made an uncharacteristic error in the box after a pinpoint pass from Berterame and Columbus keeper Patrick Schulte came up with a series of saves.

Columbus came close to scoring in the 65th when St. Clair saved a header from Gonzalo Tapia and Miami's Micael blocked the quick follow-up from Brais Mendez.

Gonzalez inserted Suarez with 15 minutes remaining, but Miami still couldn't capitalize on their chances. A Casemiro header was disallowed for offside and Berterame missed a header.

Egor Demin’s standout outing leaves Nets wanting more

📅 Nov 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

TORONTO — To say the Nets have had a disappointing start to the season would be an understatement.

But on Thanksgiving, the Nets can at least be happy about struggling Egor Dëmin having a bounce-back performance. Probably the best outing of his heretofore underwhelming campaign.

"The guys off the bench, Egor, Noah [Clowney], they were amazing," coach Jordi Fernández said after the Nets' slump-busting victory in Milwaukee.

The Nets snapped their five-game skid that had been tied for the worst in the NBA. And Dëmin showed promising signs of snapping out of his own not-unrelated malaise, coming into Friday's game in Toronto.

Perhaps most auspicious was the fact that it wasn't just a hot shooting showing, but an all-around night.

Part of Dëmin's allure as the eighth overall pick was his size, a 6-foot-9 guard who could not just space the floor but rebound and use his wingspan on defense.

That's happened all too infrequently so far. But it did Wednesday against the Bucks.

Dëmin finished with 15 points, four assists, three rebounds and a game-high plus-15. And despite being 0-for-5 from deep, Dëmin — who has struggled horribly inside the arc — was 5-for-6 from 2-point range. He even added feisty defense.

For a player too often faced with the question of "What can he do but shoot?" this was the answer. One that has to get repeated, starting against the Raptors.

Egor Dëmin, who scored 15 points in the Nets' win over the Bucks, drives past Ryan Rollins during the victory. AP

"Yes, he went back to the simple things and started with this physicality on defense," Fernández said. "They felt him the whole game, his length. Then he can rebound. He can be in the gaps. And then offensively, just simple.

"And I know he's capable of [hitting] every shot. Every 3 that he takes, I think it's going in. He was 0-for-5, but guess what? I think all his shots were great. So that's the way I want him to play: with energy, touching the paint, chasing the ball with his teammates, four assists. Proud of him, for sure."

Three games ago, Fernández had pulled Dëmin from the starting lineup for the first time since November of his rookie campaign.

Egor Dëmin rises up for a layup during the Nets' win over the Bucks in Milwaukee. Jeff Hanisch-Imagn Images

Dëmin went into Milwaukee having averaged 11.6 points on just 32.2 percent shooting over his prior five games.

And his woes were the worst on the other end of the court, posting a horrid 137.9 defensive rating during that skid. Dëmin was an aggregate minus-57, with Fernández admitting the Russian needed to be better in terms of his physicality.

Dëmin acknowledged a need to improve and did so against the Bucks. His defensive engagement helped spark a game-changing third-quarter spurt and helped the Nets run — his blown dunk notwithstanding.

"Before anything else, I've got to be present mentally for this team, especially because I'm trying to step in front as a leader role, and help Julius [Randle] out from that standpoint — from that kind of perspective — and help Mike [Porter Jr.] and older guys out with the leadership role," Dëmin said.

"So first off, I've got to be in my own mind out there present, and enjoy the game. And for me, it all came from the defensive side of the game. I was locked in with that, and everything else came to me naturally. Because we were getting stops and getting in transition — not counting that missed dunk — but we were getting wide-open opportunities that we were able to execute."

Dëmin had a block and a steal in the same game for the first time this season. He had three deflections and got in passing lanes.

And when Dëmin checked in with 5:16 left in the third and the Nets down 65-60, he helped ignite a 13-2 run. He had five points and two assists in just 1 ¹/₂ minutes, giving them the lead for good.

"Everybody was able to just put their minds together and not just focus on team defense but their own island, how we say 'guard your yard,' " Dëmin said. "That's where it came from."

The next goal is for Dëmin and the Nets not to lose it before Friday's game in Toronto.

It’s a Shaolin Thanksgiving! Wu-Tang Clan, Chad Smith Light Up Halftime at Detroit Lions NFL Game

📅 Nov 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The hip-hop legends hit a nearly seven-minute tag-team medley of greatest hits that was announced as "the wrap-up" of its Wu-Tang Forever: The Final Chamber farewell tour.

Wu-Tang Clan created a Shaolin Thanksgiving experience in Detroit on Thursday afternoon (Nov. 26), joined by Red Hot Chili Peppers drummer — and homeboy — Chad Smith for a halftime performance at Ford Field for the Lions’ annual Thanksgiving game.

The collective — recently inducted into the Rock And Roll Hall of Fame — lit up the stadium with a nearly seven-minute tag-team medley of greatest hits that was announced as “the wrap-up” of its Wu-Tang Forever: The Final Chamber farewell tour.

Flanked by flags bearing the Wu-Tang and Lions logos, and with 500 fan volunteers moshing on the field in front of the stage and on its wings, the nine surviving MCs — plus Young Dirty Bastard in place of his late father Ol’ Dirty Bastard and DJ Mathematics — kicked off with “Triumph,” then rolled through bits of “4th Chamber,” “Da Mystery of Chessboxin’,” “Protect Ya Neck,” “Method Man,” “Shimmy Shimmy Ya,” “Ice Cream” and “C.R.E.A.M.” The RZA-led “Wu-Tang Clan Ain’t Nuthing ta F’ Wit” finished the set with the group members on the field amidst the fans, with smoke jets shooting up from the stage.

The Lions cheerleading squad also took part in the show, sporting T-shirts with a glittering Wu-Tang/Lions mash-up logo.

“Who’s got it better than me?” Smith — who participated in a 2025 remix of “Wu-Tang Clan Ain’t Nuthing Ta F’ Wit” — enthused to Billboard during rehearsals at the stadium on Wednesday. “This is the…Wu-Tang Clan. It’s the halftime show, the biggest game of the year, and the Lions. I’m in my home town, and I get to play drums at halftime with the legendary Wu-Tang — come on! I’m the queen of the ball. And I love those guys. I mean, as far as iconic ’90s hip-hop goes, there’s Nas, maybe, but I love Wu-Tang.”

The Wu-Tang booking was also a personal pleasure for Lions general manager Brad Holmes, an unabashed fanboy who attended the rehearsal as well as the game.

“I would put myself in the die-hard category,” said Holmes, who became a Wu-Tang fan while growing up in Tampa, Fla., and even met U-God after “stalking” the troupe at its hotel before a concert there. “Their music, it really just hit my soul. It’s truly art; I didn’t know how much art it really was, and as I’ve gotten older, the more I listen to it, it just gets even better. When they said Wu-Tang Clan on Thanksgiving, I just” (makes gesture of head exploding).

The Detroit Thanksgiving show — one of three on Thursday, plus U2’s performance on Wednesday night in Los Angeles — was the second of a three-year deal between the Lions with Eminem and his manager and Shady Records President Paul Rosenberg. (Jesse Collins Entertainment stages the show.) Last year’s inaugural installment set a high mark, with Eminem joining fellow home town hero Jack White on stage before a TV audience of nearly 48 million, and Rosenberg said they knew there was a lot to live up to when they began planning this year’s halftime show during spring.

“Last year, with the exception of (Eminem) participating and rapping, it was a rock ‘n’ roll show, so we decided that we should probably move towards our roots this year and do something more hip-hop based,” Rosenberg explained. “So we put a short list together, and Wu-Tang was at the top of our list.”

The fact that, last year, White had just been inducted into the Rock Hall and Wu-Tang Clan is a new inductee this year was “coincidental,” according to Rosenberg. But it did factor into this year’s determination.

“I don’t think it hurts,” he said, “because it’s the right timing, and a good thing for them for their profile. We knew they were going to be inducted, so the timing worked out well.” And like Holmes, Rosenberg added, Wu-Tang has personal importance for both he and Eminem.

“Back in the days when I was in law school in Detroit, going to the Hip Hop Shop every Saturday, the Wu-Tang Clan was a bit part of the soundtrack and fabric of what everybody was into and the scene was gravitated towards,” Rosenberg recalled. “So our fandom is certainly something we are very tapped into, and we don’t shy away from that when making these decisions.”

Adding the Chili Peppers’ Smith to the performance, meanwhile, was an intentional nod to their shared home town. “We always want to include something tied to the city if we can,” Rosenberg said, “and Chad’s a big Lions fan and a Michigander, and he was super excited to jump on board.” Smith, who’s participated in Lions festivities in the past, added that he felt a particularly strong energy around the game, an NFC North rival Chicago Bears, with the teams tied for the division lead with 8-2 records.

“I can just feel it, y’know?” he said. “Everybody’s got the spirit. When a Detroit sports team is doing well, the city embraces it. We love our sports, we love our music. It affects the whole community, the vibe and the mood and everything. It feels really good.”

Actor Tim Allen, a regular attendee at the Thanksgiving game, has his own fresh connection to Wu-Tang during the second season of his ABC show Shifting Gears. “We just finished taping, and Chill (Daryl Mitchell) and I dance to a Wu-Tang Clan song at the end,” Allen said on the sidelines before Thursday’s game. “I think he’s friends with them…so now all of a sudden I’m a big fan of theirs.”

Rosenberg said he and Eminem resisted the idea of having the rapper make another guest appearance, despite his regard for and friendship with Wu-Tang Clan. “The idea was not to have him perform every year at all,” he said. “That was not something we had discussed, and we’re not something we’re obligated to do.” Rosenberg added that he, Eminem and the Lions have not yet talked about extending the Thanksgiving halftime show production deal beyond 2027. “We’re certainly having a good time,” he said, “and we really enjoy the process. I think that people are digging it, so we’ll see.”

During the game the Lions also honored the late Marth Firestone Ford — the team’s principal owner from 2014-2020 (her husband William Clay Ford Sr. bought the team in 1963) with a moment of silence. Ford passed away on Tuesday at the age of 101.

Everybody, partnered on a line of T-shirts and hoodies, the latter of which were worn by  fans on the field during the halftime show. Sarri: ‘Atalanta cannot always recover with comebacks’ after another wild night

Maurizio Sarri warns Atalanta ‘cannot always expect to resolve games with these comebacks’ after a 3-2 away to Borac in the Conference League, while Gianluca Scamacca is ‘lacking confidence.’

La Dea really are turning into the most unpredictable team in Italy, as just days after an epic comeback from 4-2 down to hold Inter 4-4 in Serie A, they went even better in the Conference League.

Borac made the most of dismal defending to go 2-0 up in the first half-hour, while Atalanta wasted numerous scoring opportunities with Jonathan Rowe and Charles De Ketelaere literally missing open goals.

It was only after Borac went down to 10 men that they broke through with Nikola Krstovic, then as Sebastijan Herrera also saw red for hacking down Nicola Zalewski, the turnaround in stoppages from Sead Kolasinac and Davide Zappacosta.

“The truth is that we had a good performance and created many scoring opportunities, but unfortunately right now we have this defensive fragility that makes every match more complicated,” Sarri told Sky Sport Italia.

“If you look at the statistics, we scored three goals on four chances late on, but before that managed to miss two goals from a metre out. We made mistakes in our finishing and continue to have a few defensive problems too, so naturally it’s going to get tricky with those two elements.

“The team showed great character, as it has been doing quite a lot recently, so we have to keep working on the problems that are making our matches so complicated.”

Injuries ruled out Marco Carnesecchi, Odilon Kossounou and Isak Hien, while others are making their way back from long lay-offs.

“We’ve got to keep working, try to dig out some time to do work in training, which between injuries and the fixture list is tough, but we absolutely must find a way of working on the defence and attack. It’s about the movements and distribution, which certainly weren’t of the right level tonight, and it has been a repeated problem in recent matches.

“We cannot always expect to resolve games with these comebacks, we’ve got to stop them getting this complicated in the first place.”

Notably, Atalanta only managed to get on the scoresheet once Krstovic came off the bench to replace Gianluca Scamacca, while Jonathan Rowe was again misfiring.

“Before the season began, I’d have said Scamacca was perfectly suited to my style of football, because he is very physical and has technical quality to work with the team,” noted Sarri.

“The difference is that one striker right now is feeling very confident and optimistic, while the other is in a difficult momenta and slowly trying to rebuild. I have always said that the two forwards start on the same level, and I pick whoever is in the best form. Right now, Krstovic is doing better, so he plays more, but it could just be a moment in this season that will pass.”

There was tension before the game, with some Atalanta fans complaining that their buses were delayed crossing the border into Bosnia, then they were banned from visiting the city centre and corralled into the stadium by police.

“I want to thank our fans, who travelled a long way to be here and for 97 minutes gave us a big hand. Fortunately, we managed to repay them this evening,” added Sarri.

This result puts Atalanta mid-table in the Conference League, after two victories, a defeat away to Ajax and draw with Kairat Almaty.

MSU 20, Rutgers 14: The Spartans have not quit on the season despite their injuries, and neither have the Scarlet Knights – even after a shocking opening-week at home against Massachusetts. Luciano Spalletti admits Andrea Cambiaso’s stunning goal ‘got Juventus back on track’ for a 4-1 Europa League win over Omonia Nicosia, and Jeff Ekhator provides different solutions in attack.

It was a tough test for the Bianconeri, as Neto’s howler had allowed Omonia Nicosia to take the lead from an early corner with the Lubomir Satka header.

However, it had been turned around by half-time with a Cambiaso stunner and Ekhator’s first goal in the Juve jersey, with Francisco Conceicao and Jeremie Boga rounding out the scoreline in the second half.

“Until the Cambiaso goal, it looked a lot like the Venezia game,” confessed Spalletti with a smile on Sky Sport Italia.

“We were uncertain and the opponents took advantage, we felt the pressure of a potential counter-attack and everything started to feel complicated, but Cambiaso got us back on track with that wonderful goal, and from there the team played with greater calm.”

The jeers had been starting to rain down from the stands before that equaliser sparked the comeback, but Cambiaso badly needed it after recent struggles.

“It’s not easy to find form when called in, and some of these players hadn’t started for a while, so lacking consistency is difficult. However, we had some good moves, kept an acceptable level of pressing, and entertained the crowd with four fantastic goals.”

There were curlers from Boga and Cambiaso, albeit not quite the style of Juventus icon Alessandro Del Piero, who was in the Sky Sport Italia studio as a pundit.

“The Boga goal was my favourite because Gatti won it back with that high press, and sometimes the players are a little hesitant to go that far forward, as it leaves gaps behind them. Gatti then ran to follow the move and was in a position to score himself,” noted Spalletti.

“When you stop seeking something new, that is when you get pinned back. Ekhator didn’t get enough service in the first half, because at times we consolidate possession, and slow it down with too many touches, allowing the opponents to close up. It is particularly a problem when we have a striker like Ekhator who needs space to run into.

“He still managed to find the space himself two or three times, so we have to compliment Ekhator.”

I grew up climbing with my Dad, but it was always his sport. I enjoyed it, but pursued other sports. In a way, I don’t think I’ve found a climbing partner that was ever as dialed as he was. I remember being out climbing with him, when he would tell some story like, “We were out here in the ‘80s, blasting a boom box and climbing.” It turns out, he would be talking about a first ascent.

When Chris arrived in Yosemite in 1972, he brought a foundation of climbing experience from the Gunks of New York. In retirement, Chris continued to explore and enjoy the Sierras, while taking great pride in perfecting the art of being a grandfather. Chris also became an unofficial voice of Yosemite locals, keeping friends and former park residents connected to the place they loved by sharing updates and insights into the ever-changing landscape of Yosemite.

With an eye for the extraordinary as well as the everyday, he captured the moments, places, and details that might otherwise go unnoticed. Through his lens and storytelling, Chris shared his deep appreciation for Yosemite. In so doing, he preserved a piece of its story for others.

The 10 Most Overrated Rock Songs of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

There will never be a better genre of music than rock and roll. Rock is the basics, with a band of guitar players and a drummer singing songs they wrote themselves, free of anything synthetic. Rock is real, a genre free of restraints. The listener feels it all, from the energy of the beat, to the emotion behind the lyrics. Today, even as newer rock songs lose their hold on mainstream popularity, classics from the past continue to reign. Still, some rock classics get more credit than they deserve. While none of them are bad by any means, being overplayed, or simply being unworthy of their all-time greatness status, has made these 10 rock songs overrated.

Putting The Beatles on such a list is just wishing to be hated. Let's get one thing clear right up front. The Beatles are the best band of all-time. But this status has also made it so that every song they ever released is put on a pedestal, and not all of them deserve it. Among them is "Come Together." The first song from their iconic Abbey Road album, "Come Together" is written and sung by John Lennon. While it has a cool opening riff and hook, the song repeats itself with lyrics which make absolutely no sense. Gone is any emotion, replaced instead with lines like "He wear no shoeshine, he got toe-jam football." What?

This song refuses to die. Being overplayed over the decades has caused listeners to cringe any time Journey's "Don't Stop Believin'" comes on. The piano intro is pretty standard, and there's nothing all that impressive with basic lyrics like, "Just a small-town girl livin' in a lonely world." The beat feels like a throwaway song without much effort put into it, and the theme is so spot-on that it's more cheesy than anything else. Throw in how it now will forever make us immediately think about the unsatisfactory ending to The Sopranos, and this song is one that can please stop.

Cheap Trick's "I Want You to Want Me" has two versions. The studio version is completely lifeless and forgettable. The entire band sounds bored, and Robin Zander is twisting his voice in ways that don't work. The song only became a classic because of the live version. There, Cheap Trick comes to life, a band set free. Still, there's only so much you can do with such a dull original song. Zander's voice is much better here, and we won't deny how catchy it is, but a live version of a song no one wanted isn't worth the hype.

The Police are one of the greatest bands of the 1980s, and it can't be understated what Sting means to rock history. The band's final album, Synchronicity, is one of the best of the decade, and it's carried by the second single, "Every Breath You Take." The song lacks any real energy that The Police had on other big hits. What really makes it annoying is how the song was treated by the public. Despite being a song about a man's obsession with the woman who got away, so many listeners refuse to look at the deeper meaning, instead treating it like a love song worth getting married to. Come on, people!

Folk rocker Bob Dylan was the voice of America and the forgotten man and woman in the 1960s. What he means to music deserves to be celebrated. This doesn't mean that every song of his was some genius recording worthy of his reputation. "Lay, Lady, Lay" is an unusual one. Gone are the powerful political protest songs Dylan was known for. In its place is lyrical simplicity about either true love or an affair. And missing from it all is Dylan's well-known nasaly whine.

Many lists will recognize "Hotel California" by the Eagles as one of the greatest rock songs of all-time. And that's why it makes this list. This is an American classic, but it's also hyped beyond belief and has been overplayed to death. At six plus minutes, it's much too long. The lyrics, about the excess of fame and living it up in southern California, are a little too vague. As for the music itself, outside of the usual guitar solos, "Hotel California" seems stuck in first gear. It doesn't deserve the hate it gets, but this is not one of the best songs ever made.

Thanks to albums like The Joshua Tree and Achtung Baby, the Irish band U2 were one of the biggest and best bands of the late 1980s and early 1990s. Then they got experimental, and in the late 90s, as rock changed, U2 was left behind. The 2000 album All That You Can't Leave Behind was seen as a comeback return to form. It's carried by the first single, "Beautiful Day," a song that was everywhere to begin the 21st century. Although it's a nice song, it can't live up to their classics. Rather than being a challenging song, "Beautiful Day" is overly cute, the lyrics a far cry from what the band once was.

Green Day are one of the better modern bands still out there and going strong, but oh man, if you grew up in the late 90s like this writer, did it get old hearing "Good Riddance (Time of Your Life)." The punk pop power the group is known for is stripped away in favor of an overly sappy acoustic number. At just two-and-a-half minutes, the song doesn't overstay its welcome, but the millions who heard it weren't paying attention. Billie Joe Armstrong is saying goodbye to an ex, trying to be heartfelt while also saying good riddance. Yet, for years after, it was part of every damn high school and college graduation in the country.

"In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida" by Iron Butterfly is one of the most well-known rock one hit wonders. It's also a song that doesn't hit as hard the more you listen to it. Just the title itself, a mispronunciation of "In the garden of Eden" is annoying. That opening hook, with an organ leading into a sick guitar riff and drum beat, is admittedly badass, and lead singer Doug Ingle sounds a bit like Jim Morrison. But once you get past the first minute, this one starts to fall apart. It's the same riff and basic structure repeated over and over. If you have the patience, check out the whopping 17-minute album version.

In the late 1990s, as rock was trying to figure out what came next in the post-grunge era, along came Semisonic and their song "Closing Time." It's a catchy song from a decent band, but it's basic verse-chorus-verse style isn't anything original. If it stood on its own as a minor hit, this one might not be on the list. It's how it was received that makes it so overrated. Not only was it played on every Top 40 and rock radio channel non-stop, but good luck being at a bar at closing time in 1998 and the years after. Drunken sing-a-longs turned a fun little song into something rather annoying.

Few video game franchises wear their cinematic influences on their sleeve as proudly as the Grand Theft Auto franchise. As the most recent entry in the series, Grand Theft Auto 6, continues to break records, already pulling in billions of dollars in sales and receiving near-universal praise, many have begun to notice that the game's story shares plenty of similar tropes and narrative threads with some of Hollywood's biggest and best films. From the world of organized crime depicted in Goodfellas, the elaborate heists of Heat, or Scarface's violent confrontations, the games of Grand Theft Auto have always been influenced by movies, leading to some of the franchise's best moments. Scarface may be most closely associated with the franchise, inspiring much of Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, right up to the movie's climactic showdown in a drug dealer's mansion. But with Grand Theft Auto 6 returning to a new interpretation of Rockstar Games' fictional Miami, it's another crime thriller that feels particularly relevant.

Directed by Tony Scott and written by Quentin Tarantino, 1993's True Romance is a wildly entertaining combination of crime drama, action thriller, and unconventional love story. Starring Christian Slater and Patricia Arquette, the film follows the two young lovers as they find themselves caught up in the world of organized crime, after their relationship sets them on a collision course with gangsters and drug dealers. Given that Grand Theft Auto 6's story is also centered on a couple attempting to navigate the criminal underworld while also becoming criminals themselves, it makes True Romance a perfect companion piece for any fans of the game.

Clarence Worley (Slater), a comic book store employee in Detroit, and Alabama Whitman (Arquette), a call girl hired by his boss, quickly fall in love. Both of their lives forever change when Clarence stands up to Alabama's former pimp, a confrontation in which he unexpectedly acquires a suitcase filled with drugs. As they head to Los Angeles to start a new life after selling the drugs, embarking on a chaotic adventure involving mobsters, Hollywood filmmakers, and police, the film follows the sort of storyline that would feel right at home in the world of Grand Theft Auto.

Christian Slater and Patricia Arquette starred in the cult classic as Clarence and Alabama, respectively.

Similar to True Romance, Grand Theft Auto 6 has a similar plot as it follows Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal couple caught up in a world that is as ruthless as it is rewarding. It's easy to see how True Romance makes for such an appropriate comparison to the game, as Jason and Lucia share many of the same motivations as Clarence and Alabama: They want a better life together, and their willingness to break the law in the pursuit of that dream makes them all particularly compelling, albeit reckless, protagonists.

One of the defining characteristics of the Grand Theft Auto series is its ability to balance dramatic storytelling with humor and outrageous, over-the-top violence. Rockstar's fictional worlds are populated by eccentric personalities, dangerous foes, and people whose egos ultimately lead to their demise. "Pride comes before the fall" is a recurring theme of the Grand Theft Auto franchise, and it's a trope that few writers understand better than Tarantino. So it's only natural that a movie written by him would share such similarities with the GTA series.

While Tarantino didn't direct True Romance, his fingerprints are all over the movie, which is full of unorthodox dialogue, colorful characters, and sudden violence that make it remarkably compatible with Rockstar's style of storytelling. His writing finds a perfect balance with the direction of Tony Scott, as it gives the film the momentum of a blockbuster action thriller similar to the director's classic Top Gun. The movie has the ability to transition from heartfelt dialogue into dark comedy and explosive violence without losing its identity as an atypical love story. It's a tonal balancing act that requires all the strengths of the director and the writer, a similar problem that Grand Theft Auto deals with masterfully.

More than three decades after its release, True Romance remains one of the best crime thrillers of the 1990s. Its combination of memorable characters, dark humor, action, and romance makes it one of the best entries in Tarantino's screenwriting filmography. For fans of Grand Theft Auto 6 fans, it's also a reminder that the most compelling crime stories aren't always about money or power. Sometimes they're about finding the right person who you're willing to risk everything for.

3 Bengals legends still alive in Pro Football Hall of Fame race for Class of 2027

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

It's that time of year again, Cincinnati Bengals fans. The turkey's thawing, the Bengals are in the thick of the stretch run, and we're once again refreshing our feeds to see if Canton is finally ready to give Cincinnati its due.

On Tuesday, the Pro Football Hall of Fame trimmed its Class of 2027 player pool down to 52 names, and three familiar stripes are still on the board: quarterback Ken Anderson, offensive tackle Willie Anderson, and defensive tackle Geno Atkins.

If you're wondering why Ken Anderson's name is showing up on the same list as Ben Roethlisberger and Rob Gronkowski, it's because this is a whole new ballgame. Back in September, the Hall announced the biggest overhaul of its selection process in its history.

The separate seniors category is gone, so every eligible player, no matter when he retired, is now in one pool. The selection committee was cut roughly in half, to 25 voters plus three non-voting participants. And the final ballot now has room for only 13 players, joining one coach and one contributor. Those two spots already belong to Bill Belichick and Robert Kraft, by the way.

The 52 will be cut to 25 in about two weeks. The final 13 will then go into the room ahead of Super Bowl LXI, with the class revealed at NFL Honors in February.

For the Bengals, the stakes are pretty simple. This franchise still has just two career players enshrined in Canton: Anthony Muñoz and Ken Riley. That's the fewest in the league. Let's look at the three men trying to change that.

The résumé hasn't changed, and it never needed to. Anderson spent all 16 of his NFL seasons in Cincinnati. He won four NFL passing titles, was the 1975 NFL Man of the Year, and took home league MVP honors in 1981 while leading the Bengals to their first Super Bowl. He's still the franchise's all-time leader in passing yards, and he remains the only eligible quarterback with four passing titles who isn't in the Hall.

The path to Canton has been a long, winding, and frankly maddening one. Anderson was a finalist back in 1996 and 1998, then fell into the seniors pool, where he reached the semifinals four straight years. Last cycle, he finally broke through as one of three senior finalists for the Class of 2026, only to come up short in a year when voters were also weighing Belichick and Kraft.

Now, with the seniors category scrapped, Anderson doesn't have to wait for a small committee to pick him. But he also has to beat out every modern-era candidate on the list for one of those 13 spots. There's real momentum behind him, though. Even Joe Montana, the guy who beat him in Super Bowl XVI, has publicly said Anderson's call is long overdue.

"Big Willie" was the anchor of the Bengals' offensive line for 12 seasons (1996-2007) before a final year in Baltimore. He made 184 career starts and earned three first-team All-Pro nods, and he did it at right tackle, at a time when that position rarely got that kind of recognition. He was one of the most dominant run-blockers of his era, and he did it for a lot of bad Bengals teams.

The frustrating part is that Willie has been about as close as you can get without walking through the door. He's been a finalist five years in a row and made the final seven for the Class of 2026. Under the old bylaws, that would have made him an automatic finalist for 2027. The new rules wiped that safety net away, so he has to earn his way back to the final 13 from scratch, alongside a loaded crop of first-time eligibles.

If there's any silver lining, it's that voters clearly already know his case. Getting back into the room is the hurdle. Getting the votes once he's there feels like it's been a matter of when, not if.

This is the one that should get Bengals fans excited, because it's new.

Atkins was a fourth-round pick (No. 120 overall) out of Georgia in the 2010 NFL Draft, and he turned into one of the best interior pass rushers of his generation. Over 11 seasons, all in Cincinnati, he piled up 75.5 sacks from the defensive tackle spot. He made eight Pro Bowls, earned two first-team All-Pro selections, and was named to the Hall of Fame's All-Decade Team of the 2010s. That last one matters, because All-Decade nods tend to carry real weight with voters.

Along with Aaron Donald, Fletcher Cox, and Gerald McCoy in a similar era, Atkins helped redefine the interior defensive line position and its importance to franchise success.

At his peak, Atkins was a nightmare. He was undersized by traditional standards, but his first step and leverage made him nearly unblockable from the 3-technique. He posted a monster 12.5-sack season in 2012 before a torn ACL in 2013 derailed things for a stretch, and he came roaring back to re-establish himself as one of the league's most disruptive interior linemen through the back half of the decade. For a good chunk of the 2010s, Geno and Aaron Donald were the conversation at defensive tackle.

Atkins last played in 2020, which made him eligible for the first time with the Class of 2026. Eligible and noticed aren't the same thing, though. Last year he didn't make the Hall's initial cut to 52, let alone the semifinals. Plenty of folks, including longtime voter Rick Gosselin, have pointed to the lack of postseason success and a ring as the biggest knock on his case.
