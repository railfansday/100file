Kobe Bryant No. 8 Lakers jersey fetches huge money at auction

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Mamba Mentality continues to draw attention.

A game-worn No. 8 Kobe Bryant jersey from the 2003-2004 season sold for $1.83 million at The Realest on Sunday, Nov. 8. This is the highest sale of a non-rookie jersey from the Los Angeles Lakers star before he switched to No. 24. It also lands among the top-five highest public auction sales of Bryant's NBA jerseys.

The jersey is autographed and was certified by The Realest's authentication program, which utilizes photo-matching. Its only previous owner purchased the jersey with a certificate of authenticity directly from the Lakers via a charity auction in 2017 before the NBA icon passed away.

The jersey is believed to have been worn in 17 games that season – with a record of 16-1, including 8-0 in the playoffs – when the five-time champion starred alongside Shaquille O'Neal. Bryant wore it for his 31-point, 10-assist double-double in Game 5 of the first round to eliminate the Houston Rockets, for his 42-point performance in Game 4 of the conference semifinals against the San Antonio Spurs and in Game 6 of the next series against the top-seeded Minnesota Timberwolves to win the Western Conference finals.

The jersey has an NBA Finals patch and is the only No. 8 jersey of Bryant's to come to auction with such a detail. It could not be proven if Bryant wore it the NBA Finals, where the Lakers lost to the Detroit Pistons in five games.

The highest-selling Bryant jersey was the one from his NBA debut in 1996. It sold in April of 2025 for $7 million.

Bryant played 20 seasons in the NBA all with the Lakers. He was drafted No. 13 overall in the 1996 NBA Draft by the Charlotte Hornets out of Lower Merion High School in Philadelphia and was traded to Los Angeles that day. He won five NBA championships with the Lakers, was named Finals MVP for two of the titles and was named an All-Star 18 times.

Bryant died in a helicopter crash in January of 2020 at the age of 41 with his 13-year-old daughter, Gianna.

Last month, The Realest auctioned off a Shohei Ohtani jersey from the 2025 Tokyo Series for $2,055,700. It was the most expensive jersey worn by a baseball player in the 21st century.

Gotham FC head coach apologizes for poor pitch quality at Sports Illustrated Stadium after NWSL quarterfinal win over Seattle Reign

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Gotham FC's NWSL quarterfinal win over the Seattle Reign was colored by glaring issues with the field quality — with the team set to host next week's semifinal on the same surface.

Throughout Gotham's 1-0 win on Sunday, there were clear issues with the pitch at Sports Illustrated Stadium, where Gotham has played since 2020. Large sections of grass were worn out, with sand visible in the corners and in front of the goals.

After the loss, Reign head coach Laura Harvey did not hold back when speaking about the field quality on the broadcast. Harvey said that the league had fined Gotham $10,000 for the field's poor quality during the team's regular-season finale last week, with multiple outlets later confirming the fine.

"Let's be real, there's no way a playoff game should be played on that s***," Harvey said. "I just can't believe they've allowed it. They knew it last week."

Postgame, Gotham head coach Juan Carlos Amorós said in a press conference that the team did its best to get the field to improve the pitch quality, but that it had fallen short of acceptable expectations.

"I feel the obligation to almost apologize for the state of the pitch. I think at this level, in this kind of game, as a league we should be having a better surface," Amorós said. "(Gotham) did absolutely everything in their power to make sure that that was the case. Unfortuantely, it wasn't. But luckily it looks like nobody got hurt."

Reign goalkeeper Claudia Dickey makes a save during the match, on a visibly degraded field in Sports Illustrated Stadium. (Mandatory Credit: Brad Penner-Imagn Images)

Players from both teams also expressed their dissatisfaction with the pitch quality. Gotham defender Jess Carter said postgame the field "was so bad," while Reign midfielder Jess Fishlock called the pitch "atrocious," via ESPN.

"It's pretty sandy out there and a little unstable," Gotham midfielder Jaelin Howell said, via ESPN. "I want to be able to give my all to the team, and everything I've got, so obviously, you can't think about that. The other team has to play on that, too."

Gotham, like many NWSL teams, is a tenant in the stadium and does not control operations in its home venue; that duty goes to MLS team New York Red Bulls. Sports Illustrated Stadium hosted a Red Bulls game on Saturday and a rugby match last week, which only degraded the pitch quality after last weekend's fine.

Gotham, as the No. 1 overall seed, will host a semifinal at Sports Illustrated Stadium next weekend, meaning another high-stakes game played on the poor pitch. The Red Bulls, who failed to make the MLS playoffs, will be playing a friend against Club América the day before.

Gotham announced this summer that it will be moving to the new Etihad Park in Queens in 2028; the stadium, which is set to open next year, will be owned and operated by New York City FC.

NWSL pitch quality has come up as an issue in the past, particularly with shared venues like San Diego Wave's Snapdragon Stadium. The field quality at Snapdragon was heavily criticized ahead of the 2023 NWSL Final, with retiring Reign star Megan Rapinoe suffering an Achilles injury during the match in what was her final professional game. In 2024, the Wave relocated its final game of the season due to the degraded field.

On Sunday, Gotham ended up with the win after forcing an early Reign own goal, managing to get the win without a single shot on target.

"Obviously, it was not the best football match you will ever see, that's for sure. It's not the best football match that we've ever played, that's for sure. But we found a way to win," Amorós said postgame.

Gotham will host the No. 4-seeded Kansas City Current in the semis, on the way to defending their 2025 NWSL championship.

Diamonds are forever - phoenix club seek FA Cup upset

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The notion of your football club ceasing to exist is unthinkable for many fans, but for supporters of Rushden & Diamonds it became a sad reality in 2011.

The Northamptonshire-based side, backed by the late Max Griggs, rocketed through non-league in the 1990s and in 2003 reached the third tier of English football before financial issues after he stepped away ultimately saw them fold.

A community lost its focal point, and within years their Nene Park stadium had been razed to the ground.

"It was heartbreaking - we knew we'd been robbed." reflected Rob Usher, the chairman of phoenix side AFC Rushden & Diamonds, who will make their FA Cup first-round debut this weekend.

"I think it was that anger that probably fuelled the fans saying 'no, we don't accept it. We're not gone, we're not done and we want our club back."

Some 2,000 travelling fans will make their way to see the now eighth-tier Diamonds take on League One side Burton Albion on Saturday - a bittersweet reminder of days the supporters of the original club could have once taken for granted.

Usher, like many of those involved with the Northern Premier League Division One Midlands side, has followed both incarnations of the club - firstly as a fan, but also as a groundsman and turnstile operator before becoming chairman in 2022.

"My first game was an FA Cup tie against Shrewsbury Town in 1998. It was the year we ended up playing Leeds United in the third round." he said.

"Rushden & Diamonds gave us some great times. Winning leagues, the Duane Darby's, the Onandi Lowe's of our past. But I tell everyone I've never traded for what we have with AFC Rushden & Diamonds.

"This is a community, this is an army of people who turn up and give their time and their money, their care, their devotion... not because they want the club to exist, but because they want the club to thrive. That's why it's so special".

Diamonds chairman Rob Usher said the club was at the heart of its community [James Burridge/BBC]

Diamonds, who now play their home games at Hayden Road, started their journey to the first round proper with a 5-0 win at Kimberley Miners Welfare in the extra preliminary round on 8 August.

They have also beaten Long Eaton United, Desborough Town, Peterborough Sports, Braintree Town and Morecambe to reach this stage.

Along with Stafford Rangers, who host Grimsby Town on Sunday, they are the only side left in the competition to have played from the earliest round and have so far earned £23,194 from the FA's prize fund.

The winners of the tie at Burton's Pirelli Stadium will collect £47,750, with the losers taking £15,800.

Joining AFC Rushden & Diamonds' attempt to make more history will be Billy Sharp, the Championship's all-time record goalscorer, who has reversed his recent decision to retire.

The 40-year-old, best known for his spells with Sheffield United and Doncaster Rovers, scored his first nine professional goals while on loan from the Blades to the original Diamonds in 2005.

"They [Rushden & Diamonds] made me as a player," said Sharp, who will be targeting a landmark 300th goal while filling in for forward James Clifton who is serving a suspension for accumulated yellow cards.

"I've stayed in contact with the club over last few years, I should've been here for a legends game and I couldn't make it. But I always look out for their results.

"Elliot [Sandy, the club's manager] asked me if I'd consider playing, I said let's see what happens v Morecambe. Within 20 minutes of the result he text me an eyes emoji.

"I just smiled and said give me a day or two. It just seemed right. The opportunity to score another goal, to play another game in the FA Cup. It's special. This club means a lot to me, it gave me my chance when I was a young kid. Hopefully we can cause a shock, if we get through to the next round James [Clifton] can get his chance."

Sam Brown has played 350 games for AFC Rushden & Diamonds and supported the original club [James Burridge/BBC]

Defender Sam Brown, who grew up watching Rushden & Diamonds on the terraces at Nene Park, remembers chanting Sharp's name and expects it to feel "weird" to now play alongside him.

Brown was involved in the phoenix club's inaugural season and has made 350 appearances across two spells.

"It's been a rollercoaster," he said. "It's a big journey and I'm glad to have been a part of it.

"The club is owned by the fans so it needs all the fans to pull together to be able to grow. They turn up every Saturday no matter where we are and it's nice to have a chance for them to enjoy a different kind of environment on Saturday."

This is AFC Rushden & Diamonds' first appearance in the FA Cup proper. Their predecessors, Rushden & Diamonds, last featured in 2010-11, losing 3-0 to Yeovil Town in the first round [Shaun Frankham]

While the travelling supporters will hope to see an upset against Burton, for many, the experience of bringing their community together for such an occasion will provide the most lasting memories.

Diamonds say they have put on six coaches "at cost price" to help transport fans to the game in east Staffordshire.

Many are part owners and volunteers, including husband and wife Teresa and David Hales who help look after the club's kit.

"It's brilliant, absolutely brilliant." said Teresa. "For a little club like us it's absolutely amazing. I'm just so proud of everybody. Players, volunteers, everybody.

"There have been times over the years where I've sat with my head in my hands thinking, 'oh God, what's going to happen next?' But we're on the way up. We're really on the way up."

Teresa (left) and David (centre) Hales are part owners of the club and volunteer as kit managers [James Burridge/BBC]

"It would be the shock of shocks," said club chairman Usher. "I've seen this squad this year pull off unbelievable things when the odds are against us, so I don't count us out.

"I've seen this club come back from death quite literally in 2011 and once again four years ago [when the phoenix club faced financial troubles].

"Diamonds are never done. Diamonds are forever."

Nene Park, the home of the original Rushden & Diamonds, was demolished in 2017 [Shutterstock]

Rushden & Diamonds were formed in 1992 following the merger of Rushden Town and Irthlingborough Diamonds.

The merger was the brainchild of Max Griggs, the owner of Northamptonshire-based Dr. Martens, with the aim of getting the single, larger club into the Football League.

The club reached the Conference, now known as the National League, in 1996 and realised Griggs' dream of EFL football in 2001 under the management of Brian Talbot.

In 2003, Diamonds were promoted as champions to Division Two, now known as League One, but Griggs' retirement as chairman signalled the beginning of their ultimate demise.

By 2006 they had been relegated back to non-league and mounting financial pressures saw them kicked out the Conference in 2011 before ultimately folding.

Rivals Kettering Town moved into the club's Nene Park stadium at Irthlingborough for a brief and ill-fated period between 2011 and 2012, before the ground was demolished in 2017.

It was the kind of thinking that used to govern most of football. A few years ago – although it could really have been many examples from the last 50 – a promising manager was being pursued by one of the game’s wealthiest clubs. He had built up a fine team with an upwardly mobile club, and it looked like they could be on the brink of something special… but that one thought wouldn’t go away.

“These opportunities don’t always come along, so you have to seize them.”

The manager ended up taking it, but not quite seizing it. He didn’t perform to the level required, albeit amid a lot of debate around the circumstances he walked into. His career has never reached the same heights, but the key point is that its trajectory was, in that moment of acceptance, drastically changed.

The old thinking was wrong. That job just wasn’t right for him.

He’s not the only one. There are a few examples, from Graham Potter to Julen Lopetegui, and there’s much more to it than just “backing yourself”.

Unai Emery is at a very different point of his own career, having experienced historic Europa League success  – with three different clubs – over 22 years, but it’s impossible not to wonder whether any of this weighed on his own thinking as he now discusses a new deal with Aston Villa.

The Basque will be all too aware of the noise around Manchester United’s interest, and the latest developments – including Villa’s very ‘LinkedIn’ post about "long-term strategic direction" – all felt very pointed ahead of Sunday’s trip to Old Trafford. Many in football even believe that one reason his club resorted to the absurdity of last week’s legal threat against Jamie Carragher was aggravation about their manager consistently being linked to “a bigger club”.

This, it should be acknowledged, is still Manchester United. It’s Sir Alex Ferguson, Sir Matt Busby and his babes, and so much glory and legacy. Despite all the current problems at Old Trafford, there is a history and consequently inspiration for the future; of what you might be able to do with that.

Others in football believe Emery might previously have been open to joining such a club, primarily because of the will to finally win a major league and the Champions League.

That isn’t to say either are indefinitely impossible at Villa given the club’s rapid recent growth, but their own fans have been frustrated over regulatory constraints.

And yet such strands lead into the nub of this, where this new thinking is warranted.

Emery’s record above Europa League level might raise considerable questions over whether he would suit a club of United’s scale, and those at Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain have long insisted his outlook didn’t fit.

Far more relevant, however, might be that United just don’t suit Emery.

There are numerous persuasive arguments as to why he should absolutely sign that contract and stay at Villa.

None of them are to deepen these debates about club size, or even Emery’s own career.

It’s more that the 55-year-old is in the very rare modern position of having earned an almost ideal job.

At Villa, everything is built around him, in a way you just don’t really see at this level any more. The only comparison in the Premier League or any of Europe’s wealthiest clubs is at Arsenal, where Mikel Arteta was only given such authority after initially proving himself. Emery has almost complete control, and those circumstances have ensured everything is in tune. The work of a very good manager is consequently greatly amplified, so that Villa consistently over-perform.

That can even be seen in how they’ve responded to the great frustration of the summer. They’re already in far better shape than would have been expected. Emery might have had his Europa League winners ripped up, but it is already impressive how he has built a coherent unit. Sure, there are still issues and gaps that are difficult to fill amid constraints, but there’s a bite there. This is personified in Nicolas Jackson.

And, from that, and a relatively volatile Premier League, there is a real opportunity for top five again. That can bring further growth… but maybe more? He’s already had more at Villa than most managers will ever enjoy, since he is an adored club legend.

By contrast, links to United have been met with scepticism by tranches of the Old Trafford support. They are also fully aware that the actual problems are way above the manager. United are unlikely to give Emery the keys to the club, in the way Villa have done. Sir Jim Ratcliffe has already been insistent on building a modern sporting director culture, even if this iteration has so far failed.

Worse, a U-turn on that, to make someone like Emery manager, would actually just look even more indicative of dysfunction; that they’re going from extreme to extreme again. And that’s even before you get to the amount of rot that still needs to be cleared at Old Trafford. United are not yet close to being a club who just need a coach coming in and imposing his influence to get them winning. They require a patient rebuild, but the wait can derail coaches.

Put bluntly, if this comes down to a chance of winning the league or Champions League, there’s an argument that Emery specifically has a better opportunity at Villa, certainly in the medium-term. There would be so much more work to do at Old Trafford, with a lot more risk.

Maybe most pertinently, he would be giving up a very good thing, a very rare thing, that has the potential for so much more. And it’s that context that is also so key to such debates. It’s not just that the thinking has started to change. So has the reality. With more and more clubs looking to head coaches in wider infrastructures, the jobs are much more transitory. Such opportunities actually come along more frequently, but they’re far more contoured than before.

Better-run clubs than United are now geared in such a way that they only suit certain profiles, or certain ideologies. Tactical shifts can take time, and be painful, as currently being seen at Newcastle United. On Premier League pitches, the game is so systemised and optimised that the margins have become very thin. Hence wild swings in results when clubs aren’t in tune.

At Villa, it’s almost note-perfect for Emery. That isn’t something to be given up easily.

There was an extraordinary fightback, a victory plucked from the jaws of defeat. Carrick had said he knew it sounded crazy when he said he was looking forward to this game. By half-time, his optimism appeared delusional. But now and again, even when they are at their lowest ebb for decades, United can justify the faith.

Carrick had talked on Monday about a magical night against Roma. That was 20 seasons ago, when United destroyed them 7-1. Fast forward from 2007 and, in 2026, out of nowhere, this version of United stumbled across some magic of their own.

Roma could be forgiven for wondering what happened, if they are cursed on this ground. Two goals to the good, they almost made it three on the stroke of half-time. Gian Piero Gasperini’s side were showing it was possible to play dynamic attacking football and produce a coherent team in a 3-4-3 formation at Old Trafford, something Ruben Amorim never accomplished despite his best efforts.

Roma's first win in England in 17 attempts and 25 years appeared a certainty. Instead, they departed defeated, taking regrets for eternity back to the eternal city.

Roma could have finished United, and perhaps Carrick, off. But, if only for one night, this was like the United of old.
