Spurs youngster Williams-Barnett signs new contract

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Tottenham youngster Luca Williams-Barnett has signed a new contract with the club.

The 18-year-old's previous deal was set to expire in June 2028, but he has now reportedly extended his stay at the club by five years.

Williams-Barnett made his Premier League debut for Spurs last month in their win over Crystal Palace, while also featuring off the bench against Leeds United before starting against Ipswich Town.

Against Ipswich on Saturday, Williams-Barnett (18 years 51 days) became the second-youngest player ever to both score and assist in a Premier League game, older only than Michael Owen in 1997 (17y 364d).

He was also the youngest player to score for Spurs in the Premier League since Gareth Bale against Fulham in September 2007 (18y 47d), and the fourth-youngest player to score on his first Premier League start behind Daniel Jebbison in May 2021 (17y 276d), Danny Cadamarteri in September 1997 (17y 343d), and Mark Cullen in May 2010 (18y 12d).

Williams-Barnett, who joined Spurs at the age of 10, has made five senior appearances in total, and could play an important role under Roberto De Zerbi.

"This is a special moment for me and my family, and one I have worked really hard to reach. But this is only the start," the teenager told the Spurs website.

Iraola: Ngumoha must have the time to go step by step

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Andoni Iraola insisted Liverpool talent Rio Ngumoha must be given "the time to go step by step".

Ngumoha continued to shine for the Reds on Sunday as he played a crucial role in their Premier League win over rivals Manchester United.

He (18 years 85 days) became the youngest player to score and assist in a Premier League game for Liverpool since Michael Owen on Boxing Day 1997 (18y 12d v Leeds United).

Ngumoha has also completed more dribbles than any other player through the first four matchdays in this season's Champions League (25). His dribble success rate (73.5%) was also the highest of any player with 10+ completed dribbles (25/34).

However, ahead of Liverpool's Champions League encounter with Club Brugge, Iraola has tempered expectations around the teenager.

"The most difficult part is to keep a very high level consistently," said Iraola.

"You can play a good game then three normal or bad games; especially at a big club like Liverpool, you have to do very well in every game.

"With Rio, I always say we have to give him the time to go step by step. You can't rush it; it wouldn't be fair on him.

"He is in the right mindset and is becoming a better player, adding more things to his game. We know he is good, but we have to leave him sometimes.

"He'll have not so good games, and we have to trust him as he deserves it."

This will be the first meeting between Brugge and Liverpool since the 1978 European Cup final, won 1-0 by the Reds at Wembley.

Brugge and Liverpool have faced each other on three previous occasions, with all three of those being in European finals. Both of them were won by the Reds: 4-3 on aggregate in the 1976 UEFA Cup (3-2 at home, 1-1 away) and 1-0 in the 1978 European Cup.

"It's not that they surprised me; I knew they were very good. But honestly I've enjoyed analysing them," Iraola said about Brugge.

"Sometimes you have to watch teams, and it's a little boring, but that's not what happened with Brugge. They are very energetic and have had an incredible run of games, 10 wins and a draw.

"I think we will have to be at a very, very good level, otherwise it is going to be very difficult. They have players who can make the difference, and obviously we also have.

"I expect quite an open game. We are both aggressive, both want to press, and there will be spaces, and hopefully, we can win the one-v-one battles."

Montella describes ‘dream’ Milan opportunity, Berlusconi and the Supercoppa: “An incredible feeling”

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Vincenzo Montella’s time with AC Milan was ‘a dream’ for him, but he failed to achieve much during his time at the helm.

Looking back on Montella’s time leading Milan is tricky. On paper, the Italian had a strong tenure at the club, achieving a 50% win rate and only losing 18 of his 64 games in charge at San Siro, not a terrible record by any means.

He even ended a five-year trophy drought with a win against Juventus in the Supercoppa Italiana and returned the Rossoneri to Europe, with a sixth-place finish in the 2016/17 season. In itself, that was a first since 2014.

This is where context matters, though. The start of the 2017/18 season was tough, as the Diavolo won six of their first 14 games and he was sacked before December. Despite that, he still views the club in an excellent light.

Speaking to La Gazzetta dello Sport’s ‘La Tripletta’ podcast, Montella went over his time with the club and how he fulfilled a dream by taking the job, as MilanPress relays.

“It was a fantastic experience there [at Milan] too, it was my dream. Then, as a professional, it’s a little different, but when I was thirteen I was a fan of that AC Milan team. My older brother took me to see Maradona’s Napoli team twice to make me a Napoli fan, and Maradona scored three goals.

“Despite this, my idol was Van Basten because I was a striker: I studied him and dreamed of becoming like him, and so I was a Milan fan. I knew everything about Milan, so for me it was an incredible feeling to join Milan.”

“He liked to talk about football; he liked to give advice even when it was a bit out of context at the time, because he always had the mentality of having Van Basten as a centre forward with Papin, a two-time Ballon d’Or winner, on the bench. He wanted me to play with two strikers, and I tried to plug the gap for months.

“I told him it was difficult; I knew all the lineups of those years. I was a Milan fan: the starter was Van Basten and the reserve was Papin, both Ballon d’Or winners. So, ‘If you buy me some players like that, rest assured that I’ll play them both.'”

“It was a year when we returned to Europe. I think it had been two or three years since Milan had been there, and it was something new at the time. We won a trophy, a small one. Just the other night I saw Galliani at a dinner; he reminded me of it and remembers it perfectly.

“I have to say that I found him to be the perfect manager at the time, because he was always on my side when it came to pressure; he gave me complete autonomy in my decisions and he helped me with everything, from the number of collaborators to new things; whatever I asked for, he was always on the coach’s side.

“And so for me he was truly an incredible manager, who in that phase, in that change, was fundamental even in achieving these small results, especially after Berlusconi’s great AC Milan.

“We deserved to win even in regulation time: we had some easy chances to win it, then it came to penalties. Gigio saved the penalty from Dybala, who kicked it really hard, but he filled the net with it.”

“I was a little worried from the beginning because there was this obsession with publicising everything, almost like a publicity stunt, so I was a little worried. In theory, there was sharing, at least that’s what it looked like, but I couldn’t back out when in reality that wasn’t always the case.

“And so sometimes there were players I hadn’t even consulted about, who would arrive and then say, ‘It was shared,’ and when you’re there, you can’t say otherwise. Certainly, when you build a team and have the budget, the first thing you do is: we had a goalkeeper, I’ll get a centre forward, we were missing a centre forward.

“In the final selection, the centre forward was the only one left, with a budget of 15-20 million. And then Kalinic… yes, I chose him because that was the final budget. So our, let’s say, second-choice striker was paid thirty-eight million, if I remember correctly, who was André Silva, and the first striker was paid half that.

“And so, in my opinion, a misunderstanding of this kind was created, with so many changes, even though at that stage we weren’t even playing badly for having fifteen new players.

“We still had Calhanoglu, who wasn’t feeling well, coming off months of suspension. The team was still being built: we weren’t in line with the Scudetto objectives that had been announced, but certainly, given our development, we were absolutely on target.

“Who would I have signed up front? At the time, we were probably negotiating for Aubameyang, if I remember correctly. Ronaldo? No, I didn’t believe it, not at all.”

CAN 2025: Senegal-Morocco, huge drama after the CAS verdict!

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The series of the CAN 2025 final is not yet over. A few hours after the decision of the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS), which confirmed Senegal’s victory against Morocco, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) officially reacted. In a particularly firm statement, the Moroccan body expressed its disagreement with this decision and announced its intention to initiate new legal procedures.

“The Royal Moroccan Football Federation expresses its protest against the fact that its request was not taken into consideration”indicates in particular the press release published this Tuesday. The FRMF considers that its arguments concerning the alleged irregularities of the procedure have not been sufficiently examined by the sports court. It considers in particular that certain elements call into question “the integrity and credibility of the decision. »

To support its protest, the FRMF highlights a specific element: “It was confirmed that contact had taken place between the German referee, Ulrich Haas, and the president of the Senegalese Football Federation, 48 hours before the decision was given. » The Moroccan Federation believes that this situation should have been taken into consideration when examining the file.

The Moroccan body also contests the interpretation adopted by the CAS concerning the temporary departure of the Senegalese players from the field during the final. She believes that the decision “still does not take into account the need to comply with the direction enshrined in recent decisions of the International Football Association Board (IFAB) and the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA). »

The FRMF particularly emphasizes the regulatory consequences of the behavior of players who leave the field without the authorization of the referee. She thus evokes “the resulting responsibilities and disciplinary sanctions”considering that this aspect was not correctly taken into account in the decision rendered in Lausanne.

Despite the CAS verdict, the Moroccan Federation does not intend to turn the page. She clearly announced her desire to continue the process: “As a result, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation has decided to initiate all necessary legal procedures to challenge the CAS decision. »

As a reminder, the Court of Arbitration for Sport annulled the decision of the CAF Appeal Jury, which had awarded a victory on the green carpet to Morocco (3-0). He reinstated the decision of the Disciplinary Jury and confirmed the result obtained on the field, a victory for Senegal (1-0). The reaction of the FRMF now opens a new sequence in this particularly tense issue between the two federations.

Posted November 23, 2026 | Reviewed by Monica Vilhauer Ph.D.

Fans of the X-Men film franchise, which premiered in the long-ago year of 2000, were treated to the original version of Hugh Jackman’s now-longstanding portrayal of the character Wolverine. Appearing frequently shirtless in the movie, at the time Jackman seemed muscular enough to be super-heroic. He has played Wolverine nine more times in the decades since then, but rather than growing older and more relaxed, Jackman’s physique has seemed to develop in the opposite direction. Bigger, leaner, and more defined, Jackman could stand for an increasingly familiar male ideal.

Like him, many men spend hours in the gym trying to bulk up. They may carefully monitor their diets, timing their protein intake for maximum effect. They may indeed grow larger and more muscular; family, friends, and partners will notice. They may share images of weight-lifting transformations on social media, or even consider anabolic steroids or testosterone injections. Despite it all, some of these men will continue to see themselves as too small, too ill-defined, and not muscular enough.

This condition is known as muscle dysmorphia, a form of body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) characterized by an unrelenting preoccupation with the idea that one is not muscular or lean enough. Even when a man is objectively well built, he may perceive himself as physically inadequate. It’s not common, but it’s real: a 2025 study of nearly fifteen hundred boys and men between the ages of 15 and 35 (Ganson et al., 2025a) concluded that the disorder likely existed in approximately 2.8% of those surveyed — across demographic groups, not only among weightlifters or athletes. (Indeed, a 2024 study in the Journal of Eating Disorders found higher muscle dysmorphia symptom scores among men who wanted to increase their body weight than among those who wanted to reduce it.)

As I’ve suggested, while physical fitness is usually worth aspiring to, there’s a significant difference between ordinary, healthy fitness and muscle dysmorphia. Even when a man is objectively strong, “cut,” and full of muscles, he may look at himself in the mirror and see only weakness. This preoccupation can then lead to repeated, muscle-seeking behavior, such as checking himself in the mirror, researching new exercise routines, measuring body parts, or seeking new nutritional information. It can also involve excessive weightlifting, spending too much time at the gym, or taking on rigid dietary pursuits that can even become unhealthy or dangerous.

But as all that exercise produces results, it never seems like enough: getting bigger just feeds back into the perception that they still aren’t big enough. And when men with the disorder can’t soothe their anxiety by working out, they can feel real emotional distress, as well. The central characteristic of muscle dysmorphia is being absorbed by the perception that one is not muscular enough; it describes men whose daily routine and sense of self are dependent on the pursuit of muscularity, not those who just don’t like missing a workout (Cooper et al., 2020).

These days, muscle dysmorphia is particularly associated with male body-image concerns, although women can experience it as well. As we’ve seen in Hugh Jackman’s transformation, men in popular culture are expected to be physically strong and imposing, with muscles sharply defined and standing out through their clothing. (So many superheroes!) Women face body image concerns as well, of course, but the pressure on women often focuses on losing weight and appearing thin rather than strong. For men, being muscular can stand in for physical attractiveness, competence, or even dominance.

It’s easy to find examples of these masculine norms on social media nowadays; just as in the past, we’ve been treated to male movie stars whose bodies are designed to show off a great deal of time in the gym; adherence to these norms has been associated with the symptoms of muscle dysmorphia (Grunewald et al., 2022). Research by Ganson and colleagues (2025b) supported this, finding that greater exposure to muscle-oriented social media content was associated with higher levels of probable muscle dysmorphia.

Filters, strategic lighting, and increasingly sophisticated photo-editing tools can offer further enhancement to these already extreme physiques, and sometimes the men in these social-media posts use performance-enhancing drugs, which pushes them still further out of the realm of the realistic. Overall, the fitness content on social media can complicate the distinction between healthy exercise and unhealthy physical enhancement. Psychological vulnerabilities may also play a role: in a 2024 study of men attending gyms, Kandemir et al. found that perfectionism helped explain the relationship between vulnerable narcissism and the risk of muscle dysmorphia.

Muscle dysmorphia can be harmful in a number of ways. Training too hard, even through pain, can lead to injuries — some of which can be lasting. Restricting one’s diet can also have serious consequences for one’s health, such as hormonal disruption (meaning reduced production of testosterone, among other things), reduced bone density, and an increase in the risk of fractures or hypotension (Mountjoy et al., 2023).

The obsession with being muscular can take its toll on one’s social life, too, when men seem to prioritize their nutrition or their workout schedules over their partners’ needs. Daily work obligations can be affected when muscle dysmorphia affects one’s ability to concentrate (Cooper et al., 2020).

And of course there are the serious emotional consequences of feeling ashamed of oneself — like depression, anxiety, or self-hatred — because of body-image-related beliefs. Even when a man’s friends seem to admire their discipline or dedication, or comment in positive ways about the change in his physical condition, these would-be compliments can have a paradoxically damaging effect if they spur a man to try even harder to achieve his unrealistic muscular aspirations, or if they make his beliefs seem more reasonable than they are.

As I’ve noted before, men may be comparatively less likely to seek help for emotion-related problems than women; when the topic is body-image concerns, they may be less likely still. Some may not even believe they have a problem, as the effort to become (and look) strong seems to be valued by their peers and by the culture overall (Brown et al., 2023).

In therapy, however, it is possible to draw a subtle but meaningful distinction between a realistic, healthy body image and a source of compulsive striving. Irrational or distorted cognitions may be examined; other sources of self-worth can be identified. In other words, the effort to become “big” may also have an idiosyncratic meaning to each individual man, and this meaning stands a chance of being explored and identified in the context of the therapeutic relationship. (Medical intervention may become necessary as well, in cases involving potentially harmful medications or nutritional restriction.)

In the end, muscle dysmorphia creates a slippery slope: trying to be big seems to promise better self-esteem, but the effort only results in further, more extreme beliefs about how big one needs to be to achieve those benefits. The only real way out is to grow in a different direction — to learn that feeling good means accepting yourself as you are, and trying to live in a healthy way without wondering what you still need to change.

Serie A | Monza 3-0 Fiorentina: Viola misery as Varela hits 11 goals in 11 games

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Gustavo Varla is now up to 11 goals from his first 11 appearances in Serie A after netting a brace in Monza’s 3-0 victory over Fiorentina on Monday evening, which has seen the Biancorossi leapfrog their opponents in the Serie A table.

Goals from Varela (x2) and Jay Robinson handed Monza a much-needed three points in their Serie A match at home against Fiorentina on Sunday night, which has lifted them out of the relegation zone and into 14th place.

Fiorentina had begun the evening in 14th, but have dropped down to 16th as a result of tonight’s game, just one point above the drop zone after 12 matches.

Fiorentina were presented with the first real goalscoring opportunity after six minutes: Monza goalkeeper Demba Thiam, starting ahead of Noel Tornqvist, was off his line and managed to pass the ball straight to Christ Inaou Oulai under little pressure. Thiam was able to recover and make the save after the Ivorian’s weak shot at an empty net.

It was Monza who took the lead right at the end of the first half, though: Varela earning and converting a penalty kick just before stoppage time.

The Portugal international had lined up a shot in a congested Fiorentina penalty area, which came off the arm of a flailing Oulai. The in-form Varela stepped up and converted into the right-hand side of the net, sending David De Gea the wrong way in the process.

As per Opta, Varela is one of only three players over the last 50 years to hit double digits for goals within their first 11 Serie A matches, along with Vincenzo Montella in 1997 and Diego Milito in 2008.

Varela’s second goal of the game came shortly after the re-start of the second half. Jay Robinson did well to shrug off Alex Jimenez before sending Matteo Pessina on the run down the left flank. Varela burst into the area and applied a smart, back-heel finish to convert Pessina’s low ball into the six-yard box for 2-0.

Robinson made it three less than 10 minutes later. Samuele Birindelli’s cross into the area was headed half-clear, but only as far as the Englishman on the edge of the area, who slammed a volley into the roof of the net with the help of a deflection on its way in.

Monza now find themselves in 14th place in the Serie A table, whilst Fiorentina have dropped down to 16th, one point above the relegation spots.

Arsenal pose major Champions League test for Dortmund, says Kovac

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Niko Kovac was under no illusions about the size of the task awaiting his Borussia Dortmund players ahead of facing Arsenal in the Champions League.

Dortmund make the trip to the Emirates Stadium fresh from beating Borussia Monchengladbach on Saturday, a result that moved them two points clear at the Bundesliga summit.

BVB have started a Bundesliga season unbeaten in their first 10 matches for only the third time, after 2002-03 and 2018-19, while their 24 points at this stage is just one fewer than their club record (25 points in 2010-11 and 2013-14).

However, they face an Arsenal side six points clear at the top of the Premier League despite Mikel Arteta's side drawing 2-2 with Newcastle United at the weekend.

The Gunners are also unbeaten in their last 19 Champions League games (W14 D5 – excluding penalty shootout defeats).

Should they avoid defeat here, they will be just the third English team to go 20+ games without losing in the European Cup/Champions League, after Manchester United (25 – September 2007–May 2009) and Manchester City (26 – September 2022–October 2024).

Dortmund also have a miserable record in England, losing eight of their last nine Champions League away games when facing off against sides from the Premier League.

Arsenal have won three of their four Champions League home games against Borussia Dortmund (L1). Their only loss was in October 2013, with Robert Lewandowski scoring a late winner for the visitors in a 2-1 victory.

"We are not only playing against the best defence in England and Europe, but also against the reigning English champions, Champions League finalists last season and the current Premier League leaders," Kovac told reporters.

"We're aware how difficult this is going to be. They have a great team and a great coach. But we're also difficult to play against, and we'll have our chances."

"You do not know my private life, you have no right to comment on my private life. It was an unpleasant situation for me and my wife."

When pressed further on his future in Naples, De Bruyne added: "I can't say very much at this point. I've played at 400% so far.

Gone was the era of network television, and in came the era of streaming.

Miniseries in particular find their roots in the British serials of the 1950s, but it was when American television adapted the format in the 1970s that it really became a cultural phenomenon. From classics like Roots to 21st-century masterpieces like Band of Brothers, the era of network TV delivered many a great miniseries. The streaming era of television has definitely not fallen behind, though, giving us in the likes of Adolescence and xxx some of the greatest miniseries of all time. Only streaming originals can qualify for this list.

After actor Stephen Graham read a news article about a young boy stabbing a girl to death, he proposed telling a story inspired by this wave of violent attacks by young boys against girls in Great Britain through the miniseries format. He and Jack Thorne then co-created Adolescence, a scathing critique of incel culture, male rage, toxic masculinity, and the "manosphere." It's an awfully timely yet sensitive topic, but the series treats it with precisely the care it deserved.

The result is one of the best miniseries of the last five years, composed of four episodes all shot in a single take, which lends the series an air of urgency and raw authenticity that elevates both its emotional and intellectual impacts tremendously. Bolstered by its exceptional cast (including Graham himself and a revelatory Emmy-winning turn by young Owen Cooper) and its excellent writing, it's one of the most important crime drama series of the 2020s.

Beth Macy's non-fiction masterpiece Dopesick: Dealers, Doctors, and the Drug Company that Addicted America deals with the origin and evolution of the opioid epidemic that began in the United States around 1996 with the release of OxyContin. In 2021, Hulu adapted that story in Dopesick, which just so happens to be one of the most perfect miniseries of the last 25 years. It's every bit as ambitious, expansive, and insightful as the book it's based on.

It's such a devastating investigative drama that it's most definitely not an easy watch, but boy, is it an important one. It's a 360-degree systemic portrait of absolutely every link in the chain of the opioid crisis, from the Purdue Pharma boardroom to the victims of the crisis. Elevated by its incredible cast (including the likes of Michael Keaton and Peter Sarsgaard) and its non-linear structure, it's about as urgent as streaming-era miniseries get.

Far more than just one of the best movies of the last 100 years, Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather is one of those artistic masterpieces so great and so important that they straight-up transcend their medium. What really makes it such a special movie, however, is that the story behind it is every bit as interesting and compelling as the film itself; and that story is the subject of Paramount+'s The Offer.

Making-of dramas can either be so full of propulsive energy that they're irresistibly entertaining, or so cold in their approach that they feel like Wikipedia pages come to life. Western television hadn't really been a particularly prominent part of the mainstream for several years now, but Taylor Sheridan apparently never got the memo, because with his Yellowstone universe, not only did he become one of the kings of streaming-era television, he also made Western shows mainstream again. Leaving behind the soap-opera tone of the franchise's flagship show and stripping the Western genre of its nostalgia and problematic myth-making, it's a series fully dedicated to showing just how unforgiving the Oregon Trail really was. One of the scariest TV shows of all time, Haunting of Hill House is a landmark in the history of streaming-era horror television. The 2010s saw the rise of "elevated horror" on the big screen, referring to a form of horror filmmaking that proved the genre could be full of artistic merit, and this was one of the first true "elevated horror" projects that the small screen ever saw.
