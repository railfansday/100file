Mendoza is slated to make his first career start against the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks at home on Sunday, Nov. 15 (4:05 ET).

Mendoza's lone regular-season action came during his debut, at the end of a Week 4 road loss to the New England Patriots on Oct. 11. He finished 6-for-8 passing for 48 yards.

Mendoza's first two opponents boast vaunted defensive units. Seattle's defense, dubbed the "Dark Side" for a reason, did not allow a touchdown last week to the undermanned Arizona Cardinals. Looking ahead, the Raiders face the Denver Broncos on the road in Week 11. Denver ranks in the top 10 in points allowed per game (20.7), sacks (26) and takeaways (12).

"I feel that Fernando has done a great job," Cousins told reporters Oct. 26. "He has a bright future ahead. I'm going to try and go out there and play as well as I possibly can and try and win and give him a good example to learn from."

On his Instagram story, Cousins offered a goodbye to "Raider Nation" and a message to Mendoza, whom Cousins tagged in the post.

Toughening matters for Mendoza on the field will be the lack of a rushing attack for the Raiders. First-round talent Ashton Jeanty from the 2025 draft has not worked out, although rookie Mike Washington has shown some juice in the backfield. Still, Las Vegas ranks 31st in rushing yards per game (71.3) and is last in yards per rush (2.9).

Similar to Cousins, Mendoza will likely have to shoulder much of the output in the Raiders' offense.

"The game has to be balanced," head coach Klint Kubiak said following the team's most recent loss to the San Francisco 49ers that dropped their record to 4-5. "You can't be a running and throwing team when you're throwing it every time. That's not easy.

"You got to start the game a lot faster. You got to go toe-to-toe in the first half so that the second half isn't a throwing fest."

The good news is that tight end Brock Bowers ranks ninth in the league in receiving yards and provides a crucial red-zone target with six touchdowns already this season. Wide receiver Tre Tucker emerged as a reliable downfield option for Cousins, which gives Mendoza more relief in the passing game.

One of the huaco erótico sculptures in Fertility Park, near Trujillo, Peru Photo: Richard Avila, via Wikimedia Commons

A controversy over 3m-tall erotic sculptures in northern Peru reignited recently after a senator requested that the cultural ministry remove them from a public area. Located in a park outside the city of Trujillo, the sculptures are replicas of ceramics from the pre-Incan Moche civilisation.

“What happens when children see these images? Neurological and psychological factors are affected, because they see body dimensions that are completely different from how they see themselves,” Milagros Jáuregui, a conservative senator from the Renovación Popular party, said in a speech on 26 October.

The sculptures are located in Parque de la Fertilidad (Fertility Park), a public space where in 2021 then-mayor Arturo Fernández of the Moche district began installing giant fibreglass reproductions of ceramic depictions of sexual acts to promote tourism.

Known as huacos eróticos, the much smaller original ceramics date from the 3rd to 8th century AD. No more than 30cm in height, they were placed in tombs as burial offerings. But the debate about their much larger versions in the park goes beyond political prudishness.

“It’s a complicated issue,” the Peruvian archaeologist Walter Alva tells The Art Newspaper. “There’s freedom to express or re-create themes from the past, but these ceramics were created to be placed as offerings to the dead, with a sense of revitalising life. They are associated with both fertility and an entire system of beliefs.”

An original huaco erótico ceramic offering made by the Moche civilisation, Larco Museum, Lima Photo: László Varga, via Wikimedia Commons

The Moche civilisation covered 700km of the coast of what is now Peru, between the regions of Piura and Ancash, centuries before the Inca dominated the area. “Moche art is primarily ceramic, and these vessels depict erotic scenes or concepts of sexuality from that era,” Alva says. “The sculptures in the park were created outside of their historical context.”

One of the greatest archaeological discoveries from the Moche civilisation was that of the Lord of Sipán near the city of Chiclayo—a discovery Alva made in 1987. A 3rd-century monarch, the Lord of Sipán’s body was found covered with gold along with eight other people, a dog and two llamas and nearly 500 objects including ceramic vessels, ornaments and textiles.

The Moche have the largest number of erotic representations among pre-Hispanic civilisations. The most complete collection of its ceramics is in Lima, at the Larco Museum of pre-Columbian art, which celebrated its centennial in July. Before the 1960s, children were forbidden from entering the room that housed the huacos. Today, minors must be accompanied by an adult. When would aliens contact Earth? Astronomers test a new idea

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Astronomers are searching for alien signals at a moving point opposite the Sun, testing a strategy that could help distant civilizations make contact.

Searching for alien signals sounds like a question of which star to aim a telescope at. It’s also a question of when to listen, because a civilization sending a focused radio beam toward Earth might transmit only at selected times.

For the first time, astronomers have specifically searched a moving point in the sky that could offer a shared cue for when to transmit and listen. They found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

Sofia Sheikh of the SETI Institute and Eleanor Hort of the University of New Mexico led the search, which used the Allen Telescope Array in Hat Creek, California.

Three nights of pilot observations in the summer of 2023 turned up 1.9 million radio signals, and all of them could be explained by human technology.

Two people who agree to meet in a city but forget to pick a time and place still have a decent chance of finding each other.

Each one picks the landmark the other is most likely to pick. Game theorists call that shared choice a Schelling point.

The team applied the same logic to a distant transmitter. From some directions in the galaxy, an observer would see Earth cross the face of the Sun once a year.

That crossing tells them a planet exists and how long its year is. It also shows the planet orbits at a distance where water can stay liquid.

Halfway through the crossing, Earth lies exactly between the transmitter and the Sun.

Seen from Earth, the transmitter would be in the direction directly opposite the Sun, a spot astronomers call the anti-solar point.

A sender could beam a signal toward Earth at that alignment, while astronomers on Earth listened toward the anti-solar point, without either side arranging contact beforehand.

“The challenge in SETI isn’t simply deciding where to point a telescope,” Sheikh said. “It’s figuring out where and when another civilization might choose to transmit.”

The idea is not new: a 1982 paper proposed it for Jupiter’s crossings, and a 2003 paper argued Earth’s were the better bet.

Yet by 2020, the Breakthrough Listen archive held not a single pointing within half a degree of the anti-solar point.

Two large radio surveys of the 1990s likely spent about 0.0004% of their time there, by accident.

The spot never stays put. As Earth moves around the Sun, the anti-solar point slides along the sky against the background stars, so the telescope has to follow it night after night.

The survey plans repeated observations two to three nights a week over about four years to build coverage along Earth’s orbit.

For the pilot, the team pointed 20 of the array’s 42 dishes at the anti-solar point for about 17 hours over three nights. The scans ran five minutes each and added up to 32 terabytes of data.

They listened between 1.06 and 2.38 gigahertz, a band that includes the radio frequency of hydrogen that early searchers once favored.

Every scan also recorded a second patch of sky nearby.

A signal from a distant point source should show up strongly in the target beam and faintly in the other. Interference from a satellite or a phone tower tends to appear in both.

The two-beam comparison alone removed 1.3 million of the 1.9 million signals. The team then plotted the most promising 2,114 and inspected each one by eye.

None of them looked like a deliberate alien signal.

Most were narrow and barely shifting in frequency, the signature of ground-based transmitters, and the few that drifted lined up with known sources of human interference, including satellite links.

A search that finds nothing still says something, because the old Arecibo planetary radar sent out a tight beam with an effective power of roughly 20 trillion watts.

The pilot was sensitive enough to catch a transmitter that strong from 44 to 124 light-years away.

The exact reach depends on where the signal fell in the beam and how fast its frequency drifted.

The pilot sets the first limit of any kind on alien signals timed to the anti-solar point. The limit applies to a narrow strip, though.

The telescope’s beam spans only about 5% of the width of the zone from which Earth’s crossings are visible. A transmitter slightly above or below that line would have been missed.

The full survey began after the pilot, two to three nights a week at higher frequencies chosen to dodge the worst interference. It has already logged more than 121 nights.

The team plans to report the first year of data in a later paper.

A detection would need a lot to be true: a civilization within reach, one that has noticed Earth, and one that reasons about meeting points the way people do.

The team doesn’t assume any of that; the search is a way to test it. It also assumes the sender has accounted for how long light takes to cross the gap, so the signal arrives when Earth expects it.

Whether anyone is transmitting at the anti-solar point is still an open question, and three nights can’t settle it.

The multiyear survey will test the idea across a much broader stretch of sky.

The full study was published in the journal The Astronomical Journal.

GLP-1 Pill Shows Benefit in Sleep Apnea

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

— If approved, orforglipron could become the first oral treatment for obstructive sleep apnea

The oral GLP-1 receptor agonist orforglipron (Foundayo) improved sleep-disordered breathing in patients with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea and overweight or obesity, two phase III trials showed.

For the treatment regimen estimand, mean change of apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) was significantly improved from baseline to week 52 with orforglipron compared with placebo both in patients receiving continuous positive airway pressure (PAP; -17.3 vs -5.3 events/hour, P<0.001) and in patients not receiving PAP (-17.8 vs -4.5 events/hour, P<0.001).

Orforglipron also showed significant improvements in secondary outcomes, including the sleep apnea-specific hypoxic burden, as well as reductions in body weight, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, and blood pressure, reported Vaishnavi Kundel, MD, of the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in New York City.

"These data support the potential use of orforglipron as a promising oral treatment option for moderate-to-severe OSA [obstructive sleep apnea] in adults with obesity or overweight," Kundel said at the American Heart Association (AHA) annual meeting held in Chicago.

AHA commenter Michael Grandner, PhD, from the University of Arizona in Tucson, said having an oral option to treat obstructive sleep apnea would be "game-changing."

"As a reminder, to treat sleep apnea right now, we have instruments that work really well, but they can be complicated and difficult to use," Grandner said. "Not only is it undertreated, but among those who are treated, use is relatively low. This might help get people toward the treatment that they need."

The results of the ATTAIN-OSA trials were published simultaneously in JAMA.

Orforglipron is a small-molecule, non-peptide oral GLP-1 receptor agonist that has already received FDA approval for weight management.

In an editorial accompanying the publication, Diego Mazzotti, PhD, and Damien Stevens, MD, both of the University of Kansas Medical Center in Kansas City, pointed out that for patients with severe disease, "it is expected that orforglipron will be one component of a broader treatment plan."

They suggested that pragmatic trials comparing and combining orforglipron with PAP, oral appliances, tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), or other treatments are needed. "For many years, individuals with OSA had few alternatives when PAP was inadequate," they wrote. "Orforglipron adds an oral option to a growing number of treatments from which clinicians and patients can select and work together to build better individualized care."

AHA discussant for the presentation, Ildiko Lingvay, MD, MPH, of the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, compared the results for orforglipron to those reported for another GLP-1 drug, tirzepatide, which is already approved for obstructive sleep apnea.

She pointed to apparent advantages for tirzepatide over orforglipron in terms of obstructive sleep apnea disease control, with greater improvement in AHI as well as in hypoxic burden, and in safety measures. Cardiovascular risk reduction is also not yet established with orforglipron, while tirzepatide is already FDA-approved for cardiovascular risk reduction in people with diabetes.

"The biggest benefit of orforglipron is really the fact that it comes in a tablet form, for once-daily administration, while tirzepatide requires a cold chain supply and injections," Lingvay noted. "The take-home message is that orforglipron is the first ever oral therapy to have shown improvements in disordered breathing as well as beneficial effects on cardiovascular risk factors, and that includes weight, systolic blood pressure, and inflammatory markers."

The ATTAIN-OSA program included two 52-week, phase III, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials involving patients on PAP (N=349) or not on PAP (N=363). The studies were carried out at 66 sites in the U.S., Argentina, Brazil, China, the Czech Republic, Germany, Japan, Mexico, and Taiwan.

Adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea and a body mass index of 27 or higher were eligible. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either once-daily orforglipron at the maximum tolerated dose, equivalent to a 9-, 14.5-, or 17.2-mg tablet dose, or placebo.

Gastrointestinal events were the most commonly reported adverse events, occurring in 68% of patients on orforglipron versus 37.4% of those on placebo among the trial participants not on PAP, and in 70.6% of those on orforglipron versus 36.6% of those on placebo in the PAP trial.

Webb reveals the hidden activity inside a turbulent stellar nursery

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Webb peers through thick cosmic dust in NGC 7129, revealing hidden young stars, protostars, glowing gas, and powerful outflows.

Today’s Image of the Day from the European Space Agency reveals a stellar nursery where young stars are changing the clouds that gave birth to them.

The James Webb Space Telescope captured NGC 7129, a star-forming region about 3,300 light-years from Earth.

Thick clouds of gas and dust obscure much of the activity at visible wavelengths.

Webb’s infrared vision can penetrate that material, bringing previously hidden stars and structures into view.

The result shows much more than a collection of newborn stars. NGC 7129 contains objects at several stages of early stellar development.

Some are still gathering material. Others have moved closer to the long, stable period of life that includes stars such as our Sun.

Stars do not all mature at the same rate. More massive stars generally form and evolve faster than smaller ones.

That difference allows astronomers to study several chapters of stellar development within the same region.

The brightest central star in this scene is LkH(alpha) 234. It has about five to eight times the mass of the Sun and produces the image’s most noticeable diffraction pattern.

It is a pre-main-sequence star, meaning it has largely finished collecting mass but has not yet begun the sustained hydrogen fusion that will define its main-sequence life.

At this stage, gravity continues to pull the star inward. As it contracts, its interior grows hotter.

Eventually, conditions in the core will become intense enough for hydrogen fusion to begin.

This transition matters because a young star does not develop quietly.

Even before reaching the main sequence, it can send powerful streams of material into the gas around it.

Those outflows can reshape a stellar nursery across surprisingly large distances.

The clearest evidence appears to the left of LkH(alpha) 234. A golden cavity stretches roughly 3.5 light-years across.

Outflows produced during an earlier stage of the star’s development carved into the surrounding molecular cloud, which consists largely of hydrogen.

Radiation and outflowing material from the star energize the gas and make it glow.

Some gas gets swept away, but the process does not simply shut down star formation.

Other material becomes compressed. Dense pockets can then provide the conditions needed for additional stars to develop.

Several young stars are visible inside the cavity. Some are also pre-main-sequence stars, and they produce stellar winds of their own.

As these winds push into surrounding gas, they create curved structures called bow shocks.

The stars also create a hot environment beside colder, denser molecular material. The boundary between these environments is known as a photodissociation region.

Energetic radiation breaks hydrogen molecules apart into individual atoms there.

The process changes the chemistry, heating and cooling of the cloud and helps determine how it evolves.

Molecular clouds are the raw material for star formation, but the stars born inside them gradually help destroy them.

Radiation, winds, and outflows can disperse gas or alter its chemistry.

Over millions of years, those effects can erode the cloud and change where new stars are able to form.

NGC 7129 gives astronomers a particularly useful view of that process because its stars have reached different developmental stages.

Their combined activity shows how star formation can both disrupt a cloud and compress parts of it into conditions favorable for further stellar growth.

The right side of Webb’s image tells an earlier part of the story.

Clumpy red material conceals protostars that are younger than the pre-main-sequence stars visible elsewhere in the region.

Protostars form after cold molecular gas and dust collapse and fragment into dense concentrations.

They continue gaining mass from surrounding material.

At the same time, they can eject narrow, extremely hot outflows that slam into the dense matter around them.

Those collisions produce shocks within the gray material surrounding the protostars.

They help create the textured appearance seen by Webb and contribute to the region’s red glow.

Several protostars are active at once, so their outflows overlap from Earth’s viewpoint. That makes this side of NGC 7129 look especially tangled.

Yet the apparent disorder contains valuable information about stars that would otherwise remain hidden behind dust.

Infrared observations give astronomers access to these deeply embedded objects before they emerge from their birth clouds.

In NGC 7129, Webb captures stars at different points along that path while also revealing how strongly they affect their surroundings.

The nursery will continue changing as its young stars mature.

Gas will disperse, dense pockets may collapse, and some newly formed stars will eventually settle into the hydrogen-fusing stage that powers the Sun today.

Forget Mayo: Spread This Rich Ingredient On Bread For A More Flavorful Grilled Cheese

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Butter gives your grilled cheese great richness, and using mayo makes it far crispier, but neither adds a ton of strong flavors that can shine through high-quality cheese. That's why you need toum, a Lebanese garlic spread that delivers intense taste with very little effort.

Toum is as rich as mayonnaise and as light and fluffy as whipped cream, created by smashing garlic into a paste and then gradually whipping in oil until it has an airy but glossy consistency. For texture, its incredible fluffiness makes it easy to slather on, covering every last inch in a swiftly melting browning agent that makes the bread crispy. But for flavoring, it makes the end product taste like garlic bread, full of rich fats and pungent aromatics that only requires a swift spread with a knife. It may seem like you're using a lot and will over-season your sandwich, but since so much of it is air, you're actually only using a moderate amount that's quite easy to thoroughly apply.

The combination of melty gooeyness inside and crisp, garlicky exteriors is a second-to-none combination that pairs well with a wide variety of cheeses. Add in the sharpness of the lemon juice, and you get something that can brighten up even the heaviest, fat-laden cheeses quite well. The only caveat is that all that garlic gives it a fairly cool smoke point, so you'll need to use low to medium heat to avoid making it grow bitter.

Since its main flavors are garlic and lemon, toum isn't quite as universal a pairing as mayonnaise or butter. It goes great with anything semi-hard and creamy, but its intensity may clash with anything stronger. You'll also want to know the type of oil used in your toum. Something made with grapeseed, canola, or other neutral varieties is far more compatible than a spread made with a flavorful extra virgin olive oil.

Cheeses with a nuttier profile, like Jarlsberg, Gruyère, and Manchego, are all solid pairings. They tend to be a bit earthier, grounding the brightness of the toum, so it isn't too intense. Since it has no eggs, you can even combine it with melted vegan cheese since nutritional yeast is typically quite aromatic and nutty. Richer, well-rounded varieties can also be quite nice, so some shreds of gouda, raclette, and any triple-cream variety are always welcome additions.

Flavored cheeses are one of the easiest ways to upgrade any melty sandwich, but not all are compatible with toum. While something rich in herbs or wine can complement the garlic quite well, a variety with a lot of sweetened fruit or more lemon can be a bit much. Since toum is already quite tangy, you may also want to steer clear of acidic cheeses like chèvre, feta, or cream cheese. While some may like the brightness, it can quickly become overly intense, and you may find yourself making a new sandwich.

The Lord of the Rings may be famous for its Elves and Orcs, but the human characters are legends in their own right. Éomer is one of the Rohirrim, a people well-known for their horsemanship, physical toughness, and martial culture. As viewers are seeing, Isildur was noted for his heroism and fought on the front lines in many battles against Sauron and his forces. At first, the attempt went wrong; Beren and his companion Finrod were captured by Sauron. A King of Rohan, he became known for his tremendous strength, and for fighting his enemies with his bare hands. 📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Maurissa Tancharoen and Jed Whedon, co-creators of Marvel’s Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D, are under new management.

The duo have signed with Range Media Partners.

It comes six months after it emerged that they were writing a pilot script for a series adaptation of Skyward, the first book in Brandon Sanderson’s Cytoverse franchise, for Tomorrow Studios.

They served as showrunners for Marvel’s Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D, which ran for seven seasons on ABC between 2013 and 2020, making it Marvel’s longest-running live-action television series

Panthers restructure Taylor Moton’s contract to free up cap room

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The NFL trade deadline is 4:00pm Eastern time today (Tuesday). There's been more activity around the trade deadline in recent years as teams have started to find the utility in becoming buyers or sellers based on the way their seasons are going. The Carolina Panthers are just 3-5, but they do technically lead the NFC South. That would suggest they're more likely to be buyers at the deadline if anything, and they did a bit of bookkeeping to confirm that.

According to Spotrac, the Panthers restructured offensive tackle Taylor Moton's deal to save themselves $3.5 million against this season's salary cap. It's the typical restructure move. They converted some of his base salary (which counts entirely against this season's cap) to a signing bonus (which is spread across the length of the deal). The team was right at the salary cap before this move, and now they have some wiggle room to add a piece at the deadline if there's one that suits them.

The $3.5 million number isn't going to fit any blockbuster acquisition, even if it is only a player's base salary. If anything, the Panthers will peruse the league's worst teams for some inexpensive veterans that can fill the void in the secondary while Jaycee Horn, Mike Jackson, and Will Lee recover from their injuries. Same could be said for the offensive line, where injuries have caused problems and subpar play has caused even more.

However, it's more likely that his move is to give the team some wiggle room to sign or claim free agents. The team hosted cornerback Jack Jones for a visit the other day but haven't yet offered him a contract. Moves like that would require the kind of space the Moton restructure opens up, so that seems like the route in which the Panthers will spend the extra money they've freed up.
