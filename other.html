Pisilli: “Gasperini is a master. I’ll always be grateful to Mourinho.”

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Niccolò Pisilli gave an interview to Spanish newspaper Marca in which he discussed his experience with the Giallorossi.

“I’ve never hidden the fact that I’ve been a Roma fan since I was very young. It was an honor. A series of circumstances arose that led to the armband ending up on my arm. It’s not like I’m the captain now. Every minute I played with the armband was a source of great pride and a great responsibility.”

The Roma player’s number 61 on the comparison with Totti and De Rossi: “They’re giants who can’t be compared to my 20 minutes. I don’t know how many games they played with that armband; they made the history of this club. I’m still a kid, I’m 22, and I played 20 minutes as captain by chance. They are legends of the city.”

Niccolò Pisilli spoke about the Scudetto dream: “Overall, this is a very good championship. There’s more play, it’s more dynamic, there’s more actual time, and many more goals are scored. I think it’s more enjoyable for fans at home to watch the games. We’re doing well, both Roman teams, but we’ll settle the score at the end, at the end of the season. What would I do if I win the Scudetto? I don’t know. We’d celebrate in style, that’s for sure. I can’t say exactly what I’d do. Now we have to think game by game, and, as I said, the score will be settled in the end.”

The Giallorossi midfielder on Gasperini: “He needs no introduction from me. The history of a club like Atalanta has changed. He came to us, and in his first year we achieved excellent results and returned to the Champions League. He’s a maestro. He’s truly very good. You can see it in the way his teams play. I try to perform well in every training session and at every moment to fully understand what he’s asking of me. I try to learn as much as possible from him, too.”

The Roma number 61 concluded with a thought about Mourinho: “He identified deeply with Rome and the atmosphere of the city. I will always be grateful to him, because he gave me the opportunity to debut in this jersey and the great satisfaction of scoring my first goal.”

Not much to say about what happened on Sunday, other than a complete trouncing. The Patriots' had minimal push back in what was a rout of New England at the hands of the Green Bay Packers.

Let's go underneath the hood of the Patriots' Week 9 defeat.

New England's All-Pro cornerback's dreadful performance was the most glaring issue in this loss. Christian Gonzalez, the 24 year-old who just signed a record-setting four-year, $135 million deal, was the first one to tell the media he had a "bad game." But that might be an understatement, as this was his worst performance in his young NFL career.

According to NextGen Stats, Gonzalez allowed 10 receptions on 11 targets for 187 yards, which was easily the most he has ever given up in one game. In fact, Gonzalez nearly gave up more yardage in this game than in his first six combined this season, in which he allowed 196 yards.

He also unfortunately made history, becoming the first defender since at least 2018 to let up 80 or more yards to two receivers in the same game. Christian Watson (171 yards), and Matthew Golden (164 yards), each had career highs in receiving yards, combining for 335 of Jordan Love's 372 passing yards.

The Patriots have yet to win a game in which they force no turnovers in 2026, and are now 0-3 under that circumstance. New England has the exact same record when Drake Maye throws two or more interceptions in a game, tossing three more against Green Bay. His 11 interceptions through the first nine weeks of the NFL season, tied for the league lead alongside Justin Herbert, are the most for a Patriots' QB to open a season since Drew Bledsoe in 1994.

While he did pick up his play in the second half versus the Packers, the Patriots were already in a 17-point hole when he got the ball in the third quarter. His mistakes plus the defense allowing 31 points in the first 34 minutes and 9 seconds all contributed to the game's blowout nature.

A familiar limitation also showed itself again, adding to a game full of flaws on the New England side. Right when it felt that it was snowballing for the Patriots, it became an avalanche when Rhamondre Stevenson got stripped by Isaiah McDuffie midway through the third quarter. This is only Stevenson's second fumble on the year, the other one occurring in Week 2 against the Pittsburgh Steelers. In fact, his fumbling issues have gotten progressively better.

Since his alarming 2024 season, when he fumbled times, he only has five combined in 2025 and 2026. The second half begun with a Packers touchdown, followed by a Patriots punt, a Packers field goal, then the fumble by Stevenson. The Packers followed the turnover up with a one-play, 39 yard touchdown to Christian Watson, planting the momentum on the Green Bay sideline.

In three straight games, the Patriots have had to play from behind in the first half, having sluggish starts against the Bears, Dolphins, and now the Packers. New England began on Sunday down 17-0 after the celebration of the 2016 Patriots' Super Bowl team, not scoring their first points until 9:23 on the clock in the 2nd quarter. New England was also down 13-3 at halftime in Miami, and losing 10-0 at the conclusion of the first quarter up in Chicago. The Patriots have been outscored in the first half 51-37 in the past three games.

Patriots' head coach Mike Vrabel was asked about coming out skittish following the 45-27 loss to the Packers.

"It's tough to spot good teams any sort of lead," claimed Vrabel. "And we've done that."

The Patriots will have a long trip across the pond to Munich, Germany to figure out how to correct their numerous mistakes before they face the Detroit Lions. While the Lions' defense is not as strong as Green Bay's, their offense has fire power in Jahmyr Gibbs and Amon-Ra St. Brown. New England will need a strong gameplan to neutralize those two if they want to get back on track in Week 10.

Scott Kirby, the Chief Executive Officer of United Airlines, has taken aim at the US Federal Aviation Administration, whose understaffing he blames for long taxi times at its hub at Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD). Kirby believes that getting the airport's air traffic control towers back up to full staffing levels could cut taxi times by several minutes, with these marginal gains soon adding up overall.

Such can be the length of taxi times at O'Hare that Kirby has even likened them to "driving to Milwaukee," a city some 79 miles (127 km) from Chicago's main airport. Such a drive would take more than an hour, and, while O'Hare's average taxi times aren't exactly that long, the airport has a reputation for them being lengthier than elsewhere, as backed up by data analysis. Kirby hopes that this can be changed.

According to the Chicago Tribune, Scott Kirby made his remarks at an event in the Windy City on Tuesday evening, where he said that "we need to get the FAA fully staffed" when it comes to air traffic control at O'Hare. In addition to reducing taxi times and thus boosting operational efficiency, United's CEO also believes that fully-staffed ATC towers would be "key to unlocking more capacity at O’Hare."

Such a boost would be particularly important for United Airlines at a time when it is facing increasing competition from its US 'big three' legacy rival, American Airlines. Michael McMurray, the Chicago Department of Aviation’s Commissioner, concurs with Kirby that "once we’re able to fully staff our airport's South Tower, I think people will see taxi times decrease." The CEO of United Airlines went on to say:

"There’s a taxiway problem here at O’Hare. It’s the only airport in the country where aircraft that are taxiing can’t cross runways. That’s why it feels like you’re driving to Milwaukee sometimes."

It's one thing for O'Hare to have a reputation for long taxi times, but another for it to be backed up by cold, hard facts. In any case, the numbers don't lie, with the Chicago Tribune reporting that it takes an average of 20 minutes for inbound flights to reach their gates after landing. The situation is even worse for departing flights, which take an average of 28 minutes to get from the gate to the runway.

These figures make O'Hare the worst of the major US hubs on this front. For context, futher analysis by the Chicago Tribune found that, for arriving flights, the next-worst major US hub was Charlotte Douglas International Airport (CLT) in North Carolina. Here, the publication's research found that inbound flights took an average of 14 minutes to get from the runway to the gate. But how much time can be saved?

As noted above in comments made by Michael McMurray, the South Tower is a particular sticking point when it comes to understaffing at O'Hare. However, Kirby is optimistic that, should this issue be rectified, meaningful savings of some six to seven minutes could be made.
