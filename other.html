Personal Consumer Issues • Re: Steering Wheel Locking up while parked - at random - any ideas?

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Came across this on a website that provides vehicle service bulletins, service intervals, technical service bulletins, wiring & circuit diagrams, etc.

The info below was posted in the Honda (older) Civic section:

Can't Turn Ignition Switch? Check for Locked Steering

Got a service customer complaining he or she can't turn the ignition switch to start the engine? The reason could just be the steering column lock pin is engaged with the front wheels turned. Here's what typically happens:

Some folks use the steering wheel to help support themselves while climbing in or out of the vehicle. This is very common among elderly or disabled drivers. Doing this turns the front wheels, which twists the tires against the ground. The twisted tires apply torque to the steering column. With the ignition switch in LOCK (0), the steering column lock pin is engaged. But the applied torque on the steering column binds the lock pin, making it tough to turn the ignition switch. The ignition switch won't turn to ON (II) if the lock pin isn't disengaged from the steering column.

Tell your customer there's nothing wrong with the ignition switch itself. All he or she needs to do is turn the steering wheel from side to side while turning the switch. This usually releases the lock pin so the engine can be started. In some cases, though, your customer may need to give a good tug on the wheel. This could easily happen when parking on a hill and the wheels are turned sharply before the engine is shut off.

The album re-enters the top 10 of Indie Store Album Sales (No. 3), Vinyl Albums (No. 4), Top Album Sales (No. 7) and Top R&B Albums (No. 7) (all charts dated Nov. 7). Back to Black peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 in 2008, following its big night at that year’s Grammy Awards, where Winehouse won record of the year, song of the year, best female pop vocal performance (for the album’s “Rehab”), best new artist and best pop vocal album (for Back to Black).

For its 20th anniversary, the album was reissued in a variety of options on Oct. 23, including an expanded version with previously unreleased b-sides, demos and a live concert.

Back to Black marked the second and final studio album from Winehouse before her death in 2011, and spun off the Billboard Hot 100-charting singles “You Know I’m No Good” and the No. 9 hit “Rehab.”

In the tracking week ending Oct. 29, Back to Black earned 16,000 equivalent album units (up 185%) in the United States, according to Luminate. Of that figure, 11,000 was in pure album sales (up 885%), with the remainder driven by streaming activity and track sales. (All versions of the album, old and new, are combined for tracking and charting purposes.)

Back to Black is among seven debuts or reentries in the top 10 of the latest Top Album Sales chart. The highest debut of the week belongs to Anderson .Paak and Cordae’s collaborative project Heavy Is the Crown (No. 2), while My Chemical Romance’s The Black Parade jumps 29-1 following its own 20th anniversary reissue.

Helene Hahn Lloyd, the former Disney executive vice president and a founding executive of DreamWorks, has died. She was 78.

According to her management team, Lloyd “died peacefully in her sleep” on Tuesday, Oct. 24 in Park City, Utah, where she moved after leaving DreamWorks in 2004 and lived with her husband of more than two decades, Charles “Chuck” Lloyd Jr., who died this July.

Born July 13, 1948 in Westbury, New York, Lloyd (née Burlakoff) graduated from Hofstra University in 1970 before moving to Los Angeles, where she worked as a temp for Paramount Pictures while attending Loyola Law School, where she graduated in ’75.

In 1977, Lloyd returned to Paramount as senior vice president, earning a reputation for her dealmaking skills. Watch on Deadline

Lloyd then became executive vice president at Disney in 1981, reportedly the highest position achieved by a woman in Hollywood at the time. During her time at Disney, along with Jeffrey Katzenberg, she helped solidify the studio’s landmark partnership with Pixar.

In 1994, Lloyd became one of the first executives hired to build DreamWorks, eventually becoming its chief operating officer until her departure in 2004.

The family asks those who wish to make a donation in Helene and Chuck’s memory to do so with U.S. Ski & Snowboard, in honor of their shared love of skiing and their support for the next generation of athletes.

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

combining a modular structure with a circular green perimeter of trees, the project reimagines the relationship between florence and its river through a seasonal, reversible, and ecological intervention.

angelo renna edited by christina vergopoulou November 5, 2026 3 min

Designed by Renna Studio and architect Angelo Renna, Firenze Little Island is an architectural proposal for a demountable, seasonal public floating platform on the Arno River in Florence, Italy. Sited along the Pescaia weir, where water flow is regulated, the project introduces an amphibious public space designed for cultural gatherings, orchestral performances, and recreation during the calmer summer months between May and September.

The installation, conceived by Renna Studio’s architect Angelo Renna, utilizes a lightweight, reversible modular structure comprising prefabricated floating pontoons topped with a timber beam substructure and finished decking. Encircling the central open-air deck is a segmented, circular planter ring approximately one meter (3.3 feet) high, accommodating 39 mature trees. This planted perimeter acts as both a spatial boundary and an environmental buffer, moderating the localized microclimate through natural shading and air circulation. Engineered for seasonal disassembly ahead of autumn and winter flood conditions, the pontoons, decking modules, and segmented planters are designed to be broken down, transported, and stored, with the root systems maintained in municipal nurseries until reassembly. Originally proposed with municipal backing in 2024 and supported by Heritage Superintendent Antonella Ranaldi in early 2026 before municipal plans paused, the proposal articulates a circular, low-impact strategy for re-integrating the historic riverfront into the civic fabric of Florence.

Delta Must Face Lawsuit Alleging Over 1 Million Flyers Paid For 'Window Seats' With No Window

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Delta Air Lines must face a proposed class action lawsuit over allegations that it charged passengers extra for window seats that have no window. The lawsuit alleges that Delta sells certain seats on its Boeing 737, Boeing 757 and Airbus A321 aircraft as window seats without revealing that some are positioned next to a blank cabin wall.

It is estimated that more than one million Delta passengers may have paid for these seats. A judge has now rejected the airline's attempt to have the case thrown out, following a similar ruling in July that allowed an almost identical lawsuit against United Airlines to proceed.

On Wednesday, November 4, US District Judge Ramon E. Reyes Jr. of the Eastern District of New York ruled that the passengers' breach of contract claims are not preempted by federal law, rejecting one of Delta's central arguments for dismissing the case. Delta, represented by law firm King & Spalding, had argued that the lawsuit should be dismissed on several grounds.

According to Law360, Delta's defense centered on its Contract of Carriage, which it says makes no promise to provide passengers with a window. It also explicitly states that seat assignments "are not guaranteed and form no part of this contract." Delta added that any claims based on seat map labels, tickets or marketing materials fall outside the contract and are therefore preempted by the Airline Deregulation Act (ADA).

On the other hand, the plaintiffs — represented by New York law firm Greenbaum Olbrantz — argued that Delta's use of the word "window" is a commitment to provide an actual window, particularly when it charges an additional fee for the seat. The plaintiffs’ lawyer, Carter Greenbaum, said when the suit was first filed:

"They sold customers window seats and ended up seating them next to a wall."

The case was originally filed on August 19, 2025, as Meyer v. Delta Air Lines, with Brooklyn resident Nicholas Meyer leading the lawsuit. According to the original complaint, Meyer paid extra for window seats on a trip from LaGuardia Airport (LGA) to John Wayne Airport (SNA) in Orange County, connecting through Delta's hub at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL).

However, when he boarded a Boeing 757-200 for the second sector of his journey, he discovered that seat 23F was next to a blank cabin wall rather than a window. As a result, he spent the roughly four-and-a-half-hour flight without the view he had paid for. The lawsuit states that some seats on Delta's 737s, 757s and A321s have no windows because of air conditioning ducts and other equipment behind the panels, but this was not made clear during booking.

The plaintiffs say they would not have paid for these seats if Delta had made their lack of windows clear. They also argue that Delta could easily prevent the problem by updating its booking system. The complaint points out that the airline already identifies other seat limitations during the selection process, such as restricted recline or a lack of under-seat storage, but does not identify seats without windows.

This is unlike rivals American Airlines and Alaska Airlines, which clearly inform customers during seat selection if a 'window seat' has no window. Southwest Airlines, which introduced assigned seating on January 27, 2026, has also added a warning to its seat selection page.

United clearly believes the lawsuit has no merit.

United must also face a similar lawsuit to Delta's after losing its own bid to have the case dismissed in July. United had claimed that the term "window" describes a seat's position next to the cabin wall, rather than promising passengers an actual view. It also argued that federal law prevented the plaintiffs from bringing their claims.

US District Judge James Donato rejected both arguments, stating that United's ticketing terms "plausibly establish that United expressly agreed to provide a seat with a window." Both lawsuits are seeking millions of dollars in damages, along with changes to how the airlines identify and advertise seats that have no window.

College cross-country: Indian Hills men, women post runner-up regional finishes

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

DES MOINES — The 19th-ranked Indian Hills men made program history while the Warrior women's cross-country program repeated as both teams finished second at the 2026 National Junior College Athletic Association (NJCAA) Division I Region 11 Championship.

The Warrior men placed three individuals in the top-10 and all five scorers in the top-15 to secure a second-place finish with 46 team points. All six individuals competing for the Warriors posted personal-best times at the event, hosted by Flint Hills Golf and Country Club in Burlington.

Indian Hills was led by a pair of impressive showings from sophomores Emmanuel Kiplagat and Brian Kiprop who placed fifth and sixth overall, respectively. Kiplagat cruised to a time of 23:53.5 for a new personal-best mark. Kiprop, who was in second place overall through two miles, finished with a personal-best mark of 23:53.8 to score for the Warriors.

Freshman David Moran put together his best race of his Indian Hills career, placing ninth overall with a time of 24:34.6. Fellow freshman Mathis Avrillon placed 12th overall with a time of 25:13 while freshman Daniel Gonzalez placed 14th overall, clocking in with a personal-best time of 25:18.5.

Sophomore Kohlby Newsom rounded out the Warriors' effort with a personal-best time of 26:25.6.

Freshman Aniqah Bailey led the pack for the Warrior women, placing eighth overall with a time of 18:25.8, the second-fastest time in school history and just 13 seconds shy of a school record that has stood since 2007.

The next four placers for the Warriors all boasted personal-best marks as Indian Hills tallied 58 team points to beat out third-place Iowa Central Community College by three points.

Freshman Sophie Robertson-Dover crossed the finish line with a time of 19:00.01 to place 11th overall while fellow rookie Tierney Carter placed 15th overall with a personal-best mark of 19:17.2.

Sophomore Serene Thompson and freshman Florence Nafamba were the next two individuals to cross the finish line, finishing in 16th and 17th place, respectively. Thompson, a member of last year's runner-up squad, posted a personal-best mark of 19:21.6 while Nafamba added a new season-best 19:57.6.

Prior to last season, the Warrior women had never placed higher than third at the NJCAA Region XI Championship.

Indian Hills will now focus on the upcoming NJCAA Division I Men's Cross-Country National Championship in Huntsville, Alabama on Nov. 14.
