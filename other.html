A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication. (Image from Shutterstock)

A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication.

Warren 37th District Court Chief Judge John M. Chmura, 63, was found guilty of intoxication while driving his Ford Fusion into a parked bus that was displaying hazard lights March 25. The court rejected the charge of Chmura driving with an elevated high blood alcohol content of 0.17 or greater, according to coverage on Law360.

Novi 52nd District Court Judge David Law noted that Chmura’s judgment had been affected, but he questioned the blood alcohol concentration at the time of the crash.

“This court may think it knows the answer to whether or not this was a high BAC case,” Law said. “But thinking it knows is not enough. It needs to be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.”

A bus driver sitting inside the bus waiting to resume his route was uninjured in the crash. Sterling Heights, Michigan, police officer Jonathan Journeau testified that Chmura smelled of alcohol, slurred his speech and couldn’t maintain his balance.

Chmura’s attorney, David J. Kramer of the David J. Kramer Law Firm, argued that prosecutors could not establish the higher offense because Journeau failed to determine when the judge’s final alcoholic beverage was consumed.

During rotation, resizing, and folding/unfolding, apps must preserve scroll position, text input, and media playback. Apps must also support multi-window environments.

By August 2027, new and updated apps will be required to target Android 17 (API level 37).

Over time, this “quality framework will progressively expand to incorporate Tier 2 and Tier 1 standards across foldables, tablets, Googlebooks, and Android XR.”

Apps that meet Tier 2 (better) or Tier 1 (best) will get “premier store badging, editorial spotlighting, and enhanced merchandising across Google Play as the platform bar elevates.”

On the gaming front, titles will need to add foldable screenshots to listings by April 2027, while Tier 3 large screen quality guidelines go into effect in August 2027.

Click to expand Image Protesters attempt to march to the ASEAN Summit venue in Manila but are stopped by hundreds of police officers, November 17, 2026. © 2026 Lian Buan/Human Rights Watch

Police in the Philippines arrested and detained at least 245 people protesting the visit of US President Donald Trump to Manila for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) summit. 

Protests in Manila started on November 15 opposing the Philippine government’s plan to build a technology manufacturing hub in Capas, Tarlac province, under the US government’s “Pax Silica” initiative. The construction will displace hundreds of Indigenous people and small farmers from their homes and farmlands.

An estimated 6,000 people joined the protests despite police efforts to contain demonstrations by banning them everywhere except in “freedom parks” – designated areas where rallies can be held without a permit.

Yet police arrested 236 people on the outskirts of Metro Manila who were traveling from the Southern Tagalog region to the main protest site. Among those detained were 29 farmers including members of the Indigenous Mangyan tribe, 19 journalists, 9 environmental defenders, and 6 human rights workers. Ephraim Cortez of the National Union of Peoples’ Lawyers said 19 children were detained. 

“This is the largest mass arrest we’ve seen under [President] Marcos,” protest leader Renato Reyes told Human Rights Watch. “Our whole delegation was arrested and taken to a police camp where lawyers were not given immediate access.”

Detainees are being held inside a police camp in a hall with poor ventilation, insufficient drinking water, and inadequate restroom facilities, according to activists and lawyers.

The protesters have been detained for three days without charge. Lawyers representing them expressed concern that they could face criminal charges of illegal assembly, alarms and scandals, and disobedience to authority. 

Police detained without charge another nine protesters at the main protest site on November 15 for attempting to march to the ASEAN summit venue. News footage shows police officers slapping and kicking a protester on the ground. 

International human rights law protects the right to peaceful assembly. Law enforcement may not carry out indiscriminate mass arrests even if a protest violates local laws or becomes disorderly. Arrests need to be based on individualized grounds and comply with the requirements of legality, necessity, and proportionality. Police can arrest people responsible for specific unlawful conduct but not just for taking part in a protest. All those arbitrarily detained for exercising their basic rights should be immediately released.

The Premier League have ruled that referees made a staggering six incorrect decisions in the latest round of matches as officials endured their worst weekend of the season so far.

It was unanimously ruled that referee Farai Hallam should have awarded Kai Havertz a penalty after he was tugged in the area during Arsenal's victory over Hull City, despite being in an offside position.

That is because the laws state that if a foul occurs before an offside offence, a penalty should be awarded.

Although Havertz was in an offside position, he had not committed an offence as he neither played the ball nor interfered with play.

The key match incidents panel (KMI) said: 'Given the chronology of events, a penalty should have been awarded.'

Farai Hallam's decision not to award Kai Havertz a penalty was deemed incorrect by the key match incidents panel

Coventry were wrongly denied a spot-kick in their defeat to Everton after James Tarkowski pulled Caleb Yirenkyi's hair

1. Everton vs Coventry City: Frank Lampard's side should have been awarded a penalty when James Tarkowski pulled Caleb Yirenkyi's hair.

2. Everton vs Coventry City: The Toffees' second goal, scored by Thierno Barry should not have stood as the ball was adjudged to have gone into the back of the net off his hand.

3. Arsenal vs Hull City: Kai Havertz was denied a penalty after being dragged down. The referee did not award a spot-kick because Havertz in an offside position. However, that decision was incorrect, as he was not committing an offside offence and a spot-kick should therefore have been awarded.

4. Crystal Palace vs Liverpool: Palace were awarded a penalty in the last minute of their 3-1 defeat to Liverpool after Milos Kerkez and Dario Osario clashed heads in the penalty area. The panel saw this as a natural coming together.

5. Ipswich Town vs Bournemouth: A penalty was given after Abdoul Ouattara tumbled over. The referee gave a foul against Lewis Cook despite Ouattara stepping across him.

6. Ipswich Town vs Bournemouth: Marcus Tavernier should have seen a second yellow card for time-wasting.

They did, however, praise the VAR for asking the referee to review the incident on the monitor, even though Hallam stuck with his on-field call after watching it back.

In the same weekend, Tottenham Hotspur complained to Pro Ref after Jayden Bogle's heavy challenge on Mathys Tel's ankle, which forced him off injured.

Spurs felt Bogle should have seen red, particularly after Crystal Palace's Jean-Philippe Mateta was sent off for a similar incident the week prior.

Reviewing the decision, the KMI panel were split but eventually concluded that the foul lacked 'the speed, force or intensity' to be considered serious foul play.

Two of the five panellists, however, felt that the challenge merited a red card.

Meanwhile, Crystal Palace's consolation penalty in the 92nd minute of their 3-1 defeat to Liverpool should never have been awarded.

The KMI panel unanimously agreed that the referee was wrong to award a penalty when Milos Kerkez and Dario Osorio clashed heads when contesting the ball, with the contact inevitable given both players were jumping.

However, VAR's decision not to intervene was supported because it was not a 'clear and obvious error'.
