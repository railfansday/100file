Tom Busby’s ‘Rockhampton Hangover’ Named 2026 Queensland Album of the Year

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Busby beat out a shortlist of albums from the likes of Clea, Radium Dolls, Platonic Sex, 2 Charm and The Jungle Giants.

Tom Busby collects The Triffid’s 2026 Queensland Album of the Year with his debut solo longplay, Rockhampton Hangover.

The Rockhampton-raised singer and songwriter, Busby, one-half of the chart-topping blues and roots duo Busby Marou, beat out a shortlist of albums from the likes of Clea, Radium Dolls, Platonic Sex, 2 Charm and The Jungle Giants.

Now in its 11th year, the public-voted award celebrates Queensland’s music community and was presented at Brisbane live music venue The Triffid, part of the Live Nation portfolio of venues. Produced by Ben Kweller and recorded in the Texas Hill Country, the album is a meditation on family, home and finding your way through change.

“I’m pretty bloody stoked,” Busby says of his award. “It took me a long time to get around to making a solo record, and when I finally did, I kept coming back to stories from home. To have The Triffid name Rockhampton Hangover Album of the Year means a lot.  Big thanks to Ben Kweller for making it with me, to everyone who’s given it a spin and to The Triffid crew for backing Queensland music.”

“Tom has given so much to Queensland music through Busby Marou, so it’s great to see him take this step and have people really connect with the record,” adds John Collins, co-founder of The Triffid and bass player with Powderfinger, which is reuniting next year for what promises to be one of the year’s biggest tours down under.

“Making your first solo album is a big thing. Having the fans vote it their favorite makes it even sweeter.”

The award, explains Collins, was created to bring attention to music from Australia’s Sunshine State, and sparking conversations about the artists making it.

Following its release in February of this year, the independently-released Rockhampton Hangover strolled to No. 3 on the ARIA Albums Chart. As a member of Busby Marou, Busby has landed three albums in the national top 10, including a No. 1 with 2017’s Postcards From The Shell House, and won a hattrick of APRA Awards for blues & roots work of the year (2012, 2018, 2021).

With his latest win, Busby will receive promotional support from media enterprise The Music and community radio station 4ZZZ, an eight-hour production day at The Triffid and A$500 in merchandise credit from American Apparel.

Empty ATC Tower At Hollywood Burbank Airport Leaves Pilots To Clear Own Takeoffs & Landings

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Pilots flying into and out of Hollywood Burbank Airport (BUR) had to coordinate their own takeoffs and landings on Wednesday evening after the airport's control tower went "ATC Zero," meaning no controllers were on duty. ATC recordings captured pilots from Southwest Airlines and Alaska Airlines announcing their positions to each other over a shared radio frequency, a procedure normally reserved for airports without a control tower.

The cause of the tower closure has not been officially confirmed, although sources suggest it was likely caused by a staffing issue. It is the second time in just over a year that Burbank's tower has been left without controllers, following a nearly six-hour closure in October 2025 caused by absences during the federal government shutdown.

As first reported by CBS LA, the situation occurred on Wednesday evening. In the ATC recordings, a Burbank controller informed aircraft that the tower was going ATC Zero and that radar services were being terminated. Pilots then switched to the Common Traffic Advisory Frequency (CTAF), where they began announcing their movements to other aircraft.

For example, a Southwest pilot can be heard reporting that their aircraft was on short final for Runway 8, while an Alaska crew announced that it was taxiing from the terminal area to the holding pad. The situation reportedly occurred at least twice on Wednesday evening, with the tower left without controllers for periods of around 30 minutes.

However, pilots were not completely without ATC support, as a controller covering the wider region continued guiding aircraft toward Burbank before advising them to switch to CTAF for the final stage of their approach. One pilot was heard telling another:

"Hey, they have no controller right now. They're CTAF."

CBS LA initially cited a source who said the issue was due to staffing shortages, adding that the same situation "could even happen again later tonight." The issue reportedly did occur again, with the tower going dark in 30-minute windows. There are suggestions that the interruptions were linked to mandatory rest breaks for a lone controller on duty — however, neither the FAA nor Burbank Airport has officially confirmed the reason for the repeated closures.

The airport faced a similar situation on October 6, 2025, when its tower had no controllers for nearly six hours. On that occasion, Southern California TRACON handled operations remotely from San Diego, with average delays exceeding two hours. It occurred during a 43-day federal government shutdown, during which controllers were working without pay.

Burbank's airspace has been named among high-risk "hotspots" due to the mix of helicopter and airplane movements, and its proximity to nearby Van Nuys Airport. Passengers at the airport expressed their concerns, with one telling CBS LA that it could be a safety issue if the pilots are "trying to jockey by themselves about where to land, and who goes next."

Despite a 4% increase in air traffic, the time spent on managing air traffic has declined.

ATC Zero is one of three designations the FAA uses, alongside ATC Alert and ATC Limited, to describe reduced or unavailable services at an ATC facility. When a tower goes ATC Zero, a neighboring facility takes over part of its workload, while pilots at the affected airport will typically announce their positions and intentions over CTAF.

Via CTAF, pilots broadcast their positions and intentions, so other crews can maintain awareness of nearby traffic. This includes announcing when they are taxiing, approaching the runway, taking off, or landing. Pilots must then rely more heavily on these radio calls and their own visual awareness to avoid conflicts.

Although this is common at smaller airports, it is more significant when it happens at a busy Class C commercial airport like Burbank. The airport is served by several major airlines, including Southwest, Alaska, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines and United Airlines, and typically handles over 100 daily flights.

Despite the Barcelona loanee’s fine form, Jamie Carragher has implored his former club to move for a full-back in January if the right opportunity arises, and there might just be another one from Camp Nou.

As reported by Graeme Bailey for TEAMtalk, the LaLiga champions are prepared to sanction an exit for Alejandro Balde amid interest from Liverpool, with Barca head coach Hansi Flick indicating that the 23-year-old isn’t part of his plans.

His representative Jorge Mendes has reportedly held talks with the Merseyside club to inform them of the conditions surrounding a potential deal for the player, who’s viewed by Anfield chiefs as an ‘attractive option’.
