Steam Deck performance gets a reality check with Valve's new FPS data

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Benchmark tools for generic gaming rigs could be coming soon.

Since Valve first announced its Steam Deck Verified program back in 2021, players have found that a game’s “Verified” status doesn’t always match how well it actually runs on the handheld. Now, new Steam Deck frame rate data being shared by Valve itself can help players dig deeper into the kinds of performance they can expect from many Steam Deck games.

Valve has been collecting anonymized frame rate data from Steam players who opt in since February. Yesterday evening, the company announced it has started sharing that data on many Steam Store pages as averages and histograms for both the Steam Deck and Steam Deck OLED.

Valve acknowledges that players enabling VSync or frame rate limits can bias the collected data somewhat and that the measurements don’t account for custom in-game settings that Steam Deck users may tinker with to get a game running. Regardless, Valve says it has nonetheless “found that the framerate data consolidates around relatively narrow ranges.”

This kind of data can be extremely useful for distinguishing between the wide range of performance levels that can exist among “Verified” Steam Deck games. Both Cyberpunk 2077 and Skyrim: Special Edition earn the same Steam Deck Verified badge, for instance, but the former sees average Deck frame rates in the 30-40 FPS range, while the latter usually achieves 50-60 FPS on the hardware.

Valve’s frame rate data is even more useful for games that the company insists are “Unsupported” on Steam Deck because their “graphics setting cannot be configured to run well” on the hardware or because Valve is “still working on adding support for this game.” Despite warnings like these, games like Starfield, Silent Hill 2, and Assassin’s Creed Syndicate, to name just a few, all show extremely playable frame rates reported by actual Steam Deck users. Assassin’s Creed Syndicate in particular approaches a 60 FPS average on the Steam Deck OLED hardware despite its “Unsupported” status.

Frame rate data can also be useful for judging the single-player modes of games whose multiplayer doesn’t work under SteamOS. Watch Dogs 2, for example, is officially Unsupported on Steam Deck because of anti-cheat compatibility issues, but Steam Deck players report averages of 40+ FPS on the game’s single-player modes.

Of course, collecting and averaging frame rate data for the Steam Deck(s) is made simpler by their set hardware configuration. When it comes to communicating expected frame rates for the wide range of hardware in more generalized gaming PCs, Valve says it is “always looking for new ways to help developers understand the experiences customers are having with their games.”

Back in April, JSON files buried in the Steam client pointed to a potential Steam “Framerate Estimator” tool that could let users “Select an App and a PC config to get a chart of estimated framerates, based on the framerates of other Steam users,” according to strings contained within. That could be incredibly useful for Steam users who want to go beyond the listed Minimum and Recommended hardware requirements without relying on shady-looking third-party FPS-estimator websites. In the meantime, is sharing average frame rate data for the fixed-configuration Steam Machine too much to ask?

Webb reveals the hidden activity inside a turbulent stellar nursery

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Webb peers through thick cosmic dust in NGC 7129, revealing hidden young stars, protostars, glowing gas, and powerful outflows.

Today’s Image of the Day from the European Space Agency reveals a stellar nursery where young stars are changing the clouds that gave birth to them.

The James Webb Space Telescope captured NGC 7129, a star-forming region about 3,300 light-years from Earth.

Thick clouds of gas and dust obscure much of the activity at visible wavelengths.

Webb’s infrared vision can penetrate that material, bringing previously hidden stars and structures into view.

The result shows much more than a collection of newborn stars. NGC 7129 contains objects at several stages of early stellar development.

Some are still gathering material. Others have moved closer to the long, stable period of life that includes stars such as our Sun.

Stars do not all mature at the same rate. More massive stars generally form and evolve faster than smaller ones.

That difference allows astronomers to study several chapters of stellar development within the same region.

The brightest central star in this scene is LkH(alpha) 234. It has about five to eight times the mass of the Sun and produces the image’s most noticeable diffraction pattern.

It is a pre-main-sequence star, meaning it has largely finished collecting mass but has not yet begun the sustained hydrogen fusion that will define its main-sequence life.

At this stage, gravity continues to pull the star inward. As it contracts, its interior grows hotter.

Eventually, conditions in the core will become intense enough for hydrogen fusion to begin.

This transition matters because a young star does not develop quietly.

Even before reaching the main sequence, it can send powerful streams of material into the gas around it.

Those outflows can reshape a stellar nursery across surprisingly large distances.

The clearest evidence appears to the left of LkH(alpha) 234. A golden cavity stretches roughly 3.5 light-years across.

Outflows produced during an earlier stage of the star’s development carved into the surrounding molecular cloud, which consists largely of hydrogen.

Radiation and outflowing material from the star energize the gas and make it glow.

Some gas gets swept away, but the process does not simply shut down star formation.

Other material becomes compressed. Dense pockets can then provide the conditions needed for additional stars to develop.

Several young stars are visible inside the cavity. Some are also pre-main-sequence stars, and they produce stellar winds of their own.

As these winds push into surrounding gas, they create curved structures called bow shocks.

The stars also create a hot environment beside colder, denser molecular material. The boundary between these environments is known as a photodissociation region.

Energetic radiation breaks hydrogen molecules apart into individual atoms there.

The process changes the chemistry, heating and cooling of the cloud and helps determine how it evolves.

Molecular clouds are the raw material for star formation, but the stars born inside them gradually help destroy them.

Radiation, winds, and outflows can disperse gas or alter its chemistry.

Over millions of years, those effects can erode the cloud and change where new stars are able to form.

NGC 7129 gives astronomers a particularly useful view of that process because its stars have reached different developmental stages.

Their combined activity shows how star formation can both disrupt a cloud and compress parts of it into conditions favorable for further stellar growth.

The right side of Webb’s image tells an earlier part of the story.

Clumpy red material conceals protostars that are younger than the pre-main-sequence stars visible elsewhere in the region.

Protostars form after cold molecular gas and dust collapse and fragment into dense concentrations.

They continue gaining mass from surrounding material.

At the same time, they can eject narrow, extremely hot outflows that slam into the dense matter around them.

Those collisions produce shocks within the gray material surrounding the protostars.

They help create the textured appearance seen by Webb and contribute to the region’s red glow.

Several protostars are active at once, so their outflows overlap from Earth’s viewpoint. That makes this side of NGC 7129 look especially tangled.

Yet the apparent disorder contains valuable information about stars that would otherwise remain hidden behind dust.

Infrared observations give astronomers access to these deeply embedded objects before they emerge from their birth clouds.

In NGC 7129, Webb captures stars at different points along that path while also revealing how strongly they affect their surroundings.

The nursery will continue changing as its young stars mature.

Gas will disperse, dense pockets may collapse, and some newly formed stars will eventually settle into the hydrogen-fusing stage that powers the Sun today.

Forget Mayo: Spread This Rich Ingredient On Bread For A More Flavorful Grilled Cheese

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Butter gives your grilled cheese great richness, and using mayo makes it far crispier, but neither adds a ton of strong flavors that can shine through high-quality cheese. That's why you need toum, a Lebanese garlic spread that delivers intense taste with very little effort.

Toum is as rich as mayonnaise and as light and fluffy as whipped cream, created by smashing garlic into a paste and then gradually whipping in oil until it has an airy but glossy consistency. For texture, its incredible fluffiness makes it easy to slather on, covering every last inch in a swiftly melting browning agent that makes the bread crispy. But for flavoring, it makes the end product taste like garlic bread, full of rich fats and pungent aromatics that only requires a swift spread with a knife. It may seem like you're using a lot and will over-season your sandwich, but since so much of it is air, you're actually only using a moderate amount that's quite easy to thoroughly apply.

The combination of melty gooeyness inside and crisp, garlicky exteriors is a second-to-none combination that pairs well with a wide variety of cheeses. Add in the sharpness of the lemon juice, and you get something that can brighten up even the heaviest, fat-laden cheeses quite well. The only caveat is that all that garlic gives it a fairly cool smoke point, so you'll need to use low to medium heat to avoid making it grow bitter.

Since its main flavors are garlic and lemon, toum isn't quite as universal a pairing as mayonnaise or butter. It goes great with anything semi-hard and creamy, but its intensity may clash with anything stronger. You'll also want to know the type of oil used in your toum. Something made with grapeseed, canola, or other neutral varieties is far more compatible than a spread made with a flavorful extra virgin olive oil.

Cheeses with a nuttier profile, like Jarlsberg, Gruyère, and Manchego, are all solid pairings. They tend to be a bit earthier, grounding the brightness of the toum, so it isn't too intense. Since it has no eggs, you can even combine it with melted vegan cheese since nutritional yeast is typically quite aromatic and nutty. Richer, well-rounded varieties can also be quite nice, so some shreds of gouda, raclette, and any triple-cream variety are always welcome additions.

Flavored cheeses are one of the easiest ways to upgrade any melty sandwich, but not all are compatible with toum. While something rich in herbs or wine can complement the garlic quite well, a variety with a lot of sweetened fruit or more lemon can be a bit much. Since toum is already quite tangy, you may also want to steer clear of acidic cheeses like chèvre, feta, or cream cheese. While some may like the brightness, it can quickly become overly intense, and you may find yourself making a new sandwich.

Don't Let Conflict Erode Your Relationship

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Posted November 10, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

Couples can argue over even the slightest difference of opinion. It can be as simple as preferring different kinds of breakfast cereals. You want the healthy organic variety, and your partner wants the sugar-coated flakes he had as a child. This trivial dispute often escalates until, in the worst-case scenario, you’re ready to break up.

How did things get so out of control? If you love someone, you should be able to handle some daily bickering. Relationship researchers often point out ways that conflicts become dysfunctional. In the newest approach, focus is placed on emotional regulation as the key to neutralizing some of these unpleasant interactions.

In a new approach to understanding relationship difficulties, University of Quebec’s Aurélie Thuot-Jolicoeur and colleagues (2026) posed a conflict-ridden scenario at the outset of their paper. A couple argues over how to spend some of their time while on a Parisian vacation. But instead of luxuriating in the lovely scenery and enjoying all that Paris has to offer, they began to quibble over whether to see a museum on one of their precious days in the French capital. One partner excitedly suggested this activity, but the other shot her down, claiming to be too tired. Suspecting this was an excuse for disinterest, the protagonist in this story becomes distressed. Over the next few days, the memory popped up unexpectedly, creating renewed emotional wounds.

In posing the alternative to this unpleasant outcome, the authors suggest that the distressed partner could acknowledge feeling upset and then ask why the partner wasn’t interested. Empathizing with the partner rather than becoming upset could enhance the relationship as they “form a more understanding and insightful attitude”

If you’ve ever been in a situation such as this (maybe not in Paris…), you can undoubtedly feel some empathy for each relationship partner as the original scenario unfolded. To get from scenario one to scenario two, the authors suggest, requires that you engage your emotion regulation strategies. To do so, they propose, involves the motivational approach known as Self-Determination Theory (SDT). According to SDT, instead of avoiding conflict or reacting immaturely by sulking or yelling, you are better off using integrative regulation, in which you allow yourself to notice and ultimately accept your emotions, even the unpleasant ones. You can now enter into a conflict constructively as you seek to learn from the conflict about your own emotions and those of your partner.

Based on SDT, the U. Quebec authors proposed that it’s not conflict alone that causes problems; it’s also the tendency to ruminate unproductively over a past disagreement with your partner. The memory of an event, they suggest, can be just as important as the event itself, especially if it fails to be resolved or brings up unpleasant emotions. You need to be able to control your emotions when those memories pop up in much the same way you do when encountering the situation in the first place.

To test this idea, Thuot-Jolicoeur et al. asked participants to describe a memory of a significant conflict they experienced with a partner. To measure emotion regulation from the SDT perspective, they asked participants to rate items tapping dysregulation (“When I feel negative emotions, I can’t concentrate on the other things I have to do”) and integrative regulation (“On occasions, feeling negative emotions helped me to understand something about the situation I was in”). These were part of the dispositional emotion regulation questions. Separately, the authors rated the extent to which these strategies were used in the recalled event itself.

The conflicts couples brought into the study included these three themes: (1) sexuality and intimacy; (2) needs, values, and interpersonal differences; and (3) practical conflicts. Relationship quality was the outcome of the analyses using emotion regulation of each partner as predictors.

As hypothesized, when partners used integrative regulation when recalling a conflict, their relationship satisfaction was higher than when they used dysfunctional regulation. Over time, the authors proposed, if the recall of a conflicted memory prompts renewed use of dysregulated strategies (hostility, withdrawal, or rumination), the negative effects will only increase exponentially, driving the couple members further apart. If, instead, couples put the conflict in the rearview mirror through integrative regulation, they can move toward greater closeness and understanding.

Knowing that the dysregulated recall of a troubling event with your partner can be as detrimental to your relationship as the event itself can be helpful as you and your partner try to move past the difficult emotions. As in the Parisian vacation example, the partner who felt hurt and dismissed could use these feelings to gain important self-insights. Maybe the partner truly was tired and needed time to rest and refresh. This doesn’t mean that the partner had somehow fallen out of love or was otherwise ready to move on from the relationship.

Reworking the recall of an event that caused distress can help to nip your feelings of being hurt and rejected in the bud. Furthermore, as you try to move on, it can also be helpful to isolate the event itself as the cause of your distress rather than generalize to everything being wrong in the entire relationship. Sharing your feelings in a nonthreatening or non-accusatory way can get you and your partner onto a path toward greater understanding.

To sum up, relationships are never 100 percent smooth going, but zeroing in on the conflicts—and their memory—that shape your relationship can help you move from dysfunction to emotional acceptance.

What’s happened?

This week, The Children’s Place Inc. filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey.

In the filing, the company listed assets of approximately $742.3 million and debts of approximately $881.3 million on the books.

The filing comes just months after the company reported its Q2 2026 financial results in mid-September. Those results, which covered the period up to August 1, showed significant sales declines. 

In Q2, The Children’s Place posted revenue of $241.8 million, an 18.9% decline from the same quarter a year earlier. Gross profits fell to $83.3 million from $101.3 million for the same quarter a year earlier. 

The company reported it had 514 stores open at the end of the quarter, an increase of 19 stores from the previous quarter. It also operates an online storefront.

At the time, the company said comparables weighed on the results, particularly because “last year’s [2025] strong back-to-school demand was uniquely elevated by customer demand ahead of anticipated tariff price increases, which skewed those financial results compared to this year’s.”

Announcing the bankruptcy this week, The Children’s Place says it intends to “pursue a value-maximizing sale transaction” with the goal of selling “substantially all of its assets.”

Unfortunately, some of them will. Though The Children’s Place is putting on its best face and insisting it wants to see the retail chain continue operating under new ownership, it also says that, even if that happens, some stores will be closing.

As of the end of Q2, The Children’s Place said it had 514 locations. Those locations include stores in the United States, Canada, Bahrain, Israel, Mexico, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Venezuela.

The company so far has not disclosed which locations will close as part of the bankruptcy process.

In the United States, the largest number of The Children’s Place stores are located in California, with 50 locations. It also has a significant number of stores in Florida and New York.

PLCE stock craters in 2026

The Children’s Place was founded in 1969 and went public in 1997. In 2018, the company’s stock price hit an all-time high of more than $161 per share. But since about 2021, the company’s stock price has steadily declined, and in 2026 it has cratered.

In January, the company’s stock price hit a 2026 high of around $5.09 per share. But since the beginning of the year, its stock price has now declined more than 95%.

The Clearest Sign Of The Winter Blues

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Energy, mood or sleep lost to worry? A UK survey found a clearer winter pattern for one of the three.

Energy, mood or sleep lost to worry? A UK survey found a clearer winter pattern for one of the three.

Low energy is the clearest sign of the winter blues among three symptoms studied in young people in the UK, ahead of low mood and losing sleep over worry.

Low mood is also more pronounced in winter, although the difference is smaller.

Shorter days may play a part by shifting the body clock and affecting when young people feel alert, while changes in exercise and social routines could also contribute.

The researchers analysed almost 6,000 interviews with over 4,000 people aged 16 to 24 who took part in the UK’s Understanding Society survey.

They compared interviews conducted in December, January and February with those conducted during the rest of the year, using data from 2022 and 2023 for the main analysis.

On a 0–100 scale, the average score for low energy was about 3 points higher in winter, while the score for low mood was about 2 points higher.

The estimate for sleep lost through worry was smaller and compatible with no seasonal difference.

The figures describe differences between survey reports collected at different times of year, so they give a picture of the group average rather than trace how any one person’s symptoms changed.

Earlier Canadian survey research found that a wider range of depressive symptoms varied by season among young people than among older adults (Lukmanji et al., 2020).

Sleep schedules continue to shift through adolescence and into early adulthood, so the timing of daylight may influence when young people feel most alert.

Winter can also change the shape of an ordinary week, with fewer chances to be active outdoors or spend time with friends in person.

Feeling short of energy could make it harder to stay engaged through a day of lessons or a long shift, even when the person’s mood has barely changed.

The study was published in the journal PLOS ONE (Guan et al., 2026).

While I have tons of experience with smash dumplings, I made my very first smash tacos while developing the recipe for chicken Caesar smash tacos and coming up with the concepts for meatball sub smash tacos and Philly cheesesteak smash tacos. As I quickly learned, smash tacos are pretty simple to make, but if you haven’t done it before, there are definitely a few things to know.

Here are a few tips to make your first experience making them just a little smoother:

Whether you’re mixing in a ton of spices or keeping it simple with just salt and pepper, it’s important to work the ground meat until it feels tacky; otherwise, the meat won’t hold together on the tortilla when you sear it, and you’ll end up with a fragmented patty instead of one smooth layer.

Even if you’re using 90% lean, beef shrinks when it cooks, so if your patty is beef, spread it to the very edge of the tortilla—maybe even a little over. That gives you the best chance of getting full coverage once it’s cooked. Chicken, on the other hand, doesn’t shrink much at all (in my experience), so in that case it’s fine to leave a little border around the edges.

Let the patties sear, undisturbed, for a solid two minutes before flipping them. This gives you the best shot at that caramelized crust, and that’s where all the flavor is!

A smash taco doesn’t always require cheese (see the gyro smash tacos below!), but if you’re going there, I highly recommend using the broiler to melt it. In my experience, Kraft singles are the only type of cheese that will melt completely before the bottom of the smash taco burns. It might be an extra step, but it’s worth it to avoid an acrid bottom.

A truly amazing smash taco needs a plethora of toppings, so don’t hold back here! Load it up. While the OG smash tacos were a mash-up of a smash burger and a taco, you don’t need to limit yourself to classic burger flavors here. Ground chicken patties are topped with squiggles of Caesar dressing, a mound of romaine, crispy croutons, and tons of cheese for a dinner that manages to make even a taco feel healthy.

I love ordering a cheesesteak when I’m out, but if I’m craving one at home, these Philly cheesesteak tacos are my lazy go-to. Okarun's backstory is Dandadan's saddest and best chapter

It's hard to explain briefly why Dandadan is one of the best manga and anime that have come out in the past five years. The series' eclectic nature and mix of genres means it escapes easy categorization. "The author is a genius" and "the anime adaptation is fantastic" could be reductive when recommending it to someone. Luckily, chapter 252 of the Dandadan manga, published on Nov. 9, 2026, does a much better job of explaining why this series is a masterpiece.

One of Dandadan's oddities is that, until now, author Yukinobu Tatsu has refrained from showing his readers anything about the background and past of one of his two protagonists: the sweet, spectacled, alien-obsessed Ken Takakura, nicknamed Okarun. This did not hurt the series nor Okarun's characterization, which was developed through his interactions with the rest of the cast, especially co-protagonist Momo Ayase. Now, we finally know that Tatsu had a long-term vision for this character — and it's a brutal one.

Spoilers for Dandadan beyond the anime follow.In the current Dandadan arc, Okarun has lost his supernatural powers after fulfilling his pact with Turbo Granny. The foul-mouthed yokai has disappeared, and Okarun was left without means to defend himself when new enemies attacked him, Momo, Aira, and Jiji during a trip to a theme park. Okarun had to rely on the Ogre Club, a mystical weapon provided by Momo's grandma, Seiko, but it turns out that the tool has unpleasant side effects. Okarun lost himself to rage and attacked friends and foes indiscriminately, until Turbo Granny made her triumphant return. In chapter 252, "Ken Takakura," the spirit enters Okarun's mind to bring him back to reason, and in the process, she witnesses the boy's traumatic past.

Ken Takakura's parents split up after his mom found a new partner. She asked little Ken if he wanted to go with her, but he decided to stay with his dad. In a gut-wrenching sequence, Okarun remembers realizing his mom was actually relieved he didn't follow her, since he could have spoiled her fancy new life. Things kept getting worse for young Ken from that moment onwards. He was beaten up by other kids, bullied, and sexually humiliated. Meanwhile, his father was crushed by debt and experienced angry outbursts, leaving poor Ken a lonely, traumatized child.

One day, as he was running away from some bullies, Ken found an occult magazine that became his lifeline. He hoped and prayed every day that aliens would one day show up to "save his heart." They didn't. Okarun kept getting bullied as he grew up, until he couldn't take it anymore. In another chilling sequence, Ken is shown on top of a building, ready to jump off. However, thinking about distant, happy memories with his family made him lose courage (or rather, he found the courage to live), and he broke down crying.

After that suicide attempt, Ken kept getting abused at school. In the present, the influence of the Ogre Club mixes his memories with nefarious thoughts of murder. However, a voice (likely Turbo Granny) tells him that he has forgotten something: His prayers were answered. Someone did show up. The chapter ends with a panel of Momo sitting in front of Okarun's desk, shielding him from the bullies, a callback to the manga's first chapter.

At the beginning of Dandadan, in the much-discussed scene where Momo and Okarun are held by the Serpoian aliens, the boy said that, as he didn't have any friends, he looked up to the skies every day hoping that the aliens would show up. But they never did. Instead, Momo stood up for him. At that moment, this motivated Okarun to fully transform into Turbo Granny, breaking the Serpoians' bonds. It was a pretty traditional setup for a story that mixes romance and action, and it was hard to predict that the author would wait five more years before revealing the true depths of his protagonist's trauma.

Choosing to withhold his main character's backstory that long is a bold move for Tatsu, proving that he has confidence in his storytelling and character work. It also recontextualizes the current arc, which saw Momo lose her recent memories, including those of Okarun, which left Ken struggling to remind her of the bond they shared. Now, fans know that Momo isn't just Okarun's first crush or the girl who stood up for him at school. She's literally the person who saved his life.

Bullying, abuse, and suicide are difficult themes that are often present in manga and anime, but they're not always treated with the sensitivity required. Dandadan's long-term storytelling set up a big reveal about Okarun's traumatic past, but Tatsu has already shown that, despite his talent for action and comedy, he's not afraid to touch on other, more difficult themes. Vamola's backstory was, until now, one of the best and most traumatic parts of the manga (and one that I can't wait to see adapted in the anime). Okarun's backstory hits those same powerful emotional notes that make Dandadan a masterpiece and his author one of the best mangaka currently active.

Supreme Court Clears Way for Border Wall Construction in Big Bend National Park

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The US Supreme Court issued an unsigned decision on Monday allowing the Trump administration to resume building border barriers and installing surveillance equipment in the Big Bend region of southwestern Texas, including inside Big Bend National Park.

The ruling lifts a temporary halt on construction ordered in early October by US District Judge Kathleen Cardone in El Paso. While litigation over the legality of the 500-mile border project continues in the lower courts, construction crews can now move forward with work. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented from the decision.

For visitors and locals in the remote desert region, the physical ramifications of the project began appearing over the summer. In August, construction crews brought bulldozers near Santa Elena Canyon (one of Big Bend National Park’s most famous landmarks) and graded more than a mile of land. According to administration officials, Customs and Border Protection plans to install four- to six-foot-tall vehicle barriers, surveillance gear, and an access road in the area.

Opponents of the construction, which include a bipartisan mix of Texas ranchers, local landowners, and conservationists, warn that barriers will permanently alter the desert. Critics say the project threatens to cut off access to the river, disrupt livestock and crops, and interfere with the area’s status as the largest dark sky reserve on the planet.

Legal opposition to the project began in April when a coalition consisting of an environmental organization, a river guide, a church preservation group, and local landowners filed a lawsuit to stop the project. The coalition argued that federal officials had exceeded their authority by waiving environmental and conservation laws, such as the National Environmental Policy Act and the Endangered Species Act, without approval from Congress.

In early October, Judge Cardone temporarily blocked the construction after finding that the coalition was likely to succeed on at least one of its legal claims. After Judge Cardone denied the administration’s request to pause her order during an appeal, the government filed an emergency application with the Supreme Court, describing the lower court’s block as “baseless.”

The federal government argued that halting work would leave a gap of more than 300 miles in border defenses, which he said could draw cartels and other bad actors.

Still, the coalition urged the justices to keep the block in place. They argued the administration sought emergency relief to immediately restart bulldozing, blasting, and road building across an ecologically and culturally sensitive landscape, warning that continued work would permanently damage historic resources and private ranch lands.

In Monday’s order, the Supreme Court majority acknowledged the plaintiffs’ argument that construction in the Big Bend Sector would “diminish their ability to use and enjoy the area.” However, the majority concluded that federal law grants the Homeland Security secretary the power to waive conservation and environmental statutes, noting that the law shows “a clear preference for expeditious border construction over the very harms” cited by the challengers.

The decision marks the first major policy victory for President Trump at the Supreme Court following last week’s midterm elections, as the underlying lawsuit proceeds in the lower court.

CSS Optimization: Is Using Declarations Just Once (UDJO) Still Effective Today?

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

CSS optimization is a deceptive topic. On the one hand, it feels like something simple that we should already have all the tooling for — especially when it comes to minification. On the other hand, the idea that we already exploited “everything” there is around CSS optimization may be considered naive. Such a claim looks hard to defend for a language that evolves almost daily.

In this article, I’d like to pick a technique that’s at least 18 years old — UDJO, or “using declarations just once.”

The technique is inspired by the principle of DRY, “don’t repeat yourself”, in that it works by identifying and reducing (if not eliminating) declaration repetition.

So, if there’s something like display: flex and it appears two or more times, it gets reconciled in one rule:

The outcome of the technique depends on how intelligently the deduplication is done and how long the selectors are. Intelligence determines if UDJO leads to an actual maintainability advantage — can the change of a declaration really be done to all the selectors attached? And the selector length influences how big file size savings are.

If you’re interested, my article on “DRY CSS” describes the mechanics in more detail. The question here is: Is UDJO still effective and still relevant today?

When we try to answer this question, however, we end up with a problem: When sites are complex and their style sheet declarations have not been de-duplicated before, UDJO can require a lot of work.

To save some time, I built tooling for this work. While it didn’t seem like it would immediately be as comprehensive as manually going through and testing style sheets, it seemed realistic enough that a tool could catch some repetition.

Skipping ahead, I created CSS Dedup not only to identify repetition, but also to deduplicate what could safely be optimized (CSS Dedup has some documentation on this but in essence, deduplication affects selector locations, which is part of how the cascade works). This, then, gives us some data:

Important note: These values cannot be used to tell CSS quality—they depend on selector length and also on verifying if deduplication, in the respective case, actually leads to better maintainability. Also note that this leaves out changes after compression—this was not part of the experiment but would be fair to test, too.

For the question whether UDJO still works today, this should be a yes. It works as well as to even come with file size savings.

As mentioned at the beginning, UDJO’s effectiveness generally depends on the kind of repetition as well as on the length of selectors.

This can be managed, however: While tools like CSS Dedup make no difference between declaration use cases (and are therefore blunt), it is possible to configure them so as to reap the performance benefits.

As the data taken for this article suggest, file size savings may be achieved in most cases. Considering that this is based on safe changes, this means a low-cost performance optimization vector — especially when applied to minified output. It’s still a good idea to couch that against the use of further compression (e.g. gzip/Brotli), but at face value, deduplication shows how it can reduce the size of CSS.

For anyone generally interested in CSS optimization, maintainability, and performance, UDJO should probably be part of the overall approach to CSS. But since the technique has been niche and since tooling is new, I invite you to review, test, and explore this vector. It seems hard to argue that we have reached the ceiling of how far we can take CSS optimization.
