NBA team grades: Report card for all 30 teams after the first month

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The first month of the 2026-27 NBA season is in the books, and it's time to take stock of what's happened so far. Let's hand out some early grades.

It might be too soon to draw sweeping conclusions, but there's been enough basketball played to make preliminary judgements about the contenders falling short of expectations and the surprising teams surpassing what most experts anticipated. Evaluating each team's performance goes beyond its record, with roster construction, player development and overall trajectory factoring into whether a season is trending in the right direction.

Injuries to several stars and key contributors have shaped the league conversation through the opening month. Some teams have responded well to the adversity. Others have already dug themselves a hole that could prove difficult to escape. As a result, the grades cover a wide range of outcomes: six teams earned an A from USA TODAY Sports through the season's first month, while 10 received a D or F.

Here are grades for all 30 NBA teams now that the first month of the 2026-27 season is complete.

NBA PANIC METER: Which teams should worry after first month of season?

Records as of games played on Nov. 20. Preseason record projections via USA TODAY Sports.

The Hawks are right in the thick of the Eastern Conference playoff race again with guard Nickeil Alexander-Walker leading their backcourt, but there have been some troubling early signs. Jalen Johnson's production has dipped across the board from last season's breakout campaign, veteran closer C.J. McCollum is sidelined for at least another month and Atlanta has benefited from the NBA's second-easiest schedule to date. The Hawks' issues are flying under-the-radar, but they've struggled to show they belong among the East's top contenders when facing the NBA's best teams thus far.

After thriving without Jayson Tatum during much of last year's regular season, the Celtics haven't missed a beat in their first games since trading Jaylen Brown this past offseason. Tatum has elevated his playmaking, and Paul George looks rejuvenated as his new running mate in Boston. The Celtics have the NBA's best NET rating as of Nov. 18 and even got the better of Brown in their first matchup against the Philadelphia 76ers.

The Nets' roster is a mess because they've used seven first-round draft picks the past two years and none has demonstrated franchise-player potential to this point. Their top three guards (Egor Demin, Nolan Traore and Mikel Brown Jr.) haven't played much together yet due to minutes restrictions and skillset overlap. Their past four losses have come by an average of 24.5 points, and Julius Randle was spotted yelling at his teammates on the bench before calling their Nov. 18 loss to the Chicago Bulls "one of the worst games I've been a part of." After years of stockpiling assets, the Nets' rebuild still doesn't appear to have a clear path forward.

The Hornets perhaps expected to take a step back after trading LaMelo Ball this past offseason, but their start has muted much of the momentum generated from last year's encouraging run to the playoffs. Kon Knueppel lasted just nine minutes in his season debut before suffering a hamstring injury, and he's one of several key players to have missed games due to injury. The team, meanwhile, has the worst offensive rating in the league after finishing with the NBA's fifth-most efficient offense last season. Brandon Miller is shooting less than 35% from the field as the top offensive option, and Naz Reid, the primary acquisition in the Ball deal, was benched four games into the season by Hornets coach Charles Lee for "not meeting Hornets' standards." Just one month into the season, nearly everything that could have gone wrong for Charlotte has.

The Bulls are starting to emerge from a 1-9 start to the season, though their recent surge is happening right as No. 3 overall pick Caleb Wilson was diagnosed with a broken hand that will keep him out for the next few months. Wilson showed intriguing flashes in his early appearances, but it's still unclear whether Chicago's core featuring Josh Giddey and Matas Buzelis is viable long-term. The Bulls feel closer to escaping the bottom-half of the Eastern Conference than they have been because of Wilson, but his injury means they'll have to wait longer to figure out exactly what they have.

The Cavaliers are off to a rougher-than-expected start to the season, and it's particularly concerning because their starters are the reason they're scuffling along. James Harden is showing his age more regularly, struggling to finish at the rim and get to the free throw line. Evan Mobley has already suffered a toe injury. Peyton Watson, the team's main offseason acquisition, looked completely out of sync before missing action recently with a back injury. Rookie Meleek Thomas and forward Mario Hezonja have been bright spots off the bench, but Cleveland's core has failed to resemble the group that reached the Eastern Conference finals last season.

Cooper Flagg appears to have taken another step forward in his development after winning Rookie of the Year, and his fit alongside Kyrie Irving has been better than many expected under new coach Dusty May. They've positioned the Mavericks as a legitimate play-in contender despite playing one of the tougher schedules in the league through 15 games. Their early success together strengthens the case for Irving to remain in Dallas beyond the NBA trade deadline. With a first-year NBA coach and a 19-year-old franchise star, the Mavericks are outperforming expectations through the season's first month.

The Nuggets remain a threat in the Western Conference playoff race, but there are some early signs that Denver's reign as a legitimate title contender could be waning. Nikola Jokic's 3-point shot has largely disappeared from his repertoire, a development that began after he returned from a wrist injury last season. His points, rebounds and assists per game have decreased compared to recent years. Jamal Murray's strong play has been offset by relatively uninspiring starts for veterans Aaron Gordon and DeMar DeRozan. It's no coincidence the team has gone from first in the league in offensive rating last year to 10th as of Nov. 19. If Jokic can't return to his MVP form and cover some of the roster flaws like he so often has over the past five years, it's hard to envision this group being a championship contender.

The Pistons were a trendy regression candidate in the East, but they've already got wins over the Boston Celtics, Miami Heat, Philadelphia 76ers and San Antonio Spurs through the first month of the season. Cade Cunningham is back in the MVP conversation, and Ausar Thompson is becoming one of the league's most disruptive two-way playmakers. Early injuries to Jalen Duren and offseason addition Isaiah Joe have taken a little shine off Detroit's strong start, but guard Daniss Jenkins is beginning to blossom into the secondary perimeter scoring option Cunningham has lacked in previous seasons.

The Warriors got an unexpected gift when rookie Yaxel Lendeborg quickly emerged as the leading NBA Rookie of the Year candidate and helped solve the team's void on the wing with Jimmy Butler sidelined. They reeled off eight-straight wins with Lendeborg serving as Steph Curry's sidekick. But Golden State dropped five games in a row after Curry suffered an oblique injury on Nov. 11, a development that has exposed Draymond Green's limitations and the lack of offense creation on the roster beyond Curry. Even so, Curry's eventual return and the prospect of Butler coming back gives Golden State more intrigue and upside than most expected entering the season.

The Rockets have looked like NBA title contenders through the season's first month, with the return of point guard Fred Van Vleet helping jump start the league's second-most efficient offense. Houston's turnovers are way down and its 3-point shooting is among the best in the NBA at the moment. Alperen Sengun, meanwhile, is on pace to break his career highs in points and assists as part of the Rockets' deep front court, and Kevin Durant remains one of the NBA's elite scorers. This appears to be one of the NBA's most complete rosters, blending ascending young talent with veterans who have extensive playoff experience. Considering the Rockets also possess the assets to make another significant move before the trade deadline, they may be San Antonio's biggest threat in the Western Conference.

The Pacers were hoping to rebound from last season's injury-induced tank, but they lost star Tyrese Haliburton to a hamstring injury after just four games. Indiana is one of the worst shooting teams in the league after one month, and it's wasting another strong start from Pascal Siakam. The lack of shooting has complicated Ivica Zubac's transition to the lineup, with poor spacing creating fit issues for a player Indiana spent considerable assets to acquire. He's on pace for his worst statistical season in six years. The Pacers entered the season expecting to rejoin the Eastern Conference contender tier, but they look much closer to a lottery team than a playoff threat right now.

The Clippers are in rebuilding mode and off to an uneven start, with the expectation they could be sellers at the trade deadline. The good news is that Darius Garland, the centerpiece of last year's James Harden trade, appears to have rediscovered his All-Star form after an injury-riddled 2025-26 season. The development of first-round pick Keaton Wagler alongside Garland is likely more important than the results the rest of the way, and he's shooting better than 40% from 3-point range since Nov. 2. The Clippers are one of the league's better shooting teams overall, but they've offset that strength by ranking among the NBA's most turnover-prone teams. They're likely headed for the lottery, but that's exactly where most anticipated them to be.

The Lakers are off to a fantastic start with Luka Dončić a leading MVP candidate and on pace to surpass Michael Jordan for the highest scoring average (38.1) since the 1976 NBA/ABA merger. Walker Kessler is also providing the dominating interior presence Los Angeles hoped for when it traded for him this offseason and gave him a huge contract. Kessler is averaging a double-double, while Austin Reaves is scoring more efficiently than at any point in his career. If Dončić continues to play at this level, the Lakers will be in the NBA title discussion come playoff time.

The Grizzlies have been the biggest surprise of the 2026-27 season thus far, vaulting toward the top of the Western Conference standings when many expected them to begin a rebuild after trading away Ja Morant, Jaren Jackson Jr. and Desmond Bane. Instead, the team's recent draft picks, particularly Cameron Boozer and Cedric Coward, have helped spearhead a balanced and remarkably deep roster that already looks like a playoff contender. A recent injury to veteran Ty Jerome looms large, but Cam Spencer has filled the void admirably thus far. Memphis owns the NBA's best offensive rating as of Nov. 20. Few saw that coming.

NBA observers were in panic mode when the Heat began the Giannis Antetokounmpo era with four losses in a row, but Miami is 9-2 since then with an increasingly potent offense built around pace and space. Antetokounmpo is leading the Heat with the fourth-highest scoring average in the NBA (28.2 points), and Davion Mitchell leads the NBA in assists per game. Only the Spurs' starters have a better NET rating than the Heat lineup featuring Antetokounmpo, Bam Adebayo, Klay Thompson, Mitchell and Pelle Larsson among groups that have played at least 65 minutes together. One month into the season, Miami appears to have already discovered the lineup it can trust most when playoff games are on the line.

The Bucks were expected to be bad this season after trading Giannis Antetokounmpo, but they're off to the worst start in franchise history with the league's worst record and own the worst NET rating in the Eastern Conference as of Nov. 20. With veterans like Myles Turner, Kyle Kuzma and Gary Trent Jr. taking up salary cap space after being acquired in prior seasons, this rebuild appears to only just be beginning. Milwaukee entered the post-Giannis era without a clear franchise cornerstone, and the first month of the season has done little to change that. It will be painful for Bucks' fans this season.

The Timberwolves have done a fine job keeping pace in the competitive Western Conference after losing Anthony Edwards to a knee injury that required arthoscopic surgery. LaMelo Ball, Ayo Dosunmu, Jaden McDaniels and Rudy Gobert have formed a well-rounded core that could be dangerous if Edwards returns before the playoffs. There is no timetable on his recovery, though Edwards told reporters on Nov. 19 he is planning to to come back this season. Minnesota's ceiling ultimately depends on his health.

The Pelicans got off to a bad start, won four games in a row and now appear headed nowhere fast once again. Dejounte Murray's recent trade request brought renewed attention to one of the NBA's most confusing roster situations. New Orleans hasn't been able to win consistently despite Zion Williamson enjoying one of the healthiest stretches of his career, and Trey Murphy III is increasingly viewed as one of the league's more attractive trade targets. Jeremiah Fears and Derik Queen are intriguing young players, but both have contributed to the defensive issues that have left New Orleans among the NBA's worst teams on that end of the floor. Murray, meanwhile, is owed enough money that moving him won't be simple. If this start continues, Joe Dumars would have to consider a roster overhaul.

The Knicks deserve the benefit of the doubt after a sluggish start coming off an NBA championship, though the early-season struggles of Josh Hart and Mikal Bridges, as well as the ongoing contract situation involving Karl-Anthony Towns, bear watching. Jalen Brunson and Towns continue to form one of the NBA's most potent duos, and New York remains firmly positioned in the Eastern Conference playoff picture thanks in large part to one of the league's best defenses. The Knicks proved last season they can reach another level when the games matter most.

The Thunder are doing their best to stay afloat in the crowded Western Conference standings playing without Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace due to injuries. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander remains one of the league's best players, and OKC's deep reservoir of first-round picks is paying off with younger players like Ajay Mitchell and rookie Aday Mara successfully stepping into larger roles. The Thunder have survived a difficult opening month and came out looking capable of remaining in the playoff mix. But bad injury luck could prevent them from contending for a title this season.

The Magic hoped to emerge as Eastern Conference contenders, but they finished the opening month of the season below .500 and lost star Paolo Banchero to injury after just two games. A recent four-game winning streak that included a victory over the Boston Celtics showcased the potential of a group built around Franz Wagner, Desmond Bane, Anthony Black and a healthy Jalen Suggs. But the franchise is still no closer to determining whether Banchero and Wagner can thrive together as a long-term foundation. Until Banchero returns, that question will remain unanswered, and Orlando appears destined to be part of the crowded pack of Eastern Conference teams battling to avoid the play-in tournament.

After considerable preseason hype and past stumbles out of the gate for LeBron James-led superteams, the Sixers finished the first month of the 2026-27 season tied with the Spurs for the NBA's best record. James and Jaylen Brown have blended in well with Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and VJ Edgecombe, though the team's starting lineup had a surprising -4.9 NET rating playing together through 16 games. It speaks to some of the unexpected depth Philadelphia enjoys, with Dean Wade, Anfernee Simons and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope forming a versatile rotation. One month into the season, the 76ers look like the team to beat in the Eastern Conference.

The Suns have rebounded from a rough start to the season despite playing one of the toughest schedules in the league, and Devin Booker remains among the NBA's top 12 scorers. But their two most-used lineups have been outscored by an average net rating of 15.5 points per 100 possessions. Jalen Green is having the least productive season of his career, and centers Oso Ighodaro and Khaman Maluach have looked overmatched while replacing the injured Mark Williams. As currently constructed, Phoenix looks capable of reaching the play-in tournament. But unless the Suns can find an upgrade before the trade deadline, their ceiling appears to be a first-round playoff exit.

The Trail Blazers have taken a step forward with wins over the Lakers and Thunder already. Damian Lillard is providing a perimeter scoring punch, and his presence hasn't come at the expense of Deni Avdija. It's turned a lackluster offense into one with a top-10 NET rating thus far. It remains to be seen how Ja Morant's return from a hamstring injury affects the rotation, but early returns suggest Portland's crowded backcourt can co-exist. This might not be a legitimate title contender, but the Trail Blazers look as feisty as they have at any point since Lillard led them to the Western Conference Finals in 2019.

The Kings appear to be the most dysfunctional team in the league, with a core of expensive veteran players who don't seem interested in remaining in Sacramento but lack significant trade value. Rookie Darius Acuff Jr. has been a bright spot, on pace to average the most points by a first-year player since Michael Jordan. But the Kings haven't translated that production into wins and they have the league's worst NET rating. Meanwhile, last year's breakout rookie, 23-year-old center Maxime Raynaud, has yet to play due to injury. This directionless franchise is no closer to finding its way.

The Spurs have looked every bit like a team on a mission to avenge last June's NBA Finals loss, highlighted by a recent 12-game winning streak. Victor Wembanyama is an MVP candidate once again, and the returning core of Dylan Harper, Stephon Castle and De'Aaron Fox has made the absence of free-agent acquisition Tobias Harris largely an afterthought. A recent hip injury to Harper does not appear to be serious, and San Antonio is deep enough to withstand his absence if he misses additional time. One month into the season, the Spurs look like the favorites to win the NBA title.

After emerging as one of the NBA's pleasant surprises last season, the Raptors have stumbled out of the gate this year. Kawhi Leonard has yet to play because of a knee injury, and Scottie Barnes has also missed time. The team's defense has gone from a strength to a weakness, particularly in close games, and Toronto ranks among the NBA's worst rebounding teams because of a wing-heavy roster that lacks a reliable center. The Raptors traded for Leonard with championship aspirations in mind, but the first month of that pursuit has been anything but encouraging.

The Jazz's hopes of emerging as a playoff contender after a lengthy rebuilding have been decimated by injuries to Lauri Markannen and Jaren Jackson Jr., as well as a disastrous start that featured a 12-game losing streak. Keyonte George played more like a rising star in two recent Utah wins and, along with rookie Darryn Peterson and 20-year-old Ace Bailey, gives the franchise a promising young core. But the Jazz expected to compete for a postseason berth and that might not be possible after an opening month that could hardly have gone worse.

The Wizards remain near the bottom of the Eastern Conference standings, and their decision to give Trae Young a $212 million contract extension is already drawing scrutiny. Young is shooting a career-worst 30.4% from 3-point range, and Washington ranks as the NBA's second-worst 3-point shooting team. Opponents have also targeted Young defensively in crunch time. The team's struggles are particularly concerning because Anthony Davis is healthy and playing well, while rookie AJ Dybantsa has shown encouraging flashes despite struggling to shoot from outside since returning from injury. Washington may still have enough talent to reach the playoffs in the East, but its biggest investment is underperforming.

10 Crime Shows With the Greatest Villain Performances, Ranked

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

One of the greatest strengths of crime television is its ability to skew moral compasses and deliver fascinating, complex characters that challenge our understanding of humanity. This is true of detective drama and organized crime suspense, and it is particularly true of the many great villains that populate these series, whether they are psychotic serial killers defined by their sinister presence or oddly charismatic crooks who are as charming as they are chilling.

It goes without saying that the performances of such characters are instrumental in determining their quality. Everything from the subtleties of facial expressions to the rhythm of line delivery, and to the physicality they are presented with, is all key choices actors can make in defining the brand of villainy their character exudes. Whether viewers hold a wicked love for them or dread every second of screen time they receive, these crime series find astonishing highlights in the form of their biggest and baddest antagonists.

Whether he’s erupting into a fit of rage or hurling oddly elegant insults, Cannavale makes Rosetti one of the most intimidating and unpleasant TV antagonists of all time.

As a grim descent into the criminal underbelly of Gotham City, The Penguin marks one of HBO’s more compelling productions of the decade so far, a rich mixture of comic-book style and gangland brutality. Colin Farrell’s lead performance is a driving force of this, but the miniseries benefits from its vast array of striking characters, with Cristin Milioti’s turn as criminal heiress Sofia Falcone being the noteworthy standout as she blends an erratic, unpredictable ruthlessness with underlying vulnerability and sympathetic sorrow.

Milioti worked closely with both movement coach Julia Crockett and the production’s costuming department to perfect Sofia’s physicality. Through every movement, she embodies the volatility of her explosive aspirations fighting to break free from her past of repression and institutionalization. In isolation, she is exceptional — the fourth episode’s focus on her time in Arkham Asylum was instrumental in her Emmy Award win — but the crackling, high-octane tension she brings to scenes with Farrell is her true triumph, crafting a complex yet purposeful obstacle to Cobb’s own ambitions of controlling organized crime in Gotham.

Purely as a superhero villain, Wilson Fisk (Vincent D’Onofrio), otherwise known as Kingpin, is a perfect foil to Daredevil (Charlie Cox), with his brutish strength challenging the hero’s more precise and calculating fighting style. However, it isn’t the imposing physicality that makes Fisk such a great antagonist, but rather the depth, nuance, and tragedy D’Onofrio realizes in the character in addition to his intimidating frame.

D’Onofrio’s ability to navigate such a polarizing dichotomy is astonishing, bringing both the physical gravitas and the timid personality together to make for one of the best mob boss bad guys in the medium’s history.

Even within Tony’s crew, he is a disconcerting, unpredictable presence. At its best, Killing Eve presents a mixture of high-octane spy thrills and international crime drama that is nothing short of addictive. Mercury is about to receive a robotic visitor from Earth.

The BepiColombo mission — a joint effort of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) — will arrive in orbit around Mercury today (Nov. 21), more than eight years after leaving its home planet.

The big moment will come after an orbit-insertion burn, which is scheduled to begin today at 1:24 p.m. EST (1824 GMT) and last for 37 minutes.

BepiColombo consists of two main spacecraft: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (MMO), also known as Mio.

The duo launched — along with a power and propulsion element called the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) — aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana in October 2018, beginning a long and looping trip through the inner solar system.

Mercury is a difficult planet to reach because it's so close to the sun. Spacecraft approaching the solar system's innermost planet are accelerated to high velocities by the immense gravitational pull of our star and must shed a great deal of kinetic energy to be captured by Mercury's much weaker tug.

So the BepiColombo team designed a trajectory that featured one flyby of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury over the course of about seven years. That flight time was increased by a year, however, after the mission suffered an issue with its solar panels, which reduced the power available for the MTM's electric thrusters.

Today's orbital insertion is the latest milestone in BepiColombo's Mercury arrival phase, which is expected to take about six months in total.

Arrival began on Sept. 3, when the MTM released MPO and Mio. The two orbiters remain connected today; they won't separate until Dec. 9 or thereabouts.

Shortly thereafter, Mio will settle into its final orbit, a highly elliptical path that will take it over Mercury's poles. MPO is also destined for a polar orbit, though a much less elliptical one than Mio's; the ESA probe will conduct a series of engine burns before finally getting there on March 10, 2027.

BepiColombo won't start doing science work until April 6, 2027. The two probes will gather complementary data from their two different vantage points, shedding a great deal of light on a mysterious world.

"The mission will study all aspects of Mercury, from the structure and dynamics of its magnetosphere and how it interacts with the solar wind, to its internal structure with its large iron core, and the origin of the planet’s magnetic field," ESA officials wrote in a BepiColombo mission description.

"The data will enable scientists to understand more about the origin and evolution of a planet located close to its parent star, and a better understanding of the overall evolution of our solar system," they added.

S&P 500 correction: Five forces behind the market sell-off

📅 2026-11-20  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Esteemed Wharton professor Jeremy Siegel was more animated than usual on Friday afternoon... with good reason.

The normally staid author of "Stocks for the Long Run" spoke to CNBC on Friday as the market careened toward some ugly milestones. The S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average closed in correction territory, off more than 10% from their records. The Nasdaq Composite lost its gain for the year and the more concentrated Nasdaq-100 entered bear market territory on an intraday basis.

"These things are like straws that break the camel's back. You know what I mean? Everyone [is] all, 'I don't care, I don't care cause everything else is good' and when those straws keep on going... Bang," said Siegel.

Just a month-and-a-half ago, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite were at record highs. But the straws were already adding up by then and a market in denial is just now recognizing them.

Here are the reasons behind the sell-off:

The breaking point came this week in the form of the initial public offering of artificial intelligence leader Anthropic and a pick-up in the selling intensity for one-time AI chip leader Nvidia.

Anthropic upped its offering amount a day before going public despite the already dimming climate around AI-related stocks. The move ensured it would be the biggest IPO ever, but played into the fears that this debut — following SpaceX's monster offering over the summer — was a collective cash-out moment for the AI complex that will leave the investing masses holding the bag.

In other words, it could be the ultimate "sell the news" moment for the AI trade.

On Thursday, Anthropic traded from its $83 IPO price to a high above $115 before instantly rolling over and taking the AI trade with it. Anthropic's sell-off intensified Friday, and when the dust settled Anthropic closed the week at $88.91.

The Nasdaq posted its worst week since the Covid plunge of March 2020, down 10%.

Nvidia fell 14% on Friday with the selling intensifying after a Reuters headline hit the tape that the chipmaker's accounting is the focus of a Securities and Exchange Commission probe.

Nvidia is now down nearly 40% in November alone, a month that saw the AI bull market's leading salesman, Jensen Huang, resign as its CEO because of an affair with an alleged Chinese spy that raised speculation about whether the company's chips were ending up in Beijing's hands despite government restrictions.

To start the week, the Commerce Department tightened restrictions on U.S.-made chips to stop China from accessing them remotely from other countries.

A solid earnings report on Tuesday fell flat as Nvidia's board definitively ruled out any chance Huang would return, disappointing some of the stock's fanatics still holding out hope for his far-fetched return. Nvidia ended the week with a 23% loss.

The S&P 500 traded below its 200-day moving average — what's considered a measure of a security's long-term trend — on Tuesday and made a convincing break of it on Thursday. This turned many on Wall Street— like commodity trading advisors — into automatic sellers on the market. So-called CTAs are individuals or organizations that advise others on trading futures and swaps across a range of assets.

"We know there's a lot of trend riders on these markets," said Siegel. "They have their trend lines. They've ridden it up and as soon as it goes down, they're selling and they're selling and they're selling and they just perpetuate" the decline, he said.

The Iran conflict has caused the price oil to surge and while it's off its highs it still remains above $83 a barrel, while the fees for transiting the Strait of Hormuz and the rest of the region keep persistent pressure on global prices.

A series of numbers earlier in the month showed inflation is still far from the Federal Reserve's 2% target, sparking a run of central bank officials to warn they may have to get more aggressive with interest rates.

After increasing rates in September by a quarter point, the Fed skipped a hike in late October and sparked concern among bond investors it may be falling behind the inflation fight. The 10-year Treasury yield subsequently jumped above 5% and stayed there until a flight of safe haven fixed income buying brought it back below that level on Friday.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said this week that the Fed may need to do a half percentage point hike next month to catch up.

"I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," Waller told CNBC.

"Don't fight the Fed" has long been a maxim on Wall Street and right now expectations are that the central bank will need to raise rates in December and again in January.

And late-breaking on Friday was a report from the Financial Times that the Trump Administration is considering restarting strikes on Iran.

Before the grim market milestones were met on Friday, it had long lost its mojo on fears that the pace of growth driven by AI companies was slowing.

As Anthropic prepared to go public, its third-quarter figures released earlier this month showed revenue growth last quarter was 47%, down from 144% in the second quarter.

AI momentum trades, like the VanEck Semiconductor ETF and the iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF, all topped out during the summer.

The fears of slowing growth picked up this month after the Democrats won control of Congress and key governorships, fueling speculation that states — or even the whole country — could implement stricter rules on data center construction.

CoreWeave, which builds data centers using high amounts of debt, is down 71% from its high. Caterpillar, which has reinvented itself as an AI play because of data center construction and generators, is down more than 50% from its high.

The view of Siegel and many market observers is that the economy remains in good enough shape that this will just be a run-of-the-mill correction that resets a bull market that had raised people's expectations too high because of its early ferocity.

The S&P 500 returned more than 20% in 2023 and 2024 and then gained 16% last year.

"The average over long periods for the stock market is 8%, 9% a year up," said Siegel. "People began to think that 15 to 20 [percent] was going to be the norm with AI and we have to bring that down to reality."

The only problem with that theory is that the stock market and its wealth effect was one of the major factors underpinning the economy. Stocks are a greater source of household wealth — especially for baby boomers — than they ever have been before.

With the S&P 500 clinging to a 1% gain for the year, this stock market rout could end up tipping off a virtuous cycle that slows the economy, and in turn, leads to bigger equity losses.

Of the videos examined, the analysis shows that nearly half contained inaccurate and potentially damaging information.

The researchers say that approximately 44% of TikTok creators who share dementia content lack credibility and appropriate qualifications.

Although misleading dementia content may appear on other social media platforms, this research examined only TikTok content, warranting further investigation.

The survey was commissioned by Homewatch Caregivers and has not been published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal.

A spokesperson for TikTok told Healthline that if health-related content is potentially harmful, it is flagged for removal. The spokesperson added that 99.6% of misinformation deemed violative was proactively removed from the platform in 2026.

The spokesperson added that TikTok relies on fact-checking and guidance from public health authorities to help assess the accuracy of content. The platform may also apply warning labels to content that cannot be verified as accurate.

Looking to social media for health information is not uncommon. A recent poll reports that 31% of adults in the United States use social media at least once a month to look for health information and advice.

Experts advise seeking information about dementia from trusted sources, such as a primary care doctor and organizations like the National Institute on Aging (NIA) and the Alzheimer’s Association, rather than social media, where content creators who share advice often lack expertise.

“It’s normal to want answers quickly when you’re caring for someone living with dementia, and a short video from someone who seems to know what they’re talking about can be really convincing,” said lead researcher Jennifer Winarski, senior manager of care operations and quality for Homewatch CareGivers.

“Despite there being some genuinely helpful information out there, you don’t always know whether it’s backed by medical evidence, so we wanted to bring our own expertise to uncover whether popular TikToks can be trusted,” she told Healthline. “We hope our report will encourage people to be a bit more careful about what they act on.”

Researchers then transcribed the video data to assess its accuracy.

“It’s clear that many of the creators mean well, but much of what we saw was personal opinion rather than proven information, and in a few cases, general good health tips were presented as if they were specifically helpful for individuals living with dementia,” Winarski said.

“If families act on that, they could end up spending time and money on things that won’t help — and in the worst of cases, it could delay getting the support their loved one really needs,” she added.

Dung Trinh, MD, an internist for MemorialCare Medical Group and chief medical officer of the Healthy Brain Clinic in Irvine, CA, said it’s important to be aware of the volume of misleading dementia information on social media. Trinh was not involved in this survey.

Some people may be making decisions about their health based on incomplete, misleading, or incorrect information, he said.

“For example, someone might see a video claiming that a particular supplement can reverse Alzheimer’s disease or that certain memory problems automatically mean someone has dementia,” Trinh said.

“These messages can lead to unnecessary fear, false reassurance, wasted money, or delays in getting a proper diagnosis.”

“We are entering an exciting era in Alzheimer’s care, with advances in blood-based biomarkers, earlier detection, and treatments that may slow disease progression in appropriately selected patients,” Trinh continued.

Distributed LLM Training Across Metros: Streaming DiLoCo in MaxText

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Traditionally, training state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) relies on a monolithic cluster of accelerators, where all devices are collocated and tightly coupled via high-bandwidth to constantly synchronize gradients. However, physical and cost constraints make scaling a single slice increasingly difficult.

To scale further, we can connect multiple compute slices across geographically distributed datacenters. However, when training across multiple datacenters over wide-area networks (WAN), standard data-parallel training suffers from severe network stalls because slow WAN transfers cannot be fully overlapped with computation.

To address this, the Google DeepMind and Google Research teams developed DiLoCo (Distributed Low-Communication), a distributed optimization algorithm designed to make training resilient to these low-bandwidth, high-latency connections. In this blog post, we discuss the core concepts of DiLoCo, how it is implemented in MaxText (Google's open-source JAX-based LLM training framework), and the performance benefits it brings to large-scale LLM training on Google Cloud.

DiLoCo was introduced by Google DeepMind in the paper DiLoCo: Distributed Low-Communication Training of Language Models. Instead of synchronizing gradients across all devices at every single training step (as in standard Data Parallelism), DiLoCo partitions training across M independent compute groups, referred to as learners, and allows each learner to perform many local optimization steps independently before communicating across groups.

Despite communicating significantly less frequently (by a factor of H, e.g., 1-2 orders of magnitude less) than standard synchronous training, DiLoCo has been shown to achieve comparable convergence and model quality.

The Vanilla DiLoCo described above reduces total communication volume by a factor of H, however, all learners still transmit their weight deltas for the entire model all at once at every H-th step. On a thin network pipe, that full-model transfer creates a large periodic stall every H steps. This issue is alleviated by Streaming DiLoCo, which is described in the next section.

In multi-slice training, communication within a single slice is handled by high-speed Inter-Chip Interconnects (ICI). When scaling to multiple slices, synchronization across slices must traverse the Data Center Network (DCN) or, if the slices are geographically distributed, wide-area networks (WAN).

Within a single datacenter, DCN bandwidth is high enough that JAX and XLA can often hide standard Data Parallel all-reduce collectives by overlapping gradient communication with backward-pass computation. Across datacenters or congested links, however, bandwidth drops and latency rises sharply. Standard DP stalls at every step waiting for global gradient synchronization, collapsing Model FLOPS Utilization (MFU).

Streaming DiLoCo eliminates this synchronization stall by partitioning the model's parameters into P disjoint fragments. Over the course of H steps, every single fragment in the model is synchronized exactly once, achieving the exact same outer-loop cadence as vanilla DiLoCo without spiky bandwidth utilization.

Using a roofline model (similar to the JAX Scaling Book), we can calculate the network bandwidth required to prevent communication stalls for a model with ∣Θ∣ parameters (at b bytes per parameter, e.g., b=2 for bfloat16) and per-step compute time tstep=tfwd+tbwd. For example, the standard Data Parallel algorithm transfers 2∣Θ∣b bytes per sync via an all-reduce collective, which is a distributed reduce-scatter where each node reduces a different part, followed by an all-gather so all participants receive all parts. To achieve zero communication stalls, we overlap the reduce-scatter of the gradients with the backward pass, which produces them and takes tbwd time. Therefore, the peak required bandwidth is Bdp ≈ 2∣Θ∣b / tbwd.

Rather than routing through a single central coordinator, the host-CPU syncer shards execute a distributed all-reduce across the network in the background to average the fragment deltas while the TPUs continue running inner-loop training steps. A season pass is not just a ticket with a longer expiry date. You need to decide who can use it, whether the holder can bring a guest, and what happens when someone presents the same code again at the entrance.

I built Tickets & Passes for WooCommerce with a separate Pass product type for admission across a period. It supports guest passes, cardholder photos and a cooldown between scans. Here is how I would plan a WooCommerce season pass for a venue without confusing admission with a broader membership system.

The plugin adds Ticket, Timeslot Ticket and Pass to WooCommerce's Product data dropdown. A Ticket is plain admission with a validity window and a set number of uses. A Timeslot Ticket is for a particular date and time, with capacity per slot. A Pass is valid across a period, which suits season passes and annual admission cards.

I would choose the product type from the admission rule, not the marketing name. If each visit must reserve a specific session, look at Timeslot Tickets instead. Do not assume that calling a product a membership makes it a reservation system.

The WordPress.org readme documents the setup. WooCommerce must already be active, and the phone scanner needs HTTPS for camera access.

Before selling publicly, I would make a test purchase and follow it through from checkout to check-in. Check the holder details, the validity you intended, and what staff actually see on their phone.

A pass can carry a cardholder photo collected at purchase. At check-in, staff see that photo beside the scan result and can compare it with the person at the door. The QR code identifies the pass; the photo gives staff another piece of information.

This is not a promise of automatic identity verification. I would write a simple entrance procedure explaining when staff should compare the photo and what to do if the person does not match. Test that procedure with the people who will use it, not just the administrator who configured the product.

A Pass can include guest passes that the holder gives away from their account page. That provides a guest route without treating the main holder's QR code as something everyone should share.

A cooldown does something different: it prevents the same pass code being scanned again immediately. The product also determines the maximum number of uses. I would decide those rules before advertising the pass, then test repeat entry against the intended policy.

Do not describe a cooldown as a complete sharing-prevention system. It is a rule about repeat scans. The cardholder photo, the guest allowance and the staff procedure address different parts of the admission process.

Customers find their passes in the Passes tab of WooCommerce My Account, with an on-screen QR code and a printable PDF. Live status shows whether a pass is active, used, expired, cancelled or not valid yet, along with uses remaining. WooCommerce order emails are extended with pass details.

At the entrance, staff log in to the scanner page, allow camera access and point it at the code. The scanner shows who it belongs to; staff tap Check In to confirm or Cancel to scan again. For an unreadable code, the scanner menu offers manual entry. The admin dashboards also provide manual check-in.

Every scan is checked against your server. The phone needs a connection, and the scanner does not work offline. I would test reception at the actual entrance rather than assume a successful office test covers the door.

Incorrect validity, use limits or cooldown rules can make the entrance behave differently from the promise on the product page. A missing connection prevents live validation, and camera access requires HTTPS. Test those conditions before a busy opening.

To stop a trial, deactivate the plugin. The readme says deactivation clears its scheduled tasks but leaves settings and records intact; reactivation picks up where you left off. Admission features stop while it is inactive, so this is not something to do while customers are relying on them.

Deletion is different: settings, scheduled tasks and the Scanner role are removed, while tickets, passes and check-in records are kept by default. The readme also documents an optional permanent-data deletion setting. Do not enable that to undo a trial; permanent removal cannot be undone. Keep a backup before testing removal.

If your membership requirement is recurring billing or access to restricted website content, this admission walkthrough is not evidence that those features are included. Choose tooling that explicitly documents the job you need. Likewise, a venue that requires offline scanning needs a different check-in approach.

For period-based admission sold through an existing WooCommerce shop, I would start with a Pass, test the holder and guest paths separately, and make sure the entrance rules match the offer before taking real orders.

PDF object streams saved 4.15% on a text-only file. Here is where those bytes were hiding

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A ten-page text-only PDF of 137,782 bytes looks like an easy target for a compressor. It barely moves. On these samples the biggest single lossless change to it came from qpdf 12.4.0's --object-streams=generate, which took it to 132,065 bytes (−4.15%). The same switch moved the image-heavy files by only −0.02% to −0.74%. Both make sense once the bytes are sorted by object type. Scope: five sample files, one Apple-silicon Mac, every setting run once.

The accounting: take each top-level object's offset from qpdf --show-xref, size it as the distance to the next offset, and classify it from its dictionary with PyMuPDF 1.26.5. On the text-only file, called X1 below, fonts came to 121,442 bytes (88.14%), content streams 9.16%, page and structure objects 1.95%, and the xref table plus trailer 0.71%. The image-heavy samples were 95.93% image (T1, two text pages with a photo), 98.27% (X2, an eight-page NASA fact sheet) and 99.84% (T2, a scan of T1). Structure in the narrow sense, meaning page objects, xref and file header, added up to somewhere between 0.04% and 2.70% across these four files. None of the four had object streams to begin with.

It is tempting to read "cross-reference stream" as the headline feature and expect the xref to be the win. On X1 the xref table and trailer are 977 bytes. Deleting them outright would save 0.71%, so most of the 4.15% is something else.

In a classic PDF, every object that is not a stream is written as plain text between obj and endobj. That covers page dictionaries, font descriptors, and the /W width array of a CID font, which can be a long run of numbers. Flate only ever applied to stream data, so this layer sat in the file uncompressed. The schematic below is written from the PDF spec to show the shape. It is not copied from the sample files.

An object stream (PDF 1.5) packs a batch of those objects into one stream, so Flate gets to compress them for the first time. The xref turns into a cross-reference stream at the same time and is compressed as well. Stream objects themselves cannot go into an object stream. Image data and embedded font programs stay exactly where they were, already compressed. A control run agrees: writing X1 with --compress-streams=n --decode-level=generalized, which decodes every stream, made it 2.25 times its original size. Its streams were already compressed; the dictionary layer was what sat raw.

A constructed sample, D1, makes it visible. It stores the same self-made 800×600 JPEG (91,844 bytes) and the same font subset five times over five pages, 756,741 bytes in total. With object streams alone it went to 633,713 bytes (−16.26%). The bytes classified as font dropped from 266,381 to 134,332, and the new object stream itself was 12,727 bytes. Those five copies of the font carry five copies of the non-stream font objects, /W arrays included, and that is the part Flate could now reach. The font bytes alone fell by 132,049, more than the whole file's drop of 123,028, so the images contributed nothing, which fits the rule that stream data stays out of object streams. That −16.26% comes from a file built to have duplicates, not an average.

The image-heavy samples follow from the same rule: T1 −0.74%, X2 −0.25%, T2 −0.02%. With 95.93% to 99.84% of the bytes in image streams, the slice that object streams can touch is tiny.

Recompressing Flate streams at level 9 (--recompress-flate --compression-level=9) gave X1 −1.11% on its own, and stacked on object streams it reached 130,933 bytes (−4.97%). A default qpdf in out rewrite gave X1 −0.29% but made D1 1.03% larger. --remove-unreferenced-resources=yes without --pages removed nothing on these files, and --decode-level=specialized changed nothing. PyMuPDF's save(use_objstms=1) on its own gave X1 −0.99%, well short of qpdf's −4.15%, while garbage=4 plus deflate plus object streams reached 132,169 (−4.07%). Why the PyMuPDF object-stream switch alone did so much less was not looked into here, and it is the number I'd chase next.

In the browser I use ImgIng https://imging.ai/. On the same Mac, its lossless structure mode took X1 from 137,782 to 131,156 bytes (−4.81%), the same range as qpdf with everything on, and the scan T2 moved −0.03%. Page count and full-text readback matched the originals. How it gets there internally isn't something visible from the outputs.

On the text-only sample a structural rewrite topped out around 5%, and object streams were most of it; they only reach the non-stream part of that 88% in fonts. Before expecting any gain, qpdf --show-xref on the input is a quick check: entries listed as compressed; stream = … mean the file already uses object streams, and the biggest structural switch has already been spent.

A mount point at a path that does not exist in the image is created owned by root, and the TSDB fails to open with a permission error. If port 9091 is already taken on the host, for example by a Pushgateway, pick another port.

Let the canary run through at least one full cycle of your slowest scrape interval, plus a rule group evaluation or two. A day is better if you have daily batch jobs that expose metrics.

The cost is real but small: roughly the memory and CPU of a second server for the canary period. On a very large instance, that may mean scraping only a subset of jobs with a trimmed config.

Once the canary is clean, take a snapshot of the production data volume, then swap the binary or image tag. If you run the server under systemd or Kubernetes, change only the version in this step. Put unrelated tuning in a separate change so a regression has one suspect.

Confirm that the config loaded with the prometheus_config_last_reload_successful metric in the verify section. Expect a restart to replay the write-ahead log, which takes longer as the head block grows.

Alerts with for: clauses restore their pending state from the ALERTS_FOR_STATE series stored in the TSDB. That restore is governed by --rules.alert.for-outage-tolerance and --rules.alert.for-grace-period. After a short restart, most pending alerts resume rather than reset, but a longer outage can still delay firing. Schedule the restart outside deploy windows and let the replay finish before judging health.

Rollback: keep the previous binary or image tag and the snapshot. Whether a given minor can safely read data written by a newer one is stated in the release notes, so check before relying on a downgrade. Restoring the snapshot is the fallback that does not depend on that.

A target that is up on the old server but down on the canary points to a scrape-behavior change, so look in the changelog for the matching entry. After the first compaction cycle, check prometheus_tsdb_compactions_failed_total and the server logs for errors.

Official reference for flags, configuration and metrics: prometheus.io documentation. More operations guides live at kuryzhev.cloud.

Allow Google-Agent Traffic: CDN, WAF, and Web Bot Auth for Agency Sites

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 20 • Originally published at apogeewatcher.com

Google’s crawling infrastructure docs now include a dedicated page on how to allow Google-Agent traffic through a content delivery network, web application firewall, or bot-detection service. The page exists for a practical reason: agencies and hosts have spent years tightening “block AI agents” packs, so user-triggered agents that run on Google’s infrastructure often never reach search, product detail, or checkout. Allowlisting is edge operations. It does not replace crawl readiness for indexing bots, and it does not replace lab and field Core Web Vitals on the URLs that still convert when a human loads the page. What follows is a working translation of Google’s rules for multi-tenant portfolios, plus the monitoring boundary that keeps the retainer honest.

Google-Agent is the name Google uses for agents hosted on Google infrastructure that navigate the web and perform actions when a user asks. In Google’s user-triggered fetchers table, the User-Agent strings include compatible; Google-Agent with a documentation URL, for both mobile and desktop shapes. Traffic for this fetcher uses IP ranges published in user-triggered-agents.json.

That is a different job from Googlebot, which crawls for Search. It is also a different product conversation from Google Cloud or Gemini “agent builder” marketing pages that dominate results for the bare query google-agent. When a client asks you to “allow the Google agent,” it helps to confirm they mean this crawling User-Agent and IP list, not a Vertex or Workspace product toggle.

It is also distinct from classic AI crawlers such as GPTBot. Those systems are usually discussed through robots.txt, fetch budgets, and whether the HTML becomes parseable quickly. Our earlier guide on why AI crawlers need fast, crawlable pages covers that lane. Google-Agent allowlisting sits one layer earlier on many stacks: if the WAF challenges or drops the request, crawlability and performance never get a chance.

Lighthouse’s experimental Agentic Browsing audits ask whether a page is structured for machine interaction; they do not configure your CDN. Lighthouse agentic browsing scoring belongs in the page-construction conversation. The CDN and WAF rules below belong in the edge-allow conversation.

Google documents three signals, and the split matters in production: the first two (User-Agent and IP) work as the baseline today, while Web Bot Auth stays optional for as long as the experiment remains partial.

Participating signed requests use the identity https://agent.bot.goog. The public key set lives at https://agent.bot.goog/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory. Google’s Web Bot Auth guide is explicit: not every agent request is signed yet, and verification still falls back to User-Agent plus IP. A rule that only accepts signatures will fail a large share of legitimate traffic for as long as the experiment stays partial.

In provider language, the safe shape is usually: confirm User-Agent contains Google-Agent, confirm the IP is on the published list, verify the signature when headers are present, and for verified requests skip bot challenges. Matching the User-Agent string alone is not enough, because anyone can send that string. The IP check (and signatures when present) is what keeps the allow rule from becoming an open door for spoofed bots.

Provider verified-bot or managed allowlist features are the least fragile starting point. Cloudflare, AWS WAF, Fastly, and several bot-detection vendors already document Web Bot Auth or Google crawler categories. Their supported path usually ages better than a hand-rolled regex, but the security event log still matters: a checkbox is not the same as an end-to-end journey that clears search and cart APIs.

Google’s troubleshooting list calls out on-site search, filtering, detail pages, add-to-cart, booking, and the underlying XHR, GraphQL, or api.* endpoints. An allow rule that only clears / still fails the moment an assistant filters a catalogue or posts to a cart API.

Load balancers and reverse proxies often rewrite the picture. When the edge sees a substituted client IP, a stripped User-Agent, or missing Signature / Signature-Input / Signature-Agent headers, verification fails even if Google sent a valid request. IP-based rules also drift after a one-time paste of ranges. Teams that already refresh other verified-bot CIDR feeds can point the same job at user-triggered-agents.json, or use a provider feed that pulls Google’s lists.

When support says “we allowed it” and journeys still die, Google’s failure modes tend to appear in a predictable order.

Rule priority. Allow rules need to evaluate before broad cloud or data-centre ASN blocks, managed bot packs, and category-level “block AI agents” policies. On agency accounts, the last pack someone enabled for “AI scraping” often sits above a carefully written Google-Agent exception. The rule order is usually the bug, not the rule text.

Interactive challenges. Skipping a 403 is not enough. Challenges still break journeys when CAPTCHA or JavaScript widgets fire on search or form submit, even after the HTML document itself loads. Agents that can fetch the first page and then stall on a widget never finish the path.

In-journey actions and APIs. Coverage has to include the paths and hosts the agent calls after the first document, because search autocomplete, filter endpoints, GraphQL, and api. subdomains are common blind spots when the homepage rule looks green in the UI and the journey still dies one click later.

Shared-IP rate limits. Multiple users’ agents share Google’s egress pool. Per-IP rate limits that treat those addresses like a single abusive scraper cut legitimate concurrency. Exempting or raising limits for verified Google-Agent ranges is the usual fix.

Stale IP lists. CDN logs that show Google-Agent User-Agents from new ranges while your rule misses them almost always point at sync, so it is worth confirming that automation still pulls user-triggered-agents.json rather than a months-old paste.

Proxy header loss. Original client IP and signature headers need to survive every hop. Stacked Cloudflare → origin WAF → application firewall setups are a frequent place where each layer “helpfully” normalises traffic and breaks verification before the application ever sees a clean request.

That is a clearer problem than a silent block. It is still worth scheduling and alerting on before the next client review, because the board will ask about speed long after the WAF ticket closes.

Is Google-Agent the same as Googlebot? No. Googlebot crawls for Search. Google-Agent identifies user-triggered agents on Google infrastructure that navigate and act on request. Allow rules and IP lists differ; the allow Google-Agent and user-triggered fetchers docs are the source of truth.

Do we need Web Bot Auth to allow Google-Agent? Not as the only control. Google still expects User-Agent plus IP verification, and signatures are experimental and incomplete. Web Bot Auth helps when headers are present and the provider supports verification; the IP fallback stays required.

Will allowing Google-Agent open the site to spoofed bots? A User-Agent-only allow would. Pairing the User-Agent with Google’s published IP ranges (and signatures when present) is the control Google documents. Broad bot packs can stay in place for unverified traffic.

Does robots.txt control Google-Agent the way it controls GPTBot? Crawl directives and WAF allowlists are separate layers. A robots.txt change does not clear a Cloudflare challenge on an API path. Edge logs are the proof.

What should we tell the client after we change the WAF? That verified Google-Agent traffic should now reach the journey paths you listed, that bot defence remains for unverified traffic, and that lab and field performance on those checkout, search, and product URLs stay on the monitoring programme because allowlisting does not measure LCP or INP.

When a client’s “block AI” pack and a new Google allow doc collide, the durable pattern is UA + IP (plus Web Bot Auth when present), an allow rule above category AI blocks, challenge skips on the full journey, and a sync job for user-triggered-agents.json. After the edge change, checkout and search still need proof for humans. A lab check now, those URLs on a schedule with budgets, and an alert path when a theme or WAF pack regresses them again keep the retainer honest.

A multi-page domain PageSpeed check is enough to spot-check after a WAF edit. Apogee Watcher sign-up fits when the same checks need to run across many client organisations without a separate cron and key for each site. Allowlisting gets the agent through the door. Scheduled lab and field data tell you what happens after it walks in.

Yosemite At Last! — John Muir Trail Day 21

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It was a fitful, fretful night.  It continued to rain for a while, off and on, and the wind came up again, continuing throughout the night.  The whole time I was worrying about staying warm, staying dry, and making the fewest possible excursions outside to pee.

On one of those, in the middle of the night, I did something clever.  I picked up my poncho, which was just lying on the ground soaking wet, and I draped it over a pine tree, so it looked like a scarecrow.  Or maybe a scare-bear?

Above all, I worried about what I would be facing in the morning.

Indeed, the morning came.  I got up reasonably early, at 6:30, and had breakfast in my tent.  In my warm, now semi-dry tent.

The good thing was that the rain had stopped.  The poncho I stood up as a decoy in the night, was now wind dried.  So, I used it for staging.  I put it on the wet ground and pulled all of my stuff out of the tent and placed it on the dry poncho.  If things were dry, they stayed dry; if wet, at least they didn’t get any wetter.

Things looked a LOT better at my camp site in the morning!

This mattered, because I had a lot of wet stuff.  I had a wet hoodie.  I had wet shorts, wet socks, and wet shoes.

After evacuating the tent, I took it down.  Thanks to the wind, the fly – that had been soaking wet — was basically dry.  But the ground cloth and the bottom of the tent were very wet.

What to do?  I could either dry them and be done with it, or pack them wet, and dry them later.  While thinking about this, I put them on logs, and laid them out to dry, so that my indecision became a decision.  When the sun peeked out a little, I put them on rocks.  Yes, it ate up a lot of time, but when they were finally dry, I could stop worrying about that issue and just hike.

At 9:00 a.m. I set off.  I was wearing wet-cold shoes, wet-cold shorts, wet-cold gloves, a wet-cold hoodie, and a wet-cold hat.  Only one thing was not wet and cold:  I decided to go ahead and use my dry socks.  I hoped that my shoes were just moist and not soaking wet, and my dry socks would be a barrier to that moisture.  That plan worked out pretty well.

Because it was cold in the aftermath of the storm, I started with a lot of energy, to get my blood flowing.  I needed it too, for the trail sang one refrain:  up, up, up, and up.

Looking back at the hill I climbed — but at least I was warm, and getting dry!

By the time I reached the ridge, and looked down on Garnet Lake, I was mostly dry and all warmed up.  Then I got lost.

I could see Garnet Lake, I just couldn’t see how to GET there!

I knew I had to go east, and I spotted a little trail to the east, so I started down that trail until it kind of disappeared.  Then I spent about ten minutes wandering around up there, trying to find my way, unable to believe the east-going trail was a false trail.  Finally, I retraced my steps far enough to notice a separate west-going trail.  That was counterintuitive, but it was the correct trail, because it eventually looped around, lower down, and went east, as I knew it should.

I name this “One Pine Tree Island” in Garnet Lake

Walking along the shore of Garnet Lake I envisioned what might have happened if I had made it to this destination in the storm the day before.  I could imagine myself, struggling with my tent in the rain, and having a huge gust of wind lift the tent like a beach ball and deposit it in the middle of the lake!  It would have been bad!  Yes, hilarious, but bad.

To reinforce the power of the storm the day before, this was Ninja Queen’s  description of her experience, as she sent it to me via text, with almost no editing:

“…you can imagine how horribly wrong the day of the storm went for me.  I should have hunkered down early like most other hikers had, but I foolishly trusted my InReach weather forecast a little too much, went over Island Pass (a tiny pass but a pass nonetheless) when the storm hit full force two hours early.  It was bad.  Really, really bad.  The trail flooded very quickly, got washed out and started diverging in multiple directions from the torrential downpour.  They were ankle-deep and I kept losing the actual trail; it had become mini rivers at that point.  Needless to say, I got wetted out through and through, shivering intensely, my hands no longer functioning, unable to take another step.  I ended up setting up camp on some random flat-ish spot over some vegetation that also started pooling water.  I was so afraid my tent was going to get flooded.  Almost everything inside the tent was wet and muddy as well, though I was able to change into dry sleep clothes and sleeping bag, thanks to my nylofume pack liners.  It took me a couple of hours, but I eventually warmed up in the bag, cooked and ate food inside (bears were the last of my worries at this point) and drank as little water as possible so I wouldn’t have to go out until the next morning.  Putting back on cold, dripping wet clothes the next morning felt torture-adjacent; something I hope I’ll never have to go through again.”

“It gave me a small chuckle seeing other hikers also emerge from non-established tent sites the next morning.  I quickly learned what I went through was not a typical rainstorm experience.  Almost everyone I talked to said it had been one of the worst storms they’ve seen on trail.  One guy, a firefighter, told me this was far worse than the hurricane weather he had caught on the AT a few years back.  Now I felt like a survivor, instead of feeling like a fair weather backpacker who crumbled at the slightest hint of bad weather.”

I filled up my water at the bridge where a stream exits the lake.  There were a lot of folks around there, laying out their tents to dry from the storm.  It was surprising, and a little disturbing, to see how much foam, and even spume-like foam, was on Garnet Lake, which looks otherwise pristine.  I didn’t ask anybody about it at the time, who would I ask?

But later I Googled AI and discovered:  “…foam usually forms naturally when decaying plants, algae, and organic debris release compounds that lower water surface tension, and wind or wave action whips air into bubbles.”  In other words, the wind from the storm had produced the foam, not some giant industrial complex just out of view!

I continued up, from Garnet to beautiful Ruby Lake, then to Emerald Lake, and finally the Thousand Islands Lake.  This was an exceptionally scenic stretch.

I think this was Ruby Lake… if the water were sparkling red, it would be easier to tell!

Of course this was Thousand Islands Lake — with a pretty terrific backdrop!

There was very little elevation gain from there to Island Pass.  Once the trail reached the ridge on the opposite side of the lake it seemed to stay at a mostly constant elevation for a very long time.  The longer it went on, the more I wondered, “Pass?  Is there really a pass up here somewhere?  Where?”

No pass, but a pretty cool-looking mushroom!

How can you tell when you have reached a pass?  When you can see the other side of the mountain.  It felt like that was never going to happen.

Finally, it was getting to be lunchtime.  I decided to sit next to a charming little pond up there, and to eat my lunch.

Basically, I ate half of the food that I had brought as lunch, saving the other half for “second lunch.”  But afterwards, I just didn’t feel like I had much energy.  Even finally reaching the edge did not inspire me.  I saw the edge of the pass and started heading down, but my legs felt heavy.  My legs felt like limp spaghetti.

When your legs don’t want to go, you need your brain to execute an override.  So, I forced myself keep going.  Finally, I stopped again and made myself a second lunch in that sort of halfway space between Island Pass and Donohue Pass.  About five miles separated them and Donohue represented the entrance to Yosemite National Park.

A dead tree by a stream; dead is how I felt before second lunch!

After lunch I had more energy, and I continued.

Throughout the JMT, whenever I approached a location where I knew there was a pass in the ridgeline, I always looked up to see if I could figure out where it might be.  “Is that it?  No, is that it?  Wait, is that it?”  I suspect most hikers do this.

With Donohue Pass it was hidden around the corner of an extending hump of rock, so of course I guessed wrong.  Just short of the summit, I saw a full backpack about 25 yards from the trail.  That seemed strange and I looked around.  Was someone off going to the bathroom?  There was nothing but silence.  I continued on, but with a nagging worry for whoever was not connected with their pack.

When I arrived on top, at 11,066 feet elevation, I was all alone.  In honor of my son Gabriel, who coined the pun, I bellowed, “YO!  SEMITE!!!”  I got no answer, not even an echo.  Even so, I was in Yosemite.  It felt strange to be just a few days from the end — barring injury, of course.

As I headed down from Donohue, I thought, “On the one hand, I don’t want to set up in the dark.  But there are not too many campsites up here and, of course, I am a couple of miles behind schedule because of the storm.”  I resolved to take a chance and push it a little bit.

As a result, the riverside campsites just below Donohue Pass did not tempt me; they were filling up in any case.

The first camp north of Donohue Pass was by this ugly little lake…

Instead, I descended rapidly and with determination.  As I did, I encountered a group heading southbound, up to the pass behind me.  One young man was not carrying a pack.  Apparently, he had come down to assist his family in making the ascent.  He told me, “I left my pack up near the Pass so that I would not be encumbered in helping my family.”  I breathed deeply; mystery solved.

The first stretch went through rocky scree.  I asked the mountain gods, “Why is there so much rocky scree?”  They rewarded me with forest.

See the meadow, far below there? My campsite was somewhere down there…

The descent continued sharply through the woods, and I passed several gorgeous camping sites near the steeply dropping steam.  Each time I told myself, “No!  Not yet!” and then each time I regretted it, and wondered if I would ever find another spot, after that perfect one I had just rejected.

At last, the steep downhill tapered off, and I reached the valley at the bottom.  The stream, now wider and larger, and the trail continued to parallel each other.  The going became easier for both the water and my legs.

Following the river there were a few more possible places.  As the sun slipped behind the mountains, but still with plenty of light, I saw a knoll overlooking a wide meadow.  On that knoll was a lovely camping spot.  I was home for the night.

The sun remains on the hillside, even as it gets dark in the valley

My dinner was rice and beans and chicken, Santa Fe style.  I let it soak as I pitched my tent and arranged my site.  I let it soak some more as I explored higher up, in the giant granite boulders above the site, to find a cat hole location.  It soaked still longer as I dug the hole in preparation for the next morning.  But then, with no chores left to do, I got impatient.  I ate.

The dish was very well-seasoned, very tasty.  This was certainly a meal I would recommend having again, on another hike, in another place.  But rice takes a long time to absorb water when cold-soaking, and I didn’t wait long enough.   The flavors were great, but the rice was… crunchy.   I didn’t care much.  It had been a strenuous day, coming the day after the intense storm.  I was tired and hungry and starved for calories.  I ate it all with gusto, crunch and all!

I was feeling good.  It was a long day, and I had crossed two passes.  I was finally in Yosemite.  I haven’t mentioned the aftermath of my fall too much in recent posts, but my ribs were finally hurting less.  My wrist was still a problem, to some degree, but it was functional.

The next day, I would be passing through Tuolumne Meadow.  There I would either get a permit for Half Dome, or I would not.  If I did, I probably had three days of hiking left.  If I did not, then it would likely be two days.  I had well under forty miles to go.

As the twilight deepened, I was loving my campsite facing the open meadow.  I was proud of the mileage I had achieved, after the shortened day in the storm.  My eyes closed and I welcomed blissful sleep.

Canva is one of those companies, and it's introduced a bunch of generative AI tools to its design platform. So the email I received yesterday made me do a double take. On the day that Anthropic completed the world's biggest IPO in history, it introduced an 'AI off switch'.

Canva has added two AI controls for both Free and Pro users. The generative AI switch can be used to hide all of its generative AI tools, including Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Expand, Dream Lab and Canva Code. It can be switched on or off at any time.

There's also an option to “See less AI content” on Canva's library of templates and design elements. This addresses complaints about the glut of AI-generated content making it harder to find quality work, something that's been causing problems on platforms as varied as Etsy and ArtStation.

Canva says AI-generated content remains a "small portion" of its library and that such content is identified in its metadata. Turning on the new function isn't guaranteed to remove it completely, but it should put human-made templates and design elements first in search results and recommendations.

The addition of a couple of toggles to the UI wouldn't normally be breaking news, so the fact that Canva feels this deserved its own announcement is telling. After heavily promoting AI tools, it's now presenting the ability to turn them off as a groundbreaking advance – and it is. This might be the first time that a major AI-forward creative software developer has recognised that a sizable portion of its user base just isn't into it.

An 'AI off button' is something that some creatives have been calling for. Part of the difficulty in implementing one is that it needs a definition of AI. Programs like Adobe's Photoshop have used machine learning for many years; it just wasn't branded as 'AI'. Many artists welcome tools that speed up their work, but not ones that do the creation part for them, especially if they're powered by models that were trained on unlicensed work.

A 'no AI button' also requires a willingness from the provider. Since generative AI features are often charged on a per-use basis via credits, software companies generally want to encourage their customers to use them.

Adobe teased some impressive-looking AI-powered tools in the Adobe Max Sneaks presentation last week, but it's also clear that some customers detest generative AI, which has created a market for new tools like the artist-led Niva app, which is promoting itself as AI-free art software.

Isaac Asimov’s Book Series Was Unfilmable — Now It's Apple TV's Greatest Space Opera

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sci-fi fans would collectively agree that some stories of the genre are better off staying on the page. For long, it was also believed that Isaac Asimov's books could not be translated to the screen, and for good reason. Especially when it comes to the Foundation book series, it was hard not to believe it was largely "unfilmable."

Some of the greatest sci-fi books are written in a way that it is easy to directly adapt them for the screen. Isaac Asimov's Foundation books, in contrast, defy almost every rule of visual drama. It unfolds less as a fictional drama and more as a deep dive into everything from sociological mechanics to human psychology. While at it, it also spans across centuries, shifting its focus from one character to another.

The books' massive time jumps, anti-cinematic action, and prioritization of ideas over character beats made it impossible to adapt for the screen. This explains why many planned big-budget adaptations of the books also struggled to see the light of day in the past. Fortunately, however, Apple TV's Foundation took a massive risk and achieved the impossible by successfully adapting Isaac Asimov's books.

Impossible feats demand extreme risks. Owing to this, Apple TV's take on the Foundation books significantly changed the source material's approach. The biggest adaptation challenge came from the books' revolving characters. Every few chapters, the books experience time jumps, where old characters die of age and new ones emerge.

On the screen, viewers need rootable visual anchors who appear for longer periods.

Apple TV's Foundation carefully navigated this issue by introducing cloning, cyro-sleep, and digital consciousness. These became narrative devices that allowed the series to logically sustain the same characters and cast members across centuries and seasons. Even an immortal humanoid, named Lady Demerzel, emerges in Foundation from Asimov's wider universe.

Unlike the books, Apple TV's Foundation also visualizes key conflicts and collapses from the original story instead of solely focusing on their political implications. Even "psychohistory" from Isaac Asimov's Foundation gets a visual and more tangible representation in the series.

Armed with the Socratic method, Apple TV's Foundation understands that "The beginning of wisdom is the definition of terms." Instead of treating Asimov's story as a plot to be faithfully transcribed, the show first understands the true essence of its terms. It then turns into a more sprawling space-opera by wisely redefining its ideas.

Many readers have complained that Apple TV's Foundation is too distinct compared to its source material. They argue that it is unfair to even call it a worthy adaptation. It is hard not to agree with them. An adaptation cannot be considered great if it is a far cry from its source material.

Similar to Stanley Kubrick’s The Shining and Denis Villeneuve's Arrival, Foundation fails as a book-to-screen adaptation on many levels.

However, if purely judged as a modern television space opera, Foundation is easily one of the best sci-fi shows of our time. While Asimov's books seemingly argue that historical forces matter far more than individual stories of heroism, Apple TV's Foundation carefully bends the original narrative to focus on almost mythic heroes and solo characters.

It is also arguable that a direct page-to-page TV adaptation of Isaac Asimov's Foundation books would likely have failed. Whether Apple TV's adaptation approach is ideal is still debatable, but it at least gives us an epic sci-fi space opera.
