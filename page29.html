Netflix's 8-Part 'Squid Game Meets Stephen King' Series Is Officially Taking Over The World & Season 2 Is Possible

Netflix's Squid Game has absolutely nothing to do with Stephen King's horror. One might find a few thematic overlaps between King's stories and Squid Game, but the hit Netflix K-drama lacks traditional horror elements. Interestingly, though, a new show on Netflix seems to perfectly combine Squid Game survival horror with Stephen King supernatural terror.

The show in question, Dead-End Job, follows a simple premise. It focuses on a broken and desperate man, Hyuk‑jun, who decides to take up part-time jobs through a mysterious agency. Soon, every job turns into a battle for survival, reminding one of Squid Game's titular games. With every job comes a supernatural threat, seemingly straight out of a Stephen King book.

Dead-End Job's Squid Game parallels become even more evident when it gamifies Hyuk‑jun's jobs. With every job, Hyuk‑jun has a ticking clock over his head, and he is expected to navigate a set of rules and conditions to be able to survive. Like Stephen King's books, the Netflix horror series also finds terrors in the mundane and everyday struggles of blue-collared jobs.

In its 8-episode runtime, Dead-End Job even features Stephen King-esque monsters like an evil clown, a deadly mist, and a self-driving car. Considering how brilliantly Dead-End Job gamifies its horror by combining elements from both Squid Game and Stephen King's stories, it is not surprising that it is performing incredibly well on Netflix.

In the last week of October, Dead-End Job rose in the ranks to become one of the most-watched non-Engish shows on Netflix for the duration (via Tudum). It has already garnered over 5 million views just a few days after its release. Considering how more and more viewers are checking it out, its viewership numbers will likely increase in the second week.

Dead-End Job's early success makes sense. It is not exactly a Squid Game replacement. However, it scratches the same survival horror itch with its gamified format and supernatural twists. It also keeps viewers glued to their screens by introducing a new part-time job/game in every episode while delivering subtle social commentary on real world concerns surrounding capitalism and job insecurity.

It is almost surprising that Dead-End Job is currently being touted as a limited series on Netflix. Its storytelling format holds immense potential, and the fact that it adopts an original story leaves a lot of room for more follow-ups. Not to mention, Dead-End Job also ends with a massive cliffhanger that perfectly sets the stage for another season.

In the past, Netflix and other streaming services have not shied away from renewing miniseries for more seasons. Given how well Dead-End Job is performing on Netflix, it also seems to have a good chance of returning in the near future. However, the Netflix horror show's return will primarily depend on how it performs in the upcoming weeks.

If Dead-End Job's viewership only grows from strength to strength moving forward, it could become Netflix's next Squid Game. Even if it does not match Squid Game's virality, it has the potential to etch its own solid identity in the horror genre and potentially beat some of Stephen King's direct TV adaptations. Hopefully, Dead-End Job will get to return for at least one more season.

Its coffee is tastier and better for the environment than Nespresso (and, obviously, Keurig). I just wish there were a few more flavors available, but this could change in time.

Pod espresso machines are often reviled by serious coffee snobs. Nespresso, Keurig, and everything in between — it’s just not “real coffee”. Their words, not mine. But the Lavazza Tablì is the closest to “real coffee” a pod machine has ever got.

I’ve not been this excited about a coffee machine in a while. The Tablì doesn’t use plastic-encased or aluminum-bound pods, but 100% coffee “Tabs”. It’s as pure as you can get. At first, I wondered if this was more a gimmick than an actual innovation, but after testing the Tablì for over a week (and using up all my Tabs), I have to say: the Lavazza Tablì is seriously special.

Is this enough to make the Lavazza Tablì one of the best espresso machines? Yes, without a doubt. I’m a tough crowd, but the Tablì has done the impossible: foolproof usability, affordability, and genuinely lip-smacking coffee. It’s a home run. Still not convinced? Find out the full story in this Lavazza Tablì review.

The Lavazza Tablì is available in a few different variants. The machine alone costs $129 from Amazon U.S. and £199 from John Lewis in the U.K, which is incredibly affordable (especially in the U.S., where list prices are typically higher than in Britain). When I saw the U.S. price, I actually gasped.

You can also buy the Tablì with a Lavazza milk frother (which is the version Lavazza sent me). This will set you back $199 from Amazon U.S. You can’t buy this bundle in the U.K., but the milk frother is an extra £79 from John Lewis, making it £278 overall. I’m unsure why it’s so much more expensive in the U.K., but it is what it is.

In the U.S., you can also get the full set: Tablì, milk frother, and 60 Tabs for $249 from Amazon U.S. A similar variant in the U.K. is: Tablì, milk frother, and 48 Tabs for £199 — but only directly from Lavazza.

I will do my very best to explain just how affordable this is. $129 for the Tablì is fantastic. I was honestly expecting $200-$250 when I unboxed it. Similar Nespresso machines would be the CitiZ or Pixie, $319 and $179, respectively. Add the Nespresso Aeroccino on top of that and you’re looking at an extra $100.

True, Nespresso Vertuo machines are cheaper: you’ve got the $129 Nespresso Vertuo Pop+ and the $229 Nespresso Vertuo Up. Still, the $100 price difference between the Tablì and Vertuo Up might not be enough to justify the latter’s ice mode if you’re on a tight budget.

The Lavazza Tablì is gorgeous. While it’s quite large — 15 inches deep and 13 inches tall — nothing about its design suggests “bulky”. This is a modern, stylish piece of tech that’ll elicit some interested questions from houseguests. I can’t count the times my colleagues directed “Oooh” or “Aaah” sounds towards it. Of all the coffee machines I’ve tested, this received the most interested comments — matched only by the beautiful Lelit MaraX3.

On the machine itself are four buttons: one for power, one for a double shot, one for a single shot, and one for a long shot.

Three lights on top of the machine correspond to the water tank, grounds drawer, and cleaning. These light up should you need to refill the tank, empty the drawer, or run a cleaning cycle.

There’s nothing confusing about the Tablì. It’s a straightforward, simple machine that doesn’t skimp on aesthetics or charisma.

The sole negative is the small space between the pouring spout and drip tray. You must use a shot glass or small mug as your receptacle; normal-sized mugs will not fit.

I’ll quickly mention the Tablì app. Yes, there’s an app. No, you don’t need to download it. I would recommend avoiding the app — not because it’s horrendous, but because it’s completely superfluous.

You can make espresso recipes, but because this is literally just a pod coffee machine, the only thing you can do is adjust temperature and volume. You can save said recipes, as you can see in my screenshots, and you can send these recipes to the machine. At first, I thought the app was glitchy, but it just takes a bit of experimenting to find out how to actually navigate it. Of course, you can buy Tabs in the app, which is probably the only genuinely useful feature.

I’d skip the app unless you’re a smart home fiend.

Now onto the good stuff: taste. As mentioned earlier, the Tablì is extremely easy to use. All you do is slide up the top, insert the Tab, and press the brew button. That’s it. The shots come out as two single shots or one double, which you can control by flipping up the pouring spout (single) or leaving it down (double).

At the time of writing, you can get five flavors of coffee: espresso intenso, qualita rossa, qualita oro, ristretto, and decaf. Of the bunch, my favorite was oro. I’d like to see Lavazza introduce some new flavors in time: ideally, some kind of juicy, fruity African bean. Those who’ve been here long enough know that I much prefer a lighter-roasted Ethiopian coffee to a dark-roasted Brazilian coffee. Obviously, this is just personal preference, and if you like those rich, full-bodied, chocolatey espressos, you’re the perfect candidate for Lavazza’s default beans anyway.

I do wonder if, assuming the Tablì (rightly) takes off, we’ll start seeing some third-party “Tabs”. Or if some coffee whiz will invent a way to make your own tabs. Or if Lavazza will introduce a Tab-making product itself, so you could theoretically grind your own beans, then compact them into a tab. However, that’s a lot of effort, and the Tablì isn’t really a lots-of-effort type of machine.

As for the coffee itself, well, it tastes so much better than any other pod machine I’ve tried. Although it’s definitely over-pressurized espresso — you can tell by how aerated and fluffy the crema is — it tastes wonderful. There’s zero bitterness whatsoever, and a well-rounded, strong coffee flavor.

I tested the Lavazza Tablì and the $1,999 Philips Cafe Aromis 8000 Series back to back, and which one tasted better? The Tablì, and boy, it wasn’t even close.

The prevailing issue holding the Tablì back — right now — is the lack of variety in the pods. Sorry, Tabs. Nespresso makes a huge variety of flavors, using every type of bean from around the world, and even has special Christmas, fall, and summer flavors. If you’re the kind of coffee drinker who jumps at the chance to try maple praline or pumpkin spice-flavored pods, then I might recommend sticking with Nespresso.

However, it’s still early days for the Tablì, and you never know what Lavazza might be cooking up behind the scenes.

Even though it doesn’t come by default, I’ll quickly discuss the milk frother. If you get the Tablì with the milk frother, you won’t be disappointed. Here’s a photo of the gorgeous milk texture.

As you can see, the bubbles are velvety and small, with no textural unevenness. While I can’t classify this as an explicit “Pro” — the Tablì doesn’t come with the milk frother — I would recommend grabbing the milk frother too if you like milk drinks. You don’t even have to use it with the Tablì; you could make hot chocolates or cold foam.

As I’ve mentioned, the Tablì isn’t exactly a compact machine. The Nespresso CitiZ is relatively smaller, at 11 x 12 inches compared to the Tablì’s 15 x 13. If you need a seriously small coffee maker, I might suggest the Nespresso instead.

However, the Tablì wins in every other contest. It’s prettier, tastes so much better, and is better for the environment. Nespresso pods can be recycled for free, which is better than Keurig charging $10 to recycle 96 pods (I still can’t believe that; it’s horrifying). However, manufacturing and recycling aluminum pods still generates waste and uses resources.

The Tablì’s Tabs are 100% coffee. No aluminum wrapping, no plastic casing. You can literally take the pucks out of the machine and throw them on the garden compost, or in your food waste. They’re just coffee. Of course, the Tabs come in a little cardboard box — that looks like a little chocolate box — but everything comes in a package. Whole coffee beans come in plastic bags.

Tabs cost around $1 a pop. This is par for the course with pod machines — Nespresso pods will set you back around $0.90 each, and Vertuo pods are around $1.40 (flavour dependent).

As the water tank is filled from above, you’ll need a decent amount of clearance space. However, like every other coffee machine I’ve ever reviewed, the grounds drawer is removed from the front.

Lavazza offers a 2-year warranty, which is the same as Nespresso, De’Longhi, Breville, and Ninja.

I’m sure you can tell from this review, but I adore the Lavazza Tablì. I’ve not been this excited by a coffee machine since I tested the Lelit MaraX3 earlier in the year. I’ve never been this excited by a pod machine.

And it’s just $129? How? How is this possible? $129 for a machine capable of producing this quality of espresso is unheard of. Sure, Nespresso Vertuo machines can be found for around $129, too, but none taste as good as the Tablì. Vertuo pods are extracted by centrifugal force, while Tablì uses traditional espresso pressure (espresso literally means pressure in Italian).

Want a pod machine that makes actually delicious espresso? The Lavazza Tablì is for you. I can’t wait to see how this innovation transforms the coffee machine world.

Berkshire Hathaway's Stock Portfolio Is Worth About 3 Times What It Paid. The Gains Alone Equal About 19% of the Company.

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Berkshire's capital gains on investments it sells work like the typical investor's capital gains taxes do. Just don't expect to notice it if you're a shareholder.

Anyone familiar with Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +3.93%) (BRKB +3.95%) probably knows it owns a bunch of individual stocks largely hand-picked by former CEO Warren Buffett, or more recently, with some input from current CEO Greg Abel.

Have you ever thought about how these holdings are taxed though? It's not like a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund, which require a yearly reckoning. As a C corporation, Berkshire's stock holdings are taxed much like an individual investor's are. That is, the capital gains tax bill is incurred for the tax year in which the asset is sold.

Regardless, there's a pretty big future tax liability sitting on Berkshire's books right now. Just don't worry about it if you're a shareholder. It's unlikely you'll even notice when the time comes to sell any of these positions.

As of the middle of this year, Berkshire Hathaway was sitting on $323.8 billion worth of individual stocks, including Coca-Cola, Apple, and American Express. It only paid a total of $106.5 billion for these picks over the course of the past many years, however, translating into unrealized net gains of $217.3 billion that will eventually become realized, taxable gains. For perspective on that figure, Berkshire's total market cap at the time was $1.1 trillion. All of these numbers have changed somewhat since then, although not by much.

Current shareholders don't need to worry too much about capital gains taxes chipping away at the conglomerate's net profits, however. Like his predecessor, Abel moves big positions around pretty slowly, using time to the company's full advantage.

And this raises the question: If its stock portfolio is currently worth $361.3 billion, while the organization's market cap stands at nearly $1.2 trillion, what accounts for the remaining $828.7 billion in Berkshire Hathaway's market valuation?

Another $365.5 billion of it is cash or near-cash equivalents, such as short-term government bonds. That means the other $463.2 billion reflects the perceived value of all the privately owned businesses that are part of the Berkshire Hathaway family, including Clayton Homes, insurer GEICO, Duracell batteries, and Dairy Queen, just to name a few.

Data source: Berkshire Hathaway. Chart by author.

Yes, you're seeing that right -- Berkshire's stocks are the smallest piece of its total value, and the businesses you rarely hear about are the largest.

Moreover, this $463.2 billion in wholly owned enterprises is on pace to produce on the order of $48 billion in operating earnings this fiscal year. From an income-based perspective, these privately owned companies may still be collectively undervalued by the market, even if their typical profit-growth rates are in the modest single digits.

But that big looming tax bill? Again, don't worry too much about it. It's unlikely you'll even notice it if and when the time comes. The conglomerate's sold out several positions this year alone, and investors didn't bat an eye. In fact, Berkshire's unrealized net gains were also $202.7 billion five years ago, when its stock portfolio was worth $307.9 billion. Plenty of those positions, however, have changed in the meantime.

The goal reflects the company's intention to gain market share through GomSpace' integrated systems capabilities, continued international expansion and greater participation in higher-value solution and constellation programs.

Alongside its revenue target, GomSpace aims to reach an EBITDA margin above 20% by 2030. The company expects economies of scale, increased standardization, greater industrialization and continued product innovation to support this goal, potentially complemented by inorganic initiatives to enhance competitiveness. To support the pace and scale of its planned growth, GomSpace during the period expects to supplement internally generated funds with additional capital.

As larger opportunities involve longer sales cycles and create value over several years, results may vary between periods, but I remain confident about the long-term path. We will assess progress over time against our new financial targets.

People, values and culture will remain fundamental to consistent execution as we scale."

The EBITDA margin target to reach above 20% is supported by expected scale benefits, increased standardization, greater industrialization and continued product innovation. The margin may vary depending on the mix of business between the business units.

Growth and profitability may vary from quarter to quarter and year to year, but the company believes its medium-term financial targets can be achieved through disciplined execution of its business-unit strategy, expansion of its international commercial footprint and deeper penetration of existing and adjacent markets. Investments in scale, industrialization and innovation will remain priorities for sustainable growth and competitiveness.

The business units Products and Satellite Systems, GomSpace's two largest business units, are expected to form the core of the growth trajectory going forward. His career has never reached the same heights, but the key point is that its trajectory was, in that moment of acceptance, drastically changed.

The old thinking was wrong. That job just wasn’t right for him.

He’s not the only one. There are a few examples, from Graham Potter to Julen Lopetegui, and there’s much more to it than just “backing yourself”.

Unai Emery is at a very different point of his own career, having experienced historic Europa League success  – with three different clubs – over 22 years, but it’s impossible not to wonder whether any of this weighed on his own thinking as he now discusses a new deal with Aston Villa.

Despite all the current problems at Old Trafford, there is a history and consequently inspiration for the future; of what you might be able to do with that.

Others in football believe Emery might previously have been open to joining such a club, primarily because of the will to finally win a major league and the Champions League.

That isn’t to say either are indefinitely impossible at Villa given the club’s rapid recent growth, but their own fans have been frustrated over regulatory constraints.

And yet such strands lead into the nub of this, where this new thinking is warranted.

Emery’s record above Europa League level might raise considerable questions over whether he would suit a club of United’s scale, and those at Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain have long insisted his outlook didn’t fit.

Far more relevant, however, might be that United just don’t suit Emery.

There are numerous persuasive arguments as to why he should absolutely sign that contract and stay at Villa.

None of them are to deepen these debates about club size, or even Emery’s own career.

It’s more that the 55-year-old is in the very rare modern position of having earned an almost ideal job.

At Villa, everything is built around him, in a way you just don’t really see at this level any more. The only comparison in the Premier League or any of Europe’s wealthiest clubs is at Arsenal, where Mikel Arteta was only given such authority after initially proving himself. Emery has almost complete control, and those circumstances have ensured everything is in tune. The work of a very good manager is consequently greatly amplified, so that Villa consistently over-perform.

That can even be seen in how they’ve responded to the great frustration of the summer. They’re already in far better shape than would have been expected. Tactical shifts can take time, and be painful, as currently being seen at Newcastle United. On Premier League pitches, the game is so systemised and optimised that the margins have become very thin. Hence wild swings in results when clubs aren’t in tune.

At Villa, it’s almost note-perfect for Emery. That isn’t something to be given up easily.

FX's 27-Episode Sci-Fi Masterpiece Deserves Kudos For Breaking Every Storytelling Rule

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

For all that sci-fi television has become much more experimental narrative formulas in the age of streaming, one corner of the genre remains surprisingly attached to tradition: superhero shows. Even the most adventurous and ambitious superhero TV shows seem to eventually fall back into old storytelling habits. Even the series that deliberately lean into strangeness for a selling point do so with the promise that everything will eventually click into place and the true heroes and villains of the story will meet for a final showdown no matter how morally ambiguous they’d been up until that point.

That makes an often overlooked 2017-2019 FX series is deserving of much more recognition. Before Disney+ explored superhero surrealism through MCU shows like WandaVision and VisionQuest and Prime Video lambasted the entire genre with The Boys and Invincible, Legion was already dismantling expectations. Creator Noah Hawley was tasked with making a Marvel adaptation, and instead delivered a series that treats every scene as a chance to experiment. The show’s 27 episodes are dripping with confusion, discomfort, and unpredictability, and it makes for some thoroughly entertaining viewing.

In terms of the show’s premise, Legion follows David Haller (Dan Stevens), a psychiatric patient who discovers his mental health struggles are linked to extraordinary mutant abilities. While the series is loosely connected to Fox’s X-Men movies and draws from Marvel Comics mythology, Legion’s plot stands on its own, and Noah Hawley used that independence to turn what could have been a standard superhero TV show into something truly unique.

From the moment it begins, literally nothing about Legion plays into expectations surrounding its plot and narrative structure. For example, most superhero shows spend time establishing a status quo for reality before introducing something that threatens it. Legion makes reality itself the problem. Memories can be manipulated, seemingly ordinary spaces become mental prisons, and it’s always unclear whether a scene is actually happening or just playing out inside someone’s head. Because David’s mental state means he can’t trust his own experience, the same applies for viewers too.

This combination of atmospheric surrealism and an incredibly unreliable narrator defines how Legion’s story is told. The show’s 27 episodes include some incredibly avant-garde installments. Season 1's “Chapter 7” turns escape from a mental institution into a black-and-white silent film sequence, while “Chapter 10” in season 2 depicts the confrontation between David and central antagonist The Shadow King using dance. This psychedelic approach gives Legion’s psychic conflict its own unique style, making the show’s superpowered struggles more unsettling than a simple exchange of energy blasts or city-leveling fist fight.

It’s not only the scene-by-scene storytelling in Legion that flips expectations on its head. The wider narrative also dismantles a core theme of almost all superhero shows: discovering immense power means inheriting a heroic destiny. David eventually comes to see himself as burdened with glorious purpose, but his self-perception as a savior and the reality of his place in the world couldn’t be more different. His certainty that he deserves love and redemption increasingly justifies the harm he causes others, inverting the traditional message that power, heroism, and responsibility all go hand-in-hand.

Several superhero shows that arrived after Legion have also embraced unreliable perspectives, unconventional formats, and morally compromised "heroes". Few, however, are able to sustain the sense that even their storytelling methods can unexpectedly change at any moment across three whole seasons. Legion may not be the most accessible series out there, but its experimental style shapes the entire experience. Its underlying uncertainty is inseparable from its explorations of what ideas like “truth” and “reality” even mean when everyone’s perception is subjective, and this thematic interest in turn means it breaks almost every narrative rule.

Calling Legion a superhero show is accurate as a description of its source material. The show incorporates X-Men characters such as David himself, the Shadow King, and even Professor Charles Xavier. As a description of actually watching it, however, the “superhero TV show” label is incredibly misleading. Sure, there are genre staples like mutants, secretive organizations, and potentially catastrophic powers, but the show itself has little interest in using these elements to construct a good vs. evil superhero narrative about saving the day.

If one were to assess Legion in a vacuum without considering its X-Men roots, psychological horror is a much more appropriate genre label. David’s mind is a thoroughly unsafe place where childhood memories conceal terrifying intrusions from outsiders, and apparent understanding is often just a trick to disguise more secrets. Alongside psychological horror, Legion also works as an experimental sci-fi series, using concepts like psychic spaces and, eventually, time travel to explore identity, responsibility, and the desire to rewrite painful experiences.

A new UK law banning porn featuring choking and “fauxcest,” or simulated incest, may have unintended consequences for content creators and smut fans all over the world, some experts warn.

As of November 6, amendments to the Crime and Policing Act will outlaw the “possession or publication of pornographic images of strangulation or suffocation”—a law intended to curb violence against women and girls. Yet some experts warn that the legislation is written broadly enough that it could potentially criminalize far more innocuous content, including “breast smothering,” “jeans facesitting,” and even potentially oral sex, ostensibly because people could have their airways impeded during these activities.

“I would encourage both content creators and consumers to underscore the risks of this law,” says Myles Jackman, an obscenity lawyer in the UK. “Because to me, it genuinely seems like one of the greatest acts of suppression of free speech in global history.”

The new amendments were inspired by a lengthy pornography review authored by Baroness Gabby Bertin, a Conservative member of the UK House of Lords. The legislation bans various depictions of sex acts, including “pornographic images of strangulation or suffocation,” as well as content showing performers role-playing as minors under the age of 16.

It also takes aim at “fauxcest,” an extremely popular porn niche that depicts simulated sexual relations between family members who are not related by blood, such as stepsiblings or stepparents. In Bertin’s review recommending the ban on such content, she cites “anecdotal evidence” suggesting that fauxcest “can be used between an abuser and child to permit interfamily child sex abuse,” describing it as a “fig-leaf” of sorts for actual sexual abuse.

Under the new law, those caught distributing or in possession of such content face up to five years in prison and a fine.

The impending UK legislation has prompted panic among some adult creators, many of whom are currently combing through their content libraries and removing clips that could get swept up in the ban.

“The problem is always how broad [the legislation] is,” says Alexandra Snow, an adult content creator and educator. “And we won't know how they're going to interpret it until they start making people's lives hell.”

Snow says she is particularly concerned about how the aspect of the legislation pertaining to “suffocation or strangulation” will affect kink creators, who may make content related to collaring or leather fetishes. She’s also worried about AI image-recognition software, which is already in widespread use among the platforms, potentially flagging content that isn’t violative at all. “They use a very broad, heavy-handed approach,” she says.

Cami Strella, an OnlyFans creator in the US who estimates that 60 to 70 percent of her content depicts some form of role-play, including step-“fauxcest,” is also concerned about the legislation. She says that the content she makes is “just playing a fantasy,” and that more sex education and literacy is required to teach people about the risks of various behaviors.

“I don’t think banning it is going to do anything,” she says. “If anything, it’ll just push even worse content that is actually illegal or immoral underground, so you’ll have to access it away from these platforms. This is just a slippery slope we’re getting into.”

The new UK law is seemingly targeted at the trend of choking in porn, which has been linked to real-life instances of fatal choking during sex. Though the practice is highly risky, potentially leading to brain damage or death, it’s increasingly common among young adults, largely due to the prevalence of eroticized choking depictions in the media and in porn. The death of a 16-year-old Australian girl in August, who authorities say lost consciousness after she was choked consensually during sex, has led to renewed calls for legislation banning depictions of the practice in online porn, which has “absolutely sort of normalized this kind of sexual violence,” Bertin recently told the BBC.

The UK has a history of imposing stringent restrictions on internet pornography, particularly compared to the United States, where statutes like Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act of 1996 largely exempt platforms from liability for hosting objectionable content. Long before many US states started requiring users to verify their ages before visiting porn sites, for instance, the UK communications regulation agency Ofcom has been slapping websites with fines of up to 10 percent of their worldwide revenue for failing to comply with age-verification requirements.

Jackman, the lawyer, says that he has no issue with the provision banning strangulation, as its risks are proven and there are existing laws banning strangulation in the UK. “It would be up to a court to decide this on a case-by-case basis, but given that it would appear that face-sitting by a vagina owner is now arguably suffocation, the insertion of a penis could equally be considered suffocation because it blocks the airways,” he says. The subscription-based platform OnlyFans is particularly vulnerable, he adds. Though most American porn companies don’t have a substantial presence in the UK, making it difficult for Ofcom to go after them, that doesn’t apply to OnlyFans. “If I were OnlyFans,” Fattorosi says, “I’d be very worried about this.” (In a statement, OnlyFans tells WIRED the platform “complies with applicable laws and regulations in all jurisdictions in which it operates and has longstanding, proactive safety measures in place that meet UK legal requirements.”)

📅 2026-11-05  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

BEIJING — Democrats taking power from Republicans in Congress raises risks for U.S.-China tensions around Taiwan, while hardening tech competition, analysts said.

The Democrats swept this week's midterm elections, taking majorities in the House and Senate and flipping governors' offices in places like Texas, Nevada and Iowa. Most of the new election winners, however, won't begin their terms until January.

Before then, U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping will likely maintain their fragile truce by meeting twice: in Shenzhen this month, and in Miami, Florida, next month.

"As a lame duck who is increasingly focused on his personal legacy rather than the Republican Party's success, Trump may resist increased pressure for confrontation with China and continue his pursuit of 'strategic stability,'" Gabriel Wildau, managing director at Teneo, said in an email.

High on the Democrats' agenda is removing Trump's tariffs, as part of a broader focus on addressing domestic inflation, CNBC has learned.

"On balance as a result of the midterms the Democrats may nudge the U.S.'s China policy in a more hawkish direction through hearings and legislation, particularly on economic security issues and Taiwan," said Scott Kennedy, senior advisor and trustee chair in Chinese business and economics at the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

But any efforts will "be constrained by the executive branch's wide range of latitude and China's growing power and leverage, which can't be easily undone," he said.

Beijing has hosted a flurry of global leaders this year, while major economies have struggled to slow a surge of imports from China. Chinese dominance in rare earths has given Beijing leverage in trade talks with the U.S. and the EU.

"Taiwan remains the most dangerous issue in U.S.-China relations and, in my view, the area where a Democratic Congress is most likely to challenge Beijing," wrote George Chen, partner and co-chair of digital practice at consultancy The Asia Group.

"Congress has repeatedly shown a willingness to pursue Taiwan initiatives even when administrations are more cautious," he said, noting Trump's balancing act will likely become more difficult.

Nancy Pelosi's visit to Taiwan while House Speaker in 2022 angered Beijing, which considers the democratically self-governed island part of its territory. The U.S. maintains unofficial relations with Taiwan under a "One China" policy.

Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.), who is poised to become the first Black Speaker of the House in U.S. history, met in 2023 in New York with Tsai Ing-wen, who was then president of Taiwan.

During Trump's trip to Beijing in May, Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan would put U.S.-China relations in "great jeopardy."

Maintaining stability over Taiwan has grown more critical in the artificial intelligence race as the island is home to semiconductor giant TSMC, which manufactures many of Nvidia's chips.

Over the last several years, the U.S. government has tightened restrictions on the ability of Chinese companies to buy advanced chips from Nvidia, spurring Beijing's efforts to develop its own version of the technology.

U.S. stocks surged Wednesday after Trump accepted the Democrats' election wins, and oil prices fell following indications Washington would do more to end the Iran war. Apple and most "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks surged, despite declines in Nvidia.

But there's an "emergence of a broader consensus in Washington that America's technological lead should not be used to strengthen China's military or strategic capabilities," Chen said, noting a Democratic Congress may be motivated to be more aggressive.

"As a result, Beijing may discover that technology becomes a growing source of friction even if Trump and Xi continue to maintain relatively cordial summit diplomacy," he said.

The tech outlook ultimately depends on bigger questions about AI.

Chinese tech stocks climbed Thursday, extending a recent rebound from a sharp sell-off last month.

Beijing's latest regulatory intervention has helped support the market, said WisdomTree's Liqian Ren. But she said Chinese tech stock performance is ultimately tied more to global investor sentiment on AI.

"There's a lot of uncertainty on how much AI can deliver for U.S., or for China," she said.
