10 Upcoming Anime Based on Manhwa That Could Be The Next Solo Leveling

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Vanessa Piña has been writing as a journalist for over two years. As a senior writer, she focuses on timely coverage of the latest anime releases and popular comic franchises.

Solo Leveling is one of the world's most popular manhwa, with an adaptation that has propelled it to the pinnacle of the anime industry. While this might lead fans to believe that no other series will ever reach such heights, the outlook for webtoons within the anime landscape has never looked brighter. As of 2026, the world is turning its attention to manhwa's greatest gems, with partnerships set to adapt several popular webtoon series in the coming years. As a result, some major, heavyweight webtoon adaptations are currently in production that might rival Solo Leveling's popularity when they premiere.

Some Webtoon adaptations have failed to reach the level of quality expected for titles as popular as their source material, The Beginning After the End being one such example. Fortunately, some upcoming anime based on manhwas come from well-regarded studios or are the result of collaborative projects driven by genuine enthusiasm to make them grow into global IPs. This suggests that, beyond Solo Leveling, there could be other major hit anime coming from the webtoon world premiering soon.

The next Otome Isekai webtoon to receive an anime adaptation is Villains Are Destined to Die, which has managed to carve out its own legacy, cultivating a massive fanbase and even surpassing Solo Leveling's sequel on its platform. Combining video game mechanics, strategy, complex characters, and romance, Villains Are Destined to Die's story remains intriguing from beginning to end, appealing to a far broader audience by placing a much greater emphasis on tension and survival than most otome isekai.

Other otome isekai webtoons that have their own adaptations include Why Raeliana Ended Up at the Duke's Mansion and Who Made Me a Princess.

In the series, the protagonist unsuccessfully attempts to clear a dating simulator in "hard mode," which leads to her waking up as the game's villainess, Penelope Eckhart. Unlike most isekai, where the protagonist knows exactly how to complete the game, here, any slight mistake that lowers the affection level of the male characters can result in Penelope’s death, adding a more ruthless approach to the villainess subgenre. Perhaps the greatest testament to the series' quality is that the anime adaptation was announced shortly after the webtoon concluded, a clear sign of the strong desire to bring this story to screens worldwide.

Although not strictly an anime, Hero Killer's animated adaptation was announced at the Annecy Animation Festival in June 2026, the result of a collaboration between Naver Webtoon, ADN, and the studio Passion Paris, which was tasked with translating the series' distinctive visual style. French animation has already delivered high-quality titles like Arcane, and, given that Hero Killer is a long story, spanning nearly 300 chapters so far, it holds enormous potential to become a series with global reach.

Hero Killer manages to get readers to take the side of the villains. The protagonist, Ihwa, seeks revenge against the hero who killed her sister. She begins eliminating heroes one by one while trying to uncover the person responsible. With its exploration of the relationship between heroes and villains and the dark secrets they harbor, along with highly creative, unique powers known as "Gifts," and a strong female lead, Hero Killer is a fantasy-action series that has everything to shine in an animated format.

Anime fans love good underdog stories in which the hero starts from the bottom, but Hardcore Leveling Warrior is an unusual case where the protagonist begins at the top, only to be forced to start the process of becoming strong all over again. The story takes place within Lucid Adventure, a VRMMORPG where Ethan Gong, the top-ranked player with maxed-out stats, is suddenly attacked, causing his progress to be reset all the way back to Level 1.

Ethan must level up again, forming a party and seeking revenge against the mysterious player while getting progressively stronger and contending with real-life troubles, making the series capture the same adventurous spirit found in power fantasy titles like Sword Art Online and Shangri-La Frontier. Hardcore Leveling Warrior is part of the first wave of adaptations co-produced by WEBTOON and Warner Bros. Animation, announced in late 2025, so it's likely that it won’t be long before more details about the project are revealed.

Fans of Solo Leveling will surely enjoy SSS-Class Revival Hunter, as both are power fantasy series that begin with low-rank hunters as protagonists. The series follows Kim Gong-ja, whose sad life, marred by jealousy, takes a turn when he awakens an S-class skill. He has to be killed to use it, but in exchange, it allows him to copy the power of the person who killed him.

While SSS-Class Revival Hunter is a regression story (an overused trope in manhwa), it manages to feel truly fresh. Part of its charm lies in its blend of genres, incorporating not only time travel but also martial arts and romance, shifting settings and tones across different arcs and developing a rich cast of supporting characters. The series also features surprisingly emotional moments, earning it a level of recognition that's only set to grow once its adaptation premieres. SSS-Class Revival Hunter is the first anime produced primarily by Studio Fu, and it's scheduled to premiere in Winter 2026.

The Boxer is a critically acclaimed manhwa, and for good reason. The story centers on Yu, a young man apathetic toward life but possessing an immense talent for fighting, who is suddenly thrust into the world of competitive boxing. The series features thrilling matches, packed with dynamism and the psychological tension experienced by the athletes.

Beyond offering just a realistic take on boxing, The Boxer carries significant philosophical weight, featuring flawed characters and serving as a profound exploration of passion, trauma, life, and human nature. An anime adaptation was announced in 2023 for a 2025 release; the production plan has since been revised, and it will now be a collaboration between the Japanese and South Korean studios, Breccia and STUDIO JEMI. While an official release date has yet to be set, the first teaser for The Boxer already offers fans a gorgeous glimpse of what the anime might look like.

Teenage Mercenary is the anime that could reach the highs of Solo Leveling when it comes to stylized fight choreography and hand-to-hand battles. In the series, Yu Ijin, a former child mercenary, returns home after years on the battlefield but can't entirely leave his training behind as he uses his military experience to protect his family and friends from dangerous bullies and criminals.

Solo Leveling's still the hottest anime around in many circles, but fans craving another Redice Studio manhwa brought to life are in luck.

That way, Teenage Mercenary feels like a fusion of John Wick and Weak Hero with a tactically skilled protagonist capable of taking down entire rooms of attackers and threatening underground organizations, who's also an unassuming student shattering school hierarchies and defending the weak. The anime is set to release in 2027, and while J.C. Staff has been criticized for its work on One-Punch Man, the studio's latest manhwa adaptation, Overgeared, has enjoyed better quality. This, along with the fact that Teenage Mercenary became the top webtoon in Japan, gives fans hope that the series will get the adaptation it deserves.

Latna Saga is one of those stories that showcase the potential of isekai, with the capacity to become the revolution that the genre needs within anime. In Latna Saga, Ryu Han-bin is summoned to another world, only for a system bug to leave him trapped in a cycle where he is forced to constantly restart his leveling-up process for two decades. This grants him immense power despite his seemingly low level, making him an overpowered protagonist who conceals his true abilities, much like Sung Jinwoo from Solo Leveling.

While Latna Saga explores a harsh, unforgiving fantasy world, the series also incorporates plenty of comedy, with supporting characters who help lighten its serious moments. The anime is being produced by Studio Pierrot in collaboration with the Korean studio Red Dog Culture House, a partnership intended to kick-start numerous webtoon adaptations. Despite the lack of updates, since Studio Pierrot has recently wrapped up Bleach: Thousand-Year Blood War, Latna Saga could be next on their list.

The Stellar Swordmaster is easily one of the current biggest webtoon titles, having surpassed 250 million cumulative global views, a level of success that could well be mirrored in its anime adaptation. The series is among the titles set to be adapted as part of the partnership between Warner Bros. Animation and WEBTOON and is a medieval fantasy following Vlad, an orphan who lives in the slums and aims to become a knight.

In addition to its impressive art, The Stellar Swordmaster perfectly captures that traditional fantasy atmosphere that has been set aside in modern series in favor of RPG-style worlds. It tells a story of honor and survival, set in a world rich in lore, featuring social divisions, ancient bloodlines, unique sword arts, and even its own power system, as the protagonist rises to find his own brilliance. Its multidimensional characters and watching Vlad’s relentless growth in power make it a highly engaging story, so the series is sure to generate plenty of buzz once it gets animated.

Anime has secured its next martial arts sensation with Gosu, and there's no better studio to bring it to life than the one behind Dragon Ball. Murim webtoons has gained popularity for their depiction of ancient clans and societies centered on martial artists clashing for power, and within this genre, Gosu has emerged as one of the most iconic titles, featuring some of the best battles found in any manhwa.

The series is also not your typical revenge story, as the protagonist, Gang Yong, spends years training to exact his vengeance on those who killed his master, only to discover that his targets are all death. The art in Gosu is simply magnificent, and it also incorporates humor, while the scale of its battles reaches truly unimaginable levels. As such, it serves as Dragon Ball's perfect successor with the potential to become anime’s new flagship title. Furthermore, Toei Animation's collaboration with the Korean studios Studio Mir and Studio N holds great promise for truly doing justice to Gosu's source material.

The adaptation of Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint has kept fans in suspense since it was first announced in July 2024. Although the anime was confirmed by Aniplex and Crunchyroll, neither a release window nor the studio behind the project has been revealed. Yet, even this uncertainty has failed to dampen the hype surrounding the adaptation as one of the few webtoon titles that rivals the impact of Solo Leveling.

Readers trying to fill the void left by Solo Leveling will find the following manhwa teeming with monsters, powers, and the fall of civilization.

It's no coincidence that Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint frequently ranks among the best web novels ever written, as the work has it all: from its post-apocalyptic setting and stunning plot twists to a complex protagonist who manages to navigate danger by making the most of the knowledge he possesses. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint has surpassed 3 billion views worldwide and continues to go strong, with the webtoon having recently entered its second season. All in all, it has already laid the groundwork for its eventual anime adaptation to potentially become just as popular as Solo Leveling.

The 5 Biggest Airbus A220 Fleets In 2026 Don't Even Include The Jet's Launch Operator

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Air France took delivery of its 60th Airbus A220-300 on November 6, closing out a five-year narrowbody renewal program. That milestone underscores the regional jet's growing global fleet and its place among the world's largest operators. Joining the French flag carrier on that podium are two airlines that exclusively fly the A220: Breeze Airways, with 57 and growing, and airBaltic's 55-strong fleet.

The budget carriers on opposite sides of the Atlantic Ocean join Delta Air Lines, which boasts 92 as of now, and JetBlue Airways with 65, to round out the top five. Notably, SWISS International Air Lines, which was the launch customer for the airframe, still owns just 30 aircraft. The jet's difficult development and launch after Airbus acquired the platform from Bombardier is just one part of the story.

The A220 began as the CSeries before Airbus acquired a controlling stake in 2018. The Swiss order ended after it replaced its aging Avro RJ100 regional fleet, partly due to the ongoing engine crisis that has plagued the plane despite the otherwise popular airframe. Persistent durability issues and supply-chain bottlenecks with the Pratt & Whitney PW1500G engines forced operators worldwide to ground jets and even strip some for viable powerplants, keeping as many flying as possible.

Latvian flag carrier airBaltic introduced the stretched A220-300 in December 2016 and retired every turboprop and Boeing 737 by 2020 to run a pure single-type fleet. The most recent data places the carrier fifth globally with 55 aircraft listed on its roster, according to Airport Spotting. However, not all of those are airworthy. Groundings peaked at 13 parked jets in early 2025, wiping out thousands of seasonal flights across Europe and forcing costly replacement wet leases.

Restructuring plans shrink the active fleet from 54 jets to 36 by year-end and put 40 undelivered orders under review, even though the airframe itself never caused the crisis. Operated purely on its merits, the airframe exceeded expectations across both scheduled flying and wet lease contracts. Its low fuel burn let airBaltic restart routes profitably at lower passenger loads after the pandemic while leasing up to 13 aircraft to SWISS and Lufthansa Group partners.

Flying one 145-seat cabin simplified crew training, cut maintenance complexity, and supported flights stretching 3,450 nautical miles (6,389 km) from Riga to leisure markets like Tenerife. The vulnerability sat beneath the wing, where the A220 certifies only Pratt & Whitney's PW1500G geared turbofan.

When metal defects in turbine blades triggered urgent inspections and stretched engine overhauls past 200 days, airBaltic had no alternative supplier to fall back on. Engine availability recovered early this year, yet years of grounded aircraft left airBaltic financially fragile when unhedged fuel bills spiked following the recent conflict in Iran. On September 14, the carrier filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New York, naming Pratt as its largest creditor.

SWISS will retire its pioneering A220-100 fleet as engine shortages favor the larger A220-300.

Air France secured third place globally on November 6 when its 60th A220-300 arrived in Paris before opening service to Prague three days later, per Airline Geeks. That handover fulfilled the 2019 commitment to retire outdated Airbus A318 and A319 aircraft serving its network. Five years after entering service, the 148-seat fleet has carried nearly 24 million passengers and established the A220-300 as Air France's new medium-haul mainstay.

Finishing the original batch roughly a year behind its initial 2025 target still left four 20-year-old A318s on the winter schedule. Industry-wide delivery delays and Pratt's PW1500G recalls tightened spare availability. As a result, Air France extended A318 operations to March 27, 2027. Rather than stopping at 60 jets, however, Paris now anticipates a 61st A220's arrival and is keeping 30 options plus 30 purchase rights.

Route economics and passenger demand are driving Air France-KLM Group leadership to envision growing toward 90 or 95 A220s. Its five-abreast cabin puts 80% of travelers in a window or aisle seat with 20% more overhead bin volume, while free high-speed WiFi already reaches 85% of the fleet, with full installation expected by February 2027.

The modern jet could pose stark competitive pressure in the long-term.

JetBlue holds second place globally after counting 66 A220-300s in October out of its 100-aircraft total order, according to the Center for Aviation. That expansion completed the carrier's September 2025 phase-out of the Embraer E190. Trading 100-seat E190s for 140-seat A220-300s cut operating costs per seat by 29% and fuel burn per seat by 40%, projecting $4 million to $5 million in annual gains per jet.

Meanwhile, passengers benefit from bigger seats measuring 18.6 inches (47 centimeters) wide, the roomiest economy seating in JetBlue's fleet. That comes alongside 35 inches (89 centimeters) of pitch in extra-legroom rows and 32 inches (81 centimeters) in standard rows. The jets also have upgraded seatback screens and free high-speed WiFi.

Even at scale, JetBlue saw disruption from Pratt's engine recall as well as the Airbus factory bottlenecks in Mirabel and Mobile. Fiscal reports showed the airline opened the year with four aircraft parked over PW1500G shortages. After engine removal, the planes spent roughly 200 days in overhaul shops. Supply-chain delays also forced Airbus to trim JetBlue's delivery schedule, pushing its final three firm aircraft into 2030 and beyond.

Eliminating the E190 still consolidated JetBlue into an exclusively Airbus operator, streamlining pilot and mechanic qualifications alongside its A320 and A321neo fleets. The transition also unlocked longer transcontinental routes for its smallest aircraft.

These airlines are the world's three largest operators of the type.

Breeze climbed to fourth worldwide when its final Embraer E190 revenue flight landed in New Orleans from Orlando on September 7, following acceptance of the 57th A220 in July, per Airport Spotting. This autumn deliveries pushed the Salt Lake City carrier over 60 jets out of 90 firm orders and 30 options for more. That fleet blueprint follows the design of Chief Executive David Neeleman when he ordered 60 jets in 2018, right after Airbus took over the program.

Neeleman revisited the decision in an Aerospace Global News story in September of this year, saying: "Our long-term strategy has always been to leverage the strengths and efficiencies of the Airbus A220-300 to help us cost-effectively scale our unique business model which centers around offering affordable, nonstop flights from unserved and underserved markets."

Breeze fits 137 seats inside the A220-300, pairing 125 economy chairs behind 12 Ascent first class recliners pitched at 39 inches (99 centimeters), flying 91 cities across more than 300 routes where 85% to 90% carry zero nonstop rivals. That 3,400 nautical miles (6,297 km) reach outclasses the Embraer E195-E2 and older Boeing 737-700s, opening cross-border flights to Cancun, Punta Cana, Montego Bay, Nassau, Costa Rica, and Santo Domingo.

Running one modern airframe slashes spare parts inventory and simplifies pilot scheduling compared with airlines splitting narrowbody flying across multiple cockpits. Meanwhile, the premium front cabin fills faster on thin leisure routes than any other regional jet could match. The airline has seen its 'long and thin' city pairs become steadily more profitable routes as a result.

A high-density model is set to enter service next year.

Delta Air Lines has consistently led the global rankings by a wide margin, with 87 counted in July before third-quarter figures raised its roster to 92 jets, per Aviation Daily Report. Unlike the single-variant fleets run by JetBlue, Breeze, Air France, and airBaltic, Delta flies a split roster of 45 A220-100s averaging seven years old and 47 newer A220-300s with 145 firm orders.

Atlanta built its fleet on an order of 75 Bombardier CS100s from April 2016. That triggered Boeing's 2017 trade complaint, which was later linked to the proposed 292% US tariff. Chief Executive Ed Bastian held firm in October 2017, according to Reuters, saying, "We are not going to pay any tariff, and we do expect to take the airplanes," just before Bombardier transferred majority control to Airbus and set up final assembly in Mobile, Alabama.

US trade commissioners unanimously threw out those tariffs in January 2018, clearing Delta to launch A220 service from New York's LaGuardia. Today, it deploys two variants from coast to coast as 25-year-old Boeing 717-200s and even older A319s are phased out. Looking ahead, the gap between top operators will widen further as Airbus eyes a 2027 launch decision on a stretched Airbus A220-500. The new variant would seat 160 to 185 passengers, with deliveries targeting 13 jets per month from 2028.

Vibecoded GTA 6 PC Ports Are Now Rendering In-Game Graphics Despite Take-Two Crackdown

It took Rockstar Games more than a decade to develop Grand Theft Auto 6. It took a bunch of anonymous vibe coders less than a week to get parts of it running on a platform Rockstar hasn't released it for.

Unofficial PC ports of GTA 6 are now capable of rendering in-game graphics and handling player inputs, according to footage circulating on social media. Some projects have achieved speeds of around 20 frames per second, despite the increasingly aggressive efforts of Rockstar parent company Take-Two Interactive to shut them down.

A developer going by AvatarManz reportedly achieved between six and 20 frames per second using an AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX, a high-end graphics card. The unofficial port consumed up to 46 GB of system memory, considerably more than what a typical gaming PC has available.

Another demonstration reported Thursday appears to show a character riding a jet ski and driving along the coast at between 19 and 23 frames per second. Performance drops to around 12 frames per second in urban areas, and patches of water are visibly missing from the scene.

new GTA 6 PC gameplay shows walking around the prologue, with textures, lighting and physics looking much better. Ray tracing is reportedly working too.

Still only 1–3fps on an RTX 5070 Ti, Ryzen 7 7800X3D and 32GB DDR5. Visuals are coming along, performance has a way to go. pic.twitter.com/ON00pzHFHX

According to community tracking data site Portwatch VI, at least six of 35 tracked projects have reportedly reached interactive gameplay. Some developers have also managed to get ray tracing working, a computationally demanding technique that simulates how light interacts with objects.

The footage and performance figures have not been independently verified, and none of the projects has demonstrated that the entire game can be played reliably on a PC.

Much of the progress comes from developers relying on vibe coding, a method of software development in which users instruct AI models to generate and debug code using natural language rather than manually writing every instruction.

The developers aren't recreating GTA 6 from scratch. They're reverse-engineering an existing Xbox build and developing software that allows its code to execute on Windows, translating console-specific instructions and accommodating differences in how PCs handle graphics and other hardware functions.

AI coding tools such as Anthropic's Claude have dramatically accelerated this kind of work, reducing some tasks that previously took months to days. Optimizing the resulting software and fixing the inevitable bugs are separate challenges, especially for a game as complex as GTA 6.

And the whole thing, of course, spaked a meme coin.

As Decrypt previously reported, notorious Rockstar leaker Cyberleek distributed what they claimed was a 209 GB Xbox build of GTA 6, withholding its main executable file as part of an attempt to push their CYBERLEEK meme coin to a $15 million market capitalization.

The token failed to reach its target, but Cyberleek released the executable on November 23 anyway. Within a day, some of the most prominent porting projects, including Leonida and GTA6Modding, went dark.

Take-Two subsequently confirmed its involvement through a copyright complaint targeting Leonida. GitHub disabled 3,007 repositories associated with the project.

In its DMCA notice, Take-Two argued that the project reproduces copyrighted material and violates Rockstar's terms of service, which prohibit unauthorized reverse engineering and decompilation.

Some developers have since taken their work into private communities, making their progress harder to independently assess.

Rockstar released GTA 6 on November 19 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, targeting 30 frames per second on consoles. It has yet to announce a release date for an official PC version.

Solo Leveling's Next Big Release Just Got A Game-Changing Update

Merlyn De Souza is a freelance writer and anime enthusiast based in Goa, India, with a Bachelor's in English Literature. When she isn't writing, she can be found exploring various realms of entertainment, including, but not limited to, K-dramas, novels, and even the occasional manhwa. The Top 10 Open World Games

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

There was a time when video games were largely linear affairs. You either moved up and down or from left to right in enclosed spaces. As technology evolved, so did the scope of digital worlds. Now players are regularly treated to expansive environments filled with quests, interesting characters, and horizons that stretch further with every console generation. These experiences consume dozens if not hundreds of hours and knowing which titles are worth the time investment has become crucial. To that end, we’ve assembled a list of 10 great modern open-world games available today to help make what can be a paralyzing decision a little easier.

Note that these are listed in release date order and don’t necessarily represent the best games of all time. Rather, this will be an ever-changing list of quality titles to arrive in recent years that you should go out of your way to get lost in.

Grand Theft Auto is arguably the blueprint for open worlds in video games, and its sixth entry is the series' peak. In a universe steeped in satire and cynicism, protagonists Jason and Lucia are instantly likable figures worth rooting for. Their love story is absolutely worth your time, but even if you made the decision to ignore it, you'd be overwhelmed by Leonida's exceedingly vast playground, ripe for exploration. When we say GTAVI's scope is staggering, we are partially talking about the literal size of the map, but we're mainly talking about its density; every corner is meticulously crafted with people to meet and places to rob. It's one of the most believable spaces in gaming history, bolstered by incredible graphics and populated with characters only a little more zany than you'd encounter in its real-life equivalent of Florida. Its dozens of cars feel great to drive, and its dozens of guns are a thrill to shoot, but if you decide to get really into birdwatching instead, that's fine by us. Grand Theft Auto VI is one of the best games of this generation – it's no surprise it made its way onto this top 10 list. | Our review

In an overly simplistic way, Ghost of Yōtei basically took everything that worked about Sucker Punch’s 2020 open world action game, Ghost of Tsushima, and improved it. Exploration, combat, visuals, and narrative are all major steps up from the previous game, which was already fantastic. Atsu’s journey of revenge, fighting and exploring her way through Ezo, is an always-compelling experience, not because it radically re-addresses or changes the mechanics that made Tsushima so great, but rather because they are carefully iterated on and improved in sometimes subtle ways. And as an open world game, it constantly excels at giving the player worthwhile distractions that all appear to the player naturally. You’re not bouncing between points on an overworld map – you’re literally following the wind where it takes you and always ending up somewhere amazing doing something incredible. | Our review

Kingdom Come: Deliverance II is one of the highest-rated open worlds on this list, and for good reason. If Grand Theft Auto VI is the best modern-day open world, and Cyberpunk 2077 the best future-set open-world game, then KCDII is easily the best medieval open-world game in some time. Eschewing the more fantastical likes of the medieval open-world format – which The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt excellently covers on this list – KCDII does all it can to remain grounded and true to the 15th Century Kingdom of Bohemia land it’s set in. You follow Henry, whom you’ll craft through your decisions and upgrade choices, on a journey of retribution, rebellion, and reflection, and you’ll meet an unforgettable cast of characters along the way. They are the grounding elements to an otherwise wide-open adventure that lets you craft a narrative only you can craft. However you invest your time in this massive, sprawling medieval landscape will be reflected in the Henry you play as and how the world responds around you – there’s no shortage of mischief and chivalry to get up to, and across its dozens of hours, you’ll be taken through one of the most rewarding open games you can play today.  | Our Review

FromSoftware proves its punishing yet satisfying style of action RPG absolutely works in an open-world format. Brimming with mystery and danger, Elden Ring allows players to make their own way, providing little guidance or hand-holding, making every discovery feel like a genuinely exciting and well-earned reward. Unlike traditional Souls games, slamming against a difficult boss isn’t the end of the line. You can simply explore elsewhere and fall down the rabbit holes of breaching an impregnable fortress, surviving a treacherous swamp, or taking down dozens upon dozens of other imposing enemies. Quality-of-life additions such as free fast travel and friendlier checkpointing before boss fights alleviate some stress, further encouraging you to hop aboard your spectral mount and uncover every secret of this incredible world. | Our Review

The greatest trick the Forza Horizon series pulls is fooling players into liking racing games by disguising them as open-world RPGs. The latest entry, Horizon 5, serves as the pinnacle of that Trojan Horse-style design. Set in a stunningly beautiful rendition of Mexico, players sit behind hundreds of vehicles to compete in a seemingly endless number of wild races. Whether you're speeding against other cars or a 747 airplane, the sheer variety of events and an over-the-top tone make Horizon more lighthearted compared to most pure racing sims. In addition to traditional races, you can enjoy Horizon 5's entertaining take on battle royale, a zombie-infection mode, and absurd story-driven scenarios (like driving a parade float). Best-in-class driving mechanics solidify Horizon 5's first-place standing | Our Review

The team closed out the game with the incredible Phantom Liberty expansion, which adds a heaping dose of Idris Elba and spy-thriller action to Night City, ensuring any V who steps into this dystopian future has hours upon hours of open-world fun to enjoy. 8 Smartest Characters In The Lord Of The Rings Movies, Ranked By Intelligence

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

In The Lord of the Rings, J.R.R. Tolkien presents what we would call "intelligence" in quite a nuanced way. It's important to realize Tolkien was no fan of the modern idea of "intelligence." As he wrote in one letter, "I have never yet heard of an 'intelligence' test that was not specially suited to select nitwits (bright perky little nitwits possibly, but not always even so). But what would you: they are invented by nitwits (or some by impostors)."

Tolkien prized carefulness of thought, not quick-thinking. He believed in the value of knowledge, but that knowledge had to be digested and interpreted. In truth, I don't think Tolkien actually believed in the pursuit of intelligence at all; rather, as a Catholic, he honored something that his faith would probably call "wisdom." All that makes it fascinating to look at The Lord of the Rings through the lens of intelligence, though, because it helps us to see the world as Tolkien did—and to learn from him.

Grima Wormtongue is one of Tolkien's most underestimated characters, in large part because Peter Jackson's movies diminish Wormtongue quite significantly. Jackson presents Wormtongue more as a channel of Saruman's power than a mastermind in his own right. When Gandalf frees Théoden from Wormtongue's influence, there's a magical backlash that knocks Saruman off his feet. But that idea is entirely absent in the books themselves.

In the movies, you see hints of Wormtongue's true nature. He's ruthless, cunning, a manipulative mastermind who can figure out any way to spin a crisis to his advantage. Grima Wormtongue is the consummate modern politician, the spin doctor if you like. It's not a coincidence that Tolkien portrays him as the villain whose intelligence—deployed so selfishly—almost brings down an entire kingdom. With Wormtongue, we immediately see that intelligence is a very dangerous thing if you're lacking in character.

It's easy to criticize Denethor. A ruler of Gondor, he is slow to respond to Gandalf's appeals, simply because his intelligence is stronger than the strength of his convictions. He's right in his estimation of Mordor's forces, and he correctly understands the strategic importance of Minas Tirith. He's something of a political mastermind in his own right, understanding the relationships between different countries. I like to think of the Bismarck of Lord of the Rings.

But again, notice Denethor's weaknesses. It's possible he was a little too intelligent; he believes he could filter the facts, and thus discounted the possibility he was being manipulated. Emotions increasingly compromised his judgment—and, again, he's unaware of it. Tolkien's characters are moral ones, and Denethor is a warning to the world that intelligence without morality and conviction can and will lead you astray. Especially if others are there to manipulate you.

It would be so easy to miss Smaug on this list. We tend to focus on Smaug as one of The Lord of the Rings' most powerful villains, but the Great Dragon displays a great deal of what we would call intelligence. He's surprisingly strong when it comes to understanding and interpreting others, as seen in the riddles he sets. Those demonstrate a remarkable depth of strategic thinking, deductive reasoning, and perception.

Tolkien reveals Smaug's intelligence in so many subtle ways. His manner of speaking is more complex than other characters, showing an intuitive grasp of metaphor and demonstrating quite sophisticated rhetorical techniques. He swiftly identifies Bilbo's psychological weaknesses, manipulating them with skill. But again, note that this interpersonal intelligence is flawed because of Smaug's character. The Great Dragon isn't necessarily a warning against the excess of power, but rather of trickery and cunning.

As physically powerful as the elves may be in The Lord of the Rings, they are also notable for their intelligence and wisdom (although the latter is often presented as flawed, compromised by excessive nostalgia). Elrond is thousands of years old, and he's been part of so many critical events on Middle-earth; you can get a sense of how important he is in The Rings of Power, where a younger Elrond is a key character in a story loosely based on Tolkien's notes.

Importantly, though, Elrond's historical knowledge informs his analytical reasoning. He's able to place the present danger in its historical context, and understand both the risks and the best strategies. Remarkably, he is willing to listen to different perspectives; he even sets aside the traditional conflict between Dwarves and Elves, allowing every member of the Fellowship a voice. But again, note that he's a little too conservative, a little too cautious. He has to be heeded, but sometimes overruled.

I suspect some readers will disagree with my placing Galadriel above Elrond. But the comparison between the two is striking; like Elrond, she has accumulated a vast amount of knowledge and experience, beyond anything mortals can ever imagine. What matters, though, is that Galadriel demonstrates what we would now call emotional intelligence. She sees into the souls of others, understanding their characters and motivations.

She is also strikingly humble. Notice Galadriel's reaction when Frodo offers her the One Ring; she knows exactly what it would do to her, and she wants nothing to do with it. Galadriel's perception is relatively untouched by ego, meaning she moves beyond mere intelligence. She's the first character on this list to truly demonstrate wisdom.

In pure intellectual ability, Saruman is almost unparalleled in The Lord of the Rings. It helps that, like the Elves, he draws on a vast reservoir of experience; but his experience is even less limited than theirs because wizards are essentially angels in Tolkien's mythology. Saruman is a scholar and a gifted researcher, an academic who asks the questions no one else dares ask. He notices the sweeping patterns of history, the decline of magic, and the potential of industrialization.

But, of course, Saruman is limited because of his own ego. He sees patterns, and believes he can master them. He looks on others with disdain, viewing them as a means to the end he seeks to accomplish. What's more, because the pattern of progress is visible, he believes he should master it and determine it. Saruman's intelligence is second to his ambition, demonstrating once again that character comes first in The Lord of the Rings.

Think of Gandalf and Saruman as two sides of the same coin. Gandalf's natural intelligence is roughly equal to Saruman's, who perhaps has the edge on him in terms of overall analytical ability. But with Gandalf, intelligence is tempered both by conviction and humility. Gandalf recognizes the patterns of history, but refuses to ally with things he believes are wrong. His morality gives him a different starting point, if you like, meaning the outcome is very different.

Time and again, Gandalf demonstrates that his intelligence is subordinate to his humility. According to Tolkien, Gandalf was always more powerful than Saruman; he just didn't desire fear or reverence, and thus never capitalized on it. The Lord of the Rings carefully tests Gandalf's humility. By passing these tests, ultimately setting aside the egotistical desire to control and risking everything by sacrificing his own life, Gandalf proved worthy to become the White. Gandalf's story is the triumph of character over intelligence.

And that, of course, brings me to the final entry in this list: Sauron himself, easily the most intelligent being on Middle-earth, and a cautionary tale from Tolkien's perspective. The Rings of Power has helped demonstrate this, revealing the range of Sauron's accomplishments; he is a craftsman and artisan as well as a military strategist, a would-be creator whose intelligence is turned towards refashioning the entire world into his own image.

If Gandalf's story is the triumph of character over intelligence, then Sauron's evil is the inverse. He is far more intelligent than anyone else in this story, a skilled strategist who is capable of weaponizing the most sophisticated abstract principles and turning them to his own advantage. But always to his own advantage, because Sauron is an egotist at heart. He failed the tests Gandalf passed, and became the greatest monster of The Lord of the Rings.

Last week, ICAO Headquarters in Montréal hosted the Seventh Worldwide Air Transport Conference, where Member States and industry stakeholders gathered to set global policy priorities on aviation economic regulation, infrastructure financing, resilience, consumer protection and sustainable growth.

Over 1,500 delegates from 154 Member States and organizations attended the week-long event thirteen years after the Sixth Conference in 2013. They discussed high-level topics in commercial aviation: modernizing economic and legal frameworks, investing in infrastructure and skilled workforces, and ensuring that innovation keeps pace with rapidly changing technologies and global demands.

“Broad agreement was reached on the need for harmonized, risk-based, and proportionate economic regulation that facilitates the adoption of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, automation, sustainable aviation fuels, and advanced air mobility solutions,” ICAO said in a press release during the Conference.

Participants agreed on the value of frameworks that support innovation and investment for economic development. They stressed this especially for smaller landlocked States, which depend heavily on air connectivity for growth.

The Conference supported modernizing air navigation infrastructure in line with ICAO’s Global Air Navigation Plan. It also supported liberalizing air services agreements without making liberalization conditional on infrastructure development, since new air links fostered by more liberal agreements are seen as key to economic growth.

However, all initiatives should be flexible enough to address local circumstances and should not burden developing States. ICAO’s statement continued: “The Conference also reaffirmed that international standards are key to building effective, market-enabling policies and avoiding fragmentation that could impede cross-border air services. Delegates pointed to ICAO’s Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA) as a successful example of this approach.”

Participants were asked to express their views on the documentation proposing various initiatives with regards to consumer protection legislations that have become an integral part of the legal framework for all air operators.

The report for the Seventh Conference invites States to modernize their consumer-protection frameworks, keeping them evidence-based, proportionate, accessible and responsive to the changing realities of commercial air transport. It also says legislation should establish clear responsibilities for carriers, intermediaries and other providers regarding information, complaints and disruption assistance.

ICAO is expected to review its Core Principles while preserving national and regional flexibility. It is also expected to assess whether existing policies adequately cover online intermediaries and other overlooked situations.

Many Member States cautioned against the ICAO Air Passenger Handbook proposed by IATA, because producing and maintaining a single repository covering many different national laws would be too costly.

The Conference asked ICAO to “examine the need for harmonized, risk-based guidance on the use of AI in aviation, including common terminology and meaningful human oversight.”

The rapid development of AI and its unknown effects on aviation operations is a topic Member States will need to monitor. ICAO will handle AI within its existing structure, as the proposal to create an ad hoc unit was not supported.

During the final two days of the Conference, ICAO held an Extraordinary Session of its Assembly to elect the four additional Council members required by the entry into force of the 2013 protocol amending the Convention on International Civil Aviation. The Council is the Organization’s permanent governing body, accountable to the Assembly and responsible for setting priorities and implementing its directions. Its membership increased from 36 to 40, and there are no permanent seats.

Six candidates competed for the four new seats: Ethiopia, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Oman, Thailand and Türkiye. In the vote on the final day, Kazakhstan and Thailand were not elected, and the other four States won seats.

Never put the backup server in the same Windows domain as production

Welcome, once again, to PWNED, where we’re thankful every time someone leaves a password sticky note on a monitor or fails to delete a terminated employee’s admin account. But neither of those common occurrences is what we’ll be talking about today. Instead, our subject broke the number-one rule of backups: you gotta keep ’em separated.

Our story comes courtesy of Da Li, who serves as global alliance manager at Info2Soft, a company that provides data protection and recovery software. At one point, a partner called him into a case where a Chinese vehicle parts manufacturer fell victim to a ransomware gang. Unfortunately, its backup was compromised too.

The manufacturer had religiously backed up its manufacturing execution system (MES) and SAP orders, inventory, and finance systems every night. There was even a green dashboard that showed the backup process had succeeded, and someone checked that every morning.

However, one Friday evening, crims broke into the network and found out that the backup server was joined to the same Windows domain as the rest of the network. The hackers found a plaintext password for the backup service account in a maintenance script on a file share and discovered that someone had used the very same password for a domain admin account.

So the first thing the crooks did was delete every restore point on that backup server. Then they started encrypting the production servers to ransom them.

“Every backup job had succeeded for three years,” Li said. “The failure was that the thing meant to save them could be reached with the same keys as the thing being attacked.”

"The time to prepare for a cryptographic breakthrough is before it happens," the team wrote.

While practical breaking of the pairing-based cryptography used by Sapling remains highly speculative, Zakura also recommended a network upgrade to disable payments into Sapling. Roughly 400,000 ZEC, or approximately 2% of supply, remains in the pool, according to Zakura, worth roughly $291 million at current prices. Zakura said most wallets support both Sapling and Ironwood, and the impact should be minimal, with miners accounting for much of the activity left in Sapling.

An earlier Zcash forum proposal in April had suggested waiting a year after a community decision to make Sapling withdrawal-only. At the time, the proposal put its balance at 600,000 ZEC, down from 1.1 million at the start of 2024. Zakura did not suggest a new deadline in its Thursday post.

Zakura pointed to a reported proof of the Riemann hypothesis, which concerns the distribution of prime numbers, and unsubstantiated rumors that AI labs had also made advances in integer factorization, the process of splitting large numbers into their prime factors. The difficulty of that task underpins RSA encryption, which is rarely used in cryptocurrencies, according to the team. Zakura said some math can be used for attacks on both RSA and pairing-based cryptography, so advances in one could help with the other, but the reported Riemann proof and the rumors do not show that either can be broken in practice.

"Practical pairing breaks are highly speculative," Zakura said, adding that transparent transactions and Ironwood do not rely on factoring or pairings.

Nevertheless, a hypothetical break in Sapling's pairing-based proof system would allow forged proofs, Zakura said, putting the funds in the pool at risk. The same cryptography does not secure users' private keys or their cryptographic records of shielded funds, so the team said developers could replace the proof system without replacing those records. However, Zcash would have to freeze Sapling before an attack compromises its state and users could then later recover their funds using a new ECDLP-based or hash-based SNARK, Zakura said.

Vitalik Buterin is taking Ethereum’s decentralization philosophy down to the silicon level. It could have implications far beyond Ethereum, and computing hardware shouldn’t remain a proprietary black box.

Could Ethereum eventually move beyond blockchain infrastructure and build its own hardware ecosystem? Vitalik’s latest exploration makes that prospect an interesting thought experiment, particularly as Nvidia dominates the computing industry.

Vitalik’s argument starts with a familiar problem in crypto: trust. Software can be audited, code can be published, and blockchain transactions can be independently verified. Hardware, however, remains considerably harder to inspect.

A chip can contain hidden vulnerabilities or malicious modifications that users cannot easily detect. Even a secure wallet application cannot guarantee safety if the hardware underneath it has been compromised. This has happened recently with Ledger, and Ledger is not the only one, with Trezor and other hardware wallets being recently compromised.

“Not your silicon, not your keys.” – Vitalik put the problem bluntly.

The phrase extends crypto’s self-custody mantra into the physical computing layer. Owning private keys might not mean much if users cannot trust the hardware handling them.

This is why Vitalik argues that “the hardware layer must be open for participation and tinkering too.” He wants hardware designs to become accessible to developers, researchers, and independent auditors, allowing them to examine and modify components rather than blindly trusting manufacturers.

This could eventually create opportunities for Ethereum hardware focused on security, cryptographic computing, and decentralized infrastructure. Ethereum validators, hardware wallets, and zero-knowledge proof systems all depend on computing components that users need to trust.

Nvidia built its dominance by turning GPUs into the backbone of artificial intelligence. Its hardware, software ecosystem, and CUDA platform have made competition increasingly difficult.

But Vitalik’s vision displays a different approach. Instead of chasing the fastest processors, Ethereum hardware could prioritize openness, verifiability, and security.

Vitalik acknowledges that open-source hardware faces performance limitations. Advanced chip manufacturing remains expensive and tightly controlled, leaving open designs behind the industry’s most sophisticated commercial processors.

Yet that trade-off might not matter for every workload. Vitalik writes that “Secure hardware may be 10x less efficient or more” while still being sufficient for many computing needs. That’s a striking contrast to an industry obsessed with squeezing more performance from increasingly expensive chips.

Specialized silicon designed for cryptographic operations, privacy-preserving computing, secure data processing, or decentralized networks could benefit from open designs. Developers could modify the architecture, researchers could inspect the circuitry, and manufacturers could compete to produce chips from publicly available specifications.

Ethereum already has an extensive developer community experimenting with cryptography and distributed computing. Extending that culture into silicon could give the ecosystem a foothold in hardware without immediately challenging Nvidia’s AI infrastructure.

The ambition could be less about building another GPU giant and more about creating a competing hardware philosophy.

The eminent evolutionary biologist and modeller John Maynard Smith was once asked about Karl Popper and the philosophy of science. His answer was not flattering. His impression was that scientists who take the philosophy of science seriously, and allow their research to be influenced by philosophical preconceptions, are "far more likely to do themselves harm than good". He loved reading philosophy of science and was always ready to argue about it, he added, but he did not believe that one should allow oneself to be influenced by it when actually thinking about science.

This is why I've recently published an academic article on the topic. These are quite radical words, and though Maynard Smith's generalisation is untenable, there is an important insight to be extracted.

If modellers approach the philosophical literature on models with the goal of figuring out what models are and what a good model is, they might easily find themselves lured into naive monistic views of models that fail to represent the full epistemic richness of modelling practices, thereby turning them into worse scientists than they could have otherwise been.

This could happen in several ways. For instance, models with epistemic functions not recognised by narrow monist accounts might be discarded. Consider, for example, models with weak predictive power, but great generality and insight into complex systems.

Perhaps Maynard Smith's worry is most strongly illustrated in economics, where Milton Friedman and Karl Popper are taken to have the "last word" in any methodological debate, and are frequently invoked to both defend and criticise the work of modellers. They are perhaps paradigm cases of monist thinking. Friedman argued that the only relevant desideratum in science concerns predictive power, while Popper argued that what matters is falsifiability.

These accounts of science have received extensive criticism over the last decades. Indeed, the philosopher Alex Rosenberg argued that Friedman's naive picture single-handedly justified the existence of philosophy of economics as a distinct profession. Economists, however, are unlikely to read much if any work on methodology beyond Friedman and Popper. As the economist Dani Rodrik notes, economic methodology is relegated to specialised journals and, more importantly, is not really being taught in graduate school.

The result is that economists seem to believe that their science operates according to a simple form of falsificationism, a picture of science that, most contemporary philosophers of science would agree, both misrepresents scientific practice and provides the wrong ideal of how science ought to function.

The obvious counter to Maynard Smith's concern is that it is not an argument against the philosophy of science, but rather against bad philosophy of science, which limits, rather than fosters, insights into the richness of the epistemic tools available to scientists.

Philosophers are not innocent here. Some debates in the philosophy of science rest on such monist motivations to seek univocal accounts of laws, explanation, species, functions, preferences, and many more. As Peter Godfrey-Smith emphasised, one of the hazards of philosophy is the temptation to come up with theories that are "too broad and sweeping". This hazard has not been avoided in the philosophical literature on models. Many philosophers of science seem to be under the impression that once we figure out what "models" are and how they work, we can finally get a handle on all the other pressing questions in the philosophy of science. I argue that this way of thinking about models is grounded in a mistake.

A philosophy of science worth reading takes the plurality, diversity, and richness of models and modelling practices seriously. It does not hand scientists a single criterion for good science.

Economists, Rodrik argues, need a better story about the kind of science they practise, and some economists have recently engaged with philosophical questions themselves, seeking contact with philosophers of economics in the process. Philosophers of science, in turn, should turn more of their attention toward both the social sciences and the philosophy of the social sciences. Their lack of engagement with this rich literature is a historical omission that may have provided a misguided understanding of how science works.

Should scientists ignore philosophers? They should ignore those who tell them that there is only one way of doing science well.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE +1.57%) told investors on Nov. 23 that it still can't get all the memory chips it wants. And it doesn't think that'll change soon.

"We expect supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained to at least the second half of calendar 2028," CEO Antonio Neri said on the company's earnings call that evening.

That seems like good news for Micron Technology (MU -4.53%), which makes DRAM (the memory computers use while they work) and NAND flash storage chips. But the memory maker's shares slid anyway. Micron closed at $707.70 on Nov. 23, just before the call. As of this writing on Nov. 25, the stock traded near $550, less than half the $1,213.56 it closed at on June 25, its record.

Meanwhile, BNP Paribas downgraded the shares, saying memory supply might catch up to demand a year sooner than it had thought.

The tech firm said in March that DRAM and NAND make up over half the cost of the parts in a traditional server. And its inventory more than doubled in fiscal 2026, from around $6.4 billion on Oct. 31, 2025, to $11.8 billion by July 31 and $14.1 billion by Oct. 31, 2026.

HPE's timeline seems to have stretched, too. In March, Neri said he expected high DRAM and NAND prices to "persist well into 2027."

CFO Marie Myers said the $14.1 billion inventory reflected higher costs for commodity parts like memory, purchases for customer orders, and what she called "strategic buys." She added that HPE's commodity costs climbed by significant double-digit percentages in 2026, and she expects smaller increases ahead. But smaller increases are still increases, not the price drops a glut would cause.

BNP Paribas thinks one could. In a note reported on Nov. 24, it cut its rating on Micron to neutral from outperform. It now says DRAM and NAND supply and demand might approach balance starting in mid-2027 and drive average selling prices down. It flagged risks to big cloud companies' data center plans for 2028 and new factory capacity coming online.

Micron's stock dropped 11% that day, and again on Nov. 25, when research firm TrendForce said NAND flash might move into oversupply in 2027. TrendForce still expects DRAM to stay tight through 2027, and DRAM made up about three-quarters of Micron's revenue in fiscal 2026.

Part of the new factory capacity BNP mentioned is Micron's own. Its first new fab in Idaho should start making wafers in mid-2027, and a facility in Taiwan should begin meaningful shipments around then too. But Micron says output at a new fab ramps up over a few quarters. And its other planned fabs, including a second one in Idaho and a new NAND factory in Singapore, aren't set to start output until the second half of 2028 or later.

"[W]e do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance," management wrote about DRAM in Micron's prepared remarks for its Sept. 30 earnings call.

HPE's 2028 date matches Micron's building schedule. BNP's earlier date seems to rely more on demand weakening.

Micron's fiscal 2028 ends on Aug. 31, 2028, so HPE's outlook covers nearly all of the year. If the outlook holds, Micron could spend most of fiscal 2028 selling into a market still short on chips. If BNP is right, prices could be dropping for most of it.

The stock price looks closer to the second case.

At roughly $550, Micron trades at a price-to-earnings ratio of about 7.4 on its fiscal 2026 results. Based on the average analyst estimate for fiscal 2028, the price is below 3 times earnings.

Even BNP sees Micron's earnings per share bottoming at $86 in 2029, over the $74.33 per share Micron earned in fiscal 2026 under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). The stock looks cheap even on that bearish estimate.

One buyer's view could be wrong, of course. But I think HPE's outlook undercuts the glut worries, especially for DRAM, Micron's largest business. A buyer still stocking up at higher prices is tough to square with a stock priced for falling memory prices by mid-2027.

I'd consider buying some Micron shares here, in a position small enough to ride out more days like Nov. 24. If HPE starts pulling that 2028 date forward, like BNP just did with its own, I'd rethink that.
