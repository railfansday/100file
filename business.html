Don't be fooled by Horses of Hoofprint Bay's hand-drawn art, this stable management game has all the drama of a Jilly Cooper novel

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Poema is my pride and joy. She was my first horse and she's a competition winner, but she's reached her potential. She'll never be a show jumper, and that's where the money and prestige are to be found. If I'm ever to grow my horse farm into a nationally renowned equestrian centre, I need the cash to expand and upgrade my facilities. Poema is a hoofstep on the path to that future, but she's not going to be able to take me all the way herself.

For all its hand-drawn aesthetics, Horses of Hoofprint Bay has hard realities like the limits of Poema's future bubbling under its surface. While the game's scripted narrative may be lacking, it's shockingly easy to fill in the gaps with drama worthy of a Jilly Cooper novel.

There may be better author comparisons, but I learned everything I know about showjumping from Cooper's Riders – much as I learned everything about independent television franchise bids in the '80s from its sequel Rivals – a book which is as much about the nitty gritty of horsetraining and competing as it is about toffs jumping into bed with one another. In my mind the two are tightly connected - showjumping and Jilly Cooper, not horses and toff bonking. In Horses of Hoofprint Bay, the only real story you get is the fact you've scrimped and saved to buy your first horse and your uncle has lent you the use of his field while you start your business. I was hoping there would at least be a similar setup to Regency Solitaire, where your motivation for touring the country's tearooms is motivated by paying off your brother's gambling debts. Yet, despite this disappointing start, I soon found its business simulation mechanics provide great storytelling material.

Horses of Hoofprint Bay has many of the trappings of a cosy game. At the start of each day, you'll prepare the stables, dragging a rake around the yard to sling away the manure, clicking buckets of water to refill the trough, and making sure there's enough hay for all the horses. But this isn't just for the larks, if you skip these tasks there are serious knock on effects for yours horses. Forget to brush down your horses' coats or pick out the stones in their shoes, and they're liable to develop saddle sores and other injuries when you ride them. Leave an abrasion untreated and it can develop into an infection. Even something like taking a dirty horse to a contest can cost you. I forgot to brush Poema before entering her into her first contest and despite taking first place, her embarrassingly unkempt coat lost my stable prestige. It was a cruel end to what should have been a great celebration.

So long as your horses are well rested, you can train them each day. You may want to take them through some confidence building lunges, where they need to walk, trot, and canter on demand, or a more physically demanding Cavaletti or showjumping course. These latter too are much more tiring, but they build a horse's strength and endurance. The higher its endurance the more energy it can expend on training sessions each day, and the higher its strength the more jumps it can do in succession before slowing to a walk for a breather - essential when you're competing in a timed competition. Overdo the exercise, however, and you'll exhaust a horse, severely reducing its energy for the next day.

Now, unlike Poema, my freshly bought stallion Kolizey has the potential to compete in showjumping competitions. He'll never go all the way to the big national contests, but he can take a ribbon in the district and regional contests. Or he will when he's up to standard; he's arrived from the breeder in a sorry state: brambles in his mane, mud on his flanks, stones in his shoes, and his strength and endurance are far too low to make it through an entire course of obstacles.

The biggest problem, however, is Kolizey's confidence: it's shot. I don't know what was happening in the stalls at the breeder's, but he arrived at my stables backing up at the first sign of a jump. Before I can hope to earn him his first ribbon, I have to build up our relationship. If we exercise together enough in the yard, his confidence will grow and he will follow my direction without question.

It may be getting ahead of myself, but I also have plans for Kolizey and Poema to produce a foal. Done right, the two should raise a horse that's able to exceed its parents' potential, becoming stronger and more endurant than both of them. A stronger horse means a ticket to the bigger contests and the bigger prize pots. Not that I can start breeding the two just yet. I want both horses to be at peak physical fitness. Poema's already there, while Kolizey can't even manage a jump without shying away.

While getting Koliszey contest ready, I still need to be bringing in cash. That means using Poema as a training horse for students' riding lessons. The better I am at the minigame where I use the Q, E, W, A, S, and D keys to tell them to sit forward or back, hold the reins higher or lower, or to level out their legs, the more they tip me. I have to be careful, because when Poema is working with students, she's not being stretched, and her strength and endurance can wither which means she struggles to maintain her winning streak in the contests.

Another reason to hold off on getting Poema pregnant is that during the second half of her pregnancy, she can't be ridden, which means no contests and no lessons for students. Currently, those are my two main sources of income besides what I'm paid in rent to board a couple of nearby farmers' horses. I don't want her out of the rota when Kolizey isn't yet ready to step into her place.

And, well, while I'm on the subject of plans, reader, I must tell you, the future lies in semen. If I can build the facilities, earn the cash, and raise Kolizey's profile, I can make a good buck on his virile stallion cream. With the money I bring in from a prizewinning semen I can expand into the far field, creating space to take on more horses, upgrade my home from the caravan I'm living in currently, maybe even build a proper training school. I'm not going to afford all that by giving riding lessons to the local children or boarders' rent. No, the big money comes from competition wins and valuable horse sauce.

While Poema continues entering contests and carrying students, I start pushing Kolizey harder. Multiple training sessions every day. I begin with Cavaletti, which is a kind of obedience competition. Your horse has to be travelling at the correct speed when it passes marker flags – walking, trotting, or cantering – and make some small jumps over cross bars. It should be straightforward, but because Kolizey's confidence is so low, he keeps refusing my orders and trying to change speed. He warns me before he's about to speed up or slow down, and if I counter it in time he will obey me and even receive a little confidence boost, but often he does it just before a jump, messing up my timing and he hits the bars. Each time he fails a jump it knocks his confidence, risking me taking me even further from where I need him to be.

I try the easier lunges sessions. And they raise his confidence a little but they're doing little for his physical health. It's all taking too long and Poema's getting older every day. In a fit of frustration, and in mark that I may not be able to cut it as an animal trainer, I skip from the Cavaletti training straight into a showjumping qualifier. The time limits on these are much stricter, so you have to clear the course at a gallop. This is also where a horse's strength really comes into play. The faster you're going, the more strength it requires to jump an obstacle. Horses only recover strength when moving at a walk, so you don't want to tire a horse out by going at a gallop through the whole course, else they won't have the strength to make all the jumps and they'll be forced to withdraw from the event. But nor do you want to go too slowly and fall foul of the time limit.

I realise my mistake as soon as the course begins: I try to stir Kolizey into a walk he refuses. Thankfully the timer only starts when he crosses the line, but it's an inauspicious start. I tap forward again and he begins to walk. I tap again and Kolizey moves up to a trot, then to a canter, before picking up to a gallop. I think he's going too fast as we approach the first jump, but Kolizey clears it. Then another jump and another. With a nervous eye on the depleting strength bar, I tap him down to a canter for the next jump. We're halfway through the course, I briefly slow to a walk to recover some strength then take the next three jumps at a canter. We're so close to the end now but a big yellow exclamation mark appears next to the strength bar. Kolizey is on the edge of exhaustion but he refuses to slow down and increases to a gallop for the last two jumps.

He completes the course with a perfect clear. Not a single obstacle clipped and we end with ten seconds left on the timer. Not only has Kolizey earned his showjumping license, his success swells his confidence. He's a natural athlete.

The next day, with my horses rested, I take Kolizey and Poema to town to compete in the showjumping and Caprilli events respectively. They both bring home first place, raising the prestige of my stables, earning me a tidy little packet, and convincing me it's time my prized horses started a family. I'm perhaps too eager, when I try to send them to the breeding station, I'm told they're both 'Done for the day'. I suppose I should give my contest winners a bit of a break. They've earned it. I groom them and send them both to bed with an apple as a treat.

Except, maybe they weren't too tired: the next morning, I'm met with a message telling me Poema is pregnant. My stable's become the Olympic Village, with my rosette-winning horses working out the highs and stresses of competition in the only way they know how. Good on them. I needn't have even made the breeding station.

Then, with horror, I remember the horses I'm boarding for the neighbours. They're both stallions themselves. Neither of them are at competition level, their strength and endurance is lower even than Poema's when she arrived at the stable. If either of them are the father, Poema is about to be out of action for a whole season and at the other end of it I'll have a foal that can't even compete in the beginner events. fetch the equestrian version of Hercule Poirot, I need to find out who impregnated my horse!

It may not be a plot worthy of Cooper, but it will do for me.

The 7 Airlines Flying Airbus A321XLRs On Both Short & Long-Haul Routes

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In evolutionary biology, conversation is often focused on 'the' thing features evolved for. For example, bird wings evolved for flying. In reality, birds will use them for whatever they can. Penguins use them for swimming, while chickens use them for changing direction while running and shading their chicks.

Aircraft are somewhat similar. They may be optimized for a specific mission, but that doesn't mean they will be used for those missions by the airline all the time. In Vietnam, VietJet sometimes flies its widebody A330s on ultra-short domestic routes from Ho Chi Minh City to Nha Trang half empty. VietJet apparently does this because the time slot is available in the A330's international schedule.

The Airbus A321XLR is optimized for longer, but thinner routes. On shorter routes, its increased weight from its extra tanks and strengthened landing gear imposes a penalty. Even so, some airlines will operate them on short routes, partly because they are available. Let's examine some of the airlines using the XLR on both short and longer-haul routes.

Aer Lingus is the flag carrier of Ireland and has a fleet of six Airbus A321XLRs. These are configured with 16 business class seats and 168 economy class seats for a total of 184 seats. This is identical to its complementary fleet of eight A321LRs. In July 2026, Aer Lingus flew more than 300 flights longer than 3,100 miles (5,000 kilometers) and more than 200 routes shorter than 1,250 miles (2,000 kilometers) with its XLRs.

Those shorter routes are European hops when the aircraft are not flying their longer transatlantic trips. An example of a long Aer Lingus XLR flight is Dublin to Nashville, which comes with a scheduled flight time of nine hours and 20 minutes. Meanwhile, during the first week of November, the XLR (registered EI-XLU) has flown hops such as Dublin to London (48 minutes) and Dublin to Barcelona (one hour and 50 minutes).

Flightradar24 shows EI-XLU's longer recent flights include Nashville (Tennessee), Cleveland (Ohio), Toronto (Canada), Indianapolis (Indiana), and New York. Being located at the far western edge of Europe, Aer Lingus has the advantage of being able to reach more destinations in the US with the XLR. The XLR is an excellent aircraft for thinner transatlantic missions, but it is also ok to use on short hops, accepting a modest penalty.

Iberia was the launch customer for the Airbus A321XLR in late 2024, and today it operates a fleet of seven examples with an eighth on order. These are configured with 14 business class seats and 168 economy seats, totaling 182 seats. Spain (excluding Russia) leads Europe with the most domestic flights, driven by the need to connect the mainland with the Canary Islands and Balearic Islands.

From its base in Madrid, Iberia flies its A321XLRs to transatlantic destinations in the United States such as Boston, Newark, Washington DC, and Canada's Toronto. Having a more Spanish and Latin focus than carriers like the UK or Ireland, it also flies them to the Dominican Republic (Santo Domingo) and Puerto Rico (San Juan) in the Caribbean (both Spanish-speaking).

In South America, its XLRs fly as far as Recife and Fortaleza in Brazil. Recent flight tracking data for the XLR registered EC-OOJ reveals it is mostly flying longer transatlantic routes. However, it has also flown European hops to Zurich and Paris (both one hour and 30 minutes) as well as Venice (two hours).

Qantas will have one of the highest passenger capacities onboard its Airbus A321XLRs.

Air Canada is one of the XLR's newest operators, with the national Canadian airline flying the aircraft since June. Now the airline has a growing fleet of five to six in service. Air Canada's official website says it has six in inventory, while Planespotters.net still records five delivered, of which one is currently grounded. That aircraft is brand new and working up.

Regardless, Air Canada is in the process of building up one of the world's largest XLR fleets, with around 24 of its 30-strong order still to be delivered. Air Canada is using the aircraft on a range of longer routes, including transatlantic flights to Europe. C-GXLR was the first XLR delivered to Air Canada. It is also used extensively on medium (three- to five-hour) flights to various destinations in the United States and western Canada such as Los Angeles and Calgary. Ultra short hops include Ottawa to Toronto (39 minutes) and Montreal to Toronto (53 minutes).

Qantas is another of the leading Airbus A321XLR customers, with a total of 48 on order or delivered. The Flying Kangaroo is currently taking delivery of its XLRs, with seven now in inventory. These are ideally suited to some Pacific routes where the range is vast, but the routes are thin. The fact that Qantas ordered 20 more XLRs in August 2025 suggests it has many Asia-Pacific routes in mind.

However, few have been announced. The XLR registered VH-OGA was the first delivered to Qantas in April 2025. Confusingly, its flight history over the last month shows it has only been used on domestic routes. The shortest route is the one-hour flight between Sydney and Melbourne, while the longest flight is the domestic transcontinental flight from Sydney to Perth, which is less than five hours.

Qantas is introducing the type progressively and assigning it to domestic trunk routes before expanding internationally. Upcoming international routes include Perth to India (expected), Adelaide to Singapore, and Brisbane to Manila. While Qantas has not announced all of its upcoming Pacific routes, interesting destinations include Fiji, Samoa, the Cook Islands, and Tahiti.

American Airlines now has 11 of its 40 XLRs ordered in inventory. As with European carriers, American is assigning these to transatlantic routes. They also have the range to reach many leisure and thinner destinations in the Caribbean, Central America, and South America.

The first XLR American took delivery of is N300NY. Its most recent flight history is dominated by transcontinental routes flying from Boston or New York to Los Angeles or San Francisco, taking around five hours. It is also flying transatlantic routes from New York to Barcelona (up to eight hours) and New York to Edinburgh (up to six hours).

As with other airlines, the American XLR fleet is working up and is yet to take on many of its intended routes. It has just received the first three of its 50-strong order, leaving 47 remaining on the order book. These are now starting to replace its aging fleet of 757-200s.

With the first delivered in May, United's XLR remains a fleet in its infancy. Flight records show they have yet to fly longer transatlantic routes to Western Europe, although they are coming. Mostly, the flights have been transcontinental, around four to five hours. One short hop in the record is San Diego to Denver, a flight time of less than two hours.

While delivered, that will make it one of the largest airlines in the world in the 2030s. The airline's fleet is overwhelmingly A320 family narrowbodies, although it has 60 A350 widebodies on order.

IndiGo has 175 A321neos in service plus four new XLRs. It has the largest order for the XLR, totalling 69 examples. This is made up of the 40 original firm orders publicly confirmed by IndiGo and 29 additional aircraft reflected in industry fleet databases. Once again, the fleet is in its infancy and is working up.

She’s adamant – when the industry is struggling, a sense of community is everything.

“I don’t write alone. I either write with a community or build stuff with actors. Of all my films, ‘Madeline’s Madeline’ is probably closest to my heart because I never felt like the idea was totally mine. It emerged from many people and many minds, not just one,” she says.

At this year’s Tauron American Film Festival, Decker will receive the Indie Star Award. Previous recipients include Jesse Eisenberg, John Waters and Christine Vachon.

Recently, Decker made “Chasing Summer” with star comedian Iliza Shlesinger.

“I think Iliza was interested in working with me because she wanted the film to feel artistic. I was thinking: ‘What’s an art comedy?’ ‘Punch-Drunk Love’ was the only one I could think of, so I watched it a bunch of times. It was fun to push myself in that way. I don’t always do that,” she recalls.

Urszula Śniegowska, director of the festival, says, “Her stories and protagonists, mostly female artists, illustrate Josephine’s own struggles as a woman in a changing art world. This retrospective has been a long time coming, and the new film provided the perfect opportunity to celebrate her complete body of work.”

Decker grew up watching ambitious films. Maybe even too ambitious for her age.

“My father is a poet and a film buff. I just remember watching esoteric Mongolian cinema with him when I was 11. One of the first films he showed me was ‘Andrei Rublev,’” she laughs.

“The other thing that really informed my filmmaking is that my mum is a mother of three, and my dad was traveling a lot. Every time we went to the movies, we were late. I missed the first 10 minutes of pretty much every movie in my childhood. My concept of cinema was that you were sculpting a narrative out of something you don’t totally understand.”

As a director, she also likes to provide the audience with a “real, visceral experience.” But the situation with indie filmmaking has changed “dramatically.”

“Since Trump came into office, it has become much harder. The first time he was elected, everyone’s integrity was still intact. But America is pretty much run by corporations and they now know how vindictive he is. Everyone has bowed to him in different ways. It’s just survival in a capitalist society.”

She adds: “The things that are getting funded are very safe and appeal to his audience, or ensure they don’t piss his audience off.”

That doesn’t mean that people aren’t trying to be creative, though.

Curry Barker’s horror, produced for just $750,000, became one of the biggest box-office successes of the year.

“Filmmaking is lonelier than ever, probably just because the world is lonelier than ever,” she notes.

“It’s harder than it has ever been in my 20 years of working in this industry. It’s crushing for young folks trying to get started. So many industry players have not been able to make money because the streamers control everything, also because of all these consolidations and mergers. What shocked me when I took my film to Sundance this year was realizing that there are now only three buyers in the American market: HBO, Netflix and Amazon.”

“We put all this pressure on ourselves to get our movie made because we think it’s going to ‘save our souls’. Art won’t save you. But the process [of making it] could,” she states.

“At a time like this, when it’s hard to get things made, it’s important to think about the parts of the process that we love, adore and crave. Making work is really fun. So, so fun! Instead of sitting alone in a room desperately sending your script to everyone in the world, you’re with your friends, writing in community,” she says.

“I’ve never really given this advice before, but find the process you love. Then, hopefully, you’ll eventually get to apply it to cinema, but maybe apply it to your life first.”

Former Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger is considering standing against Gianni Infantino as a candidate in Fifa's presidential election.

For weeks, senior figures in European football have been hoping the Frenchman would run for the job, and he is now giving it serious thought.

Wenger is 77, but some senior figures in the European game have told BBC Sport that they would be happy for him to win the election, then stand down before serving a full four-year term.

Infantino's leadership was plunged into crisis by a backlash caused by his failed plan to sell commercial stakes in the World Cup to private investors.

But he retains support in South America, Africa and Asia, and recently insisted he has the backing of the "vast, vast majority" of world football.

However, that support could be tested if Wenger stands.

Fifa's head of global development would have the backing of Uefa, which has spearheaded opposition to Infantino, and has credibility throughout the footballing world.

Sources close to Wenger say he is yet to decide whether to stand, and still believe Infantino would be favourite to win a fourth and final term in next year's election.

The deadline for candidates to confirm they are standing against Infantino for the election in March is 18 November.

Former Tottenham defender Ramon Vega was the first person to put his name forward in October.

Japan Football Association honorary president Kozo Tashima has also announced his intention to stand.

This month Fifa said "key documents" relating to Infantino's plan to sell stakes in the World Cup would be made available to members of its council, but they will have to travel to its Zurich base, and access will be under "strict protocols".

Last month Uefa gave football's world governing body until 2 November to release details about the controversial Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) proposal, and to answer questions relating to its decision-making.

That followed a council meeting at which Infantino apologised for the abandoned sell-off plot but failed to explain how the proposal was devised, asking that questions be submitted in writing.

Amid the fallout over FFE, Wenger, who has traditionally been loyal to Infantino, distanced himself from the aborted plot.

"I was not involved in this strategic plan and first became aware of the project through media reports," Wenger said at the time.

"The decision to withdraw the project was absolutely necessary and beyond question, because I firmly believe in an independent Fifa that serves our game with commitment, transparency and integrity."

Wenger has caused controversy at times in his Fifa role, notably for proposals such as biennial World Cups a "daylight" offside law.

But he also has a global profile, including in Africa, where Infantino recently received a major re-election boost when receiving the backing of all 54 member national associations after promising an increase in funding.

Known as a champion of African players when in charge at Arsenal, Wenger's support of an expanded 48-team World Cup in order to drive development in countries beyond the game's traditional powers has been well received in the continent.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration is requiring new safety warnings on all GLP-1 drugs after finding evidence of a potential increased risk of a rare eye condition, the agency said on Monday.

The condition, which can cause sudden vision loss, known as nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), is rare, and a causal relationship has not been established.

The FDA is requiring the makers of all GLP-1 medications to add a warning about the potential increased risk of NAION associated with use of these medications, it said.

NAION is a condition that can result in sudden decreased vision, including permanent vision loss. It is caused by decreased blood flow to the front part of the ​optic nerve — the underlying cause is not completely understood, however.

NAION cases have been reported for all currently marketed GLP-1 products, the FDA said.

CNBC has reached out to Ozempic and Wegovy maker Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly, the company behind Mounjaro, Zepbound and Foundayo, for comment.

The FDA decision follows a review of clinical trials, real-world patient data, and published studies.

An analysis of 126 trials involving more than 134,000 patients found 6.4 cases of NAION per 100,000 patient-years among GLP-1 users, compared with 3.8 among patients receiving other treatments or placebos.

Several observational studies also identified an increased risk, supporting the FDA's concerns.

The FDA's class-wide labeling action is "likely to attract attention but should have limited impact on GLP-1 uptake," according to Citi analysts.

It may temporarily increase patient and investor concern, but the rarity of NAION and substantial weight, glycemic, cardiovascular, renal, and sleep apnea benefits should preserve a favorable benefit-risk profile and limit changes to prescribing the medicines, they added.

While GLP-1 drugs have been shown to lead to benefits beyond weight loss and controlling blood sugar levels, many also carry the FDA's strongest safety warning, known as a boxed warning, over a potential risk of thyroid tumors.

Studies have shown that semaglutide, the active ingredient in Wegovy and Ozempic, and tirzepatide, the active ingredient in Mounjaro, cause thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents. It remains unknown whether the drugs cause these tumors, including a rare form of thyroid cancer, in humans.

Posted November 9, 2026 | Reviewed by Davia Sills

I was an actor in New York. Yes, that’s right—I didn’t emerge from the womb asking people how they felt for a living. I had dreams of being on Broadway, and after years of work, I finally got invited to the final callbacks for the lead role in a Broadway musical. For this final audition, I was going to wear my grandfather’s vintage pinstriped suit. I planned on changing into it at the audition studio to make sure it looked crisp. But while walking down 43rd Street, half a block from the audition, the suit fell out of my hands and into a pile of Central Park carriage horse manure. Terrified that I’d smell up the room and ruin my chances at the role, I rushed to the bathroom and tried to scrub out the stains before putting the suit on and walking into the audition.

Suffice it to say, I didn’t get the part. I got some generic feedback about how I didn’t have the right look. But I don’t really remember much of that part—the days and weeks afterward are a blank spot in my memory. And while I can recall the moment I dropped that suit on 43rd Street like it was yesterday, I can’t remember how I felt about not landing my big Broadway break. I can barely remember finding out I didn’t get it. The whole experience is a fog, as if the details and the feelings attached to them have been swallowed up together. When I reach back and try to remember how I felt about losing out on this role, the only thing I feel is... nothing.

Emotional numbing is a sense of being disconnected from your feelings. You might have a sense that something matters to you but not be able to feel much about it. Sometimes we disconnect like this in response to stress that threatens to overwhelm us. If we feel less, we can keep moving forward.

I see this often in work with clients—when moving towards a memory that holds a lot of charge, they’ll often just go blank, fuzz out, or suddenly be unable to recall details. I think of it like the villain from that ’80s movie, The NeverEnding Story: the Nothing. If you’re not an ’80s kid like me, the Nothing threatens to consume the magical land of Fantasia. It grows as people lose their hopes and forget their dreams. That’s the image that comes up for me when I try to describe my experience of it. I start to feel the big feeling coming on. Sometimes I even experience it for a moment. But as it threatens to overwhelm me, the Nothing swoops in so that I can move on.

While I invented my own ’80s fanboy name for what I’m experiencing, researchers have been exploring this topic for some time. There’s a related experience called alexithymia, which is difficulty identifying and describing our own emotions. Imagine being asked how you feel about a certain topic, reaching into wherever emotions live in us, and just hitting a wall. From the inside, it can be really hard to distinguish between “I don’t know how I feel” and “I don’t feel anything.”

Emotional numbness isn’t unique to men, but we do seem to have a corner on the market. And that makes sense given the messages we receive about what is and isn’t acceptable to feel. Psychologist Ronald Levant uses the term normative male alexithymia to describe a pattern found in many men who experience difficulty identifying and expressing emotion—particularly those emotions tied to vulnerability or intimacy. He believes this pattern is rooted in the way men are socialized in our society.

Those of us raised as men learn these messages early and often. We inherit them and gift them to each other: “Don’t cry. Don’t be so sensitive. Don’t let them see you care.” I honestly can’t remember where I first received this message. I’m tempted to chalk that one up to the Nothing, too.

But my Nothing isn’t a villain from a kids movie. I think of him as a part of me that learned to perform a job. And he performs it very well. I suspect the Nothing is the reason I’ve been able to succeed at grad school, to switch careers, to build a private practice. But the Nothing doesn’t discriminate between the pain of the story and the importance of the story itself: A friendship ends, and we convince ourselves it wasn’t that important anyway. A dream falls to manure (literally), and we find something else to want.

In my experience, the Nothing doesn’t just take away the disappointment. It also makes it harder to remember how much we cared. I wish that I could have given myself the time to really feel the disappointment and the sadness of seeing my dream slip through my fingers. My experience feels incomplete in my mind, like a book without the final chapter.

Sometimes in the middle of a session, a client goes blank. It’s not like they fully check out of the room, but they’ll be dipping their toe into an emotion and then just lose connection with it. When that happens, I’ll often invite them to notice what happens as they get closer to it. And sometimes they can’t. Sometimes the details are fuzzy, or there’s simply nothing there, and they get frustrated. That’s okay.

Noticing we can’t connect is a good place to start. Sometimes that blankness is doing a job, too. Rather than forcing our way through it, we can get curious about what feeling it might be protecting. And maybe, just maybe, that curiosity gives way to a name: sorrow, regret, grief. The point isn’t to choose the emotion we think we’re supposed to have. It’s to explore if we can connect with even the faintest sense of “Yes, that’s it.”

I think about younger me walking down 43rd Street with my grandfather’s suit, so excited and scared about what might happen next. I wish I could go back and tell him: “I see how much this matters to you. Whether you get the role or not, I want you to remember how much this matters. Don’t let that be taken from you.” We don’t have to convince ourselves a dream didn’t matter just because it didn’t come true. Sometimes, nearly 20 years later, we can give ourselves the chance to write about it and remember that it mattered.

2021 Mustang Mach-E electric SUVs have kept most of their battery and range after thousands of miles on the road.

Real-world battery life for phones, laptops and other electronic devices can diminish over years of use, but does the same happen to electric cars after thousands of miles and hundreds of recharges? According to data published by Ford this week: Yes, but not as badly as you might fear.

The automaker published a report analyzing the battery state of health of more than 36,000 Mustang Mach-E electric SUVs sold to customers across the US and Canada during the initial 2021 model year. After more than five years on the road — and an estimated 2 billion cumulative miles driven — the fleet averaged 92% battery health.

I call that impressive, but not totally surprising. That battery durability data is largely in line with what I’ve seen reported across the industry by other automakers and independent analysts.

One of the biggest concerns of early EV adopters a decade ago (also shared by fence-sitters today) is long-term reliability. The battery is by far the most expensive component of an electric car, and owners want to know their shiny new EV will last as long as the combustion car it replaces.

Battery longevity is particularly important as more used EVs enter the market. Many experts (myself included) have touted pre-owned and off-lease EV deals as a way to bypass the electric-car price premium, but shoppers may be hesitant to roll the dice on higher-mileage vehicles. Many fear a used EV won’t meet their range needs or, worse, could blindside them with an expensive battery replacement.

Ford’s study is another data point that could help allay those worries.

Ford examined more than 36,000 individual vehicles delivered to customers across all 50 US states and Canada during the EV’s first year of sales. A little more than five years later, the automaker says that around 95% of vehicles surveyed retained between 87% and 97% of their battery health (an average of 92%) across the entire 2021 fleet after an estimated 2 billion cumulative miles driven.

Better battery health means an EV keeps delivering more of its advertised range as the odometer climbs. The 2021 Mustang Mach-E rolled off the assembly line with an EPA-estimated range between 211 and 305 miles, depending on the spec, which means that owners lost around 16 to 24 miles of range over time, according to my back-of-the-napkin math.

That said, there’s no beating the chemistry. Around 2,000 vehicles in the group crossed the 100,000-mile mark, retaining around 91% health. A smaller subset of about 30 drivers reached 200,000 miles in their Mach-Es, with an average battery health of 88%. More miles, it seems, means more wear.

At the extreme anecdotal end of the spectrum, one owner put more than 400,000 miles on his original battery — though Ford didn’t state the specific health of that 2022 Mach-E.

An independent study published by Aviloo earlier this year adds credence to Ford’s reported numbers. An Austrian EV battery diagnostics firm analyzed over 500,000 EVs from 20 global brands to measure battery degradation — at 50,000, 100,000 and 150,000 kilometers — finding that many popular models maintained over 90% battery health at high mileage.

Tesla’s Model Y, which competes directly with the Mach-E, was found to hold around 90.3% battery health at 150,000 kilometers (93,205 miles). Aviloo reported that the Volkswagen ID4 and Hyundai Ioniq 5 showed 90.6% and 92.8% retention, respectively, over the same surveyed mileage. The first-generation Nissan Leaf — a much older EV with a smaller battery — showed more degradation at 86.3%, an unfortunate double whammy given its shorter initial range.

According to Ford’s engineers and battery chemists, the Mach-E’s battery was designed to last at least 10 years, adding that the 2021 models in the study use an older nickel-cobalt-manganese, or NCM, chemistry.

Ford’s new Universal Electric Vehicle platform, which will underpin the upcoming Fathom small electric pickup, will use a newer lithium iron phosphate chemistry. That will come with unique charging needs and different battery management calibration than its Class of 2021. The UEV architecture is also built around robust bidirectional charging, so we’ll have to check back in five or so years to see how using your car to power your home affects long-term battery health.

Researchers Recover DNA from 7,000-Year-Old Leather, Revealing a Link to Goats Alive Today

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Ancient DNA recovered from a remarkably preserved piece of 7,000-year-old leather is shedding new light on how prehistoric communities in Spain used domesticated animals.

A 7,000-year-old piece of goat leather discovered in a Spanish burial cave has yielded ancient DNA, allowing scientists to trace a genetic connection between prehistoric livestock and goats living in Spain today. The exceptionally preserved material also provides direct evidence that Early Neolithic communities were processing the hides of domesticated animals.

The findings, published November 4, 2026, in the journal PLOS One, come from a study led by Jolijn A. M. Erven of University College Dublin and colleagues. Their analysis offers a rare glimpse into animal husbandry and leatherworking on the Iberian Peninsula thousands of years before written records.

The leather fragment was recovered from Cueva de los Murciélagos, a burial site in southern Spain known for preserving ancient organic materials. Radiocarbon dating placed the specimen at approximately 7,000 years old. Marks on its surface indicate that the hide had been tanned and perforated, suggesting it was once part of a garment or protective covering before being buried alongside human remains.

Recovering DNA from such ancient leather is particularly difficult in warmer regions, where heat and environmental conditions accelerate the breakdown of genetic material. Despite extensive fragmentation, researchers extracted both mitochondrial DNA, which is inherited through the maternal line, and nuclear DNA, which contains genetic information from both parents.

Francisco Martínez-Sevilla emphasized the unusual preservation conditions: “The preservation of this 7,000-year-old goat leather is exceptional. Cueva de los Murciélagos has preserved organic materials that rarely survive in the archaeological record, offering us a unique opportunity to investigate prehistoric technologies, animal husbandry, and the relationships between human communities and domestic animals.”

Genetic analysis identified the leather as coming from a domestic goat rather than a wild Iberian ibex. Its DNA showed a close relationship to the modern Bermeya goat breed of northern Spain, indicating that a related genetic lineage has persisted on the Iberian Peninsula for thousands of years.

The researchers also detected no evidence of African genetic ancestry in the ancient specimen, unlike the ancestry found in modern European goats. One possible explanation is that genetic exchange involving African goat populations occurred after the Neolithic period. However, the incomplete DNA and limited sample size prevent firm conclusions about when these connections developed or what they might indicate about prehistoric interactions between Iberia and Africa.

Kevin Daly highlighted the genetic link between ancient and living animals: “It is rare to for such ancient, organic materials to retain DNA – but this was one of the few exceptions. With a tiny amount of surviving DNA, we were able to show a link with Iberian breeds today – a genetic connection spanning 7,000 years. This study demonstrates what can done through the collaboration of international multidisciplinary teams – transforming an archaeological object into a link with the living present.”

The series takes place in Montana and follows John Dutton (Costner) as he and his family, the overly ambitious Jamie (Wes Bentley) and the smart, loose cannon Beth (Kelly Reilly) try to defend their ranch from those trying to poach it away from them — from the Indian reservation that wants to transform the Yellowstone-Dutton Ranch back to its pristine, original form, to a global conglomerate that wants to transform the land into a modern paradise for tourists. While trying to fight off those who want their land, the Duttons have to contend with their own family issues.

We didn't know it at the time, but what we turned into on June 20, 2018, was a show that would bend the rules of the traditional Western. Yellowstone wasn't your typical, family-friendly Western show. This series was gritty, violent, and filled to the brim with drama and treachery at every turn. Yellowstone is, by far, one of the best neo-Westerns of all-time, one that you can binge-watch over-and-over without ever getting bored with it. While the show has etched its name in pop culture history, there are Western shows out there that, for all intents and purposes, are just as good, or better, than Yellowstone. Set in the 1860s, Bonanza follows the Cartwright family, a wealthy clan who lives in Virginia City, Nevada during the Gold Rush era. Bonanza has all the Western tropes that one would love, and its opening theme is iconic in TV history. Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather has provided movies with an abundance of parody opportunities. Released in 1972, the crime epic established a remarkably distinctive cinematic language, from Marlon Brando's gravelly delivery and intimidating presence to the elaborate rituals of loyalty, family gatherings, and underworld negotiations. Yet, The Godafther's influence extends well beyond gangster movies, with countless filmmakers borrowing its imagery, dialogue, performances, and instantly recognizable musical themes.

Part of what makes The Godfather such an effective target for comedy is the contrast between its characters' deadly seriousness and the situations surrounding them. Vito's quiet authority, Michael's increasingly elaborate schemes, and the formalities of organized crime are all wonderfully easy to exaggerate. Even relatively minor details, such as kissing a mob boss's hand or making somebody an offer they cannot refuse, can become hilarious when transplanted into an entirely different setting.

The Godfather’s most recognizable sequences provide an endless supply of opportunities for visual comedy. The wedding celebrations, infamous horse-head incident, and climactic baptism montage have all become familiar enough to audiences that a relatively straightforward recreation can deliver an immediate hilarious punchline.

Animaniacs has a knack for sending up popular movies within a short scene. Its recurring "Goodfeathers" segments, which follow a trio of pigeons modeled on characters from gangster cinema, introduce an especially memorable take on Vito Corleone with the Godpigeon.

An obese, 96-year-old pigeon, the Godpigeon is the boss of the Goodfeathers and makes his authority known by expecting the trio to kiss his foot whenever he appears. His raspy, largely unintelligible mumbling is a direct nod to Brando's performance in The Godfather, and he usually needs Bobby to translate what he's saying.

Despite being treated like an intimidating criminal patriarch, the Godpigeon is generally jovial and would much rather enjoy life as a pigeon than get caught up in the trio's problems. The Godpigeon also appears in several Goodfeathers segments in Animaniacs, so the joke isn't limited to one fleeting appearance.

South Park finds an appropriately petty reason to recreate one of The Godfather Part II's most chilling sequences. In "Toilet Paper," the boys toilet-paper their teacher's house, but Cartman becomes increasingly concerned that Kyle's guilt will eventually urge him to confess. His solution is to take Kyle out on a boat at Stark's Pond and kill him.

The setup deliberately echoes Michael Corleone's attempt to have his brother Fredo murdered during a fishing trip in The Godfather Part II. The accompanying music also references the original film's "Godfather Waltz," giving Cartman's ludicrous plan the sort of ominous atmosphere usually reserved for a major betrayal within a criminal empire.

Of course, Cartman isn't Michael Corleone. He's a vindictive child trying to cover up a school prank, and his murderous plan is undermined by the fact that he only has a Wiffle bat.

Angelica Pickles may be a toddler, but she already understands the value of having people owe her favors. In Rugrats in Paris: The Movie, she adopts the persona of the "Bobfather," complete with a tuxedo and her beloved doll Cynthia positioned in her lap. It's an unmistakable take on Vito Corleone's introduction in The Godfather.

Angelica's fellow babies come to her with requests, and she treats their problems like business propositions. The scene climaxes with Phil and Lil producing the broken head of a wooden rocking horse they found in their crib. They ask the Bobfather what happened, only for Angelica to explain that it's what they get for wiping their boogers on Cynthia.

It's a wonderfully silly adaptation of the infamous horse-head scene, transforming one of cinema's most disturbing threats into a punishment for a pair of toddlers with poor manners.

Jim Abrahams’ Mafia! is an entire comedy built around sending up gangster movies, with The Godfather and The Godfather Part II among its principal targets. It depicts the Cortino family's rise from Sicily to the criminal underworld of America, with the story moving between generations in a deliberately familiar gangster-movie structure.

Mafia!'s most direct Brando parody comes through Lloyd Bridges as the older Vincenzo Cortino. Bridges gets to send up the famous tomato-and-death scene, while the movie repeatedly turns familiar mafia conventions into opportunities for ridiculous sight gags.

Even the family's journey to America gets a joke, when Ellis Island officials attempt to rename the immigrant Vincenzo after his Armani windbreaker. It may not be as consistently sharp as Abrahams' Airplane!, but its affectionate mockery of gangster cinema gives it plenty of entertaining material.

One of the funniest uses of The Godfather in modern animation comes from Disney's Zootopia, which introduces Mr. Big, an Arctic shrew who happens to be one of the city's most intimidating crime bosses. The character recreates Brando's Corleone with a tiny body, a raspy whisper, a formal tuxedo, and an entourage of enormous polar bear enforcers.

This contrast is particularly funny, juxtaposing the gangster grandeur with the diminutive creature. Like The Godfather, Judy Hopps and Nick Wilde are brought before Mr. Big during his daughter Fru Fru's wedding reception.

The entire sequence deliberately recreates the atmosphere and staging of the original film's opening, with Judy and Nick discovering that their investigation has taken them into considerably more dangerous territory. The filmmakers even recreated elements of the original film's set and dialogue.

History Says Energy Led All Sectors in the Last Bear Market. Here's What's Different Now.

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Energy stocks have delivered exceptional gains in 2026, but a surprising shift in the oil market could change the outlook for investors.

Energy stocks were the clear winners during the 2022 bear market. The S&P 500 energy stocks delivered a total return of 65.7%, while the broader S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.31%) lost 18.1%, including dividends.

Energy stocks are leading again in 2026, but without a broader bear market. S&P 500 energy stocks have gained 42% so far this year, compared with a 12% gain for the broader S&P 500 index, excluding dividends.

In 2022, rising oil prices boosted energy companies' earnings, while inflation and aggressive interest rate hikes weighed on the broader market. The 2026 rally has been supported by oil supply disruptions in the Middle East, with unusually high refining margins. But a recovery in global oil supplies could put pressure on energy profits and stock valuations.

ExxonMobil (XOM +0.34%) reported earnings of $55.7 billion in 2022, compared with $23 billion in 2021. Chevron's (CVX -0.15%) earnings also more than doubled to $35.5 billion in 2022. Both companies returned billions to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.

However, April 2025 offered a different picture. President Donald Trump's tariff announcements triggered a sharp market sell-off. The S&P 500 fell 21.3% from its February 2025 peak during intraday trading, but did not officially enter a bear market based on closing prices. But S&P 500 energy stocks fell 13.7% in April 2025, making energy the worst-performing sector.

Rising concerns about slowing economic growth and weaker oil demand negatively impacted energy stocks in April 2025. This also demonstrated that energy stocks do not outperform during every market downturn.

The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) estimated that 4.8 million barrels per day of Middle East crude oil production remained shut down in September 2026, compared with a peak of 10.9 million barrels per day in May. But damaged pipelines and risks to oil tankers continued to affect shipments.

However, EIA expects global liquid-fuels production to fall short of consumption by around 1.3 million barrels per day in 2026. The production is then projected to exceed consumption by approximately 5 million barrels per day in 2027. This represents a potential swing of nearly 6.3 million barrels per day in the global liquid-fuel supply-demand balance.

This forecast assumes that supply routes will reopen and production will also recover. If that happens, rising inventories could reduce oil prices even without a recession in the U.S. The EIA forecasts annual average Brent to decline from $96 per barrel in 2026 to $84 per barrel in 2027. However, renewed geopolitical tensions or slower infrastructure repairs could delay the recovery.

Oil markets are already responding to expectations of easing supply disruptions. Brent crude prices dropped below $100 this week. Hopes of peace agreements involving Iran and Ukraine contributed to the decline, even though the underlying supply disruptions had not been fully resolved.

In the third quarter of 2026, ExxonMobil's Energy Products segment, which includes refining and fuels, reported earnings close to $7.2 billion. Chevron's downstream business earnings also more than quadrupled to $4.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

However, these strong downstream earnings may not persist. Disruptions to refineries in the Middle East and Russia have constrained gasoline and diesel supplies. This has pushed their selling prices higher relative to crude oil. However, as damaged refineries resume production and fuel supplies recover, the difference between refined product and crude oil prices could narrow.

Lower crude prices could also hurt earnings from oil production. ExxonMobil estimates that every $1 per barrel change in Brent crude prices would affect its annual upstream after-tax earnings by approximately $700 million, based on expected 2026 operations. Applying this sensitivity to the EIA's projected $12-per-barrel decline in annual average Brent crude oil prices suggests a potential $8.4 billion annual headwind for Exxon's upstream earnings. This estimate assumes that Exxon's production volumes, costs, and other operating conditions remain largely unchanged.

According to FactSet Research Systems' Oct. 30 report, S&P 500 energy stocks traded at 13.2 times expected earnings over the next 12 months, significantly lower than S&P 500's forward 12-month price-to-earnings ratio of 18.7x. However, the valuation of S&P 500 energy stocks was above its own five-year average forward 12-month PE of 12.6.

According to analyst estimates compiled by FactSet, S&P 500 energy sector earnings are expected to decline 7.8% in 2027, compared with projected earnings growth of 16.3% for the broader S&P 500. This suggests that energy's lower valuation relative to the broader market may reflect expectations of weaker future earnings, rather than necessarily making the sector a bargain.

I would avoid aggressively buying energy stocks after their substantial gains in 2026. A recovery in oil supplies could weaken earnings, even if the broader stock market remains strong.

TL;DR: Coinbase’s November 5 product update describes an integrated set of services for token issuers, from sales and distribution through listings, custody and liquidity. It cites Monad, Ethena and Hyperliquid as examples, but the examples are company-selected and do not eliminate the risks or regulatory limits facing token projects.

For a blockchain project, issuing a token may be the relatively easy part. Finding real users, managing custody, listing on exchanges and supporting institutional trading each introduce complicated operational decisions. In a November 5 announcement, Coinbase presented those tasks as parts of one integrated platform for token issuers.

The pitch is straightforward: use fewer separate service providers and reduce the number of operational handoffs. For developers and investors, the interesting question is how much of that convenience comes from genuine technical integration and how much reflects the commercial advantage of a large incumbent bundling its own services.

Coinbase pointed to a public MON token sale involving an affiliate of Monad Foundation, describing more than 85,000 purchasers in over 70 countries. It said the token now has more than 150,000 unique traders and $7.2 billion in spot volume on its platform. Those are company-reported measures from a chosen example, not a representative result for every issuer that uses similar services.

With Ethena, Coinbase described a relationship that expanded from token-management tools into institutional custody, wallets and trading. It also cited a USDC-related product and hundreds of millions of dollars in new deposits. For Hyperliquid, the company emphasised its role in deploying USDC treasury infrastructure and supporting large transfers.

The three examples involve different business models, market structures and needs. They demonstrate possible uses for the platform, but readers should not assume that success in one project translates directly into another token’s demand or risk profile.

Bundling services can remove duplicate work for a project team. One integration may simplify onboarding, compliance coordination and support. It can also make changing suppliers harder if critical functions become dependent on the same provider.

A token issuer should examine who legally controls assets at every stage, what happens if a service is paused, how withdrawal requests are handled and whether exchanges and custodians have the right safeguards for each market. Convenience is not a substitute for operational resilience.

That issue connects to NewsBTC’s earlier coverage of Coinbase’s restrictions on certain stablecoins for EEA customers. An exchange service can be available in one region but restricted in another without changing the underlying token network. Eligibility and local regulation remain part of any credible launch plan.

Coinbase also said it is broadening work around tokenised equities and access to private markets. Those plans should be distinguished from products already live and from securities that may require additional identity or jurisdictional checks.

The practical questions go beyond transaction volume: what are the total fees, the terms of custody, the geographic eligibility limits, support commitments and data-portability arrangements? What functions can an issuer replace if its needs change? Where do responsibilities sit when an incident affects users across several services?

NewsBTC recently examined the operational requirements of tokenised securities venues, where ownership records and corporate actions remain essential despite the appeal of blockchain settlement.

Coinbase’s new presentation shows how major crypto platforms are positioning themselves as end-to-end service providers rather than standalone exchanges. Whether that gives issuers better outcomes will depend on transparency, competition and the performance of the services after the marketing presentation ends.

The 71-year-old US-born pope is recovering from a lung cancer surgery that took place just three weeks ago.

Latin America is home to around 40% of the world's 1.4 billion Catholics, for whom the pope is their spiritual leader. However, fewer and fewer people on the continent identify as Catholic.

Pope Leo's first leg of the tour takes him to Uruguay, the most secular Latin American country.

It was the first country in the world to legalize recreational marijuana use and has since legalized abortion, same-sex marriage and assisted dying, all against opposition from the Catholic Church.

On Sunday, Leo will cross over to neighboring Argentina, the home of his papal predecessor Francis, who, however, disappointed his native compatriots by never returning home as pope.

There is excitement about his visit to Peru, with the pope planning to celebrate Mass in his old diocese of Chiclayo as well as in the mountain village of Santa Cruz de Succhabamba.

The pope will also celebrate Mass in the former Incan capital city of Cusco before honoring Peru's indigenous population by traveling to Pucallpa in the Peruvian Amazon.

The excitement can be seen in the street vendors selling T-shirts and rosaries with his likeness, while bakeries have been selling Leo bread.

Lost audio recordings of Louisiana Creole give us a rare chance to hear the past voices of early New Orleans

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Recordings from 1935 reveal how Louisiana Creole speakers switched languages, capturing speech patterns that written accounts often missed.

To understand how Louisiana Creole sounded in the 1800s, scholars have had to rely on writing. But some writers changed what they heard.

One researcher wrote in 1933 that he had “purified the dialect” in his transcriptions.

Recordings made two years later, now analyzed for the first time, capture something that those written accounts often missed: people moving between Creole and French as they spoke.

Lorenzo Dow Turner, remembered as the first African American linguist, recorded those voices on 13 aluminum discs, way back in 1935.

Oliver Mayeux, a linguist at the University of Cambridge’s Faculty of Modern and Medieval Languages and Linguistics, tracked down the digitized recordings and worked out who was speaking.

The discs contain the earliest known recordings of spoken Louisiana Creole and Louisiana French. Both languages now have dwindling numbers of elderly native speakers.

“Turner’s lost recordings contain far more linguistic diversity than the texts we relied upon until now,” said Mayeux.

“This is the language of the levees and cane fields. The language that drifted out from French Quarter saloons along with early jazz music at the turn of the last century.”

Louisiana Creole shares almost all its words with Louisiana French, but the two languages use different grammar.

Creole developed on southern Louisiana plantations, where enslaved people, mostly from West Africa, came into contact with settlers who spoke regional varieties of French.

“Africans with little common language were forced onto plantations where French was the language of administration. Everybody is learning approximations of French and then approximations of approximations. Quite quickly, a new language is born,” said Mayeux.

By Turner’s arrival in July 1935, younger people and city residents were already moving toward English. Mayeux, who has family ties in Louisiana, knows that history through his own family.

“By the middle of the 20th century you see the end of intergenerational transmission. My grandfather wanted his children to speak English,” explained Mayeux.

“This was typical of many Louisianans who saw their future as American rather than Creole or Cajun,” he added.

Many children, therefore, grew up without learning the language their parents spoke.

Turner was known for studying Gullah, a creole language with English roots spoken by Black communities in Georgia and South Carolina.

He visited Louisiana to compare the two languages’ sounds, especially how speakers’ voices rose and fell.

“From casual observation I find that the intonation of Creole resembles very much that of Gullah,” Turner wrote to a funding body after the trip.

His recording machine weighed about 100 pounds (45 kilograms). He had to drive it to places with electricity, which limited where he could work.

“Turner relied on a network of Black doctors and intellectuals in New Orleans and out in the country. The physicians found him Creole-speaking interviewees and let him record in their offices,” said Mayeux.

“These were trusted spaces for the participants during the era of Jim Crow segregation and, crucially, were wired for electricity.”

In Lafayette and Opelousas, doctors Julius Blackburn Matthews and August C. Terrence hosted the sessions. One participant, Marie Raymond, said she had never known a doctor until she was 69.

A woman also helped Turner conduct many interviews. She seemed more comfortable speaking French than he did, and Mayeux thinks she was probably Turner’s wife, Geneva Calcier Townes Turner.

Turner never analyzed the Louisiana Creole recordings, and his applications for funding to continue the comparison were rejected. The Guggenheim Foundation turned down one application and awarded a fellowship to his former student Zora Neale Hurston instead.

Mayeux suspects Turner simply set the discs aside. Indiana University’s Archives of Traditional Music later digitized them, but their labels offered little information and sometimes got it wrong.

One clue came from a disc that a Turner biography described as two people singing. It actually contained solo piano music by Jessie Covington Dent, a New Orleans pianist and civil rights activist.

Dent’s family papers at the Amistad Research Center led Mayeux to Turner’s papers. An address book there named the doctors who had hosted him.

Another disc listed “Mme Emma Bronssard,” which Mayeux recognized as a misspelling of Broussard. The woman said she was 69, had been widowed for 20 years, and had raised 12 children of her own plus four orphans.

Census and death records matched those details to Emma Benoit, born Emma Broussard.

Her great-granddaughter, Valencia Pradier-Linton, later shared a magazine interview in which Benoit’s son said his mother “didn’t know how to talk English at all.”

Turner recorded three Creole speakers in New Orleans, where people shifted to English earlier than in the countryside. The city’s variety of Creole likely has no speakers left.

Mayeux identified two speakers: George Doyle, born in 1867, and Odelia Edmond, born in 1862. Doyle had worked as a police officer before the city segregated its police department.

Some of their speech matched the old texts closely. Doyle’s word for policemen, “lapolis,” came from the French “la police,” but the French word for “the” had become a permanent part of the noun.

Edmond used mo for “my” when she said “my mother.” French would use a feminine form of the word instead.

Other details challenged what scholars thought they knew. The speakers used rounded vowels, like those in the French words “peu” and “plus,” which researchers had treated as a later borrowing.

Edmond also moved between old and newer forms of the same word. While explaining that she was three days old when her mother died, she used a long form of “have” known mainly from 19th-century writing.

Just 15 seconds later, when talking about being nine days old, she used the short form. Hearing both from the same speaker suggested that New Orleans Creole was more varied than the written record made it seem.

Outside New Orleans, speakers often began interviews in Louisiana French, the more formal language. Others in the room then asked them to speak Creole.

Emma Benoit started with a polite French greeting. In Mayeux’s translation, a woman interrupted with “no no no! Creole!” and then “no no that’s French; that’s not Creole anymore.”

Benoit replied in Creole, “I forget that,” and said she would talk about her troubles. A few minutes later, she returned to French and received another reminder.

Anastasie Dumas switched languages too, answering in French when Turner addressed her in formal, European-sounding French. When his co-interviewer asked in somewhat stiff Creole how to make gumbo, Dumas returned to Creole.

Most participants could not read or write and did not own a radio. Speaking into a visiting professor’s recording machine would have been an unusual, formal experience.

The people in the room and the occasion helped shape how they spoke. Mayeux concluded that French signaled politeness and respectability, while Creole emerged in more relaxed moments of joking and storytelling.

For most speakers today, moving between the two languages no longer carries the same degree of social meaning.

Each recording lasts about two to five minutes, and static makes some speech hard to follow. The New Orleans speakers also came through one circle of doctors, educators, and musicians, so their voices cannot represent the whole city.

Mayeux describes his reconstruction as partial and interpretive, shaped by his position as a white researcher listening nearly a century later. He hopes others will offer their own readings.

He also cannot yet explain why the city recordings differ from the old texts. The language may have changed, writers may have removed variation, or both may have happened.

The next step is comparison with recordings that Turner’s former student Raleigh Morgan Jr. made in the 1950s. Researchers can study those voices alongside the 1935 discs and the Creole spoken today.

For some young learners, Creole offers a way to reconnect with their family history. Many are grandchildren of native speakers whose parents never learned the language, Mayeux said.

“Young people are learning Creole to connect with the richness of their heritage, helping them discover a distinct identity in an increasingly homogenized world,” said Mayeux.

The study is published in the journal Language Documentation and Description.

Zillennials are ‘loud working ’: They’re spending nearly 5 hours a week on self-promotion—and driving colleagues to quit

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

So you’ve heard of the trend quiet quitting, and its sister, loud quitting—but now workers are doing the complete opposite: “loud working.” Instead of doing the bare minimum to get by or turning their anger on their employers, they’re pouring all their energy into making their work more visible.

Think of the colleague who posts every win on LinkedIn or pings the whole team on Slack the second a task is done.

New research from Tide, a finance platform, found that the average U.K. worker spends 131 hours a year—more than 17 working days or 2.8 hours a week—making their work visible. That includes singing their own praises on social media, posting updates on Slack or Teams, speaking up in meetings, and vocalizing their wins to anyone who will listen.

And the loudest of all are younger workers: In fact, nearly 70% of 25- to 34-year-olds are “loud working.” They’re spending about 5 hours every week self-promoting. And it’s drawing attention from management, clients and even angry coworkers.

Perhaps unsurprisingly, it’s not always the most competent worker who gets rewarded—it’s the one who makes the most noise about it.

According to the research, 77% of “loud workers” have had at least one career opportunity in the past year alone, including new customers or clients, pay rises and promotions. For 15% of respondents, getting vocal about their wins has even gotten them awards.

And all the new noise around their success is attracting the attention of other employers; In fact, 17% say they’ve not only been poached, but they’ve actually landed a new job because they’ve been more vocal about their work.

But worryingly, it’s not just “loud workers” who are at risk of being snapped up and leaving the business.

Not everyone’s cheering. More than one in four (27%) workers have considered leaving a job because of a loud working culture, or because colleagues are too vocal about their own achievements.

Zillennials are both most likely to be guilty of “loud working,” as well as the ones walking off.  They’re posting about their success at more than three times the rate of over-55s. Yet nearly half of 25 to 34-year-olds have considered quitting a job because of it—and one in five already have.

Some industries are more forgiving of office braggers than others: Workers in IT, telecoms, and finance are the most likely to have had enough of performative culture. Meanwhile, men are more likely than women to have already quit over it.

“Loudworking has mixed reactions in the workplace and it’s easy to understand that a colleague who turns every small win into an announcement might be off-putting,” said George Schmidt, Tide’s CEO for the U.K. and Europe. “But our research shows there’s a difference between being performative and backing yourself.”

He said that for small business owners, sharing their work, wins and expertise is often the most effective marketing they have. And the same goes for anyone who has left their employer to go solo or who is eyeing up the exit.

Ultimately, though, many workers want to be at a business that rewards people for actually doing the hard work—not just shouting the most about it.

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We all know that French girls have that special je ne sais quoi (so much so that it’s inspired countless essays on this very site). When it comes to their hair, the style is nonchalant—and yet, somehow also skillfully cultivated. It’s hair that’s air-dries, falls just right, and retains that slightly tousled movement that gives no hint of effort or styling. So how exactly do Jeanne Damas, Brigitte Bardot, Jane Birkin, and more do it?

Hairstylist and French girl herself Daphné Narcy has an idea: It all starts how you wash your hair. “You may be damaging your hair without even realizing it while in the shower,” Narcy says. “When you’re hair is wet, it’s at its most fragile. But habits like washing hair too quickly, scrubbing too hard, or using water that’s too hot may be causing breakage and frizz.”

She offers up eight simple shower-time adjustments to turn your hair into a French girl dream.

The first—and by far the most common—mistake: People wash their hair instead of their scalp. “We rub the lengths against each other, which damages them, and we neglect the roots, where sebum, pollution, and residue accumulate,” says Narcy. Her philosophy can be summed up in one sentence: A healthy scalp is the foundation of healthy hair. It’s also what determines how light the roots feel once the hair is dry.

The heat from a scalding shower lifts the hair cuticle, which can be bad for shine. “The hair loses moisture, becomes more porous and duller, and the color fades faster,” Narcy says. Hot water hurts the scalp, too, making it drier and more irritated. “Water that’s too hot also softens the hair follicles, which can lead to hair loss.” Your new rule of thumb here is disarmingly simple: “If the water feels pleasant on your wrist, without feeling hot, it’s the right temperature for your hair.”

Use your fingertips in small circular motions, without ever scrubbing. “It’s meant to be an invigorating massage, not a scrub,” insists Narcy. It’s also a moment of pleasantness. When you enjoy it, washing your hair becomes a ritual rather than a chore.

If you’ve ever read the back of a shampoo bottle, you know the instructions: Lather, rinse, repeat. And Narcy says that isn’t just to get you to use more product; but instead the double-shampoo is key to keeping hair clean—though only use a walnut-sized amount each time.

“When you use too much shampoo, residue can remain on the scalp, weighing it down, potentially irritating it, and making your hair look dirty faster The result: you end up washing your hair more often, and you get caught in a vicious cycle.”

The lengths should only be lightly touched when it comes to shampooing. “Press your hands together and slide them down, using very light and gentle friction, all the way to the ends. While rinsing, the lather that runs down finishes cleansing the lengths.” Less friction means fewer tangles, less breakage, and mid-lengths that not only stay hydrated but also dry smooth.

So of course we were like, “we’ll we’re 6 hikers…” And voila! We checked in, showered, and an hour later we were on our way to have one of the best meals of my life. Three workers at Burgeon ate with us in their on-site stock room. They were cool and fun and we had a great time. This was one of the coolest and most exciting trail magics we had. Loon asked if they needed a Colorist to work for them since I work in fashion as a colorist! Thanks for trying to get me a job, Loon!

After the dinner, Singe’s partner offered to pick us up and take us to Super Secret Ice Cream. An ice cream shop in Bethlehem, NH that Singe had been talking up since probably day 2 of me knowing her. As an ice cream connoisseur and an AT Dairy-Blazer, I couldn’t wait. We left the boy Binguses behind and Loon, Singe, Outlaw, and I got ice cream. I am absolutely obsessed with this place. The branding, the colors, the unique small-batch flavors, the ice cream flights, the posters–all of it is perfect. New favorite ice cream. I think it’s extremely tragic that I live so far away from it and also probably for the best. If you’re even remotely close though, take the detour! Best ice cream I’ve had on trail and also in years hands down.

All of this together made for such a magical day and one of the standouts from trail. Tackling a hard and anticipated mountain but feeling strong? That view?! Reuniting with our friends unexpectedly! Surprise trail magic pasta dinner?! AND SUPER SECRET ICE CREAM?! What a way to kick off the Whites.

The next day we slackpacked the Kinsmens with The Notch. They have a deal that if you stay two nights, the slackpack is included. Yes please! The Kinsmens were some of the most difficult parts of the Whites. It wasn’t my worst day there, but quite a challenge. The final ascent up is basically bouldering/rock scrambling. I was so glad to not have my full pack weight, but I did feel extremely rushed trying to catch the shuttle back to the Notch. The options were a 4pm or 7pm. We got a tiny bit of a later start that ate into our hiking time. Thus, I ended my hike around 4:10pm while feeling extremely rushed and stressed the last part of the day. The trail was super rocky and frustrating. We missed the shuttle but since Singe did know the staff well, they came and picked us up. Thank you! When we got back to the hostel, someone had decided to make dinner for everyone as trail magic. What a godsend. Two covered dinners in a row. I was so incredibly exhausted after the Kinsmens that I probably would have cried if I had to figure out my own dinner. Moosilauke felt like a warm up but the Kinsmens felt like an ass-kicking. I was worried more of that was to come but Singe told me she thinks the Kinsmens are some of the hardest 4kers in the Whites and it shouldn’t feel worse than that which really helped. Tomorrow, we’d be back to full packs and really getting into it!

There is a challenge in New Hampshire hiking called the New Hampshire 48. This refers to a group of 48 prominent peaks all above 4k. The AT goes right over a few of them, and grazes along the side of a few others. Some people choose to tag the summits and take the detours to hit as many summits as they can. In hiking this pursuit of summits is called “Peak Bagging.” While we went through the Whites, Loon was all in on peak bagging as many of the 48 as she could. She was a rockstar! Singe and I supported her and encouraged her but did not have the same drive. I was too worried about my ankles on all the rocky ups and downs that I didn’t want to risk anything by doing extras. Because of this, Singe and I sometimes had slightly different days than Loon. Like for example, when Loon walked an extra like 7 miles to get 4 extra peaks in a day and Singe and I walked 5 extra miles the next day to be able to go get ice cream. It’s important to know where your priorities are.

Still, on the AT, I got my fair share of the 48 done. The trail follows a section of the Whites called the Pemigewasset Loop (or Pemi Loop) for about 10 miles after the Kinsmens. Most notably, it follows along Franconia Ridge which is an exposed ridge walk that goes over multiple peaks. You can see the circle of the Pemi Loop through the mountains while up on the ridge. I absolutely loved Franconia Ridge. It’s like nothing else on the trail. It most closely reminded my of a hike I had done in the Dolomites! And yet it’s here! Just sitting up in New Hampshire! Wow! Franconia is known for being beautiful but also extreme due to the exposure. Like everything in the Whites, it’s quite steep (like straight up rock ladders for miles) to get up to the top and then open to strong winds and weather with nowhere to hide. We were lucky to have gorgeous weather throughout the Whites. While we did have some wind (including being pushed over by the wind a few times), it was nothing unmanageable or unpleasant on Franconia. Still, the exposure is tough. There was even a Search and Rescue on this section the same day we were up there. But we got through it just fine along with my new can of Honey Mustard Pringles that we became obsessed with and didn’t last more than 6 miles. We were in such awe the whole time. When I first got to the top of the ridge, I ran into McMurphy! Another mountain top reunion! Loon started a little further back to get more peaks, so we also reunited with her on a summit!

Towards the end of the ridge, the novelty had worn off a little and we were ready to get out of the wind and set up camp for the night. We went down and then up and over Mount Garfield where I had another summit reunion! I ran into Sniper, a woman I had met in Georgia and North Carolina. She hiked the AT years ago and is attempting a Yo-yo of the full trail this year! This means she hiked from Georgia to Maine and will turn around and hike all the way back down to Georgia. Incredible!! It was so cool to run into her and see her still crushing it! After leaving her and wishing her the best on the rest of the journey, I made my way down to the Garfield Campsite where of course we ran into several other friends!

The Whites are known for their Hut-to-Hut system where you can stay in little lodges along the way. This is awesome for a stand alone trip, but not really an option for thru-hikers. They are quite expensive (especially multiple nights in a row) and require reservations far in advance. That would be nearly impossibly to schedule on a thru-hiker’s flexible and ever-changing timeline. Stealth camping is also not allowed/highly discouraged in the Whites due to the extreme weather and the delicate nature of the alpine plants. There are designated campgrounds throughout the Whites that each have caretakers. They are all first come first serve, but the caretakers are great at finding spots for everyone so we were never too stressed about it. At this first campsite, we registered for a thru-hiker pass with the caretaker that allows us to camp at all the campgrounds for a discounted price and also get free pastries and soup when we stop into the huts! Wahoo! We opted to stay in the shelter the first night because of the wind.

The next day we started off our hike by basically having to walk straight down a sort of rocky waterfall. The Whites are on another level. We made it to the Galehead Hut for lunch where we cashed in our free soup pass. Then we continued up South Twin Mountain. For some reason, when we summited, I could not get “Breathe” by Faith Hill out of my head and I continued to sing it as my anthem the rest of trail. South Twin was a long push up, but the descent felt flat compared to the climb. What a treat! We went to a campsite a little off the path since there weren’t too many options otherwise. You’d either have to do a really short day or a really long one. Given the strenuous terrain, we opted for a short day. Singe and I got to the campsite around 3pm while Loon went off to go bag three more peaks! It felt really nice to have time to relax and recover. I don’t think I got to camp before 4pm since North Carolina. We stayed in the shelter this night too and watched a beautiful sunrise from the comfort of our sleeping bags the next morning.

Our next day in the Whites was a bit of a Choose Your Own Adventure. This was one of the only nights on trail (the only night on trail?) that Singe, Loon, and I were all apart. I had a field day telling people we were all apart because we were fighting. Nobody believed it for a second. We had a bit of an easier day planned passing by Zealand Hut and aiming for Ethan Pond Shelter. But Singe’s friend told us she could meet us at the trailhead for Zealand Hut and take us to lunch in Bethlehem and then return us to trail. Loon decided to pass and stick with the original plan since she was already doing so many extra miles and peaks but all Singe had to tell me was we could get Super Secret Ice Cream again and I was in. Singe left a little before me in the morning but we met up at Zealand Hut. It was super special to be there with her because it was the first place she decided she ever wanted to Thru-Hike back in college. <3 We took a side trail that was super flat about 2.5 miles to a parking lot. I timed myself on how long it took from Zealand to the lot because I had the idea that I might try to return later for Work-For-Stay if the timing worked out. We had a great lunch and ice cream of course and were returned to the trailhead. It felt like we snuck off campus in the middle of the day with our cool older sister. We were cutting it close but I wanted to try for the work for stay at Zealand since it’s a unique thru-hiker experience and I didn’t know when else the timing would work out to try. I ZOOMED through the 2.5 miles back up to Zealand in record time and ta-da! I was granted one of the three work for stay spots that night! Singe ended up camping solo sort of in between Loon at Ethan Pond and myself at the hut.

Two more hikers I knew rolled in and we hung around the pretty waterfall waiting for our duties to begin. We got leftovers from the dinner that night and then were assigned the tasks of sweeping, wiping down tables, and reorganizing the freezer for the hut croo. In exchange, we got the dinner leftovers and were able to sleep in the dining room which was much appreciated since it rained that night. We had to get up super early so the staff could make breakfast for the paying guests. We hung around outside until breakfast leftovers were set out and then we descended on those. From there we made our way to the AMC Highland Center where Loon, Singe, and I met back up for a zero. I had a job interview over the phone the next day and I was touched by Singe and Loon’s efforts to make sure we were somewhere I could take the call with reliable service. We were excited for a rest too since the Whites are so demanding even when dropping the number of miles you’re doing each day. The Highland Center was also super neat because we got to run into our friend, Mosey, who we met during the first half of trail and was an original Bingus! The Binguses also trickled in over the next day and we got to catch up with them as well!

The interview went well (though did not ultimately land me a job…but that’s story for Maine) and I was feeling excited. There was no laundry so we did our best to freshen our clothes up in the bunkhouse sink. We enjoyed taking over the on-site library to do our nails and watch TV on our phones together. It was nice to have a little break before the Presidential Range or “The Prezies.” We actually lucked out by taking a zero for my interview because it let some bad weather pass and gave us a perfect weather window later!

After our time at the Highland Center and mapping out our next stretch, we made our way back up into the range. We made our way to the Mizpah Hut and Nauman Campground. Mizpah was one of my favorite huts. There was something more remote, cozy, and mellow about it more than the others I had seen. The Croo was also very friendly. We checked on a work-for-stay just to see but they didn’t have any so we camped at the Nauman Campground next door. Singe, Loon, and I got an entire tenting platform to ourselves! We didn’t know it then, but this would be the last night the three of us would camp together without anyone else. We had a magical time. Somehow, it wasn’t too cold so we decided to cowboy camp on our platform under the clear sky. We watched a beautiful sunset and had such a fun and special night. We were laughing so much. This night was such a highlight on trail. We slept under the stars and I remember waking up at different times during the night and seeing how the milky way was moving across the sky. What an experience.

The next day we got breakfast leftovers at Mizpah Hut with another female hiker who was traveling with her partner. I think she enjoyed having some girl time with us and we loved it too! We made our way from the hut up to the Mount Pierce summit (President Pierce not Pierce Brosnan apparently) and were officially in the Prezies! The first summit was not so bad but we were hyped! We could see the others stretching before us, most of which we’d tackle that day! We took a moment at the summit to talk about how excited we were. Before we continued on, Singe said, “Does anyone have words they’d like to share about Pierce before we continue?” which made me laugh all day thinking about it.

Mount Eisenhower was next and it was so beautiful! We had a gorgeous day and could see Mount Washington’s summit for the first time! Though it was quite windy, Eisenhower was one of my favorite mountains in the Prezies because the 360 views are so stunning! What a view of Washington! After a snack break and some photos, we kept it moving before getting too cold. We went up and over Little Monroe and Mount Monroe where we got a look at Lakes of the Clouds Hut where we would be staying. We decided to go ahead and book a full guest spot for Lakes of the Clouds because we wanted to experience the hut culture as guests and because it’s tricky to find a place to camp if you show up to this hut and there’s no room for thru hikers. Everything is exposed and there’s no camping around. By this point, you’re at the base of Mount Washington and frankly, out of options. We felt like we had such a magical day hopping from summit to summit and squealing about the views and the weather. Also, mileage wise, Lakes of the Clouds is not so far from Nauman Campground so we made it to the hut in really good time. Such good time, in fact, that we decided to go ahead and summit Mount Washington that afternoon! Originally we were going to wait until the next day but when you get good weather and you have the time…don’t waste that opportunity. We decided to go up the 1.5 trail to the summit with slackpacks and take a trail around the next day.

Washington was….fine. Loon, Singe, and I pressed play on the Hamilton soundtrack at the same time to get us up the mountain. It’s so strange to make your way up this infamous beast of a mountain only to be met with a bunch of ill prepared tourists who drove or took the Cog Railroad up. It was crowded and touristy, but hey, at least we weren’t hit with 100mph winds! We didn’t stay terribly long before making our way back to the hut. We knew 3 of the Binguses were making their way to the hut tonight too and we were excited to see them. Our friend, Mosey, was also able to work on-site at Lakes of the Clouds that day and spend the night too! The Binguses had to come from further so they had more of an exhausting day but and were wiped. Still, it was great to see them and spend some time with them after dinner when everyone went outside to take in one of our best sunsets of trails. I heard Brewski say, “We got to do stars tonight” about remembering to stargaze and it cracked me up enough that that phrase became a part of our lexicon the rest of trail.

While the first half of the Prezies were a dream, the second half were a bit of a nightmare. I did not enjoy day two of the traverse. The landscape was completely exposed all day and so rocky and unstable. It felt endless and frustrating. Maybe everything we’d been doing physically caught up to me and the novelty wore off? Either way, yikes. We made our way around Washington towards Jefferson where I did decide to tag the summit even though it was technically off trail. I got to the top and felt very regretful that I wasted the effort. I did not go up to tag Mount Adams. We made our way to Madison Hut where we ran into half of the Binguses again and commiserated about the day with them. I remember us telling Brewski “Can you please fake a medical emergency so we can all get airlifted out? You’re still on your parents’ insurance so it has to be you.”

We were trying to explore other options or side trails for getting down. We were going to meet Singe’s parents this night and stay with them in an airbnb and zero the next day but the idea of having a bed to sleep in felt too far away while we were traversing around. We wanted very badly to not have to go up and over Mount Madison and down to Pinkham Notch but there was really no other route. Eventually, we just had to go. The ascent up Madison really wasn’t so awful but the trail down? If you can even call it a trail? Was the absolute worst part of the entire AT for me. Everything was so exposed and unstable and endless and I felt like I would be stuck on the mountain forever. The progress was glacial and the steps were slow. I was without a doubt angry hiking. I remember looking for any sliver of hope and saying, “It’s only .9 left to the treeline!” and Loon, the beautiful and literal hiker she is, said, “And then it gets even steeper!” To which I yelled something like, “SHUT UP WHY WOULD YOU SAY THAT TO ME?!” We weren’t really angry at each other and it’s a moment we laughed about a lot throughout the rest of trail. I seriously think the descent down Madison is extremely dangerous and I cannot believe it isn’t talked about more. ANYWAYS. We did get down to the treeline and for me and my knees, it felt at least a little better walking on dirt and having trees to cling to if it was too steep.

We made it down to the Osgood Campsite where a random man appeared out of nowhere and tried to give me unsolicited advice. I was not having it. We kept joking that throughout this hike our faith in humanity has gone up but our tolerance of men has gone down. Once Singe and Loon made it down too, we took the original AT instead of the detour so that we could make it to Pinkham Notch for Singe’s parents to pick us up. What looked flat on the map ended up being flat with some random sharp ups and downs. Normally this would be okay, but when we’re already feeling so worn out, this was maddening. One of Singe’s friends had originally planned to meet us SOBO during the hike but wasn’t able to make it all the way up so she left us a bag of trail magic which we found towards the end of the day. It was a really sweet mental boost. We were about .2 from the Mount Washington Auto Road and 2 miles from Pinkham Notch. It was almost 7pm and we knew we were losing daylight quickly. Loon ran ahead to see if we could hitch down to the auto road entrance with the last of the cars leaving Mount Washington. The road up closes at 6pm but we hoped we could catch a hitch from some stragglers on the way out. We saw exactly two cars. One was a minivan that slowed down to tell us they didn’t have room even though we could clearly see they did. I started to cry. The next was a truck. I continued to pathetically cry while we asked for a ride to hopefully sway the decision. Unfortunately, it was a Washington worker who wasn’t allowed to give a ride in the work vehicle. We completely understood and that was disappointing but fine with us. The real kick in the pants was actually when he said, “The quickest thing for you to do will be to take the AT 2 miles down to Pinkham Notch.” WE KNOW SIR! My knees and feet were beat to crap from the day and I knew I didn’t have two more trail miles in the dark in me. We decided to walk down the auto road to where Singe’s dad could pick us up.

Loon, Singe, and I stayed in an airbnb with Singe’s parents near North Conway. We had a zero where we got our errands done and were also able to do a big fat nothing. Thank you Mr. & Mrs. Singe for your generosity once again!! The Binguses also took a zero at Mosey’s so we were all aligned for the next stretch! On Sunday, we set back out for the stretch between The Wildcats (where we listened to High School Musical) and the Carters (where I listened to Beyonce). We stayed overnight at Imp Shelter. The weather was drizzly but still could have been worse and was better than we expected. This is quite a steep section to do in the wet. Singe and I got to the shelter first so we watched Hacks on her phone before others showed up. I did spill an entire new bag of mini M&Ms but luckily my spirits were restored enough to think this was funny and not too tragic.

The next day, us Golden Girls and half of the Binguses hiked from Imp Shelter over Mount Moriah and down to the 1900 mile marker! We are right on the heels of Maine! We were greeted by The Ice Cream Man who gave us Klondike bars from his camper. We met some cool SOBOs and took pictures at the final 1900s mile marker before getting picked up by a friend I had made hiking out in the Dolomites last year. We met on a practice hike I did in Italy (I used practicing for the AT as an excuse to hike in really cool places and I recommend you do the same). I actually twisted my ankle (shocking) and he helped wrap it up since he is a SAR volunteer in the Whites! We got to talking while staying at some of the same huts and he told me to reach out when I came through on the AT and he and his wife would host me and whomever! It felt surreal to know this other milestone had been met. I was getting to see the New Hampshire connection I had before hiking.

My friend and his wife were so lovely and generous to us. We showered and enjoyed their company. My friend told us stories of his best SARs. We made taco bowls for them in exchange for the homestay and Strider made an apple pie from scratch!! The next day, we stayed talking over breakfast way longer than we planned because they were just so fun. My friend ended up letting me drive his car back to trail and leave a pin for him to pick it up later. Like I said, they were extremely generous to us. It felt a little funny to drive my trail friends in his car. We made it to the trail head and continued on! We had a long day pushing to the first shelter in Maine! We went up and over Mt Success which is the last mountain in New Hampshire. At the top, we learned the news that Dolly Parton had died. RIP. We also found one of our favorite FarOut comments on trail. If you scroll to the very beginning of the Mt Success comments it says “take that Dad!” Nice.

Soon we crossed into Maine! It immediately became about 10x more extreme just like everyone had promised. But also 10x more breathtaking. We saw a double rainbow while crossing over the Goose Eye mountains which I like to think was for Dolly. I thought maybe I’d feel more emotional crossing into Maine. Certainly it’s a big moment, but there’s still a lot of work to be done. Still, I did it. At the very least, my legs and feet have taken me from Georgia to Maine.

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services on Tuesday finalized its annual outpatient hospital payments rule with a lower-than-proposed rate bump and its major, industry-opposed policy shifts moderated, but largely intact.

The 2027 Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment (OPPS) and Ambulatory Surgical Center (ASC) Payment Systems rule outlines a 2.3% reimbursement increase for those meeting applicable quality reporting requirements. That’s below the proposed 2.4% increase and reflects a 3.2% projected hospital market basket increase and a 0.9 percentage point productivity adjustment.

The agency is also moving forward with a substantial and controversial cut in 340B drug reimbursements, though not quite to the extent that it initially proposed.

The final rule adjusts these drug payments down from the current bar of average sales price (ASP) plus 6% to ASP minus 28%, as opposed to the proposed 33.4% below ASP. To make the reimbursement change budget neutral as required by statute, OPPS payments for non-drug services will be boosted by 7.5%.

CMS said it tweaked the proposed 340B reimbursement cut based on 340B ceiling price data from the Health Resources and Services Administration, with the baseline reductions informed by a drug acquisition cost survey it conducted earlier this year.
