How Bobby Pulido Went From Latin Grammy-Winning Tejano Singer to Congressional Elect

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Even before winning his highly contested congressional race this week, Bobby Pulido had made up his mind to give up music and focus on public service. “If people want me to represent them and they believe that I could best represent them, I’m perfectly comfortable never having a concert again,” he told Rolling Stone last month. Then he admitted bashfully, “I’m putting a karaoke machine in my house.”

Pulido said it in jest, but there’s probably some sadness as he leaves his music days behind him. After all, that’s a 30-year career as a singer he’s saying goodbye to. Pulido was born in Texas, the oldest to Tejano musicians Robert Pulido and Diana Montez, each who carved out their own respective paths in the genre. As a kid, Pulido played music with his high school mariachi and ended up in his dad’s band Los Clásicos. One of his first songs ended up being “Contigo,” a collaboration with his dad from 1994.

That song pretty much kick-started an impressive run for Pulido. The next year, he released his debut album Desvelado, spawning off his first hit “No Se Por Que,” which made its way onto Billboard’s Latin charts. The title track “Desvelado” also saw major success and remains a standard in Pulido’s catalogue. For a long time, fans thought the song was originally written for Selena Quintanilla, though Pulido dispelled the rumors in an interview.

Pulido had momentum and kept going. He followed his debut up with Enséñame, an album that blended cumbia and ranchera sounds with country sensibilities, and Llegaste a Mi Vida, another success on Billboard’s Regional Mexican Albums chart. Zona de Peligro came in 2000, followed by Siempre Pensando En Ti in 2001. Though those albums fell in the years Tejano music slowed down, Pulido picked things back up with Bobby, landing a hit with “Vanidosa.” Pulido stayed busy with more albums and a few featured roles on several telenovelas. Though Pulido’s later albums saw less commercial success, the 2020s brought some major breaks: He won his first Latin Grammy in 2022 for Best Tejano Album after the release of his record Para Que Baile Mi Pueblo. Even when he announced he was stepping away from music to pursue politics, the musical success kept coming: a second Latin Grammy came in 2025, this time for Best Tejano Album for his album, Bobby Pulido & Friends: Una Tuya y Una Mía (Vol. 1/En Vivo). He also scored a nomination at the Grammys for Best Mexican Music Album (Including Tejano) for Bobby Pulido & Friends: Una Tuya y Una Mía (Por La Puerta Grande). The second part of that album is up for a Latin Grammy at the ceremony, which will take place on Nov. 12.

Pulido announced he was running and gave an exclusive interview to Rolling Stone at the time. When asked what he’d say to people who doubted he could run an effective political career because of his music, he responded, “I don’t blame them. I have been incredibly careful not to mix politics with my career. I understand if somebody says, ‘What does he know? He’s a singer.’ But that’s why you talk about the issues.” He also evoked the president’s career in pop culture. ” If you voted for the guy that was a reality TV star, and if you voted for the woman that is now our congresswoman that was selling insurance out of a strip mall, did you ask her the same question?”

Throughout his campaign, Pulido kept his connection to music strong. The campaign put out a public call play at local quinceañeras, which are birthday parties for girls turning 15 in Latino communities, and Pulido came out to more than 75 events. He made a show of singing his his hit “Desvelado” and also taking the time to engage with families and voters. Still, he was clear that he was focused on connecting with constituents, not performing for the masses. “I could foresee in the future maybe potentially doing a benefit and maybe using a concert here and there to help raise money for various causes that are really important to me,” he told Rolling Stone in October. “But public service to me is much more powerful. I do music for me because I enjoy it, but when you’re serving, it’s not for you. It’s for other people, and I think my life would be complete that way.”

When Pulido won his race this week, he celebrated by nodding to his music fans. “For more than 30 years, you’ve played my music at your weddings, your quinceañeras and your backyard barbecues,” he said.

Tens of millions cast ballots for midterms despite worries of Trump's interference

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

People wait in line to vote Tuesday at Canfield Community Center in Dearborn Heights, Mich. Salwan Georges/Getty Images hide caption

Tens of millions of voters cast their ballots Tuesday without widespread disruptions despite concerns of interference by the Trump administration.

Around the country, election administrators responded to relatively common Election Day issues in some communities, from delayed poll openings and long lines to poor signage and voting equipment problems.

Polls remain open in much of the western United States until later Tuesday.

Flooding in parts of Florida and in the Four Corners states of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah forced local officials to relocate some polling sites and resort to emergency ballots.

The most high-profile technical problem occurred in Westchester County, N.Y., which includes part of the state's competitive 17th Congressional District. A ballot scanner issue took much of the day to get sorted out, meaning voters had to give their ballots to election workers for scanning at a later time. Some right-wing commentators online claimed the issue was a sign of fraud, but both the Republican and Democrat running in the district told voters not to be deterred.

"Every ballot will be counted," Mike Lawler, the Republican incumbent in the district, wrote in an X post after news of the issue arose.

The Trump administration's Justice Department had announced it planned to have around 1,000 monitors at polling places on Election Day, and shortly after the first polls opened on the East Coast Tuesday, the department said it was sending monitors to observe voting in every state.

"Election integrity has been one of the top priorities for this Administration and this Division," Harmeet Dhillon, the head of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, said in a statement.

Sending out election observers has been a routine way of documenting any racial discrimination at the polls under past administrations since the passing of the Voting Rights Act of 1965. But ongoing, baseless claims of widespread voter fraud from President Trump and other federal officials had heightened worries among many local officials and election experts that this administration's DOJ election monitors would interfere with voting at the polls.

As of Tuesday evening, however, no such activity has been reported.

But the New Mexico secretary of state's office received reports of DOJ monitors asking unusual questions and questioning voters in Bernalillo County, which is home to Albuquerque. "The New Mexico Department of Justice reached out to the DOJ Team Lead on our office's behalf to address this reported behavior, and we received assurances it would be addressed," spokesperson Lindsey Bachman said in a statement.

Concerns about Immigration and Customs Enforcement activity and law enforcement presence near polling sites also did not come to pass, as of 9 p.m. ET.

Instead, voters exercised their rights on what many voting observers considered to be a fairly normal Election Day.

Their ballots on Tuesday joined more than 61 million that were already cast during early voting. Nationally, early in-person and mail-in voters cast 34% more ballots compared to the last midterm general elections in 2022, according to the latest data compiled by the University of Florida's Election Lab. The pre-Election Day surge was driven mainly by in-person voting.

As the counting of ballots continues, voting rights advocates are now looking ahead to any potential controversies over close races and the certification of results. Some election officials have also warned about ballot seizures, especially after the FBI earlier this year seized election 2020 materials from Fulton County, Ga.

"Election Day is the last day that you can cast your ballot. But election season continues after today, and we're going to continue to remain vigilant," said Shaylyn Cochran, deputy executive director of the Lawyers' Committee for Civil Rights Under Law, during a press call on Tuesday.

Melat Kiros becomes the first Gen Z woman elected to Congress

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Melat Kiros looks down the line of assembled members of the Colorado Democratic Party during a unity rally put on by the party on July 2, 2026 in Denver. Timothy Hurst/Denver Post via Getty Images hide caption

Midterms coverage: Live results | What to know | Stories around the countryStay connected: NPR Politics Podcast | NPR Politics newsletter | NPR app

Democratic socialist Melat Kiros has won the race to represent Colorado's 1st Congressional District in the House, according to a race call by The Associated Press, marking the first time a Gen Z woman will serve in Congress.

Kiros, a 29-year-old Ethiopian immigrant and lawyer, cruised to victory Tuesday night in the reliably blue district, which includes most of Denver.

The win comes just months after she handily defeated 15-term incumbent Diana DeGette in the state's Democratic primary, a high-profile party upset where voter frustration with leaders in Washington, D.C., and calls for political change were at the forefront.

Kiros is one of several winning House candidates on Tuesday who were endorsed by the Democratic Socialists of America or its local chapters.

Many touted a shared core platform: championing issues aimed at expanding the social safety net for working Americans through policies like Medicare for All and universal childcare. They also advocated for more divisive changes, such as abolishing Immigration and Customs Enforcement and ending U.S. military aid to Israel.

Kiros' fierce criticism of Israel and the war in Gaza became a source of controversy on the campaign trail, sparking blowback from the independent candidate in her race, Shimon Blau, who accused her of espousing antisemitic rhetoric. Kiros has pushed back on that, saying her criticism rests with the state of Israel rather than the Jewish people.

Though the average age of a lawmaker in Congress has gotten younger in recent years, being a Gen Z member of Congress remains a rarity. Kiros will arrive on Capitol Hill at a moment when Democrats continue to confront calls from many of their voters for generational change.

In an interview with NPR in February, she spoke about the role of age in her race, arguing that many of her policy positions appeal to young voters in particular.

"Young people understand that we are in the fight of our lives," she said. "We need to have leaders that are going to be fighting for the kind of bold policies that actually get something done, and they're refusing to settle or wait for our turn or ask for permission."

Currently, just one sitting member of Congress is under the age of 30, Florida Democrat Maxwell Frost, who became the first Gen Zer elected to the House in 2022.

Georgia elects the nation's first Black female governor

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Keisha Lance Bottoms, Georgia Democratic gubernatorial candidate, arrives to speak at a news conference on Election Day, Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026 in Smyrna, Ga. Brynn Anderson/AP Photo hide caption

Keisha Lance Bottoms will be the first Black female governor elected in the country, after she won the Georgia governor's race, according to a race call by the Associated Press.

Bottoms' win marks the first time Georgia has elected a Democratic governor in more than 20 years, breaking the Republican hold on state government in Georgia. But she will likely be working with a Republican majority in at least one chamber of the legislature.

Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican Rick Jackson, a billionaire healthcare executive endorsed by President Trump. Trump won Georgia in 2024, but lost the state in 2020. Since then, Georgia has evolved from a state that consistently elects Republicans to federal and state office to a swing state with Democratic senators. But the governor's office and the legislature have remained solidly Republican.

Throughout her campaign, Bottoms made fully expanding Medicaid in the state, which would require legislative approval, a top campaign promise.

Before running for governor, Bottoms served as mayor of Atlanta for a single tumultuous term from 2018-2022, during which the city grappled with the COVID-19 pandemic and the social unrest seen in other major cities at that time. She declined to run for a second term and instead served as an adviser in the Biden administration. Her opponent used her record as mayor against her in attack ads, claiming she abandoned the city after allowing crime to fester — because Atlanta, like many other major cities, saw an increase in crime during the pandemic.

Bottoms harnessed frustration against Trump to propel her campaign. She promised to stand up to the Trump administration while in office, and to find relief for high prices. One of her proposals includes allowing local municipalities to eliminate the sales tax on groceries. She has also said she wants to eliminate the state income tax for public school teachers.

Data centers also emerged as a top campaign issue, as the state leads the nation in new data center construction. Bottoms said she backs a moratorium on new data centers.

"We need experts in this field to sit around the table and tell us and give us guidance as to how the state of Georgia should move forward," Bottoms said in October, during her only gubernatorial debate with Jackson.

Bottoms touted Trump's endorsement of Jackson throughout the campaign. Jackson aligned himself with Trump during the primaries, but later distanced himself from the president by removing references to him on his website and leaving Trump's name out of speeches on the campaign trail. Instead, he allied himself with Georgia's current governor, Brian Kemp, who endorsed Jackson after the primary.

Bottoms gained her own endorsements from several high-profile Democrats, including former presidents Joe Biden and Barack Obama. She also regularly campaigned with Sen. Jon Ossoff, one of Georgia's two Democratic U.S. senators.

Sarah Kallis is the politics reporter at Georgia Public Broadcasting.

Spanish transport minister resigns days after deadly train crash

Spain's Transport Minister Ãscar Puente has resigned - six days after the high-speed train collision in which at least 69 people were killed and dozens injured.

He announced his decision in Congress, after giving evidence on last Wednesday's disaster near the town of Urda, south of the capital Madrid, in which a train smashed into the back of a stationary train.

"A transport minister in a country like Spain cannot remain in office after two accidents of this seriousness in less than a year," he said - a reference to January's crash on the same line which left 46 people dead.

Last week's crash was one of the worst rail disasters in Europe's modern history, and Spain's worst crash since 2013.

Rescuers had to use heavy lifting equipment to get access to derailed carriages where a number of passengers were trapped

Puente announced his decision at the end of a lengthy congressional session on Tuesday evening in which he had outlined new rail safety measures and had been grilled by the opposition.

He also said: "I am not only stepping down from my position as minister of transport, but... I am also withdrawing my name from the lists for the early legislative elections."

Puente's position immediately came under scrutiny after the 28 October crash.

The main opposition, the conservative People's Party (PP) and the far-right Vox, were both calling for his resignation - as were some of the government's allies.

They argued that the minister should take responsibility for a high-speed rail system that many Spaniards now regard as unsafe and whose credibility even Socialist Prime Minister Pedro SÃ¡nchez admitted was "broken".

The opposition also criticised Puente for appearing to blame human error for the crash, claiming the minister was trying to deflect the blame from his own failings.

But, having said he was going to remain in his post just a few days ago, his resignation appears to have taken many by surprise.

The rail accident and Puente's decision have come at a crucial moment in the Spanish political calendar, with early general elections on 29 November.

Many observers see the accident as a massive electoral setback to the Socialist-led government that will make it impossible for SÃ¡nchez to defy the polls and win the election.

Puente's predicament presented him and the prime minister with a dilemma - refusing to resign might look insensitive in the wake of the accident and stepping down could look like acknowledging he and the government were at fault.

In the end, Puente opted for the second option, insisting his decision had been driven by "political dignity".

The prime minister praised his colleague who had "faced up to the situation, provided answers and solutions, and then stepped aside".

"Out of dignity. Of his own accord. Out of respect for the victims," SÃ¡nchez added.

Many Spaniards will now ask whether Puente's successors will manage to restore faith in the country's railway system.

Last Wednesday's accident involved a train run by state-owned operator Renfe with 397 passengers on board and a stationary train run by private operator Iryo, which was carrying 147 passengers.

A reported issue with the automatic signalling system - a computer failure - had forced all trains to halt on the affected stretch.

Leaked audio recordings published in Spanish media soon after the crash revealed conversations between train drivers and a control centre just minutes before the disaster.

The three recordings of conversations between Madrid's control centre and two train drivers appear to show that the Renfe train was given clearance to move even though the other train had stopped on the track, about 25km (15 miles) ahead.

In one, a control centre operator is speaking to the driver of the Renfe train, which was having some technical difficulties.

After some exchanges, the control operator tells the driver: "Continue your journey and then proceed under [Lateral Signalling Block], at normal speed with normal operation."

The driver replies: "At normal speed?" To which the controller answers: "Yes."

The 10 Best American Rock Bands of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Name the greatest rock bands of all-time and chances are that many of them don't come from the good ol' USA. The Beatles, The Rolling Stones, Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, Black Sabbath, AC/DC, and so many others were born across oceans, but no matter how great they all are, rock and roll started in America. While the likes of Chuck Berry and Elvis Presley took the music of the new genre to the mainstream, they were solo acts. What they started led to decades of bands who showed why rock and roll was a collaborative effort that went beyond the efforts of one person. These 10 bands are the best of rock in the United States of America.

Van Halen came out of California in the late 1970s, rocketing to immediate fame with their self-titled album in 1978 thanks to hits like "You Really Got Me" and "Runnin' With the Devil." With each album, they only got better. The efforts of drummer Alex Van Halen and bassist Michael Anthony can't be understated, but it was the one-two punch of David Lee Roth and Eddie Van Halen who made the group icons. Eddie was a wizard of the guitar who brought the impossible to life, while Roth brought an intense energy to his vocals and on-stage antics. When Roth left, that should have been it. Instead, Van Halen arguably got even better when Sammy Hagar stepped behind the mic for a more grown-up band. And let's give some love to third singer Gary Cherone too, who was so much better than the hate he gets.

Aerosmith was formed in Boston in 1970. They came out during a transformative decade for rock, as the Brit pop sound gave way to something harder. Still together today, a whopping 56 years later, the band has, outside of a few hiatuses, kept the exact same lineup, with Joe Perry and Brad Whitford on the guitars, Tom Hamilton on the bass, Joe Kramer on the drums, and Steven Tyler's original and impossible to duplicate vocals leading the way. With their very first single, "Dream On," arguably being their best song, Aerosmith had to live up to some steep expectations with every new outing. They seldom slipped, even reaching a new level of fame in the 90s during their third decade. They're amazing.

California's Creedence Clearwater Revival had a much shorter run at the top of rock than the previous two examples, with the band lasting only from 1968 to 1972. They made the most out of that short time together with seven album releases. CCR didn't sound like California. They were raw and gritty, the music of both the thinking man and the working man. Tom Fogerty, Stu Cook, and Doug Clifford don't get the respect they deserve because of John Fogerty's unforgettable soulful vocals. He was the lead guitarist too! CCR's songs are ones you feel throughout your body, whether it be the anxiety of "Bad Moon Rising" or the politics of "Fortunate Son". CCR helped get a confused nation through the era of Richard Nixon and the Vietnam War.

Florida's Tom Petty feels timeless. If you listen to rock radio, he's always there, and you almost forget that he's sadly no longer with us. He is Americana, one of the greatest music icons of all time. He did so much in his solo career, and he was even better when he was backed up by his group, The Heartbreakers. Names like Mike Campbell, Benmont Trench, Stan Lynch, and Ron Blair brought out the best in Petty and allowed his unique voice to flourish. The Heartbreakers introduced a radio-friendly pop sound to rock without selling out. For years, it was one massive hit after another. With songs such as "American Girl" and "Mary Jane's Last Dance", they are the mainstream voice of America, one heard by millions and carried across generations.

California's The Eagles are the ultimate songwriter's band. With a sound that's rock meets country, the group spoke to a 1970s America which had lost faith. The Eagles heard us and gave validation to our fears, giving hope to listeners ready to break free. There were a plethora of rotating members over the years, but it keeps coming back to the trio of Don Henley, Glenn Frey, and Joe Walsh, who handled both the vocals and many of the instruments. The band has so many visceral songs to choose from as your favorites. Few bands, however, have achieved what they did with 1976's Hotel California. The album, and self-titled hit single, are among the best-selling and most played in the history of rock.

Jim Morrison was only 27 when he passed away in 1971. He lives forever though, a man frozen in time, as does his band, The Doors. The Los Angeles quartet released six albums in just four years, creating a sound that came to define the late 60s era of change. Morrison was a poet in the form of a baritone singer. Even if you didn't understand all of his lyrics, you felt them. There are few rock stars as cool as him, yet he didn't overshadow the rest of the band's talents. Robby Krieger and John Densmore are masters of their craft, and Ray Manzarek made the organ mesmerizing on songs such as "Light My Fire."

In the early 1990s, so many rock fans were screaming that the genre was dead. Then came 1991 and the outbreak of grunge, led by Seattle's Nirvana, and another rock god who died at 27, Kurt Cobain. "Smells Like Teen Spirit," the first single off of Nevermind, was a sonic revolution for the bored masses sick of hair metal. It didn't stop there. After the perfect blending of pop and rock made them famous, Nirvana got back to punk rock basics on In Utero. Krist Novoselic's bass thumps and Dave Grohl is among the greatest drummers who ever lived. To really understand how great Nirvana was though, listen to MTV Unplugged. On that perfect night, Nirvana slowed it down and showed what the future would have been like.

There has never been a heavy metal band more commercially popular than LA's Metallica. Created in 1981, their intense sound helped create the thrash era of rock, led by a blistering speed. Still as popular as ever 45 years later, the group has remained mostly unchanged. There have been three bassists during their reign, but guitarist Kirk Hammett, drummer Lars Ulrich, and singer/guitarist James Hetfield haven't gone anywhere. After ruling 80s metal with several albums, their popularity reached a new level with their 1991 self-titled album. They survived the grunge takeover, accusations that they sold out when they cut their hair, and the Napster controversy. There is no keeping Metallica down. Their intense energy can't be restrained.

Even if you're not a fan, you can't deny that New Jersey's Bruce Springsteen is the quintessential American rock star. He has been the voice of the frustrated working man and woman since the 1970s, an artist who isn't afraid to call out the woes of a country that he simultaneously loves as well. His signature growl is there in hits such as "Born to Run" and "Streets of Philadelphia," but this masterful storytelling is nothing without his E Street Band. There with him since the beginning, their symphony of sound is built on the talents of well-known names like Steven Van Zandt and Max Weinberg and the smooth saxophone skills of the late Clarence Clemons.

When The Beach Boys began their music careers in California in the early 1960s, they were impressive, sure, but with simple songs about surfing and cars, they didn't seem destined for all-time greatness. Then came the greatest American rock album of all-time, 1966's Pet Sounds. With songs like "Wouldn't It Be Nice" and "God Only Knows", The Beach Boys were just as good as The Beatles. Even they have admitted that they were heavily inspired by Pet Sounds. With the jaw-dropping harmonies, the tortured genius of Brian Wilson, and a complex sound of an unusual complication of instruments, there has never been a band who has played with the ears of their listeners like The Beach Boys.

Bomb threats target seven flights across Europe

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Watch: Sniffer dog inspects Ryanair plane in Spain after suspected bomb threat

Seven passenger planes across Europe, two of which departed from the UK, have been inspected following a series of suspected bomb threats.

Two Ryanair flights, one originating from Dublin and the other from Lisbon, were searched after landing in Rome following anonymous tips but no explosives were found, sources at the city's Fiumicino airport told the BBC.

The Dublin flight was escorted by the Italian air force until landing.

Authorities have also searched planes in Poland, Spain and Bulgaria.

It is unclear if the incidents are connected. While officially there is no link between them, authorities from various countries will be co-operating to assess whether this was a co-ordinated attack, designed to disrupt airspace.

The UK government described today's incidents as "malicious hoax threats".

In a statement it said it had "one of the most robust aviation security systems in the world, with well-tested processes for dealing with any potential threats, as well as malicious hoax threats, like those we've seen today".

Aviation security expert Paul Mason said bomb threats on passenger planes "don't happen that frequently", which made the latest incidents "unusual".

"It sounds like they've been made in a credible manner across a broad range of airlines," Mason told BBC Radio 5 Live. "That would suggest to me that it's been a very deliberate act by a typical actor rather than a load of coincidental, malicious calls."

He said "there are a lot of processes" that the onboard crew and people on the ground must go through "to validate the credibility of the threat".

A flight may be asked to divert if the credibility of a threat "is of a certain level", Mason added, and in some cases the onboard crew may not even be advised on what the ground operators were dealing with.

Two of the four flights were en route to Warsaw Chopin airport

In Rome, sources at Fiumicino airport told the BBC police bomb disposal experts carried out standard security protocols to ensure the safety of all passengers and crew.

Inspections of the passengers, luggage, and the aircraft yielded negative results, and the alert has since been cleared.

One plane which landed in Murcia, southern Spain - a Ryanair flight coming from Manchester - was also found not to have any explosives on board, Spanish authorities said.

One passenger later said she was unaware of the bomb threat until after the plane landed.

"We weren't told anything during the flight," Sarah told BBC Radio 5 Live. "The plane stopped quite suddenly and then we were kept on the runway for around 30 minutes."

"Then the fire engines turned up, the bomb squad turned up and we were asked to evacuate," she continues.

Sarah said the plane had not been on the ground a minute before it was "surrounded by fire engines and police and a bomb squad". All the passengers were then escorted by police, leaving all their belongings.

"We were only later told that it was an actual threat, a bomb threat to the plane itself," she added.

Two flights heading to Warsaw were also affected, including one from London Heathrow.

The Heathrow flight was originally meant to land at the Polish capital's main airport, Chopin, but was diverted instead to the smaller Modlin airport, briefly forcing its closure.

A Scandinavian Airlines flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw-Chopin landed as planned.

Later on Tuesday, Bulgarian Transport Minister Georgi Peev said there were bomb threats against two planes travelling to the country's capital Sofia.

He told the state-owned Bulgarian News Agency (BTA) the flights were from the northern Italian city of Bergamo and Ireland's capital Dublin.

The flights into Sofia were not diverted and no explosives were found when the planes were checked, Peev said.

Bulgaria's border police said in a statement sent to the BBC that at around 10:35 local time (08:35 GMT) it had received an email about "the presence of an improvised explosive device on a flight returning from Bergamo".

"Standard procedures for such incidents were immediately activated, and an operational headquarters comprising representatives of various institutions was convened," the statement said.

The border police added that the same email also reported a "similar threat against aircraft arriving from Dublin".

These incidents come a day after a Wizz Air flight from London Luton made an emergency landing in Budapest, escorted by a Hungarian fighter jet, after a bomb threat.

Wizz Air said later that security checks had been made and nothing was found.

BBC Verify has consulted aviation safety experts about what happens on board a plane when a bomb threat is issued.

Marco Chan, a former pilot for Cathay Pacific Airways and Hong Kong Airlines who is now a senior lecturer in aviation operations at Kingston University, said a bomb threat is considered "genuine until it is resolved, even though most turn out to be hoaxes".

Chan said crews will "quietly search" toilets, galleys and storage areas and will not touch a suspicious item unless it has to be taken to an area on the aircraft designated as the least risky bomb location.

The aircraft's captain will have multiple factors to assess, according to former airline pilot Terry Tozer, an aviation safety commentator.

He said these include what facilities are available at the airport they are being diverted to and how the plane can continue onwards should it turn out to be a false threat.

Chan added that once on the ground, the plane is generally "taken to an isolated stand, passengers are disembarked, and police and bomb disposal teams search it".
