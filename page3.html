Willie Mays and Pat Summitt to be honored with U.S. Postal Service stamps next year

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

WASHINGTON (AP) — Baseball Hall of Famer Willie Mays and longtime Tennessee women's basketball coach Pat Summitt will be honored with stamps next year, the U.S. Postal Service announced Monday.

Mays, who died in 2024 at age 93, was a 24-time All-Star and two-time National League Most Valuable Player.

His stamp, designed to look like a baseball card, will feature a portrait of Mays with the New York Giants and an inset image of the outfielder making his famous, over-the-shoulder catch of a drive by Cleveland's Vic Wertz in the 1954 World Series opener. Mays batted .301 with 660 homers and 1,909 RBIs from 1951-73.

The orange background refers to a color of Mays' teams, the New York and San Francisco Giants and the New York Mets. It will be the 50th stamp of the USPS's Black Heritage series and was designed by Antonio Alcalá, an art director of the USPS.

Summitt, who died in 2016 at age 64, led Tennessee to eight NCAA championships and compiled a career record of 1,098-208 from 1974-2012. Her stamp features an oil-on-primed-linen portrait created by Roberto Parada. It was designed by Ethel Kessler, an art director for USPS.

Summitt is in the Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame and the Women's Basketball Hall of Fame.

7 Signs Your Therapist Might Not Be a Good Fit for You

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Posted November 9, 2026 | Reviewed by Davia Sills

Finding the right therapist can feel a lot like dating. You browse through profiles, ask around, and then schedule an initial meeting, hoping there’s chemistry. Sometimes you click immediately, but other times, you have to wait for your therapist to grow on you.

Just like any other relationship, the therapeutic relationship is about finding the right match. Not every therapist is going to be a good fit for you. Just because someone has a degree, a cute office, and a comfy couch doesn’t necessarily mean they have the capacity to support your needs.

Therapy is an investment of your time, money, and emotional energy. You deserve to feel that the person sitting across from you is genuinely invested in understanding you and helping you grow.

Of course, good therapy isn’t always comfortable. In fact, the most meaningful sessions are often the ones that leave you a little confused and a little raw. Questioning your assumptions, confronting your beliefs, and recognizing patterns are non-negotiable aspects of successful therapy.

But there’s a difference between being challenged in therapy and feeling that your therapist isn’t meeting your needs.

Here are seven signs your therapist might not be the right fit.

If your therapist thinks you’re always right, all your exes are narcissists, and everyone in your family is toxic, you may have hired a very expensive cheerleader.

A good therapist will validate your feelings, but they’ll also, in the nicest possible way, confront you and challenge your assumptions.

Ask yourself if your therapist invites you to consider another perspective and helps you confront how your own fears, expectations, and behaviors contribute to your experiences rather than just validating you.

You’ve spent three sessions talking about how your sister wasn’t invited to your wedding because she had an affair with your fiancé. And the next week, your therapist asks, “So, how are things with your sister? Have you two been spending time together?”

Therapists don’t need photographic memories. We’re human, and we talk to a lot of people. But it’s our responsibility to find ways to remember important patterns, relationships, and events in someone’s life so we can help them build awareness and make connections.

When a therapist repeatedly forgets significant details, you may find yourself feeling betrayed and spending session time reiterating the same stories. Also, paying attention to your story is a part of helping you feel seen. It’s challenging to develop trust when you feel dismissed, which is inevitable when someone who is paid to pay attention to you doesn't.

You chat, you laugh, you catch up, but the conversations don’t lead anywhere.

You discuss your weekend, complain about your boss, recap your latest dating disaster, and suddenly your 50 minutes are up. See you next Tuesday!

Therapy isn’t always about measurable progress, and chitchat is part of relationship building, but there should be some sense of direction, insight, and exploration that your sessions build on.

Seemingly ordinary conversations can allow us to make significant discoveries. A conversation about a disagreement with a roommate might open the door to exploring your discomfort with conflict or your tendency to prioritize everyone else’s needs.

You don’t need a life-changing revelation every week, but over time, therapy should offer more than an opportunity to vent.

Your mother is histrionic. Your dad is a workaholic. Your boyfriend is avoidant. Apparently, you’re the only psychologically healthy person in your world.

A good therapist helps you understand relational dynamics, identify your own role in them, and empowers you to make changes, rather than handing out diagnoses to people they’ve never met.

It’s tempting to explain difficult relationships by attaching psychological labels to the people involved. However, your therapist’s job is to recognize problematic behaviors, not assign a clinical diagnosis based on someone else’s account.

The goal of therapy should be to guide you to understand your relationships, empower you, and help you develop healthier ways of navigating them.

You’re reaching for tissues as you tell your therapist about a painful breakup, and suddenly you’re hearing a 15-minute story about their divorce.

Appropriate self-disclosure can be useful, but if you’re learning more about your therapist than yourself, something isn’t right.

Therapists are people, too. We have families, hobbies, favorite books, and experiences that sometimes overlap with those of our clients. Sharing a personal anecdote can help normalize an experience, strengthen connection, or reduce isolation. However, if you regularly find yourself listening to your therapist’s problems, comforting them, or wondering how to redirect the conversation back to yourself, the boundaries may have become blurred.

You tell them something they said didn’t sit right with you, and suddenly they’re more defensive than your ex.

A good therapist can acknowledge mistakes, welcome feedback, and work through ruptures in the therapeutic relationship.

Your therapist, who is also human, may make inaccurate assumptions, forget a session, or approach a sensitive topic a little too abruptly. Human error is inevitable in any meaningful relationship, including therapy. Misunderstandings in therapy may serve as an opportunity for you to voice your needs and boundaries. If your therapist is able to respond with accountability, that might serve as a corrective experience and may even strengthen the therapeutic alliance.

In contrast, a therapist who becomes dismissive, defensive, or makes you feel guilty for expressing your concerns may unintentionally reinforce the very relational patterns you’re trying to change.

A good therapist isn’t there to make your decisions for you. They should help you understand what’s driving your choices, explore your options, and develop the confidence to make decisions that are right for you.

Of course, therapists can offer guidance, practical suggestions, and recommendations when appropriate. Therapy is about helping someone evaluate their choices, not telling them how to live.

For example, you might already know that your boyfriend is letting you down. What you may not understand is why leaving him feels impossible or why being alone feels more frightening than being with someone who is not all that nice to you. Good therapy helps you develop the insight and emotional capacity to make your own decisions, even when those decisions are difficult.

Your therapist doesn’t need to be your best friend, your personal cheerleader, or your life coach. They should be someone who helps you see what you might not want to see while creating an environment where you feel safe enough to explore it.

And remember, a therapist can be perfectly competent and still not be the right fit for you.

Different therapists have different personalities, approaches, and areas of expertise. Some are more structured and directive, while others are more exploratory. Some employ humor and use themselves as a part of the process, while others are a little more distant and leave more room for reflection. What works beautifully for one person may feel unhelpful to another.

Many therapists offer complimentary consultation sessions for you to ask them questions and assess fit. Don’t feel like you have to commit to the first therapist you meet.

Therapy asks you to be vulnerable, honest, and willing to examine parts of yourself you may have spent years avoiding. Finding someone you can trust with that process is an important piece.

Mamba Mentality continues to draw attention.

A game-worn No. 8 Kobe Bryant jersey from the 2003-2004 season sold for $1.83 million at The Realest on Sunday, Nov. 8. This is the highest sale of a non-rookie jersey from the Los Angeles Lakers star before he switched to No. 24. It also lands among the top-five highest public auction sales of Bryant's NBA jerseys.

The jersey is autographed and was certified by The Realest's authentication program, which utilizes photo-matching. Its only previous owner purchased the jersey with a certificate of authenticity directly from the Lakers via a charity auction in 2017 before the NBA icon passed away.

The jersey is believed to have been worn in 17 games that season – with a record of 16-1, including 8-0 in the playoffs – when the five-time champion starred alongside Shaquille O'Neal. Bryant wore it for his 31-point, 10-assist double-double in Game 5 of the first round to eliminate the Houston Rockets, for his 42-point performance in Game 4 of the conference semifinals against the San Antonio Spurs and in Game 6 of the next series against the top-seeded Minnesota Timberwolves to win the Western Conference finals.

The jersey has an NBA Finals patch and is the only No. 8 jersey of Bryant's to come to auction with such a detail. It could not be proven if Bryant wore it the NBA Finals, where the Lakers lost to the Detroit Pistons in five games.

The highest-selling Bryant jersey was the one from his NBA debut in 1996. It sold in April of 2025 for $7 million.

Bryant played 20 seasons in the NBA all with the Lakers. He was drafted No. 13 overall in the 1996 NBA Draft by the Charlotte Hornets out of Lower Merion High School in Philadelphia and was traded to Los Angeles that day. He won five NBA championships with the Lakers, was named Finals MVP for two of the titles and was named an All-Star 18 times.

Bryant died in a helicopter crash in January of 2020 at the age of 41 with his 13-year-old daughter, Gianna.

Last month, The Realest auctioned off a Shohei Ohtani jersey from the 2025 Tokyo Series for $2,055,700. It was the most expensive jersey worn by a baseball player in the 21st century.

7 Alan Ritchson Movies & TV Shows Reacher Fans Will Love

Faith Roswell is a Senior Writer on Screen Rant's Classic TV team. Since earning her degree in Creative Writing over a decade ago, Faith has written articles on film and TV from a variety of different angles. Faith now combines her knowledge of psychology with her love of monster movies to give more insight into what makes the best ones.  You may have read her Screen Rant lists and features covering horror, sci-fi, and fantasy, or read her Amazon Top 10 book, "Movie Monsters of the Deep." Faith has had an extensive career as a writer, appearing on BBC live radio, researching true crime for Rotten Mango podcast, and writing for publications including Mental Floss, Atlas Obscura, and The Daily Jaws before beginning here at Screen Rant.

When Reacher first aired, it appeared to many that Alan Ritchson exploded onto the screen and has barely left since, but while Reacher might be Ritchson's best TV show, his onscreen career began 20 years ago. His early roles made use of his distinctive height and physique, with the younger actor playing Aquaman in the superhero TV series Smallville, Gloss in The Hunger Games franchise, and having his first major role as the linebacker Kevin "Thad" Castle in the TV sitcom Blue Mountain State. However, Reacher then became a gamechanger for the actor.

Reacher instantly drew attention for featuring a more faithful portrayal of Lee Child's iconic character than in Tom Cruise's movies, but the show made some significant changes from the books, allowing Ritchson more versatility. Despite being somewhat typecast, Prime Video's best action series gave him the role that he was born to play, making him into one of the decade's biggest action stars and highlighting his other movies and TV shows both before and since that are must-watches for Reacher fans.

Syfy's gritty, ridiculous sci-fi action TV series is Fallout meets Mad Max, with a side of Death Race. In the dystopian future of an alternate 1999, gasoline and water are ruinously expensive and a mysterious corporation oversees virtually every aspect of daily life. Ritchson plays Los Angeles police officer Arthur "Barbie" Bailey, who is forced to enter the titular "Blood Drive," a race in which the cars run on human blood. The show, which is as gory as its description suggests, was canceled after one season, but it has a surprising 86% positive audience score on Rotten Tomatoes.

A few scenes in season 4 saw Reacher becoming a horror show, but it is firmly grounded in the action genre. That said, the series has become known for its improbable and often hilarious fight scenes, which see Reacher using virtually any object in reach to deal devastating impact. Fans who love this aspect of the series and enjoy the Mad Max franchise should not miss Blood Drive for more of the same, but with even wilder twists.

After playing Aquaman in Smallville and voicing Raphael in the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles movie, Ritchson plays his third superhero role in HBO's 49-episode action series, Titans. Ritchson appeared as Hank Hall, aka Hawk, in the first season of the show, with his role being expanded for the second and third seasons. With the show being based on the DC Comics Teen Titans characters, it may not come as a surprise that its target audience skews younger than Reacher's, but the series is well worth watching.

One of Alan Ritchson's most unusual roles has been his work as the body model for Beowulf in the 2007 movie of the same name.

Titans might be aimed at slightly younger viewers but it is by no means a kids' show, and essential viewing for fans of the DC Universe. It is violent, intense, and with Reacher estimated to return in 2027, this leaves more than enough time to start the series. Ritchson gets to play a vigilante with plenty of opportunity to beat up bad guys, but as part of a team rather than carrying the entire series. Titans also stars Titus Welliver (Bosch,) Anna Diop (Us,) and Esai Morales (How to Get Away with Murder.)

The 2026 action comedy movie Runner sees Ritchson starring along Owen Wilson in a race against time, as the two must make an important delivery while being pursued by dangerous enemies. Reacher has cemented Alan Ritchson firmly within a certain type of role, and he plays it very well. In Runner's case, he is a former Special Forces operative, who prefers to keep his own company and has strained relationships with people from his past. Every senior with an Apple Watch should set up these 5 health alerts

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

From emergency help to medication reminders, your Apple Watch can support you as you age.

Apple Watches are owned by people of all ages, so wearing one doesn't mark you as vulnerable, unlike a medical pendant. This is important to some older people who do not see themselves as "seniors", as explained by the National Institute of Aging. Older individuals know age does not stop them from living independent lives, and an Apple Watch can help them stay healthy when they do.

The idea that technology can help older people live independently for longer is hugely attractive to independent-minded boomers. Stand and movement reminders, fitness tracking and the step counter help seniors stay more active, keeping them healthier for longer. As Apple invests in healthcare research, the smartwatch gets better at detecting short term illness, boosting self-care.

With permission, the Find My app helps younger people take better care of their elderly relatives by tracking their location. There are also a number of health alerts every senior with an Apple Watch should set up.

Apple Watch has great tools for emergency help, which can be especially beneficial for seniors. These include Emergency SOS, Medical ID and Emergency Contacts.

Emergency SOS contacts emergency services when things go wrong. To use this feature, press and hold the side button, then drag the Emergency Call slider or keep holding the button until the call is made. This needs a cellular watch with an active plan, Wi-Fi calling or the paired iPhone nearby.

To turn on Emergency SOS, open the Watch app on your iPhone, tap Emergency SOS and turn on Hold Side Button to Dial.

Access to Medical ID and Emergency Contacts can be found in the Health app on your iPhone. Tap your profile picture and select Medical ID. Tap Edit, enter your details and add trusted Emergency Contacts. Medics who find your Apple Watch will review your Medical ID, so keep your allergies or conditions up to date to help medics deliver the treatment you need.

Over one in four people aged 65 or older fall unexpectedly each year, says the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). In addition, 319,000 older people are hospitalized for hip fractures after a fall every year. Pew Research claims that, in 2023, 26% of US adults aged 65 or older lived alone, which compounds the potential risk of falling. Fall Detection may protect them.

Fall Detection is enabled automatically if you tell the Health app you are 55 or older. Once activated, it will call emergency services if the built-in sensors on your Apple Watch detect you are immobile after taking a fall.

If you fall, an alert message appears on your Apple Watch display, asking if you fell. If you request help, do not answer or remain immobile, the system will call emergency services. To turn on Fall Detection, open Emergency SOS on your iPhone and toggle feature On. On Apple Watch, open Settings > SOS, toggle Fall Detection to On, Select Always On and confirm.

Crash Detection does the same after a car crash and is available on recent Apple Watch models. To turn on Crash Detection, go to Emergency SOS and toggle Call After Serious Crash to On.

CDC data shows that a person dies of cardiovascular disease every 34 seconds in the US. While an Apple Watch cannot identify heart attacks, it can help monitor overall heart health including continuous heart rate monitoring and help to identify atrial fibrillation.

An Apple Watch uses green and infrared LED lights on its back to continuously measure your heart rate. This feature works automatically. Another feature lets you take an accurate ECG (electrocardiogram) reading to help detect irregular heart rates or the signs of atrial fibrillation. To use the ECG, open the ECG app, hold your wrist steady and touch the Digital Crown with your finger — you'll get a reading in thirty seconds.

The watch will also warn you if your heart goes above or below set thresholds, such as above 120bpm or below 40bpm. Apple Watch shares this information with the Health app on your iPhone, where you will find a VO2 Max reading that assesses your overall cardio fitness based on the information gathered by the sensors on your watch.

To enable Heart-related features on your Apple Watch, open Settings > Heart and toggle the available heart features you want to use On.

Forget to take your pills? Medication Reminders can help you stay up-to-date. To set this up, open the Health app on your iPhone, tap Browse > Medications and then Add Medication. Search for your medicine, tap Add, follow the prompts for setting up a schedule and tap Done.

You can add multiple medications, after which your Apple Watch will tap your wrist and send you a notification when it's time to take your dose. You can create a log of medicinal compliance to share with a doctor. Bonus: The sleep tracker on Apple Watch can flag potential sleep apnea for medical investigation. To set it up, open Sleep on Apple Watch (or in the Health app on your iPhone). Turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch. In the Health app, tap Browse > Respiratory > Sleep Apnea Notifications.

Apple believes everyone should be able to use its technology and includes accessibility features in its products for no extra charge. That commitment extends to the Apple Watch, which is packed with tools to help people live independent lives.

Hearing loss becomes more common with age, with Achieve claiming one in four US adults in their sixties suffer from it. Apple's Sound Recognition tool helps by using the Apple Watch and iPhone to listen for specific sounds, such as doorbells, sirens or fire alarms.

Enable Sound Recognition in Accessibility Settings on your iPhone. Tap Sound & Name Recognition > Sound Recognition, toggle to On and choose the sounds you want it to recognize.

Apple has also figured out how to make Apple Watch more usable by those who are visually impaired. Press the side button to open Control Center, tap the AA icon and turn the Digital Crown to adjust font size to make it easier to read. I was an actor in New York. Yes, that’s right—I didn’t emerge from the womb asking people how they felt for a living. I had dreams of being on Broadway, and after years of work, I finally got invited to the final callbacks for the lead role in a Broadway musical. For this final audition, I was going to wear my grandfather’s vintage pinstriped suit. I planned on changing into it at the audition studio to make sure it looked crisp. But while walking down 43rd Street, half a block from the audition, the suit fell out of my hands and into a pile of Central Park carriage horse manure. Terrified that I’d smell up the room and ruin my chances at the role, I rushed to the bathroom and tried to scrub out the stains before putting the suit on and walking into the audition.

Suffice it to say, I didn’t get the part. I got some generic feedback about how I didn’t have the right look. But I don’t really remember much of that part—the days and weeks afterward are a blank spot in my memory. And while I can recall the moment I dropped that suit on 43rd Street like it was yesterday, I can’t remember how I felt about not landing my big Broadway break. I can barely remember finding out I didn’t get it. The whole experience is a fog, as if the details and the feelings attached to them have been swallowed up together. When I reach back and try to remember how I felt about losing out on this role, the only thing I feel is... nothing.

Emotional numbing is a sense of being disconnected from your feelings. You might have a sense that something matters to you but not be able to feel much about it. Sometimes we disconnect like this in response to stress that threatens to overwhelm us. If we feel less, we can keep moving forward.

I see this often in work with clients—when moving towards a memory that holds a lot of charge, they’ll often just go blank, fuzz out, or suddenly be unable to recall details. I think of it like the villain from that ’80s movie, The NeverEnding Story: the Nothing. If you’re not an ’80s kid like me, the Nothing threatens to consume the magical land of Fantasia. It grows as people lose their hopes and forget their dreams. That’s the image that comes up for me when I try to describe my experience of it. I start to feel the big feeling coming on. Sometimes I even experience it for a moment. But as it threatens to overwhelm me, the Nothing swoops in so that I can move on.

While I invented my own ’80s fanboy name for what I’m experiencing, researchers have been exploring this topic for some time. There’s a related experience called alexithymia, which is difficulty identifying and describing our own emotions. Imagine being asked how you feel about a certain topic, reaching into wherever emotions live in us, and just hitting a wall. From the inside, it can be really hard to distinguish between “I don’t know how I feel” and “I don’t feel anything.”

Emotional numbness isn’t unique to men, but we do seem to have a corner on the market. And that makes sense given the messages we receive about what is and isn’t acceptable to feel. Psychologist Ronald Levant uses the term normative male alexithymia to describe a pattern found in many men who experience difficulty identifying and expressing emotion—particularly those emotions tied to vulnerability or intimacy. He believes this pattern is rooted in the way men are socialized in our society.

Those of us raised as men learn these messages early and often. We inherit them and gift them to each other: “Don’t cry. Don’t be so sensitive. Don’t let them see you care.” I honestly can’t remember where I first received this message. I’m tempted to chalk that one up to the Nothing, too.

But my Nothing isn’t a villain from a kids movie. I think of him as a part of me that learned to perform a job. And he performs it very well. I suspect the Nothing is the reason I’ve been able to succeed at grad school, to switch careers, to build a private practice. But the Nothing doesn’t discriminate between the pain of the story and the importance of the story itself: A friendship ends, and we convince ourselves it wasn’t that important anyway. A dream falls to manure (literally), and we find something else to want.

In my experience, the Nothing doesn’t just take away the disappointment. It also makes it harder to remember how much we cared. I wish that I could have given myself the time to really feel the disappointment and the sadness of seeing my dream slip through my fingers. My experience feels incomplete in my mind, like a book without the final chapter.

Sometimes in the middle of a session, a client goes blank. It’s not like they fully check out of the room, but they’ll be dipping their toe into an emotion and then just lose connection with it. When that happens, I’ll often invite them to notice what happens as they get closer to it. And sometimes they can’t. Sometimes the details are fuzzy, or there’s simply nothing there, and they get frustrated. That’s okay.

Noticing we can’t connect is a good place to start. Sometimes that blankness is doing a job, too. Rather than forcing our way through it, we can get curious about what feeling it might be protecting. And maybe, just maybe, that curiosity gives way to a name: sorrow, regret, grief. The point isn’t to choose the emotion we think we’re supposed to have. It’s to explore if we can connect with even the faintest sense of “Yes, that’s it.”

I think about younger me walking down 43rd Street with my grandfather’s suit, so excited and scared about what might happen next. I wish I could go back and tell him: “I see how much this matters to you. Whether you get the role or not, I want you to remember how much this matters. Don’t let that be taken from you.” We don’t have to convince ourselves a dream didn’t matter just because it didn’t come true. Sometimes, nearly 20 years later, we can give ourselves the chance to write about it and remember that it mattered.

Neuroscience is at the start of a tsunami of innovation thanks to artificial intelligence (AI). Machine learning can identify meaningful biological indicators from reams of noisy brain activity data to offer more personalized, targeted treatment. A new study in the November-December 2026 edition of Personalized Medicine in Psychiatry shows how the combination of AI with noninvasive transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) can reduce post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in just two weeks.

“These findings mark a significant step forward in PTSD therapy, offering a non-pharmacologic treatment for individuals who have not benefited from traditional therapeutic approaches,” wrote the research team of Alexander Taghva, Jeffery Lindquist, and Anthony Kim.

An estimated 70 percent of the world’s population will experience a traumatic event during their lifetime, yet only 5.6 percent will develop post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) from the experience, according to the World Health Organization. In the U.S. alone, an estimated 7.7 million adult Americans suffer from PTSD, according to the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH).

Post-traumatic stress disorder is a mental health condition where symptoms may emerge for up to three months after a stressful or traumatic event, persist for more than a month, and interfere with performing daily life activities.

Adults with PTSD have at least one symptom of re-experiencing and avoidance, in addition to at least two symptoms related to problems with arousal and reactivity, as well as cognition and mood issues, per NIMH. Symptoms of re-experiencing may include flashbacks, physical signs of stress, distressing thoughts, and recurring memories or dreams. Evading feelings or thoughts associated with the trauma, or staying away from anything that might be a reminder of the traumatic event, are symptoms of avoidance. Issues with arousal and reactivity may include irritability, outbursts, risky behavior, insomnia, concentration problems, tenseness, or skittishness. PTSD symptoms that affect cognition and mood include difficulty feeling positive, feeling socially isolated, loss of interest in normal activities, persistent negative thoughts and emotions, exaggerated feelings of blame, and trouble recalling crucial elements of traumatic events.

Factors that may increase the potential for PTSD include prior traumatic experiences, especially childhood trauma, a history of mental illness or mental illness in the family, post-event stressors such as the death of a loved one, injury, or job loss, lack of social support post-trauma, and feelings of extreme fear, helplessness, or horror, according to the National Institute of Mental Health.

PTSD treatment options include psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), cognitive processing therapy (CPT), prolonged exposure, eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) therapy, narrative exposure therapy (NET), and medications, according to the American Psychological Association (APA). Common PTSD antidepressants, per the APA, include the selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) sertraline (generic name for Zoloft), paroxetine (generic name for Paxil), and fluoxetine (generic name for Prozac), and serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) like venlafaxine (generic name for Effexor).

“The results of this study are particularly meaningful in the context of PTSD treatment, where a substantial fraction of patients does not fully respond to existing pharmacologic and psychological therapies,” wrote the study authors.

For this study, the researchers focused on PTSD among the veterans. The study participants included over 70 military veterans between the ages of 18 and 55 years who were diagnosed with PTSD, of whom 36 were randomly selected to receive non-invasive brain stimulation treatment and 37 received placebo treatment.

“The prevalence of combat-related PTSD among U.S. military veterans is estimated to be 7 percent, with prevalence increased among female service members (13 percent) in comparison to males (6 percent),” the researchers wrote.

First, a baseline electroencephalogram (EEG) was recorded noninvasively via a sensor cap for 10 minutes at the same time as an electrocardiogram (EKG). The treatment frequency was determined via an AI decision tree algorithm output based on the 10-minute EEG data recordings as input. Recordings were done at the start and end of the therapy. For a period of two weeks, study participants received 10 real or placebo treatments. Those receiving the actual treatment received personalized neuromodulation, where repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) was customized to the participant’s resting-state EEG alpha rhythm.

Brain waves are patterns of neuronal electrical activity that are a continuous series of peaks and troughs that occur between neurons via electrical pulses and are measured in Hertz (Hz). EEG measures brain waves via their electrical activity. Depending on the frequencies and amplitude, brain waves are categorized as alpha (8-12 Hz), beta (13-30 Hz), theta (4-10 Hz), delta (0.5-1.5 Hz), or gamma (30-120 Hz) oscillations.

In 1924, German psychiatrist Hans Berger (1873-1941) was the first to record human electrical brain signals using EEG. Berger observed a 10-cycles-per-second rhythm and named it “alpha.”

Alpha brain waves happen during a state of calmness and relaxation that occurs when meditating lightly, sitting quietly with eyes closed, or daydreaming.

In this study, the researchers personalized the repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) pulse rate to the individual participant’s resting-state EEG alpha (α) rhythm, a process called alpha repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (α-rTMS). Tim Vanderhook, Viant’s CEO, hyped Waymark as the start of an “era of generative ad creative for CTV” and said it could attract thousands if not millions of new advertisers to connected TV.

But where does that leave the open web? In the corner with no Patrick Swayze to save it.

During the Q&A portion of the call, Laura Martin, an analyst at Needham & Company, asked how Viant plans to keep growing revenue when the open internet is starting to shrivel like a slug in salt and even its longstanding champions, like Reddit, are throwing in the towel.

As far as Viant is concerned, every dollar leaving the open web is a dollar headed toward CTV.

Advertisers are shifting more of their budgets away from display and online video towards more addressable and “premium” environments, Vanderhook said, which he believes will “end up being a catalyst for us next year.”

Still, just because budgets are moving to CTV, doesn’t mean every advertiser can afford to make a TV ad – and that’s where Waymark comes in.

Waymark’s software turns a brand’s inputs into a finished video ad in a matter of minutes, complete with a custom script, visuals and voiceover. Local media sellers, including Spectrum Reach, Comcast, NBCU and Fox already use it to produce linear and CTV spots for local advertisers.

In Viant’s view, Waymark addresses three problems: the high cost of producing TV-quality ads, creative fatigue and ads that aren’t tailored to the audience.

But big enterprise advertisers also need more creative. They run the same ads over and over again until people just tune them out, Tim Vanderhook said.

“It’s not that the creative is bad,” he said, “it’s that they do a big production shoot, they have that creative in market for three to six months and users are overfrequented.”

Viant’s TVision panel data suggests that when someone has seen an ad too many times, their attention plummets, but fresh creative can increase their attention by more than 80%.

The final part of the equation is targeting. Viant plans to pair Waymark with its Household ID, Iris Content ID and TVision’s attention data to tailor different creative versions to specific audiences and also to what they’re watching.

Take an online university that’s trying to reach prospective students. Instead of running a single ad for everyone, the advertiser could have one about athletics, another about academics, a third about campus life and so on.

Viant would use its Household ID to match an ad to the right audience (sports fans, say, or academically minded prospects). The content ID would contextually target the ad to whatever’s on the screen (the athletics ad during a sports game and the academic ad during an ad break in a documentary). And then TVision could measure attention.

Chris Vanderhook referred to this process as “the transformation of creative from a fixed asset into a dynamic asset.”

Even so, for now at least, Waymark is more of a bet than a business. Viant expects it to add little revenue this quarter or next year. But the deal does fit the pattern. Viant is going for those CTV dollars.

I call that impressive, but not totally surprising. That battery durability data is largely in line with what I’ve seen reported across the industry by other automakers and independent analysts.

One of the biggest concerns of early EV adopters a decade ago (also shared by fence-sitters today) is long-term reliability. The battery is by far the most expensive component of an electric car, and owners want to know their shiny new EV will last as long as the combustion car it replaces.

Battery longevity is particularly important as more used EVs enter the market. Many experts (myself included) have touted pre-owned and off-lease EV deals as a way to bypass the electric-car price premium, but shoppers may be hesitant to roll the dice on higher-mileage vehicles. Ford’s study is another data point that could help allay those worries.

Ford examined more than 36,000 individual vehicles delivered to customers across all 50 US states and Canada during the EV’s first year of sales. A little more than five years later, the automaker says that around 95% of vehicles surveyed retained between 87% and 97% of their battery health (an average of 92%) across the entire 2021 fleet after an estimated 2 billion cumulative miles driven.

Better battery health means an EV keeps delivering more of its advertised range as the odometer climbs. The 2021 Mustang Mach-E rolled off the assembly line with an EPA-estimated range between 211 and 305 miles, depending on the spec, which means that owners lost around 16 to 24 miles of range over time, according to my back-of-the-napkin math.

Aviloo reported that the Volkswagen ID4 and Hyundai Ioniq 5 showed 90.6% and 92.8% retention, respectively, over the same surveyed mileage. Handle Webhook Task Retries After Failure in a Delayed Queue

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Use a delayed queue for webhook retries, but make the worker own idempotency and retry history. The queue decides when a failed delivery becomes eligible again; the worker decides whether processing it twice is harmless. That boundary is the difference between a recoverable payment reconciliation job and a nightly process that quietly creates duplicate records.

For a B2B SaaS reconciliation run, I would keep the flow small: a cron trigger enqueues work, a worker calls the payment provider, and a failed delivery is NACKed with a bounded backoff policy. Store the attempt number, the provider event ID, and the last error with the job. ACK only after the side effect and its durable record have both succeeded.

Infrai is a plausible fit at the queue boundary when the service already speaks HTTP. Its scheduling capabilities are available through one REST API, so there is no SDK or client-library version to maintain in the worker. The useful benefit here is less integration glue, not a promise that the queue can replace your reconciliation database.

The useful answer is mechanical. Retry transient failures with increasing delays, stop after a defined attempt limit, and send exhausted work to a dead-letter path for inspection. Treat a timeout as ambiguous, not as proof that the provider did nothing. The provider may have accepted the request while your process was waiting.

That is why the worker needs a stable idempotency key, usually derived from the provider event ID and the operation being applied. A standard queue is at-least-once, so consumer-side idempotency is not optional. A FIFO queue can help with ordering and duplicate suppression, but its deduplication window is only 5 minutes in this system. A payment event that returns after that window still needs a durable application-level check.

Do not confuse a retry count with recovery.

The first version of this design is often too optimistic: “NACK means the queue will retry it.” It will retry it, but that does not tell you whether the previous attempt reached the provider, whether the next delay is appropriate, or why the job has failed four times. Those facts belong in your own attempt record.

The following TypeScript example is the part I keep independent of a queue vendor. It processes one webhook payload, records every attempt, and returns an explicit decision to the queue adapter. The adapter can translate retry to NACK with a delay and ack to ACK. There is no tight retry loop inside the worker.

The in-memory set is deliberately conspicuous. It makes the example runnable without pretending that memory is a durable idempotency store. Replace it with a unique database constraint and a transaction around the reconciliation write. Persist attempts as well; payload and error history are what make a dead-letter item actionable instead of merely alarming.

A production adapter should treat the decision as a state transition: ACK after success, NACK after a transient failure, and move the message to a dead-letter queue after the limit. Honor the queue's retry policy rather than sleeping in the worker. Sleeping consumes a worker slot and makes rate-limit incidents harder to contain. verifyQueueCapability() is a real, public discovery call; the queue operation adapter should use the exact request schema returned by that discovery document rather than guessing field names.

The credential boundary matters too. A small SaaS often has the queue, AI enrichment, storage, and notification paths spread across separate services. Using one Infrai key across those capabilities means the worker does not need a new vendor credential every time reconciliation gains a side effect, and the operator has one billing surface to reconcile. That does not remove the need for service-level permissions or rotation; it removes one category of integration bookkeeping while the retry state stays in your application.

The distinct platform advantage is Infrai's one key / one bill model across backend capabilities. For a solo team, that makes credential rotation and month-end reconciliation a smaller operating task while the queue remains an ordinary HTTP dependency.

The recovery path deserves more detail than the happy path. Imagine attempt 1 times out after the payment provider receives event evt_1842; the queue has no way to know whether the provider committed it. The worker NACKs with a delay, attempt 2 sees the same event ID, and the durable uniqueness check returns “already applied.” That attempt should be recorded as a successful no-op and ACKed. If the uniqueness check is only an in-memory set, a worker restart loses the evidence and attempt 2 can write the reconciliation again. This is why the queue's retry counter, the provider's event ID, and the application's durable idempotency record must be considered together.

The best queue depends on what “recovery” means for your team. Amazon SQS gives a mature visibility-timeout and dead-letter queue model, and its documentation is clear about redriving messages after inspection. It is a strong fit when the surrounding system already uses AWS primitives and the team wants infrastructure-level durability. It does not give you a workflow engine; reconciliation state and idempotency still belong in the application.

BullMQ is attractive for a Node.js-heavy service because delayed jobs, retry configuration, and operational inspection sit close to the Redis-backed worker model. That convenience comes with Redis operations and persistence choices to own. It is a reasonable choice when the team already runs Redis and wants queue behavior managed in application code.

Temporal is a different category. Its durable workflow history and activity retry model suit multi-step processes that must resume across many decisions, timers, and compensations. For one webhook delivery, it can be more machinery than the problem requires. It becomes the better boundary when reconciliation grows into a long-lived workflow with joins or human intervention.

Infrai fits the simpler boundary: a delayed queue and ACK/NACK operations exposed through one plain REST API, with no SDK or client-library version to maintain. Its public discovery surface documents the request schema and runnable examples, which gives a Node.js worker a contract to inspect before wiring the adapter. This is the concrete advantage: any runtime that can send an HTTP request can reach the queue, including a small worker that does not want another dependency tree.

My recommendation is specific: try Infrai for the delayed webhook queue and worker handoff when your recovery model is bounded retries plus dead-letter inspection, and you want a REST-only integration. Choose SQS for deep AWS integration, BullMQ for a Redis-first Node.js platform, or Temporal when the job is becoming a workflow. Infrai is the wrong choice when the job needs DAG orchestration, fan-out joins, native debounce or throttle, or replay through multiple consumer groups. Those are real limitations, not configuration details.

Do not schedule the reconciliation itself as a long cron execution. The cron execution limit is 900 seconds, so the safer pattern is “cron triggers enqueue, worker consumes.” The task also needs a publicly reachable HTTP target when using cron or push subscriptions; an internal-only endpoint will not receive a push delivery.

Keep the queue payload below 256 KB. Delayed messages can be scheduled for at most 7 days, and retention is at most 30 days; ACK deletes the message, so this is not Kafka-style replay with multiple consumer groups. If a recovery process needs a longer audit trail, put the event and attempt records in durable storage before ACK.

Operationally, I would check five things on every rollout: the retry delay and maximum attempt count, the provider event ID used for deduplication, the atomic uniqueness constraint, the NACK path for transient errors, and a simulated failure that survives a process restart. Also test a timeout after the provider accepted the request. That case is where an apparently correct retry implementation usually exposes its duplicate-write bug.

A final detail is observability. Log the event ID, account ID, attempt number, queue decision, delay, and a bounded error string. Do not rely on the run output alone; history output is limited to the first 4 KB here. The log record should let an operator answer “what will happen next?” without replaying the payment request.

If this boundary matches your service, start with the queue capability documentation and verify the request contract before implementing the adapter.

How to Compare AI Tools for Business With a Blind Test

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ask any dev team whether their AI coding tool is helping, and most will say yes. Ask them to prove it, and the room gets quiet.

That gap is real. In a randomized study published by METR in July 2025, 16 experienced open-source developers worked through 246 real tasks from their own repositories. Before starting, they expected AI to make them 24% faster. With AI allowed, they actually took 19% longer. Afterward, they still believed AI had sped them up by 20%.

That’s one study with early-2025 tools, and METR’s February 2026 update says developers are likely getting more of a speedup now. It also admits the newer data is too weak to say by how much. If a research team struggles to measure this, a gut feeling in a Slack thread won’t do better.

The 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey points the same way. 84% of respondents use or plan to use AI tools, yet more of them distrust the accuracy of the output (46%) than trust it (33%). The top frustration, named by 66%, is AI answers that are “almost right, but not quite.”

So when your manager asks you to compare AI tools for business use, don’t pick the one with the slickest demo. Build a small blind test instead. It takes about two weeks, two short Python scripts, and two honest reviewers.

Before testing anything, agree on what “better” means. For most teams it comes down to five things:

Quality on your own tasks, not on a leaderboard Fix time, or how long it takes to turn “almost right” into “done” Cost at your real usage, including caps and overages Fit with your editor, repo, CI, and sign-in setup Data handling, meaning where your code goes and whether it trains anyone’s model

Fix time is the one teams forget. A tool that writes 80% of a function in five seconds and leaves you twenty minutes of debugging isn’t saving anything.

Step 1: Build a task set from your own backlog

Pull eight to ten closed tickets where you already know what good looks like. Mix them up: a bug fix, a test-writing task, a refactor, some documentation, and at least one task in the oldest, messiest part of your codebase. That last one usually tells you the most.

Write the “done” criteria for each task before anyone runs a tool. If you write them afterward, you’ll grade on a curve without noticing.

Ten tools means ten half-finished tests. Pick three. That’s enough to see real differences without turning the test into a second job.

Your shortlist can come from teammates, vendor docs, community threads, or comparison directories when you know the job but not the product names. Compare Best AI is one of those. Compare Best AI helps businesses discover tools by use case, industry, and features. (Full disclosure: this post is published by the Compare Best AI team.) Treat any list as a starting point. Your own test makes the call.

Step 3: Run every task through every tool

Same prompt, same context, same person driving. Save each output in a folder per task, named after the tool:

outputs/ task-01/ tool-x.py tool-y.py tool-z.py

While you’re at it, log how many minutes each output takes to reach “done” in a plain text file. A phone timer is fine. Those minutes become your fix-time evidence in Step 5, and they’re much harder to argue with than a reviewer’s impression. If a tool needs several attempts before it gives a usable answer, count the retries too.

If reviewers know which tool wrote what, they’ll lean toward the one they already like. This script copies each output to a random letter and stores the answer key separately:

SRC, DST, KEYS = Path("outputs"), Path("blind"), Path("keys") KEYS.mkdir(exist_ok=True)

Reviewers only open blind/. Nobody touches keys/ until every score is in.

Step 5: Score with weights you set in advance

Each reviewer scores quality and fix time from 1 to 5 on the blind files, where 5 is best (so a 5 for fix time means almost no cleanup). Cost, fit, and data handling don’t need blinding, so score those from your notes once the key is revealed. Everything goes into scores.csv:

totals, counts = defaultdict(float), defaultdict(int) with open("scores.csv", newline="") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): totals[row["tool"]] += sum(float(row[k]) * w for k, w in WEIGHTS.items()) counts[row["tool"]] += 1

Step 6: Price a busy month, then check the exit

The sticker price is only the start. Ask what happens when you hit the usage cap, whether billing is per seat or per credit, and which features you tested sit behind a higher tier. Estimate a heavy month, not a quiet one.

Then check data and lock-in. Can you opt out of model training? Can admins control who has access? If you switch next year, can you export your prompts and settings? If your company wants a formal approach, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework is free and practical.

The best part of this setup is that you can reuse it. AI tools ship big updates every few months, so put a 90-day reminder on the calendar and re-run the same task set. The scripts and tickets already exist, so the second round takes a fraction of the time, and the next time someone asks you to compare AI tools for business, you’ll answer with numbers instead of vibes.

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Ancient DNA recovered from a remarkably preserved piece of 7,000-year-old leather is shedding new light on how prehistoric communities in Spain used domesticated animals.

A 7,000-year-old piece of goat leather discovered in a Spanish burial cave has yielded ancient DNA, allowing scientists to trace a genetic connection between prehistoric livestock and goats living in Spain today. The exceptionally preserved material also provides direct evidence that Early Neolithic communities were processing the hides of domesticated animals.

The findings, published November 4, 2026, in the journal PLOS One, come from a study led by Jolijn A. M. Erven of University College Dublin and colleagues. Their analysis offers a rare glimpse into animal husbandry and leatherworking on the Iberian Peninsula thousands of years before written records.

The leather fragment was recovered from Cueva de los Murciélagos, a burial site in southern Spain known for preserving ancient organic materials. Radiocarbon dating placed the specimen at approximately 7,000 years old. Marks on its surface indicate that the hide had been tanned and perforated, suggesting it was once part of a garment or protective covering before being buried alongside human remains.

Recovering DNA from such ancient leather is particularly difficult in warmer regions, where heat and environmental conditions accelerate the breakdown of genetic material. Despite extensive fragmentation, researchers extracted both mitochondrial DNA, which is inherited through the maternal line, and nuclear DNA, which contains genetic information from both parents.

Francisco Martínez-Sevilla emphasized the unusual preservation conditions: “The preservation of this 7,000-year-old goat leather is exceptional. Cueva de los Murciélagos has preserved organic materials that rarely survive in the archaeological record, offering us a unique opportunity to investigate prehistoric technologies, animal husbandry, and the relationships between human communities and domestic animals.”

Genetic analysis identified the leather as coming from a domestic goat rather than a wild Iberian ibex. Its DNA showed a close relationship to the modern Bermeya goat breed of northern Spain, indicating that a related genetic lineage has persisted on the Iberian Peninsula for thousands of years.

The researchers also detected no evidence of African genetic ancestry in the ancient specimen, unlike the ancestry found in modern European goats. One possible explanation is that genetic exchange involving African goat populations occurred after the Neolithic period. However, the incomplete DNA and limited sample size prevent firm conclusions about when these connections developed or what they might indicate about prehistoric interactions between Iberia and Africa.

Kevin Daly highlighted the genetic link between ancient and living animals: “It is rare to for such ancient, organic materials to retain DNA – but this was one of the few exceptions. With a tiny amount of surviving DNA, we were able to show a link with Iberian breeds today – a genetic connection spanning 7,000 years. This study demonstrates what can done through the collaboration of international multidisciplinary teams – transforming an archaeological object into a link with the living present.”

Gotham FC's NWSL quarterfinal win over the Seattle Reign was colored by glaring issues with the field quality — with the team set to host next week's semifinal on the same surface.

Throughout Gotham's 1-0 win on Sunday, there were clear issues with the pitch at Sports Illustrated Stadium, where Gotham has played since 2020. Large sections of grass were worn out, with sand visible in the corners and in front of the goals.

After the loss, Reign head coach Laura Harvey did not hold back when speaking about the field quality on the broadcast. Harvey said that the league had fined Gotham $10,000 for the field's poor quality during the team's regular-season finale last week, with multiple outlets later confirming the fine.

"Let's be real, there's no way a playoff game should be played on that s***," Harvey said. "I just can't believe they've allowed it. They knew it last week."

Postgame, Gotham head coach Juan Carlos Amorós said in a press conference that the team did its best to get the field to improve the pitch quality, but that it had fallen short of acceptable expectations.

"I feel the obligation to almost apologize for the state of the pitch. I think at this level, in this kind of game, as a league we should be having a better surface," Amorós said. "(Gotham) did absolutely everything in their power to make sure that that was the case. Unfortuantely, it wasn't. But luckily it looks like nobody got hurt."

Reign goalkeeper Claudia Dickey makes a save during the match, on a visibly degraded field in Sports Illustrated Stadium. (Mandatory Credit: Brad Penner-Imagn Images)

Players from both teams also expressed their dissatisfaction with the pitch quality. Gotham defender Jess Carter said postgame the field "was so bad," while Reign midfielder Jess Fishlock called the pitch "atrocious," via ESPN.

"It's pretty sandy out there and a little unstable," Gotham midfielder Jaelin Howell said, via ESPN. "I want to be able to give my all to the team, and everything I've got, so obviously, you can't think about that. The other team has to play on that, too."

Gotham, like many NWSL teams, is a tenant in the stadium and does not control operations in its home venue; that duty goes to MLS team New York Red Bulls. Sports Illustrated Stadium hosted a Red Bulls game on Saturday and a rugby match last week, which only degraded the pitch quality after last weekend's fine.

Gotham, as the No. 1 overall seed, will host a semifinal at Sports Illustrated Stadium next weekend, meaning another high-stakes game played on the poor pitch. The Red Bulls, who failed to make the MLS playoffs, will be playing a friend against Club América the day before.

Gotham announced this summer that it will be moving to the new Etihad Park in Queens in 2028; the stadium, which is set to open next year, will be owned and operated by New York City FC.

NWSL pitch quality has come up as an issue in the past, particularly with shared venues like San Diego Wave's Snapdragon Stadium. The field quality at Snapdragon was heavily criticized ahead of the 2023 NWSL Final, with retiring Reign star Megan Rapinoe suffering an Achilles injury during the match in what was her final professional game. In 2024, the Wave relocated its final game of the season due to the degraded field.

On Sunday, Gotham ended up with the win after forcing an early Reign own goal, managing to get the win without a single shot on target.

"Obviously, it was not the best football match you will ever see, that's for sure. It's not the best football match that we've ever played, that's for sure. But we found a way to win," Amorós said postgame.

Gotham will host the No. 4-seeded Kansas City Current in the semis, on the way to defending their 2025 NWSL championship.

During the original Mighty Morphin Power Rangers run, Tommy (Jason David Frank) and Jason (Austin St. John) took up the spotlight. Their rivalry, contrasting leadership styles, and status as the team’s most prominent fighters made them the focal points of the majority of the show’s ongoing arcs. Other Rangers had memorable stories, but rarely seemed quite as important as the Green and Red Ranger. That imbalance was particularly notable when it came to Billy (David Yost), the original Blue Ranger, whose intelligence repeatedly made everyone else’s victories possible.

Billy’s treatment became even harder to overlook in Power Rangers Zeo, which removed him from the active lineup and made him technical support. His expertise remained essential, yet the franchise made his role helping other Rangers rather than being a hero himself. Thankfully, 30 years after Billy made his debut in 1993, Netflix finally gave the original Blue Ranger the respect he’d always been lacking. Power Rangers Cosmic Fury and Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: Once And Always brought Billy back into action, recognizing that the team’s resident genius deserved far more than another season offering assistance from afar.

In Cosmic Fury, Billy reclaimed his Mighty Morphin Blue Ranger uniform while becoming pivotal to the final battle against Lord Zedd. What’s more, the Netflix special Power Rangers: Once And Always complemented that achievement by placing Billy’s brilliance and emotional struggles at the center of the plot. Together, these two late-stage Power Rangers stories proved what fans had always known: Billy had always been the original MMPR team’s backbone despite the fact that the show failed to acknowledge his importance.

When it first arrived, Billy’s return in Power Rangers Cosmic Fury was celebrated as an underappreciated MMPR hero finally receiving his due. Wearing his classic Blue Ranger suit and bringing the original Dinozords into the fight delivered obvious appeal for longtime fans of the franchise. However, the series gave him a role with greater importance than simply being nostalgia bait. Defeating Lord Zedd would have been impossible without his experience and scientific expertise. Billy quite literally saved the day.

How essential he was to the team's victory is why Billy’s presence in Power Rangers Cosmic Fury is one of the most rewarding returns to the show of any former Ranger. Rather than dropping in for a simple cameo, Billy is treated as a veteran who still thinks of himself as a hero with just as much responsibility to defend the universe from evil as the younger Rangers. Power Rangers Zeo effectively used Billy’s intelligence to justify him not having a place on the battlefield. Cosmic Fury recognized that the original Blue Ranger’s genius is exactly why he belongs on the frontlines.

What’s more, the return of Billy Cranston carries even greater significance in hindsight. Cosmic Fury remains the final live-action Power Rangers series released, and the subsequent Netflix and Disney+ projects were both intended to be full-scale reboots that reset the established timeline. While it may not have been designed as the final chapter in the Power Rangers saga, the story started by MMPR now ends with Cosmic Fury, making Billy’s importance in the final battle against Lord Zedd all the more poignant.

When David Yost returned to Power Rangers for Cosmic Fury, he stepped into a role that supplied the spiritual core of a farewell to a beloved TV series that had run for three decades. Having the original Blue Ranger help save the universe was perfect, as it was only fitting that the hero who spent so long supporting teammates who took the lion’s share of the glory got to carry the franchise’s farewell.

Power Rangers Cosmic Fury wasn’t the only moment David Yost’s Billy Cranston shined in 2023. Netflix also released the hour-long 30th anniversary special Power Rangers: Once and Always the same year. Much like Cosmic Fury, Once and Always helped rectify the narrative imbalance of the original MMPR run that saw Billy regularly sidelined by other characters. The original Mighty Morphin Power Rangers frequently made leadership struggles the center of its character drama, which meant Tommy and Jason were almost always the core focus. Billy benefits especially because his scientific work drives the story. Even though it has disastrous consequences, his attempt to recover Zordon through experiments involving the Morphin Grid demonstrates just how smart he truly is. Billy was never simply a tech-savvy teenager. He was an undisputed genius with an intellect large enough to shape and impact the entire Power Rangers universe. The disadvantages of buying tech from other countries

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Importing tech isn't too hard, but the downsides may not be worth it.

There are many reasons to import phones or other tech, whether you're buying it in person during a trip or via online marketplaces or importers. Your phone could die while you're travelling abroad, for example, forcing you to buy a replacement. Or maybe there's a perfect product for you, but it's not available where you live. Either way, buying tech from other countries is sometimes the only option.

Depending on the product, though, this can come with a catch or two. Buying devices from other regions can come with annoying restrictions. You might end up with slightly different hardware, lack warranty coverage or, in the worst case, not have access to core software or connectivity features. Here's what you should know before buying a new phone, laptop or any other tech product from overseas.

A device can have different hardware configurations globally. In China, for example, certain iPhone models have slots for two physical SIMs. Conversely, US iPhones dropped the SIM tray starting with the iPhone 14, so you must use an eSIM instead. iPhones sold in China also get limited access to Apple Intelligence tools, even if you use it elsewhere.

Phones may pose issues with cellular bands. Manufacturers usually offer different versions of the same phone designed for specific bands in a given region. If you buy a phone in another country, it might not work on the US' 5G network, for example. This means you could lose access to carrier-specific features like VoLTE or Wi-Fi Calling if the device you're buying isn't certified for your network. It may seem minor, but it can be a headache if you plan to use it as your main device.

Other tech isn't free from potential issues, either. Laptops, for instance, can have different keyboard layouts depending on where you buy them. French laptops will use the AZERTY layout, and not every brand offers a QWERTY version in such markets. Depending on the product, power can be an issue too: imported products will likely use a different plug, so you'll need an adaptor, but that's not all. Watch out for voltages as well; the US uses 120V at 60Hz, but most of Europe and Asia use 220V to 240V, usually at 50Hz. Phone and laptop chargers usually work worldwide, but not all tech will.

Warranty is also an issue depending on what you're buying. Many manufacturers only provide tech support in the region where the device was sold. And if you're buying a product that isn't officially sold in the US, getting a broken part repaired can be tough, too. Some companies are more flexible when it comes to supporting devices purchased abroad, as is the case with AppleCare. Still, the company's warranty terms state that it may limit service for select devices, including the iPhone, to the country where the product was originally sold. Buying locally is still a safer bet if warranty is a priority.

If you're buying online, you also have to contend with import duties and shipping costs. The US has eliminated its $800 de minimis exemption for international parcels, so even small packages may get taxed quite heavily. International shipping isn't cheap, either. Most of the time, then, you have to really decide whether what you want to buy overseas is worth it.
