However, it had been turned around by half-time with a Cambiaso stunner and Ekhator’s first goal in the Juve jersey, with Francisco Conceicao and Jeremie Boga rounding out the scoreline in the second half.

“Until the Cambiaso goal, it looked a lot like the Venezia game,” confessed Spalletti with a smile on Sky Sport Italia.

“We were uncertain and the opponents took advantage, we felt the pressure of a potential counter-attack and everything started to feel complicated, but Cambiaso got us back on track with that wonderful goal, and from there the team played with greater calm.”

The jeers had been starting to rain down from the stands before that equaliser sparked the comeback, but Cambiaso badly needed it after recent struggles.

“It’s not easy to find form when called in, and some of these players hadn’t started for a while, so lacking consistency is difficult. However, we had some good moves, kept an acceptable level of pressing, and entertained the crowd with four fantastic goals.”

There were curlers from Boga and Cambiaso, albeit not quite the style of Juventus icon Alessandro Del Piero, who was in the Sky Sport Italia studio as a pundit.

“The Boga goal was my favourite because Gatti won it back with that high press, and sometimes the players are a little hesitant to go that far forward, as it leaves gaps behind them. Gatti then ran to follow the move and was in a position to score himself,” noted Spalletti.

“He still managed to find the space himself two or three times, so we have to compliment Ekhator.”

Does that mean Juventus are no longer eager to sign a physical centre-forward in January, now that they have Ekhator fully fit?

“Everyone has their own characteristics, and my job is not to complain that something is missing, but to find the solution with what I have at my disposal. The first move must be mine, to find a way to provide the right service for the characteristics in the squad,” replied Spalletti.

Juventus receive another morale-boosting victory after the 4-0 against Venezia in Serie A on Saturday, and are on track to enter the Europa League Round of 16 directly after four wins and a 2-2 draw with Stade Rennais.

They are second only to Bournemouth, who have a 100 per cent record after five rounds, and ahead of Ferencvaros on goal difference.

Bournemouth are dreaming of winning the Europa League after staging a 10-man fightback to join a select band of teams to win their first five European games.

But second-half goals from Alvaro Rodriguez and Rayan gave the Cherries a 10th consecutive win in all competitions, extending their club record run.

Bournemouth are only the third side to win each of their first five European matches (excluding qualifiers and including the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup), after Nuremberg in 1961-62 and Ararat in 1972-73.

They top this season's table as the only club to win five out of five - and are closing in on a top-eight spot, which would mean they skip a play-off round and go straight into the last 16.

Some fans have told the BBC they have already booked hotels in Frankfurt for the final on 26 May.

As supporters sang about reaching that game, midfielder Alex Scott was asked for his thoughts on TNT Sports.

"Of course that's our dream. When you grow up wanting to be a footballer you dream of winning trophies," Scott said.

"Where this club has come from to where it is now, full credit to everyone who has built it.

"For players, it's a dream to be on the pitch playing for this club. We have still got wins to get to qualify into the top eight, and from there all focus is on pushing forward and hopefully we can go on and win it."

The Cherries are in unprecedented times, with their biggest trophy to date being when they won the second-tier Championship in 2014-15.

'The Walking Dead' Meets 'War of the Worlds' in Noah Wyle's Gritty 5-Season Sci-Fi Thriller

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If aliens took over our world tomorrow, what would America look like months down the line? That's the exact question that the Steven Spielberg-produced TNT drama Falling Skies aimed to answer, and it did just that over five stellar seasons. Something of a cross between the sci-fi aesthetics of War of the Worlds and the apocalyptic character drama of The Walking Dead, this underrated cult classic deserves a rewatch — and now that it's on Netflix, there's no better time than the present.

Part of what set Falling Skies apart from the get-go was that it didn't begin with the invasion. We don't see the military's first attempt at defending our world as the major cities across the globe, Boston in this case, are descended on by alien overlords with less than admirable intentions. In step with The Walking Dead, although the world before is referenced at first, the show doesn't take long to throw you into the "new normal" that these horrid circumstances have created. When we first meet Tom Mason (played by Noah Wyle, who did double duty on this show and The Librarians during the latter's first season), for instance, he has already lost one son to these invaders on top of his wife. Although he was a history professor, he's now been conscripted as the second-in-command of the 2nd Massachusetts militia led by Col. Dan Weaver (Will Patton). We're not privy to how all that played out, but it doesn't matter. This is where the story begins.

While the show is unashamedly science fiction in the same vein as War of the Worlds, it's structured much like The Walking Dead in practice. Each episode follows this unlikely band of militia warriors on weekly quests to find new supplies, uncover more intel, or save a captive they've recently seen enslaved by the invaders. Of course, the show becomes a bit more serialized as time goes on, but many of those early episodes are spent establishing the characters so that when tragedy does strike, it means something. As the widowed father of three boys — Hal (Drew Roy), Ben (Connor Jessup), and Matt (Maxim Knight) — Tom has more to lose than just about anyone, and yet continues to help lead the fight against the Espheni in the same ground-up style as the American Revolution.

The cast is further rounded out by pediatrician-turned-jack-of-all-trades doctor Anne Glass (Moon Bloodgood), who herself has suffered serious loss at the hands of the invaders. As Tom and Weaver take the fight to the aliens on the literal battlefield, Anne works to uncover the secrets of their biology. Her part in the war is pivotal, as learning all they can about their new enemy gives the 2nd Mass the power to work against them. And, how could we forget about Colin Cunningham's John Pope? The motorbike-style outlaw became a vital part of the show throughout the entirety of its run, often standing in stark contrast to Tom and his morally conscious methods of warfare. Often butting heads, the pair each found ways to contribute to the 2nd Mass — and thus the war — that led to some impressive developments.

Falling Skies always thrived in the visual department. While some of the CGI may look a bit hokey now, back in 2011 when the show first premiered, the alien mechs and the Espheni were crafted quite well and blended effortlessly into the torn-down world around them. The alien creatures, such as the Skitters and the Volm, the alien race led primarily by Tom's friend and ally Cochise (Doug Jones) beginning in the third season, were likewise constructed with feature-level quality, using a blend of practical effects with digital enhancements. As much as we watched Falling Skies (which was already underrated in its day) for its characters and to see how the battle against Earth's first extraterrestrial invaders would play out, there was always something magnetic about the aliens and the battle sequences that ensued.

Although one might argue that Falling Skies was a stronger show early on (and there may be some truth to that), the TNT drama ultimately concludes on its own terms. Across five seasons and 52 episodes, the post-apocalyptic sci-fi series builds toward an inevitable conclusion that pits Tom and the 2nd Mass against the overlords trying to wipe out and enslave the human race. It's a powerful (and profound) climax that feels earned, and proves to be worth the journey. Falling Skies was a summer show, airing in the three-month break between traditional television seasons, which meant that it was always welcomed back by fans with open arms. All these years later, it still packs a serious punch for folks looking for something as patriotic as it is sci-fi-flavored.

Falling Skies is available for streaming on Netflix.

Gerald R. Molen, Schindler’s List & Jurassic Park Producer, Dies at 91

Academy Award-winning producer Gerald R. Molen has passed away.

Molen, the iconic producer behind several of Steven Spielberg’s most critically acclaimed movies, has sadly died at the age of 91. Throughout his impressive career in the entertainment industry, Molen helped bring some of the industry's most famous films to the big screen, such as Jurassic Park, Schindler’s List, and Minority Report.

According to The Hollywood Reporter, Molen died on Monday, November 23, 2026. The producer's great-niece, Rachel Lorraine Pullan, confirmed the news on social media. The cause of death has not been disclosed at the time of this article's publication.

Molen's professional relationship with Spielberg began when he served as the unit production manager on the director’s 1985 drama The Color Purple. His work on the project opened many doors for the producer, leading him into a whole new chapter of his career. Spielberg also offered him an associate producer position on the 1987 science fiction comedy Batteries Not Included.

They went on to collaborate on many movies, such as 1991’s Hook, starring Robin Williams, Dustin Hoffman, and Julia Roberts. Molen eventually became the head of production at Spielberg’s Amblin Entertainment, where he managed the productions over several of the studio's projects.

During his time at Amblin, Molen was in charge of numerous major releases throughout the 1990s, including The Flintstones, The Little Rascals, Little Giants, Casper, and Twister. He was also involved with Spielberg’s 1997 dinosaur blockbuster The Lost World: Jurassic Park.

Universal released The Odyssey on 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray and digital platforms on November 17 with a 1.78:1 aspect ratio, filling a conventional widescreen television. Have you ever met a new friend who seemed to think you were too great? You can do no wrong, you’re the first person to ever truly understand them, you’re so much better than their other friends. But then you have a disagreement or miss a birthday party, and they seem to just…turn on you? Now they feel sure that you were only ever kind to them because you wanted something; you’re just like everyone else.

Idealization and devaluation like this are two sides of the same coin—splitting, which is most associated with borderline personality disorder, or BPD. But it, or behaviors that closely resemble it, can also occur in people with narcissistic traits, depression, anxiety, autism, and other conditions. In fact, we all do it to some degree; teenagers are notorious for it. And it happens in all kinds of relationships: friendship, family, professional, marriage or partnership.

Obviously, neither extreme is a complete or fair perception of you. The truth of who you are exists somewhere between the ideal and the devalued, but that liminal space isn’t something your friend has access to right now.

And within that inability to see the space between all-good and all-bad lies the definition of splitting. In all of us, certain cognitive processes go offline when we become dysregulated or lose homeostasis: You don’t communicate well when you’re hungry, you can’t appreciate the beauty of a sunset when you’re angry, and so on. Of course that’s not what you’re doing. So, you’re likewise blindsided by their response. Your spouse is fuming because you betrayed them, and they have some choice words for you. Every positive aspect of your relationship is read back to you in a negative light. It feels like they hate you.

The 5 Greatest Bands of the British Invasion, Ranked

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Throughout the 1960s, there was a huge influx of British rock and pop music into American culture. English bands were dominating the charts and the music scene was becoming more globalized than ever before. The overseas relationship was reciprocal – these British bands were reworking American blues and R&B within a rock format, infusing psychedelic and progressive rock. Concurrently, American rock music began to emulate the British sound. This cultural phenomenon changed the face of music forever.

The British Invasion is an interesting and necessary study in the way music reinvents itself – how every musician is influenced by those who came before. This time period introduced this wonderful merging of sound to an international audience, causing a dizzyingly steep learning curve that produced some of the greatest music of all time. Here are the top five most important bands of the British Invasion.

The Animals formed in 1962 in Newcastle upon Tyne. They drew inspiration from American rhythm and blues artists, getting their start with covers by iconic musicians such as Jimmy Reed and Nina Simone. They had a big hand in the genesis of folk rock with their signature song: a cover of the American folk classic “House of the Rising Sun.” Their version is raw and emotional, capturing the struggles of working-class Americans during this time. The band represented a darker and grittier side of the British Invasion canon, the opposite end of the spectrum from the British pop that was also making its way overseas.

In the latter half of the sixties, they changed their lineup and their name to Eric Burden and The Animals. During this time, they delved into the psychedelic rock space, departing from their earlier blues influences. They laid the groundwork for prog rock, influenced punk/ garage music, and inspired artists across many genres (including Bruce Springsteen, The Grateful Dead, and Rod Stewart, to name a few.)

The Kinks also formed in 1962, and their rise in popularity aligned with that of the British Invasion. They released their breakout hit “You Really Got Me” in 1964, quickly achieving international success. They were heavy hitters on the British Invasion circuit – that is, until 1966, when they were banned from touring in America. While the reason has never been explicitly made public, it is believed to be a combination of promoter disputes, chaotic behavior from the band, and visa and permit issues. Nevertheless, they continued to grow in popularity in the UK and America alike.

The Kinks’ satirical single “Sunday Afternoon” was inspired by British music hall – a form of working-class variety theater rooted in Victorian-era taverns and pubs. The track was the biggest hit in the UK during the summer of 1966. The Kinks are often regarded as the original punk band, with much of their music featuring fuzzy guitar distortion and a driving, bluesy energy. They influenced countless bands across rock subgenres, including psychedelic bands like The Doors and Jefferson Airplane, punk rock groups such as the Ramones, and Oasis and other Britpop bands.

The Who popularized many techniques and terms that would become touchstones in hard rock. Their incredible mastery of their instruments allowed them to get super creative with their recording and production. They experimented heavily with synthesizers and feedback, adding layers of texture that were unprecedented at the time. Guitarist and songwriter (and legend!) Pete Townshend had a massive impact on rock music unto himself. He popularized the use of power chords and also coined the term “power pop” in reference to the group’s sound. Townshend wrote the band’s most popular album, Tommy, which was the first time the term “rock opera” was used publicly.

Drummer Keith Moon also had a big hand in the band’s popularity and original sound. His playing was chaotic and unruly – he pushed the rhythm section out of the pocket and onto center stage. Moon was the backbone of The Who’s explosive, energetic live performances. They were the loudest band of the era, and maybe of all time – a quality that was understandably polarizing.

And now we’ve reached the greatest rock band of all time. The Rolling Stones (who are remarkably still together and actively performing) are pioneers of hard rock music. They began their career with blues covers (a common theme on this list) and were greatly influenced by American R&B artists such as Chuck Berry, Muddy Waters, and Fats Domino. The band was drawn to the sheer power and emotional expression these musicians brought to their work.

Eventually, the Stones moved on to writing and recording their own original music – tracks that would, conversely, influence the next generation of American blues and rock artists, a prime example of the circuitous and reciprocal nature of music. These songs were primarily written by Mick Jagger and Keith Richards, a collaboration that sometimes led to tension in the group. Not that it affected them much – they have countless international hits. The Stones’ expansive discography bridged the gap between rhythm and blues and hard classic rock, and also between America and the UK. Their swagger and flamboyant rebellion made them popular in youth culture on both sides of the Atlantic.

Bet you didn’t see this one coming – Beatlemania, baby! Formed in 1960, The Beatles are the most influential band in popular music. They started out with a clean-cut, boy-next-door look and sound – all pop, no grit. With time, they evolved into something much more. They began to travel and broaden their horizons, getting to know themselves and the world on a deeper level. Their lyrics grew deeper and more poetic, and their influences expanded vastly: Indian and Western classical music, pop, psychedelia, and folk were all incorporated. Their evolution as a band is something to be studied – and it has been, quite thoroughly and quite often.

The British Invasion would never have happened without The Beatles – they were the foot in the door to the American popular music market. They were the voice of youth culture and the counterculture, a living and breathing documentation of the Western world from 1960 to 1970. Their constant reinvention and unprecedented levels of success have never been matched or recreated, and likely never will.

Should You Buy Tesla Stock Before December?

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Merger talks might jolt Tesla stock by year-end, or they might now.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 26, 2026 at 5:15PM EST

Tesla (TSLA -1.18%) has had a challenging year. Despite its core electric vehicle (EV) business often performing better than expected, partly due to soaring oil prices, the company's shares have significantly lagged broader equities. However, the year isn't over, and there is a chance Tesla could finish 2026 with a bang, given ongoing developments. With that said, should investors consider buying the stock before December?

Tesla's CEO, Elon Musk, now leads two publicly traded corporations, the other being Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%), which went public in June. Rumors of a potential merger between Tesla and SpaceX have been circulating since before SpaceX was public, but a comment Musk made during SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call added fuel to the fire.

Musk said that SpaceX and Tesla have formed independent special committees to discuss a potential merger between the two corporations. And more recently, five board directors from both companies have reportedly been discussing a potential all-stock merger that would make the combined entity worth about $3.5 trillion, with a deal possible by year-end. Importantly, this hasn't been confirmed by either company. But it's still worth figuring out the potential benefits and risks of this proposed acquisition.

Suppose SpaceX acquires Tesla at a 20% premium in an all-stock deal: SpaceX would have to issue a substantial number of new shares, thereby significantly diluting existing shareholders. Tesla's stock might rise, at least initially, while SpaceX's could decline unless investors believe the merger will generate enough synergies to justify the acquisition premium.

There are indeed some reasons to think that the combined entity could create more value than either would on its own. For instance, both Tesla and SpaceX are investing massive sums in artificial intelligence (AI). As a single entity, they could eliminate redundant investments and lower costs, while leveraging their larger size to negotiate better deals with suppliers.

Also, some of Tesla's work, including its energy infrastructure and the Optimus humanoid robot, could be of immense value to SpaceX. So, that could be good news for the business' long-term prospects, although whether the proposed combined entity will, on balance, create more value is by no means guaranteed. However, one major source of uncertainty will likely come from regulators. The deal will probably attract significant attention from lawmakers, at least some of whom may try to stop it.

Whether they succeed or not, it's hard to predict the impact on Tesla and SpaceX. And even as a combined entity, the company will be significantly exposed to regulatory risk across businesses such as space launch, satellite-based connectivity, EVs, and robotaxis.

Putting aside the acquisition talks, Tesla's own business has somewhat attractive prospects. The company's work in the robotaxi industry looks promising. Tesla launched the Cybercab, a self-driving vehicle with no steering wheel, no pedals, and no mirrors, earlier this year. Provided it can scale its Cybercab fleet and capture a decent share of the market over the next five to 10 years, the robotaxi business could meaningfully contribute to the company's revenue and profits.

And Tesla does have an advantage over other robotaxi leaders. The company has a growing number of subscriptions to its Full Self-Driving software, which it offers to EV owners for $99 per month. A growing FSD ecosystem grants the company access to more data to train and improve its software. And as Tesla has pointed out, the FSD software is becoming one of the reasons people buy Teslas. That's great news for the company's future.

But one of Tesla's problems is valuation. At 157.3x forward earnings, the market has already priced in significant success from the company's robotaxi ambition. So, I'd be skeptical about buying Tesla's shares at current levels. I'd prefer a much lower price. The potential merger with SpaceX changes very little, in my view, and actually adds significantly more uncertainty to the mix.

The combined entity (if a merger does happen) will certainly be worth serious consideration, but investors with an average tolerance for risk and volatility shouldn't buy Tesla stock right now, whether or not they expect the company to merge with SpaceX.

Posted November 26, 2026 | Reviewed by Kaja Perina

All over the world, teachers play a crucial role in their pupils' learning experience. This is especially true for left-handed children, who, for a long time, have been forced to write with their right hand against their will in many countries. Thankfully, the awful practice of handedness relearning is now a thing of the past in the US and many other countries, and today, both left-handed and right-handed pupils are encouraged to write with their naturally dominant hand in school. This makes it important that teachers have some basic knowledge about handedness and also the ergonomic needs of left-handed kids in learning environments. As handedness typically is not a main focus in teacher education, it is, however, largely unknown what knowledge the average teacher has about handedness and the specific learning needs of left-handed children. Thus, more psychological research on this important topic is needed.

A new study entitled “Handedness literacy in teachers: Data from Greece, Serbia, and Belgium”, just published in the scientific journal Laterality, aimed at closing this gap in the psychological literature by focusing on teachers’ handedness literacy (Papadatou-Pastou and co-workers, 2026). Handedness literacy refers to a person’s general knowledge and understanding of handedness.

The study team, led by renowned handedness researcher Marietta Papadatou-Pastou, asked more than 800 prospective and in-service teachers to fill out a questionnaire with various questions about their knowledge of handedness and about themselves. The teachers came from three different European countries: Greece, Serbia, and Belgium.

The questionnaire the teachers had to fill out consisted of four main parts. The first part consisted of open-ended questions about left-handedness, such as “Do left-handed students differ from right-handed students?”. The teachers had to write answers to these questions. The second part of the questionnaire consisted of 18 questions quizzing the teachers about their knowledge of handedness. The third part of the questionnaire contained further questions about handedness, and the fourth part contained some questions about the teachers themselves, such as their own handedness and their age, among others.

The analysis of the data yielded several interesting results about teachers’ handedness literacy.

The analyses of the open-ended questions about handedness in the first part of the questionnaire showed that teachers generally had a supportive attitude towards left-handed pupils. The teachers wrote about different aspects of handedness, such as the underlying neurobiology or specific challenges left-handed pupils face, or their ergonomic and classroom needs. In general, they were in favor of letting children choose which hand they like to write with.

The analysis of the handedness literacy scores revealed that teachers knew some things about handedness, but also had quite a lot of knowledge gaps. As there were 18 questions and the scientists gave 1 point per correct question, the maximum score for handedness literacy was 18. Teachers from Serbia had an average score of 8.11 (45% correct answers). Teachers from Belgium were slightly better as they had an average score of 8.85 (49% correct answers), as were teachers from Greece, who had an average score of 8.90 (49% correct answers).

So, in all three countries, teachers had less than 50% correct answers- if there were a left-handedness test, they would probably fail! One thing that many teachers got wrong was that they thought that left-handed pupils were more intelligent than right-handed pupils. Also, many teachers thought that there were more left-handers in the population than the actual percentage (10.6%). Interestingly, left-handed teachers did not show higher handedness literacy than right-handed teachers.

Many of the teachers who participated in the study stated that they thought that handedness literacy in teachers needs to be improved. The results of the study show that this is actually true. This suggests that teacher education should include more scientific information on left-handedness, so that teachers can optimally educate left-handed kids.

Your Flight Takes 10 Minutes Longer Than It Used To—And That’s A Luxury Worth Paying For

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Flying takes longer than it used to – and, retirement of the Concorde notwithstanding, it’s not because planes aren’t as fast. There are more than twice as many commercial flights as there were at the time of airline deregulation, and air traffic control hasn’t been upgraded very much. There’s congested airspace and also congested airports. We also don’t build major new airports – the last one was Denver, 30 years ago. (Austin’s new 1999 airport was a converted military base, and there were others like that in the 90s.) By one estimate flights have gotten 8% longer. That means a two hour flight is 10 minutes longer than it used to be. And the single biggest factor is often tagged as strategic padding of schedules. Airlines are often chided for ‘schedule padding’ – claiming that flights take longer than they should. Often, this just reflects the reality of telling customers when they can expect to arrive – although there’s greater variance than there used to be, which isn’t great for having a gate available when you land early. Airlines do not like this! Though real airfares (even including fees) are down significantly since deregulation, longer flight times are expensive for staffing, fuel and efficient use of $50 – $100 million planes. And longer travel times historically disadvantaged an airline trying to sell tickets, because it meant their flights were displayed lower on the screen that travel agents and later passengers directly saw – which translates into fewer sales. A new study finds that airlines with more “padding” in their schedules tend to charge higher fares. That sounds like passengers paying more for a slower trip. But those extra minutes can also let United hold your plane with ConnectionSaver and get you home that night, and still arrive on time. It also means fewer delays and missed connections. So that ‘premium’ is actually a more valuable product to many consumers. I’ve argued for years that schedule padding often isn’t really padding. Sometimes an airline is finally admitting how long the trip takes. Customers can plan around an honest arrival time. There’s a new chapter by Embry-Riddle’s Jules Yimga, “The Price of Punctuality,” in The Economics of Airline Pricing. It combines 2019 flight time data with the government’s ticket price survey. It compares schedules against different percentiles of actual flight times, then examines how the resulting measures relate to fares. More padding is associated with higher fares, especially when the comparison uses the faster end of actual flight times. The relationship appears in both competitive and concentrated markets. The abstract itself allows that reliability could be a service customers value. (Interestingly, Yimga’s earlier research on schedule padding attributed higher prices primarily to higher costs rather than higher margins.) Even scheduling to the median arrival time means roughly half of trips take longer than published. It’s often better for a customer if an airline underpromises and overdelivers – or arrives ‘on time’ rather than late. Predictability matters when you’re scheduling connections or trying to arrive in time for a meeting. An airline that leaves little buffer is going to appear less reliable, so customers book earlier flights and longer layovers or even fly in the night before an important meeting. Then the buffer hasn’t disappeared, it just changes who’s scheduling it. That’s why I argued American’s investment in more realistic schedules could pay for itself. Avoiding rebooking, mishandled bags and aircraft and crews getting out of position can justify spending running an on-time schedule even before a customer chooses the airline again because the trip met expectations. American’s Dallas – Fort Worth overhaul that began in April combined longer scheduled flight times with a move from nine daily banks of flights to thirteen. Spreading flights into smaller waves reduced congestion that made some of the extra time unnecessary. One plane waiting for a gate can delay its passengers, its next departure, and other flights whose crews are stuck on board. Giving the operation room to recover can prevent that problem from cascading. In January, I covered American network planning chief Brian Znotins explaining that bookings for 40–54 minute connections at DFW were 4% below what its model predicted. Bookings for 55–84 minute connections were 8% above prediction. Customers have actually started choosing longer connections because they feel more reliable and less likely to leave them stranded. There's a fine line between a proxy and a counterfeit card, and Wizards is only interested in eradicating one of them from the game of Magic.

A wave of controversy hit Magic: The Gathering this week after Wizards of the Coast revealed it is taking legal action against a group of proxy card businesses, collectively referred to as 'PrintMTG Enterprise,' that produce and sell unofficial MTG cards directly to players on several marketplaces. The lawsuit is worth $28.5 million, and it includes strict conditions if Wizards succeeds, such as handing over all card-printing equipment used in the business.

The community was immediately up in arms over the announcement, as it felt like Wizards was taking a huge step forward in a war against proxy cards, but the reality is that your average proxy card is safe, and this is actually more about counterfeit cards. Is there a difference? Yes, and it could be a costly one for 'PrintMTG Enterprises.'

The difference between a counterfeit Magic card and a proxy is that a proxy card is clearly not a real Magic card, typically because it has a plain back or doesn't use official Magic artwork on the front. In most cases, proxy cards have a plain white back, so they can't be confused with an official Magic card.

Counterfeit cards are carbon copies of the original Magic design and are intended to deceive players who aren't all that familiar with the minor details that make a Magic card legit. This can also include using the same paper stock as official Magic cards to make them feel authentic in hand, as well as any details meant to fool the eye.

It's entirely fair for Wizards of the Coast to target any seller looking to reproduce Magic cards and sell them in a condition that could be used to defraud others, and this lawsuit shouldn't be confused with Wizards taking a stance against proxy makers or users.

One of the big debates that arose from this lawsuit is why now? It's fair to say that counterfeit Magic cards have been around for a long time, so why is now the time for Wizards to take a stance and look to get them off the market?

The reality is that we'll never know why, but it's a positive move for the secondary market regardless. Proxy cards, when advertised and produced correctly, aren't mistaken for regular Magic cards, and those who buy them are 100% okay with that.

Counterfeit cards become a problem when people look on typically trusted marketplaces for an official card but end up buying a counterfeit version without knowing it, because the seller is trying to make a quick buck.

This isn't to say that the companies producing these counterfeit cards do so with the intent of deceiving players in online transactions, but that's what is happening, and it needs to stop.

I've only ever purchased one counterfeit card in my time playing Magic, and it was a copy of Tibalt's Trickery. It wasn't a very expensive card, but the copy I received was clearly fake, not to the eye, but to the touch. I reported the seller, who had ghosted me after the transaction, and I got my money back, but I wonder how many players that seller managed to fool.

On the proxy front, I only own one set of proxy cards: the Goblin Storm Secret Lair Commander deck. This was perfect, because I planned to use the deck still at my local store, and with my Commander pod, I wanted to be able to say "hey, this is a proxy, does anyone have a problem with that?" as the code of honor among most groups is that, as long as you admit to what it is, there's typically no issue playing with it.

Magic is a very expensive game to get into in most formats, so proxies let you avoid extortionate secondary market prices (especially on land for Commander right now) and still play with limited-availability stuff like Goblin Storm.

Proxies are great, and they should continue to be allowed as long as certain rules are followed. I have no issue with Wizards setting a clear boundary for what a counterfeit card is and why it isn't good for the game.

The Top 10 Open World Games

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

There was a time when video games were largely linear affairs. You either moved up and down or from left to right in enclosed spaces. As technology evolved, so did the scope of digital worlds. Now players are regularly treated to expansive environments filled with quests, interesting characters, and horizons that stretch further with every console generation. These experiences consume dozens if not hundreds of hours and knowing which titles are worth the time investment has become crucial. To that end, we’ve assembled a list of 10 great modern open-world games available today to help make what can be a paralyzing decision a little easier.

Note that these are listed in release date order and don’t necessarily represent the best games of all time. Rather, this will be an ever-changing list of quality titles to arrive in recent years that you should go out of your way to get lost in.

Grand Theft Auto is arguably the blueprint for open worlds in video games, and its sixth entry is the series' peak. In a universe steeped in satire and cynicism, protagonists Jason and Lucia are instantly likable figures worth rooting for. Their love story is absolutely worth your time, but even if you made the decision to ignore it, you'd be overwhelmed by Leonida's exceedingly vast playground, ripe for exploration. When we say GTAVI's scope is staggering, we are partially talking about the literal size of the map, but we're mainly talking about its density; every corner is meticulously crafted with people to meet and places to rob. It's one of the most believable spaces in gaming history, bolstered by incredible graphics and populated with characters only a little more zany than you'd encounter in its real-life equivalent of Florida. Its dozens of cars feel great to drive, and its dozens of guns are a thrill to shoot, but if you decide to get really into birdwatching instead, that's fine by us. Grand Theft Auto VI is one of the best games of this generation – it's no surprise it made its way onto this top 10 list. | Our review

In an overly simplistic way, Ghost of Yōtei basically took everything that worked about Sucker Punch’s 2020 open world action game, Ghost of Tsushima, and improved it. Exploration, combat, visuals, and narrative are all major steps up from the previous game, which was already fantastic. Atsu’s journey of revenge, fighting and exploring her way through Ezo, is an always-compelling experience, not because it radically re-addresses or changes the mechanics that made Tsushima so great, but rather because they are carefully iterated on and improved in sometimes subtle ways. And as an open world game, it constantly excels at giving the player worthwhile distractions that all appear to the player naturally. You’re not bouncing between points on an overworld map – you’re literally following the wind where it takes you and always ending up somewhere amazing doing something incredible. | Our review

Kingdom Come: Deliverance II is one of the highest-rated open worlds on this list, and for good reason. If Grand Theft Auto VI is the best modern-day open world, and Cyberpunk 2077 the best future-set open-world game, then KCDII is easily the best medieval open-world game in some time. Eschewing the more fantastical likes of the medieval open-world format – which The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt excellently covers on this list – KCDII does all it can to remain grounded and true to the 15th Century Kingdom of Bohemia land it’s set in. You follow Henry, whom you’ll craft through your decisions and upgrade choices, on a journey of retribution, rebellion, and reflection, and you’ll meet an unforgettable cast of characters along the way. They are the grounding elements to an otherwise wide-open adventure that lets you craft a narrative only you can craft. However you invest your time in this massive, sprawling medieval landscape will be reflected in the Henry you play as and how the world responds around you – there’s no shortage of mischief and chivalry to get up to, and across its dozens of hours, you’ll be taken through one of the most rewarding open games you can play today.  | Our Review

FromSoftware proves its punishing yet satisfying style of action RPG absolutely works in an open-world format. Brimming with mystery and danger, Elden Ring allows players to make their own way, providing little guidance or hand-holding, making every discovery feel like a genuinely exciting and well-earned reward. Unlike traditional Souls games, slamming against a difficult boss isn’t the end of the line. You can simply explore elsewhere and fall down the rabbit holes of breaching an impregnable fortress, surviving a treacherous swamp, or taking down dozens upon dozens of other imposing enemies. Quality-of-life additions such as free fast travel and friendlier checkpointing before boss fights alleviate some stress, further encouraging you to hop aboard your spectral mount and uncover every secret of this incredible world. | Our Review

The greatest trick the Forza Horizon series pulls is fooling players into liking racing games by disguising them as open-world RPGs. The latest entry, Horizon 5, serves as the pinnacle of that Trojan Horse-style design. Set in a stunningly beautiful rendition of Mexico, players sit behind hundreds of vehicles to compete in a seemingly endless number of wild races. Whether you're speeding against other cars or a 747 airplane, the sheer variety of events and an over-the-top tone make Horizon more lighthearted compared to most pure racing sims. In addition to traditional races, you can enjoy Horizon 5's entertaining take on battle royale, a zombie-infection mode, and absurd story-driven scenarios (like driving a parade float). A staggering collection of unlockable cars, purchasable homes to store said vehicles, plus cosmetics and goofy dances for your avatar create powerful incentives to score the winner's purse. Best-in-class driving mechanics solidify Horizon 5's first-place standing | Our Review

Though Cyberpunk 2077’s launch was rough (and that’s putting it lightly), developer CD Projekt Red successfully righted the ship and then some. By fixing launch issues and woes, continuing to add meaningful (and free) content like new quests, Cyberpunk Edgerunners items, and more, the game literally got better each year. The team closed out the game with the incredible Phantom Liberty expansion, which adds a heaping dose of Idris Elba and spy-thriller action to Night City, ensuring any V who steps into this dystopian future has hours upon hours of open-world fun to enjoy. Speaking of Night City, it’s one of the most fully realized metropolitan playgrounds on this list. Police react to your actions in real time, as do citizens; futuristic cars and motorbikes await you to live out your Akira slide dreams; and countless weapons and upgrades will help you become the greatest cybernetic warrior gangster this side of the Pacifica. Between side content, the golden path, and the unlisted-but-worth-engaging-in hidden activities spread throughout Night City, you’d be hard-pressed to find a better dystopian open-world shooter than Cyberpunk 2077. | Our Review

Few studios craft worlds that feel as lifelike as Rockstar Games, and Red Dead Redemption 2 might be their crowning achievement. Every inch of this wild west drips with immaculate detail and feels like you’ve stepped into a time portal to the 1800s. Survival elements such as cooking, camping, and hunting allow players to role-play as a trail-worn gunslinger effectively – and it’s more fun than you’d expect. Emergent side missions mean a fascinating story or memorable stranger can pop up at a moment’s notice, and there’s plenty of diversity when it comes to the strange sights you’ll stumble upon. Tack on an incredible story led by a deeply compelling protagonist in outlaw Arthur Morgan, and Red Dead Redemption 2 rivals not only the greatest games but the classic westerns that inspired it. | Our Review

What makes Breath of the Wild magical is that it unclutters years of triple-A open-world design. Instead of littering Hyrule with icons telling you where to go, it allows players to forge their own paths. It only has one mandatory mission: destroy Ganon. Since you can do that right away, everything else is optional. Even the largest games force you to adhere to the path of a main linear narrative. In BOTW, one player’s approach to slaying Ganon can be totally different from another’s. Do you complete the four primary dungeons? Seek out the Master Sword? Build up your hearts? The beauty of it all: there’s no wrong answer.

Link can climb any surface, meaning that, provided you have the stamina, you can reach the summit of any mountain and concoct numerous paths to your destination. Hyrule also operates on realistic physics and principles for players to experiment with (i.e., all metal objects conduct electricity). It boasts hundreds of shrines with concise, entertaining puzzles and numerous mysteries, be they majestic dragons or an island that’s more than meets the eye. Breath of the Wild rocks for promoting improvisation and creativity more than anything. Its legacy has been cemented thanks to a growing number of titles borrowing from its playbook, such as Genshin Impact and Immortals Fenyx Rising. | Our Review

The Witcher 3’s gritty fantasy world is as beautiful as it is deadly. We would never want to actually live in it, though, due to its densely packed population of supernatural monsters, tyrannical rulers, and (typically) backstabbing citizens. The Witcher 3’s Continent keeps you on your toes thanks to the impact of its choice-driven gameplay. Small choices often come back around hours later in more significant ways than you expect, making every shifty thief, sultry mage, or cowardly would-be-knight worth engaging. With a compelling main storyline, dozens of meaningful sidequests, and boatloads of treasures, The Witcher 3, as well as its phenomenal expansions, succeeds in making its gargantuan setting worth uncovering. | Our Review

Every seven years, European leaders gather in Brussels for what is arguably the most consequential negotiation cycle in the European Union. As German Chancellor Friedrich Merz put it upon arrival, it is about "the future of the European Union."

This is not a typical EU summit. The leaders of the 27 member states are confronting a question that will shape all angles of EU politics for years to come: How much money should the EU have, who should pay for it, and what should it be spent on?

It is the essence of EU summit diplomacy: a painstaking exercise in give-and-take, bargaining and compromise.

The European Commission laid down the opening bid over the summer. The proposal was ambitious. Brussels wants an EU budget that reflects a rapidly-changing world: more spending on defence, innovation, research, digital technologies and competitiveness. At the same time, that inevitably means pressure on some of the traditional pillars of EU spending, particularly agricultural subsidies and parts of the bloc's regional development programmes.

Since then, every member has arrived with a shopping list of demands.

Governments have pushed back against cuts, demanded new priorities, sought exemptions and fought for national interests. There have though also been others who are pushing FOR a reduction in spending, including Merz.

The original proposal from Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has already undergone numerous adjustments as negotiators attempted to accommodate competing demands.

The result is an extraordinarily complex puzzle.

At the centre of it all stands European Council President Antonio Costa. He will be shuttling between bilateral meetings with leaders, gather small groups behind closed doors and attempt to construct a package that gives everybody something they can take home and present as a victory.

For Costa, the stakes are high. Delivering an agreement would be a major test of his authority and reputation as the EU's chief broker. Especially as he was not made top negotiator for the EU in the peace talks on Russia's war in Ukraine. Diplomats expressed confidence in his fairness and negotiating skills before the summit, viewing him as someone capable of building trust among leaders who have differing priorities.

Yet nobody in Brussels expects an easy breakthrough. Many officials acknowledge that the pressure on member states may not yet be intense enough to force the final concessions required for a deal. Leaders are still defending red lines rather than crossing them.

So, this summit will highly likely end without a grand agreement. Leaders will probably decide to take stock of the latest negotiating proposals and return home to assess them in detail. There will be a new — and perhaps final — round on the next summit in December, a few days before Christmas.

As leaders grapple with long-term budgets, across Europe, young people are rising up. They're demanding better jobs, more funding for education and lower rents. The problems they are protesting are complex and often specific to their home country.

8 Smartest Characters In The Lord Of The Rings Movies, Ranked By Intelligence

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

In The Lord of the Rings, J.R.R. Tolkien presents what we would call "intelligence" in quite a nuanced way. It's important to realize Tolkien was no fan of the modern idea of "intelligence." As he wrote in one letter, "I have never yet heard of an 'intelligence' test that was not specially suited to select nitwits (bright perky little nitwits possibly, but not always even so). But what would you: they are invented by nitwits (or some by impostors)."

Tolkien prized carefulness of thought, not quick-thinking. He believed in the value of knowledge, but that knowledge had to be digested and interpreted. In truth, I don't think Tolkien actually believed in the pursuit of intelligence at all; rather, as a Catholic, he honored something that his faith would probably call "wisdom." All that makes it fascinating to look at The Lord of the Rings through the lens of intelligence, though, because it helps us to see the world as Tolkien did—and to learn from him.

Grima Wormtongue is one of Tolkien's most underestimated characters, in large part because Peter Jackson's movies diminish Wormtongue quite significantly. Jackson presents Wormtongue more as a channel of Saruman's power than a mastermind in his own right. When Gandalf frees Théoden from Wormtongue's influence, there's a magical backlash that knocks Saruman off his feet. But that idea is entirely absent in the books themselves.

In the movies, you see hints of Wormtongue's true nature. He's ruthless, cunning, a manipulative mastermind who can figure out any way to spin a crisis to his advantage. Grima Wormtongue is the consummate modern politician, the spin doctor if you like. It's not a coincidence that Tolkien portrays him as the villain whose intelligence—deployed so selfishly—almost brings down an entire kingdom. With Wormtongue, we immediately see that intelligence is a very dangerous thing if you're lacking in character.

It's easy to criticize Denethor. A ruler of Gondor, he is slow to respond to Gandalf's appeals, simply because his intelligence is stronger than the strength of his convictions. He's right in his estimation of Mordor's forces, and he correctly understands the strategic importance of Minas Tirith. He's something of a political mastermind in his own right, understanding the relationships between different countries. I like to think of the Bismarck of Lord of the Rings.

But again, notice Denethor's weaknesses. It's possible he was a little too intelligent; he believes he could filter the facts, and thus discounted the possibility he was being manipulated. Emotions increasingly compromised his judgment—and, again, he's unaware of it. Tolkien's characters are moral ones, and Denethor is a warning to the world that intelligence without morality and conviction can and will lead you astray. Especially if others are there to manipulate you.

It would be so easy to miss Smaug on this list. We tend to focus on Smaug as one of The Lord of the Rings' most powerful villains, but the Great Dragon displays a great deal of what we would call intelligence. He's surprisingly strong when it comes to understanding and interpreting others, as seen in the riddles he sets. Those demonstrate a remarkable depth of strategic thinking, deductive reasoning, and perception.

Tolkien reveals Smaug's intelligence in so many subtle ways. His manner of speaking is more complex than other characters, showing an intuitive grasp of metaphor and demonstrating quite sophisticated rhetorical techniques. He swiftly identifies Bilbo's psychological weaknesses, manipulating them with skill. But again, note that this interpersonal intelligence is flawed because of Smaug's character. The Great Dragon isn't necessarily a warning against the excess of power, but rather of trickery and cunning.

As physically powerful as the elves may be in The Lord of the Rings, they are also notable for their intelligence and wisdom (although the latter is often presented as flawed, compromised by excessive nostalgia). Elrond is thousands of years old, and he's been part of so many critical events on Middle-earth; you can get a sense of how important he is in The Rings of Power, where a younger Elrond is a key character in a story loosely based on Tolkien's notes.

Importantly, though, Elrond's historical knowledge informs his analytical reasoning. He's able to place the present danger in its historical context, and understand both the risks and the best strategies. Remarkably, he is willing to listen to different perspectives; he even sets aside the traditional conflict between Dwarves and Elves, allowing every member of the Fellowship a voice. But again, note that he's a little too conservative, a little too cautious. He has to be heeded, but sometimes overruled.

I suspect some readers will disagree with my placing Galadriel above Elrond. But the comparison between the two is striking; like Elrond, she has accumulated a vast amount of knowledge and experience, beyond anything mortals can ever imagine. What matters, though, is that Galadriel demonstrates what we would now call emotional intelligence. She sees into the souls of others, understanding their characters and motivations.

She is also strikingly humble. Notice Galadriel's reaction when Frodo offers her the One Ring; she knows exactly what it would do to her, and she wants nothing to do with it. Galadriel's perception is relatively untouched by ego, meaning she moves beyond mere intelligence. She's the first character on this list to truly demonstrate wisdom.

In pure intellectual ability, Saruman is almost unparalleled in The Lord of the Rings. It helps that, like the Elves, he draws on a vast reservoir of experience; but his experience is even less limited than theirs because wizards are essentially angels in Tolkien's mythology. Saruman is a scholar and a gifted researcher, an academic who asks the questions no one else dares ask. He notices the sweeping patterns of history, the decline of magic, and the potential of industrialization.

But, of course, Saruman is limited because of his own ego. He sees patterns, and believes he can master them. He looks on others with disdain, viewing them as a means to the end he seeks to accomplish. What's more, because the pattern of progress is visible, he believes he should master it and determine it. Saruman's intelligence is second to his ambition, demonstrating once again that character comes first in The Lord of the Rings.

Think of Gandalf and Saruman as two sides of the same coin. Gandalf's natural intelligence is roughly equal to Saruman's, who perhaps has the edge on him in terms of overall analytical ability. But with Gandalf, intelligence is tempered both by conviction and humility. Gandalf recognizes the patterns of history, but refuses to ally with things he believes are wrong. His morality gives him a different starting point, if you like, meaning the outcome is very different.

Time and again, Gandalf demonstrates that his intelligence is subordinate to his humility. According to Tolkien, Gandalf was always more powerful than Saruman; he just didn't desire fear or reverence, and thus never capitalized on it. The Lord of the Rings carefully tests Gandalf's humility. By passing these tests, ultimately setting aside the egotistical desire to control and risking everything by sacrificing his own life, Gandalf proved worthy to become the White. Gandalf's story is the triumph of character over intelligence.

And that, of course, brings me to the final entry in this list: Sauron himself, easily the most intelligent being on Middle-earth, and a cautionary tale from Tolkien's perspective. The Rings of Power has helped demonstrate this, revealing the range of Sauron's accomplishments; he is a craftsman and artisan as well as a military strategist, a would-be creator whose intelligence is turned towards refashioning the entire world into his own image.

If Gandalf's story is the triumph of character over intelligence, then Sauron's evil is the inverse. He is far more intelligent than anyone else in this story, a skilled strategist who is capable of weaponizing the most sophisticated abstract principles and turning them to his own advantage. But always to his own advantage, because Sauron is an egotist at heart. He failed the tests Gandalf passed, and became the greatest monster of The Lord of the Rings.

5 Thanksgiving Hosting Tips That’ll Make Your Dinner Parties Feel More Special Any Time of Year

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Experts say the secret is using what you love—and skipping what you don’t.

For many households, Thanksgiving is the main hosting event they look forward to all year. But what is it about this holiday that makes it such an annual highlight, and how can you emulate that same essence more than just once a year?

We reached out to entertaining experts to learn which Thanksgiving hosting tips you can apply to year-round gatherings. These are the lessons that will make even your most laid-back get-togethers all the more memorable.

According to Whitney Ray, owner of Wyeth Ray Interiors, one reason that hosting Thanksgiving feels so special is that it's the one time of year most hosts actually break out their nice tableware. However, she believes you should be using these beautiful and sentimental pieces more often.

"So many people wait to use their best serving and dinnerware pieces at holidays like Thanksgiving, but I say those beautiful dishes should be used all year long," she says. "A Tuesday night dinner with a few friends doesn’t need to be formal, but mixing your nice china with your everyday pieces elevates a normal night into something more."

Hovik Harutyunyan is an event designer, etiquette specialist, and the founder of Harutyunyan Events. He believes that one key logistical trick a host can apply to any dinner or party they host is curating a menu full of familiar foods that are easy to prepare ahead.

"Thanksgiving menus work so well because the recipes are familiar as well as tried-and-true," he says. "For example, let's talk about the pies. Thanksgiving dessert is so easy because most of us opt for pumpkin and pecan pie. We know the dessert, and it's extremely easy to make in advance."

He also notes that it's best not to try out complicated, brand-new recipes for a big hosting event. You should opt for an elevated menu that is easy to scale and distribute across a large number of guests.

Thanksgiving is one meal where guests love contributing by bringing a side dish or dessert. While not every gathering needs to have a potluck element, there are other ways you can lighten your cooking load.

Christina Samatas and Renee DiSanto are principals of Park & Oak, and DiSanto is also the owner of Marcel’s Culinary Experience, a culinary community and cooking school. Both of them fully endorse taking a few shortcuts when it comes to hosting any big dinner.

"Not every detail needs to be homemade to feel special," DiSanto says. "There are many ways to save you a few steps, such as using pre-cut veggies or incorporating a few prepared appetizers or sides. Spend more time on the details that matter the most to you, rather than the little things."

For any event you hold, it's pretty much inevitable that guests always end up in the kitchen. "Whether you invite guests in or not, Thanksgiving shows us the kitchen is where people naturally congregate before a meal, whether they are wanting to help or simply catch up before dinner is served," Ray says.

For this reason, she says you should always plan to entertain in the kitchen at any gathering. Consider adding small bites and a drinks station on your kitchen counter or island, and try to clean as you go while making the meal.

Thanksgiving is a celebration of gratitude, and Harutyunyan believes you should apply the same spirit to any party. Anytime you gather with loved ones is an opportunity to give them a special experience.

"Thanksgiving, and the holidays in general, is one of the most magical times of the year," he says. "As stressful and chaotic as they may be, we love to gather with our loved ones to enjoy each other's company. We share stories, memories, and gratitude. The holiday spirit takes over all of us, which is what drives us to create such fabulous dinner spreads, decorate the home, and create an intentional experience for those on the guest list."

The developer's magnum opus offers an immersive and expansive world that will take us many years to fully decipher, so I can already imagine how numerous ecosystems, from video games to content creation, will be completely permeated until, perhaps, the release of the seventh installment.

However, far from wanting to theorize about future adventures of the IP, the priority is to appreciate the one we already have in our hands and savor it slowly, since that's what the game itself aims for by offering a story, mechanics, and context that evolve at a much more leisurely pace.

Therefore, in order to play fortune teller and anticipate how long we will be hanging around Leonida, I invite you to read this list of ten reasons you will be playing GTA 6 for more than a decade.

Analyzing the evolution of video games, especially considering their affinity with technology, is an activity that involves intertemporal comparisons, and GTA 6 has established itself as the generational benchmark we look to when we want to see if the industry has moved forward.

Titles like The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, BioShock, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, and The Last of Us Part II have stood for years as monoliths of their respective genres and platforms, garnering enduring attention that regularly draws us back to reaffirm their influence.

Thus, considering the technical, graphical, and design marvel that is Rockstar's title, particularly within the context of open worlds, it will be impossible for it to escape associations with future representatives of the genre, who will undoubtedly seek to learn from it.

Although comparisons are generally odious, it's also true that they constitute a timely method to contrast the evolution, or involution, of a medium, so I can already see many scholars of this art revisiting its confines to diagnose its eventual state.

While I acknowledge the omnipresence of the internet and our hyper-connected state has made the task of hunting for secrets virtually impossible, I know I'm not the only one who has deliberately avoided all discussion about the game in order to discover it for myself.

By choosing not to use guides, watch gameplay videos, or even compare opinions, the game's lifespan is instantly extended due to its immeasurable size and content, much of which is hidden behind hundreds of hours of spontaneous and meticulous exploration.

You may finish the campaign and feel satisfied with what you've experienced, but for a game that has taken so long to arrive and generated so much anticipation, I'm sure that a large portion of its player base will continue playing until there's no dialogue, NPC, mission, weapon, or random event left undiscovered.

These Rare Pokémon Cards Are No Longer Eligible for PSA Grading

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

English-language Mega Hyper Rare cards have a manufacturing issue

Mega Evolution returned to the Pokémon Trading Card Game with the launch of the Mega Evolution series in 2025. In this expansion, collectors can find extremely rare Mega Evolution Pokémon EX cards known as Mega Hyper Rare cards in English and Mega Ultra Rare cards in Japanese. These holographic secret cards show alternative artwork of Pokémon's Mega Evolutions on a beautiful gold-etched display.

Given how rare these cards are, most collectors lucky enough to find them quickly sent them to PSA for grading. Unfortunately, in many cases, this will no longer be possible. On Nov. 25, 2026, the PSA announced that it has stopped grading English-language Mega Hyper Rare Pokémon cards effective immediately.

In an official blog post, PSA explains that some English-language Mega Hyper Rare Pokémon cards are defective, and therefore, none can be accurately graded. Card grading experts "identified a manufacturing issue that can cause the gold foil on the front of these cards to deteriorate over time, leaving visible patches or fading." While the severity of the defect varies from card to card, it's impossible to determine whether a card will eventually be affected by it and deteriorate. The issue can arise even if the card is properly stored and encapsulated.

"A card can begin deteriorating after it’s graded, even with no visible signs at first, so we can’t assign a grade that will reliably hold. Continuing to grade these cards wouldn’t meet the standard of accuracy and consistency that collectors rely on us for."

Grades already assigned to English-language Mega Hyper Rare cards by PSA will not be revoked and can be submitted for Review and Reholder service, allowing collectors to reevaluate their condition. Cards in transit or submitted for grading will be returned ungraded, and PSA will waive the grading service fee as compensation.

On the bright side, PSA states that Japanese-language Mega Ultra Rare Pokémon cards do not appear to be defective, as they were printed at a facility that uses different techniques, and can be submitted for grading for the time being.

It is currently unknown how this will affect card prices. It could go either way. The price of defective cards might rise before the manufacturing error is fixed, or it could fall sharply, as collectors begin their search for a pristine Japanese MUR card or look for an already graded English-language card instead.
