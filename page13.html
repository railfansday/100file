Win at Everton would put Coventry on right side of history in survival fight

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

None of the 21 sides who had 12 points after 10 matches, over the past decade, were relegated from the Premier League

If Coventry win at Everton on Friday night they will take an enormous stride towards maintaining their Premier League status. That might seem a huge statement with 28 games remaining, but victory would put Frank Lampard’s side on the right side of recent history. For now, they stand on a precipice.

Coventry have taken nine points from nine league matches. Eight teams in the previous 10 seasons were in the same position – seven stayed up. The exception, Leicester in 2024-25, were newly promoted and have tumbled downwards ever since.

There were 21 clubs in the last decade with 12 points after 10 matches. None of them went down and eight finished in the top half. Lampard’s first return to a former club this season suddenly looks remarkably important. Even if a win cannot guarantee Coventry’s top-flight survival, they would occupy what has been a strong position historically.

Lampard has a similar managerial record to former teammate (and new West Ham manager) Scott Parker, a midfielder alongside whom he made 27 appearances for Chelsea. Excellent in the second tier - Lampard reached the playoffs at worst in each of his three Championship campaigns - he has never fully convinced in the Premier League. Given he took Chelsea to fourth place and the FA Cup final in 2019-20, perhaps that is unfair. Equally, they had finished third the year before and were champions in two of the four campaigns prior to that.

Though he kept Everton up in 2022, Lampard won only nine of his 38 league matches with the club. No Everton manager with at least as many games has a lower win percentage in all competitions, with that post followed by a caretaker spell back at Chelsea in which he won only once in 11 matches.

Regardless of Lampard’s ability, what can we reasonably expect from Coventry? Capology estimate the club have the lowest wage bill in the division, while Transfermarkt rate their squad as having the third-lowest market value. This challenging reality was reflected in pre-season predictions. Opta gave Coventry a better chance of staying up than their fellow promoted sides, Hull and Ipswich. The former have delivered the weakest performance of the three and are now deemed the most likely of the trio to go back down. They have faced all the current top three, in fairness, while Hull haven’t played any of them and Ipswich have only tackled one.

Coventry remain third-favourites for the drop, as they were in pre-season. It is just the teams below them that have changed, with Sunderland and Crystal Palace now propping up the forecast. With the possibility of Manchester City facing a points deduction, third bottom might be enough to remain in the Premier League.

That issue notwithstanding, Coventry’s underlying performance needs to improve. Not that anyone would have cared when they scored three times to beat Sunderland last weekend when only recording 0.69 in expected goals. Newcastle had the better of the chances when losing 4-1 at the Coventry Building Society Arena, too. Their match against Hull is the only one this season in which they were on the right side of the xG.

Coventry led the Championship for expected goal difference last season. Lampard’s Chelsea improved on this metric in 2019-20 from the season before and were only 0.6 behind champions Liverpool. The 48-year-old has shown he can lead teams to strong statistical performance, though Coventry are clearly far more of an underdog this season than Lampard’s sides were in his strongest campaigns.

Everton did not beat any of the top seven at the Hill Dickinson Stadium last season and lost 2-0 to Chelsea in their previous home league match. They have generally performed well against struggling sides, though. Their only home defeats to bottom-half sides last season were against Newcastle and Tottenham, two clubs who were in the Champions League.

Victory on Friday is a tall order for Coventry, but if Lampard can win at Everton for the first time in his managerial career, his side will be well set for the task of staying up.

Tesla Is Killing The Cybertruck To Make Room For More Robotaxis

The controversial Tesla Cybertruck electric pickup will be retired next year, CEO Elon Musk confirmed on X. The angular truck went on sale at the end of 2023 and instantly became a social statement—for better or worse.

Production of the Cybertruck will start winding down next summer at the company’s Texas Gigafactory, and the extra floor space will be used to build more Cybercabs.

The unpainted truck had a short burst in popularity at the beginning of its production run, but interest fizzled, and sales never reached the potential that Musk and Tesla had envisioned back in 2019, when the vehicle was first presented as a concept.

In this year’s third quarter, the Cybertruck amassed 4,323 sales, according to data from Cox Automotive. From January through September, sales totaled 11,586 units. Tesla doesn’t break out individual sales figures for the Cybertruck, but industry estimates put the vehicle’s overall deliveries at roughly 70,000.

That’s a far cry from the hundreds of thousands of sales recorded every quarter by the Model 3 and Model Y. It’s also another reminder that electric pickups haven’t quite taken off as their manufacturers had hoped, and Tesla is now taking the fall, just like Ford did with the F-150 Lightning, which went the way of the dodo last year after lackluster sales and ballooning costs.

There’s no denying that you either love or hate the Cybertruck, just like there’s no denying that Tesla dared to do things like no other car manufacturer before it. Sure, some might argue that the angular EV looks like an unpolished fridge, but it brought steer-by-wire and a 48-volt low-voltage architecture to the masses. Whether or not they’ll be adopted by other companies is another story, though.

At roughly the same time as Elon Musk’s announcement that Cybertruck production would wind down in the middle of 2027, Tesla stopped taking orders for the entry-level Dual-Motor All-Wheel Drive version of the model, leaving just the Premium and Cyberbeast trims available to order on the automaker’s website.

Additionally, the price of the remaining models has been increased. The Premium AWD model now starts at $92,490, up from $89,990, while the top Cyberbeast now costs $102,490, up from $99,990. The most recent price bumps come after a similar move in August.

Last month I had to merge two exports from our billing system into one import file. The first export had 31,207 customer email addresses, the second had 17,706, and a few thousand lines overlapped. The CRM import endpoint rejects the whole file if it contains a single repeated email, so deduplication had to happen before upload.

There was a second constraint that tripped me up: row order drove import priority. Whichever line appeared first had to stay. So sort -u, the command half of my brain reached for, was off the table from the start. Here are three approaches that do work, tested on Ubuntu 22.04 with Python 3.11.1 and GNU awk 5.1.1.

The obvious one-liner is list(set(lines)). I've written it myself under deadline pressure. Don't. A set iterates in arbitrary hash order, so the output is a shuffled file, and the CRM import would run in whatever order the hash table happened to land on.

What you want is a seen set plus a counter:

Reading line by line means the file never sits in memory as one blob. The seen set held about 45,000 short strings, roughly 6 MB on my machine. I've pushed a 2 GB access log through the same shape of script by printing to stdout and redirecting; it took a few minutes but finished. Membership checks against a set are O(1) on average, which is why this stays fast as the file grows. The same pattern exists in JavaScript as a Set, so porting the script is a ten-minute job if your pipeline is Node-based.

When I just need the result and don't want to write a file, GNU awk does it in a single command:

$0 is the entire line. The expression seen[$0]++ returns 0 the first time a line shows up (falsy, so awk prints it) and counts upward after that. First occurrence wins, original order preserved. Same semantics as the Python script. The gawk manual has a whole chapter on how arrays and their uninitialized elements behave, worth a read if this idiom feels too magical.

One honest caveat: awk accumulates every line it has seen in memory, same as the Python set. For a 40 GB file on a 4 GB box neither will end well, and you'd reach for sort with a stable flag or a disk-based approach. For anything under a few hundred megabytes it's fine.

Sometimes the input arrives through Slack as a pasted list of a few thousand lines, from a teammate on Windows who has no intention of opening a terminal. For those, I use a free online duplicate line remover. Paste, click, copy the result back out.

The second export came out of a Windows service. Every line ended in \r\n, and my rstrip("\n") left the carriage return in place. On screen the lines looked identical; in memory they weren't:

First run reported dropped 3203. After I changed the strip to line.rstrip("\r\n"), the same file reported dropped 3847. Those extra 644 "duplicates" were the same addresses, invisible until the repr showed the \r.

The export was saved as "UTF-8 with BOM", which many spreadsheet tools do by default. The very first line carries a three-byte prefix that repr makes easy to spot:

'\ufeffalice@example.com' never equals 'alice@example.com', so if that address appears again later, it survives as a duplicate. Opening the file with encoding="utf-8-sig" strips the BOM on read and the problem disappears. I now check the first byte of any vendor CSV before writing the script.

Reading a 2 km laser rangefinder module over UART with Python (LR2000E2, 55 AA protocol)

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A laser rangefinder module is one of the easiest long-range sensors to wire up and one of the easiest to misread. The wiring is four wires. The mistakes happen in the protocol: a frame header you skip, a checksum you do not verify, a unit you assume. This post walks through a small, complete Python reader for the ERDI LR2000E2, a 905 nm UART laser rangefinder module with a published range of 4 to 2,000 m, and shows where the traps are.

Everything below comes from the LR2000E2 user manual (v1.2, public edition 2026-09-06) and the product page at https://erdilrf.com/models/lr2000e2/. Data checked on 2026-11-06.

Two numbers matter for software. The rate is adaptive, so you should not build a 10 Hz control loop around it; treat it as a slow, long-reach sensor. And the "valid measurement rate" in the manual is 98 % or better up to 400 m and 80 % or better from 400 to 2,000 m, which means at long range one reading in five can legitimately be a failure. Your code has to handle failed readings as a normal case, not an exception.

Two practical notes. First, the manual states the 3.3–5 V supply range but does not separately specify the logic level of TXD and RXD. If you power the module at 5 V and talk to a 3.3 V-only microcontroller pin, measure TXD with a scope or meter first, or ask the manufacturer for the model-specific statement. A USB-serial adapter that supports 3.3 V levels is the safe starting point. Second, pin 6 is an enable input: the module expects it high to operate, so tie it according to the manual before wondering why nothing answers.

The inrush current is below 400 mA, so use a supply that can deliver it without sagging. A weak USB hub that browns out at power-on will look like a protocol problem.

Frames are 8 bytes. A command is 55 AA D1 D2 D3 D4 , where the checksum is the low byte of the sum of the function code and the four data bytes. The reply is also 8 bytes: 55 AA STA FF DIS_H DIS_L . STA = 1 means success; any other value means the measurement failed. The distance is DIS_H * 256 + DIS_L, and the manual states that all results are output as the actual value × 10, so you divide by 10 to get metres.

You can check the checksum column by hand: 0x88 + 4 × 0xFF = 0x484, low byte 0x84.

sync() instead of ser.read(8). Reading a fixed 8 bytes works until the first dropped byte, after which every frame is misaligned and you decode garbage forever. Searching for 55 AA first costs nothing and removes that failure class. Note the small gap: a data byte pair of 55 AA inside a payload would false-trigger, which is why the strict checksum option exists.

strict defaults to off. The manual text describes the reply checksum as the sum from the first header byte to DATA4. Enable strict=True on your bench first and log what you see; if your unit's replies agree with the formula, leave it on in production. If they do not, you have found a document or firmware difference worth reporting to the supplier before you design anything around it.

Failed readings are normal. ok=False is returned for a bad frame and for STA != 1. Keep the last good value with a timestamp and a staleness limit instead of passing None downstream.

Units. Dividing by 10 is specific to this model family. Other modules from other vendors, and even other ERDI families, report millimetres or centimetres. Never copy a divisor from a sibling model; take it from that model's manual.

You do not need a 2 km line of sight to test the software. Point the module at a wall at a known distance such as 5 m or 20 m, measure with a tape, and log 100 single shots. Plot the histogram. You are checking three things: the mean against the tape, the spread against the ±1 m accuracy band, and the fraction of STA != 1 readings. For targets that are well inside the range, the failure fraction should be very small. If it is not, check supply sag and the SW-SHOT line before suspecting the optics.

For the long-range claim, treat the datasheet range as a statement about a target and conditions, not a promise. A client, whom I’ll call Jon, came to me for treatment for anxiety symptoms. Jon was a highly paid manager at a healthcare company. He reported feeling sick to his stomach on Sundays as he dreaded the deluge of demands on Monday. I’m constantly stressed out. My wife is sick of me snapping at her and the kids,” Jon said.

A recent survey by the American Psychological Association found that 75% of Americans report feeling physical and/or emotional symptoms related to stress. Another study reported in Forbes found that 66% of Americans feel burned out, with younger workers feeling it the most.

The reasons for all this stress and burnout are varied but can be summarized here:

It's hard to feel like you can't control the major factors that impact your quality of life. Feeling like you have no control can leave you feeling defeated, unmotivated, and down.

We can’t control broader labor and economic trends, but we can improve how we respond to them. Science-based strategies can help reduce stress and prevent burnout. Here are three you can try now:

Take a morning walk to get the positive effects of sunlight, which helps regulate your sleep/wake cycle. You want a morning cortisol boost to start your day. This helps lower stress by increasing focus early in the day and improving sleep at night. Plus, you reap the numerous health benefits of daily exercise (Huberman, 2026).

Refrain from starting and ending your day on the cellphone

Those who grab the cellphone in bed in the morning and evening report disrupted sleep and mood, contributing to burnout. Instead of cortisol peaking in the morning and lowering at night, cellphone use flips the script, leaving you feeling more anxious, sleep-deprived, and burnt out (Kheirinejad, et al, 2023).

Eat five to nine servings (a half-cup) of fruits and vegetables daily. In a study of healthcare workers, like Jon, those who ate a nutrient-rich diet had lower burnout scores. Only 8% of 5,000 healthcare workers surveyed reported getting the minimum daily requirement of five servings of fruits and vegetables (Utter, et al. 2023). Most nutritionists recommend the Mediterranean diet. That diet is high in vegetables, legumes, and olive oil, and low in sugar and animal fat, like butter. Another study found adherence to the Mediterranean diet reduced the risk of burnout (Amiri et al., 2025).

Jon was so eager to feel better that he tried all three strategies immediately. He got up a few minutes earlier to take an early morning walk before work. He talked to his wife about cutting out sugary sodas and sweets, and they agreed to move toward the Mediterranean diet. The hardest one for Jon was the cellphone. He had a habit of checking his phone and work emails immediately each morning, before he even got out of bed. I explained how this habit disrupted his cortisol regulation and contributed to his sleep issues. Because he couldn’t resist grabbing his phone first thing in the morning, I suggested he keep it out of the bedroom and check it only after his morning walk.

After three weeks implementing these new habits on most days of the week, Jon reported a meaningful improvement in mood. “My wife says I’m much nicer to be around. My gut isn’t waking me up at night anymore. I notice that I’m able to focus better at work too.”

If you’re feeling burned out, or like you’re headed in that direction, it’s always good to get a physical exam with bloodwork first, to determine whether there is a physical cause for the low mood and other symptoms. Sometimes a vitamin deficiency, a thyroid problem, or another medical condition can produce the same symptoms as burnout. If your overall health is good, try the three strategies above. Small changes over time can reap big benefits.

Come creare un bot Telegram che risponde solo a te, con il long polling in Python

Un bot Telegram è un comodo telecomando per uno script o un agente IA sulla tua macchina. Funziona su qualsiasi telefono, non richiede una tua app e non richiede un server pubblico. Il problema è che un bot è pubblico. Chiunque può trovarne lo username e scrivergli, e il token dimostra soltanto che il tuo script è il bot; non dice nulla su chi sta scrivendo. L'app desktop osFoundry ha un ponte del genere per i suoi agenti, scritto in Rust (telegram_bridge.rs). Qui sotto trovi lo stesso schema in circa 70 righe di Python con la sola libreria standard, testato contro un sostituto locale della Bot API, insieme all'errore di una riga che ha trasformato un bot tranquillo in 183.389 richieste al minuto.

Telegram consegna i messaggi in due modi. Un webhook richiede un URL HTTPS pubblico che Telegram possa chiamare. getUpdates non richiede di esporre nulla: lo script chiede i nuovi messaggi e, con timeout=25, Telegram tiene aperta la richiesta fino a 25 secondi e risponde appena ne arriva uno. Su un portatile è la scelta giusta. Nessuna porta da aprire, nessun tunnel, nessun certificato.

Il timeout HTTP (40 s) deve essere più lungo dell'attesa del long poll (25 s). Se è più corto, ogni ciclo tranquillo finisce con un timeout lato client che sembra un'interruzione. L'app conserva il token nel portachiavi del sistema operativo, non in un file. Da Python, keyring.get_password fa lo stesso, e per un test va bene una variabile d'ambiente.

In 60 secondi di inattività, il ciclo di long poll ha fatto 3 richieste e uno short poll ogni secondo ne ha fatte 60. Un messaggio arrivato mentre una richiesta era tenuta aperta ha ricevuto la risposta da 1,5 a 6,3 ms dopo nel test locale. Su una rete reale si aggiunge il tragitto di andata e ritorno verso Telegram.

Il bot deve sapere quale persona serve prima di fare qualsiasi cosa. L'app lo fa con un codice monouso. Clicchi "Pair a phone" sulla macchina, che mostra otto caratteri casuali, e dal telefono invii /pair CODE al bot. Chi invia un codice valido entro 10 minuti è il proprietario.

Il codice viene da secrets, non da qualcosa di indovinabile come l'ora o il nome del bot, perché il codice è l'unica cosa che autorizza un mittente. Viene anche rimosso quando è usato, quindi funziona una sola volta.

Quello che viene salvato è il from.id numerico, non l'@username né il nome visualizzato. Entrambi possono essere cambiati, e uno username liberato può essere registrato da qualcun altro. Accanto viene salvato l'id della chat, per un motivo che compare più sotto.

Tre regole reggono la sicurezza, e ciascuna nasce da un errore specifico annotato nel sorgente dell'app.

I mittenti sconosciuti ricevono silenzio. Una risposta come "you are not paired" dice a un estraneo che il bot è collegato alla macchina di una persona reale e lo invita a continuare a provare. Nessuna risposta sembra un bot che nessuno esegue.

Il pairing funziona solo in una chat privata. Un bot aggiunto a un gruppo riceve i messaggi del gruppo, quindi fare il pairing lì permetterebbe a ogni membro di dare istruzioni all'agente.

A una persona associata si risponde solo nella chat da cui ha fatto il pairing. I commenti dell'app descrivono una versione precedente che controllava il mittente ma rispondeva alla chat da cui arrivava il messaggio. Un utente associato che aggiungeva il bot a un gruppo si ritrovava con un agente che rispondeva in quel gruppo, dove tutti potevano usare strumenti sulla macchina del proprietario. L'autorizzazione è per mittente e la risposta va a una chat, quindi i due controlli vanno fatti insieme.

/unpair viene gestito prima di quel controllo sulla chat, così l'accesso si può togliere dal telefono, che è dove sei quando ti serve.

offset è il modo in cui lo script dice a Telegram quali aggiornamenti ha visto. Tutto ciò che sta sotto è confermato e scartato, e tutto ciò che è pari o sopra viene inviato di nuovo. A New Era of Bionic Vision? AI Helps Scientists Unlock the Brain’s Visual Cortex

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Researchers have used artificial intelligence to improve how a brain implant stimulates the visual cortex of a blind person, potentially advancing the development of bionic eyes.

Artificial intelligence could make future bionic eyes more precise and responsive by improving how electrical signals communicate with the brain. In a study published in Neuron, researchers used deep learning to control brain stimulation in a blind man, producing more accurate neural responses with less electrical current.

The findings could advance visual prostheses that bypass damaged eyes and optic nerves, stimulating the visual cortex directly to generate perceptions of light.

Researchers from UC Santa Barbara, ETH Zurich, and Miguel Hernández University (MHU) tested the approach in a 27-year-old man who lost his sight following a traumatic brain injury. At a hospital in Spain, a temporary implant containing 96 electrodes stimulated his visual cortex, producing phosphenes, perceived as flashes or shapes of light.

Led in part by UCSB professor Michael Beyeler, the team trained a deep neural network to predict brain responses to different stimulation patterns. The AI model also incorporated measurements of resting brain activity, allowing it to account for changes in the brain’s condition before each test.

The AI-designed stimulation patterns produced targeted neural responses more accurately and required less electrical current than comparison approaches. Researchers also found that recorded brain activity predicted the participant’s visual perceptions better than the electrical settings alone.

“Engineers naturally want to treat phosphenes like pixels: stimulate more electrodes, and you should get a more complete image,” Beyeler said. “But the brain does not work that way. Electrodes interact, neural responses fluctuate, and what we put into the brain is not necessarily what the person perceives. The challenge is to learn more about that transformation.”

The implant could both stimulate neurons and record their responses, enabling the AI to learn from actual brain activity. The participant also described perceived flashes, including their brightness, size, shape, and color.

The technology could eventually help people who lose their sight through injuries or neurological and inherited conditions. Beyeler emphasized those “who have been able to see for part of their life, but then, because of an inherited eye disease or an accident, have lost their vision,” adding, “For those people, the desire may be very strong to get some vision back.”

Describing the international research effort as “a true collaboration,” Beyeler said the study demonstrated how computational models could influence experiments involving human participants.

“Building a model in the abstract is one thing,” he said. “Seeing it shape an experiment with a person is something else entirely. That ability to go from theory to something that may one day help people is what drives much of the work in our lab.”

Although the research remains an early proof of concept, it suggests that future visual prostheses may work better by continuously adjusting stimulation to an individual’s neural responses.

“A useful visual prosthesis cannot rely on a fixed recipe,” Beyeler said. “It has to learn how an individual brain responds and adapt the stimulation accordingly. Ultimately, the device should adapt to the person, not the other way around.”

The Apple logo behind the built-in display is illuminated whenever the Mac is awake. For all screen-brightness levels, the Apple logo will be dimmer in a dim room and brighter in a bright room.

For Natural Volume, Wash Your Hair The French Girl Way

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We all know that French girls have that special je ne sais quoi (so much so that it’s inspired countless essays on this very site). When it comes to their hair, the style is nonchalant—and yet, somehow also skillfully cultivated. It’s hair that’s air-dries, falls just right, and retains that slightly tousled movement that gives no hint of effort or styling. So how exactly do Jeanne Damas, Brigitte Bardot, Jane Birkin, and more do it?

Hairstylist and French girl herself Daphné Narcy has an idea: It all starts how you wash your hair. “You may be damaging your hair without even realizing it while in the shower,” Narcy says. “When you’re hair is wet, it’s at its most fragile. But habits like washing hair too quickly, scrubbing too hard, or using water that’s too hot may be causing breakage and frizz.”

She offers up eight simple shower-time adjustments to turn your hair into a French girl dream.

The first—and by far the most common—mistake: People wash their hair instead of their scalp. “We rub the lengths against each other, which damages them, and we neglect the roots, where sebum, pollution, and residue accumulate,” says Narcy. Her philosophy can be summed up in one sentence: A healthy scalp is the foundation of healthy hair. It’s also what determines how light the roots feel once the hair is dry.

The heat from a scalding shower lifts the hair cuticle, which can be bad for shine. “The hair loses moisture, becomes more porous and duller, and the color fades faster,” Narcy says. Hot water hurts the scalp, too, making it drier and more irritated. “Water that’s too hot also softens the hair follicles, which can lead to hair loss.” Your new rule of thumb here is disarmingly simple: “If the water feels pleasant on your wrist, without feeling hot, it’s the right temperature for your hair.”

Use your fingertips in small circular motions, without ever scrubbing. “It’s meant to be an invigorating massage, not a scrub,” insists Narcy. It’s also a moment of pleasantness. When you enjoy it, washing your hair becomes a ritual rather than a chore.

If you’ve ever read the back of a shampoo bottle, you know the instructions: Lather, rinse, repeat. And Narcy says that isn’t just to get you to use more product; but instead the double-shampoo is key to keeping hair clean—though only use a walnut-sized amount each time.

“When you use too much shampoo, residue can remain on the scalp, weighing it down, potentially irritating it, and making your hair look dirty faster The result: you end up washing your hair more often, and you get caught in a vicious cycle.”

The lengths should only be lightly touched when it comes to shampooing. “Press your hands together and slide them down, using very light and gentle friction, all the way to the ends. While rinsing, the lather that runs down finishes cleansing the lengths.” Less friction means fewer tangles, less breakage, and mid-lengths that not only stay hydrated but also dry smooth.

Micron Technology vs. Western Digital: Which Tech Stock Is the Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Both memory and storage stocks are riding the AI data boom, but one may be much better prepared for the next downturn.

The data-heavy world of artificial intelligence (AI) creates massive demand for storage and memory. This shift forces investors to choose between Micron Technology (MU -17.46%) and Western Digital (WDC -17.36%) as they eye long-term gains.

Micron specializes in DRAM and NAND memory used in everything from mobile phones to high-end servers. Western Digital focuses on hard disk drives and flash solutions, specifically targeting the cloud infrastructure sector. Both are booming right now, but the better buy may depend on which business holds up when the boom cools.

As one of the leading semiconductor stocks, Micron Technology designs and manufactures essential memory chips like DRAM and NAND. It serves a wide range of markets including data centers, mobile devices, and the automotive sector. In fiscal 2026 (which ended in September), over half of total revenue came from its top 10 customers, and such customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

Fiscal 2026 revenue reached $133 billion, representing massive year-over-year growth of approximately 256%. Net income grew 895% to $85 billion. This reflects a significant increase from its performance in recent years as memory prices soared due to limited supply and high demand.

As for financial health, the company has virtually no debt. Its current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term obligations with current assets, sits at a healthy 3.3x. Micron also generated nearly $59 billion in free cash flow, which is the cash left over after paying for its operations and capital equipment.

Western Digital provides storage solutions for enterprise clients and hyperscale cloud providers. The cloud end market is vital for the company, accounting for nearly 89% of net revenue in its latest fiscal year.

Trailing-12-month (TTM) revenue through the October quarter reached $14.5 billion, representing growth of 43% over the previous year. Net income reached $10 billion. Western Digital is benefiting from the same dynamic as Micron, with limited supply of storage products driving a favorable pricing environment.

Western Digital has a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.1x. The company holds a current ratio of nearly 1.4x and generated approximately $4.3 billion in free cash flow over the past year.

Both companies face risks from the extreme price volatility often found in the memory and data storage market. These stocks could also fall if anything slowed data center construction, such as new regulations or other restrictions, or if AI infrastructure spending slowed.

Micron competes against major rivals like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. Additionally, geopolitical tensions and trade restrictions involving China could limit the company's ability to sell to key infrastructure operators. Western Digital competes against Seagate and Toshiba in the storage market.

Micron trades at a lower Forward P/E, measuring price against future earnings estimates, and a lower P/S ratio, comparing its share price to revenue over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

The better buy is not the stock with the highest upside, but the one that will be more resilient during the next downturn. Western Digital is better positioned to handle a stall in data center spending, and it's the stock I would buy right now.

Micron has seen its revenue and margins soar over the last year, but that sharp swing in its results also shows how vulnerable it is to a sudden dip in memory prices. While it is signing long-term customer agreements to help smooth out future cycles, it won't cover all of its revenue. It is still vulnerable to a downturn in the memory market.

Western Digital is exposed to similar booms and busts as Micron. However, demand for storage products tends to be more stable than memory. This is because data centers will continue to accumulate more data even as spending on new server orders slows.

This is evident in each company's past gross margin. Over the last 10 years, Micron's gross margin has swung from negative 9% in fiscal 2023 to as high as 81% in fiscal 2026. Western Digital's lowest gross margin over the same period was 22% in fiscal 2023, and its highest was 49% in fiscal 2026.

Western Digital's greater resilience is also why the stock trades at a higher P/E. Investors are paying a lower P/E multiple for Micron because they expect greater volatility in its financial results if memory prices fall.

At this point in the cycle, I would rather buy Western Digital heading into 2027.

Unity Software has held talks to be acquired by a consortium that includes private equity firm Silver Lake and Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, according to Reuters. Nothing is certain. The approach came in recent weeks, and Unity has reportedly been working with an investment bank to weigh its options ever since. Reuters notes that the plan and terms could still change, and Unity declined to comment.

Silver Lake and PIF have done this before. Both were part of the consortium behind the $55 billion EA take-private that closed in August, which ranks among the largest leveraged buyouts ever. A Unity deal would be their second enormous gaming bet in a matter of months.

Silver Lake isn’t walking in cold, either. It invested up to $400 million in Unity back in 2017 and still owns around 8% of the company. Its Egon Durban sits on Unity’s board, a dual role that would normally see him recuse himself from any board discussion of a sale. That’s the kind of detail that looks tidy on paper and gets picked at later.

Most people know Unity as the engine behind thousands of mobile, console and PC games, but its reach goes further than that. It’s used in automotive design, filmmaking, architecture and industrial simulation too. The company started out in Denmark in 2004, went public in September 2020, and has since wandered into advertising technology.

That advertising side is currently pulling its weight. Unity’s third quarter beat estimates, with revenue up 32% to $622.1 million and adjusted earnings of 44 cents per share. Strong growth in its AI-powered Vector platform helped it post its first quarterly profit on a GAAP basis since the IPO, alongside a solid showing from its game-development business. The takeover report landed straight afterwards. A company that has just turned its first profit is a much easier sell to a buyer, and I doubt that’s a coincidence.

There’s a practical angle too. According to Reuters, Unity’s tools are already used by Scopely, the developer behind Monopoly Go!, and Niantic, the creator of Pokémon Go, both of which are owned by a group that includes PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners. The buyers would be acquiring something their own studios already rely on.

PIF’s route into gaming mostly runs through Savvy Games Group, which has had a mandate to invest $38 billion in the sector since 2022. Savvy bought Scopely for $4.9 billion in 2023, and Scopely then bought Niantic’s games business, Pokémon Go included, for $3.5 billion. ESL and FACEIT sit under the same umbrella. In January, PIF moved around $12 billion in listed gaming shares across to Savvy, including its stakes in Nintendo and Bandai Namco, followed by its roughly $3 billion Take-Two stake a month later. Add the EA deal on top and the list of major publishers with no Saudi money attached gets shorter every year.

The group discussed a valuation of around $25 billion, whilst Unity’s market cap sits at roughly $21.5 billion after its latest earnings report.

Savvy’s chief, Brian Ward, said at GDC that it doesn’t plan to use those stakes to push publishers into big changes. Taking him at his word, a minority shareholder is one thing. Owning the engine that other studios build on, including studios that compete with your own, is a different kind of position entirely.

Unity has been here before with developer trust. In September 2023 it announced a Runtime Fee that charged studios per install, and the backlash was fierce enough that CEO John Riccitiello stepped down within weeks. The fee was scrapped completely in September 2024, and current CEO Matthew Bromberg later admitted Unity had been “practically at war with our customers”. A leveraged buyout means debt, and debt has to be paid from somewhere. Developers are going to want to know where.

Right now, none of that is more than speculation. There are talks, an investment bank, and a number somewhere north of where the stock was trading. Studios will be watching to see which of those three moves first.

Dropbox acquires Tigris Data for $350M, releases new AI productivity features

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Dropbox Inc. is acquiring cloud storage startup Tigris Data Inc. for $350 million.

The company announced the deal today in conjunction with a major update to its product portfolio. It introduced DBX Agent Cloud, a service that artificial intelligence agents can use to store their data. Dropbox is also adding new AI productivity features to its flagship file sharing platform.

Sunnyvale, California-based Tigris Data offers an object storage service that runs across multiple public clouds. The company says that it enables applications to read and write several times faster than competing offerings. Furthermore, the service includes several features designed to optimize AI workloads.

AI agents often run in sandboxes that include, among other resources, the data they need to carry out their work. Loading data into a sandbox can be a relatively time-consuming process, which slows agent performance. Tigris Data offers a feature called bucket forms that speeds up the workflow. Furthermore, it prevents agents from modifying information that is used by other workloads.

Tigris Data’s service monitors the locations from which agents access records. When it detects a new location, it moves a copy of the records to a nearby server in order to reduce latency. The service caches the most frequently used files on high-speed storage infrastructure to further reduce access times.

Dropbox stated that Tigris Data will support its push into the AI infrastructure market. The company debuted its first entry into that product category, DBX Agent Cloud, in conjunction with the acquisition announcement. Like Tigris Data’s service, it’s an S3-compatible object storage offering optimized for AI agents.

Dropbox plans to add a file system capability further down the line. Object storage is best suited for unstructured datasets, while file systems are used to store records that are organized into folders. Dropbox plans to release the technology alongside “content processing capabilities.” The company says that those features will make it easier for AI models to process information kept in DBX Agent Cloud.

The offering is rolling out alongside a major overhaul of Dropbox’s flagship file sharing platform. An enhanced search bar will enable users to look for images by entering a description of the file they seek. Furthermore, Dropbox can now respond to questions about a file by generating a natural language summary. A worker could, for example, request an overview of the changes that colleagues have made to an important folder over the past week.

Dropbox can not only edit questions about files but also edit them. The company says that users can have the built-in AI models change a file’s format, move it to a different folder or combine it with other records into a PDF document. Several other actions are supported as well. Users can mix and match actions into multistep workflows.

The new AI productivity features are initially available to customers in the US, UK, and Australia with Standard and Advanced subscriptions. Dropbox plans to expand access next year.

Threat actor targets Indian fintechs on weekends when defenders aren’t watching

CERT-IN warns of ‘significant escalation’ in AI-powered attacks, some after victims tried to improve defences

India’s Computer Emergency Response Team has warned the nation’s financial services industry of a “significant escalation” in attacks by what it describes as an “AI-assisted financial threat actor” that has re-targeted victims after they flubbed remediation efforts.

CERT-IN first detected the attacks in October and issued advisory in which it warned digital lending platforms, fintech entities, payment service providers, and wallet operators that attackers were targeting internet-facing applications, exposed APIs and privileged endpoints. If the attackers found a way in, they tried to use payment APIs to steal money.

On Thursday, the CERT published an update in which it said it continues to hear of successful attacks, including from outfits that tried to remediate their systems after earlier incidents.

“The same entities have been deliberately re-targeted, including after incomplete remediation and attacks have been timed to coincide with holidays, weekends and other low-monitoring windows to delay detection and response,” the CERT warned.

CERT-IN has decided the attackers need a name and chose “CRAFTY FALCON.”

The security org has also revealed that the crew uses AI-enhanced tools including the ARTEX pen-testing tool, the Cairn attack search engine, and the CyberStrikeAI framework – all of which have been linked to users who appear to speak Chinese.

CRAFTY FALCON’s tactics see it go looking for internet-exposed environments used for testing or development, where it finds artefacts such as shared credentials, tokens, or network paths that help it move into production systems.

If it gets in, the crew re-writes victims’ applications so they do things like make unauthorized API calls. Victims’ defenses assume that only legitimate apps would call those APIs, so activity conducted by the re-written apps aren’t flagged as an attack.

Even if victims suspect an attack, they have a lot to cope with because CERT-IN says CRAFTY FALCON creates many “functionally identical variants refined in rapid succession; a pattern consistent with AI-generated code and iterative AI-assisted development.”

The crew also quickly exploits newly discovered vulnerabilities to keep its attacks fresh.

Once it gets a toehold, the criminal crew makes tiny payments of about $0.01 to test if its attacks work. If those transfers work, CRAFTY FALCON makes larger payments. When the target is a cryptocurrency exchange or virtual digital asset service provider, the crew steals wallet keys and empties cryptocurrency wallets.

Vendors of observability tools will be warming up their Indian sales teams today, because CERT-IN’s advice to Indian businesses includes improving their ability to log events, monitor for anomalies in real time, and send alerts. It also urged keeping test environments off the internet, and restricting access to cloud resources because CRAFTY FALCON is fond of using them to run its attacks.

CERT-IN also warns that the attacks are “active and escalating” and therefore worthy of 24x7 monitoring.

Interestingly, the CERT’s advisory mentions attacks on “entities” across banking, financial services, and insurance, but only names the kind of businesses mentioned above as having reported attacks.

The Register points this out because India is home to literally thousands of banks, many of them small operations that sometimes lack basic infosec protections. CRAFTY FALCON therefore has a target-rich environment in India should it choose to expand its activities. ®
