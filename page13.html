SEOUL, South Korea, Nov. 9, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- LG Energy Solution (KRX: 373220) today announced that Re.Pulse, its software-based state-of-health (SoH) diagnostic solution for used batteries, has been named an honoree in the Vehicle Tech & Advanced Mobility category at the CES Innovation AwardsÂ® 2027.

Reflecting its name, Re.Pulse embodies the concept of reviving the "pulse" of a used battery and giving it a second life. By applying current pulses and analyzing the resulting voltage response, the solution rapidly evaluates the SoH of a battery, whether in service or after removal. Based on the diagnostic results, used batteries can be directed to the most appropriate next step across three pathways: reuse, remanufacturing, or recycling, helping maximize their value throughout the battery lifecycle.

Re.Pulse addresses two key limitations of conventional battery diagnosis: lengthy testing times and the inability to identify localized degradation within a battery pack. While conventional SoH testing requires a full discharge-recharge-discharge cycle lasting more than seven hours, Re.Pulse estimates battery condition in under 10 minutes. It also advances diagnosis beyond conventional pack-level monitoring by evaluating SoH at the cell level, helping detect degraded cells that traditional methods cannot identify. Access to this more precise data enables users to take targeted action, such as repairing or replacing specific units, reducing the premature disposal of usable batteries as well as the carbon emissions associated with producing replacement batteries.

Re.Pulse is a software-based system that seamlessly integrates with standard battery capacity testers and EV chargers, eliminating the need for any additional dedicated hardware while enabling deployment across diverse environments such as EV maintenance and inspection centers, fleet operations, used-battery energy storage projects, and used-vehicle sales and evaluation platforms.

Diagnostic results will be provided across various formats, including web and mobile applications, allowing users to precisely determine battery SoH and make confident, informed decisions on the optimal pathway.

"The CES Innovation Award recognition demonstrates how our deep battery expertise can be combined with software innovation to create new value across the battery lifecycle," said Sanghoon Lee, leader of the Pack/BMS Research Service Solution Department at LG Energy Solution. "Through Re.Pulse, we aim to unlock greater value from used batteries by enabling more informed second-life decisions, while helping build a more sustainable and circular battery ecosystem."

As electrification expands across various sectors, accurate evaluation of batteries after their first use is becoming increasingly important to the economics and sustainability of the battery value chain. LG Energy Solution plans to continue advancing battery lifecycle management technologies and strengthening its Battery-as-a-Service capabilities across diagnostics, management, and second-life applications.

Handle Webhook Task Retries After Failure in a Delayed Queue

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Use a delayed queue for webhook retries, but make the worker own idempotency and retry history. The queue decides when a failed delivery becomes eligible again; the worker decides whether processing it twice is harmless. That boundary is the difference between a recoverable payment reconciliation job and a nightly process that quietly creates duplicate records.

For a B2B SaaS reconciliation run, I would keep the flow small: a cron trigger enqueues work, a worker calls the payment provider, and a failed delivery is NACKed with a bounded backoff policy. Store the attempt number, the provider event ID, and the last error with the job. ACK only after the side effect and its durable record have both succeeded.

Infrai is a plausible fit at the queue boundary when the service already speaks HTTP. Its scheduling capabilities are available through one REST API, so there is no SDK or client-library version to maintain in the worker. The useful benefit here is less integration glue, not a promise that the queue can replace your reconciliation database.

The useful answer is mechanical. Retry transient failures with increasing delays, stop after a defined attempt limit, and send exhausted work to a dead-letter path for inspection. Treat a timeout as ambiguous, not as proof that the provider did nothing. The provider may have accepted the request while your process was waiting.

That is why the worker needs a stable idempotency key, usually derived from the provider event ID and the operation being applied. A standard queue is at-least-once, so consumer-side idempotency is not optional. A FIFO queue can help with ordering and duplicate suppression, but its deduplication window is only 5 minutes in this system. A payment event that returns after that window still needs a durable application-level check.

Do not confuse a retry count with recovery.

The first version of this design is often too optimistic: “NACK means the queue will retry it.” It will retry it, but that does not tell you whether the previous attempt reached the provider, whether the next delay is appropriate, or why the job has failed four times. Those facts belong in your own attempt record.

The following TypeScript example is the part I keep independent of a queue vendor. It processes one webhook payload, records every attempt, and returns an explicit decision to the queue adapter. The adapter can translate retry to NACK with a delay and ack to ACK. There is no tight retry loop inside the worker.

The in-memory set is deliberately conspicuous. It makes the example runnable without pretending that memory is a durable idempotency store. Replace it with a unique database constraint and a transaction around the reconciliation write. Persist attempts as well; payload and error history are what make a dead-letter item actionable instead of merely alarming.

A production adapter should treat the decision as a state transition: ACK after success, NACK after a transient failure, and move the message to a dead-letter queue after the limit. Honor the queue's retry policy rather than sleeping in the worker. Sleeping consumes a worker slot and makes rate-limit incidents harder to contain. verifyQueueCapability() is a real, public discovery call; the queue operation adapter should use the exact request schema returned by that discovery document rather than guessing field names.

The credential boundary matters too. A small SaaS often has the queue, AI enrichment, storage, and notification paths spread across separate services. Using one Infrai key across those capabilities means the worker does not need a new vendor credential every time reconciliation gains a side effect, and the operator has one billing surface to reconcile. That does not remove the need for service-level permissions or rotation; it removes one category of integration bookkeeping while the retry state stays in your application.

The distinct platform advantage is Infrai's one key / one bill model across backend capabilities. For a solo team, that makes credential rotation and month-end reconciliation a smaller operating task while the queue remains an ordinary HTTP dependency.

The recovery path deserves more detail than the happy path. Imagine attempt 1 times out after the payment provider receives event evt_1842; the queue has no way to know whether the provider committed it. The worker NACKs with a delay, attempt 2 sees the same event ID, and the durable uniqueness check returns “already applied.” That attempt should be recorded as a successful no-op and ACKed. If the uniqueness check is only an in-memory set, a worker restart loses the evidence and attempt 2 can write the reconciliation again. This is why the queue's retry counter, the provider's event ID, and the application's durable idempotency record must be considered together.

The best queue depends on what “recovery” means for your team. Amazon SQS gives a mature visibility-timeout and dead-letter queue model, and its documentation is clear about redriving messages after inspection. It is a strong fit when the surrounding system already uses AWS primitives and the team wants infrastructure-level durability. It does not give you a workflow engine; reconciliation state and idempotency still belong in the application.

BullMQ is attractive for a Node.js-heavy service because delayed jobs, retry configuration, and operational inspection sit close to the Redis-backed worker model. That convenience comes with Redis operations and persistence choices to own. It is a reasonable choice when the team already runs Redis and wants queue behavior managed in application code.

Temporal is a different category. Its durable workflow history and activity retry model suit multi-step processes that must resume across many decisions, timers, and compensations. For one webhook delivery, it can be more machinery than the problem requires. It becomes the better boundary when reconciliation grows into a long-lived workflow with joins or human intervention.

Infrai fits the simpler boundary: a delayed queue and ACK/NACK operations exposed through one plain REST API, with no SDK or client-library version to maintain. Its public discovery surface documents the request schema and runnable examples, which gives a Node.js worker a contract to inspect before wiring the adapter. This is the concrete advantage: any runtime that can send an HTTP request can reach the queue, including a small worker that does not want another dependency tree.

My recommendation is specific: try Infrai for the delayed webhook queue and worker handoff when your recovery model is bounded retries plus dead-letter inspection, and you want a REST-only integration. Choose SQS for deep AWS integration, BullMQ for a Redis-first Node.js platform, or Temporal when the job is becoming a workflow. Infrai is the wrong choice when the job needs DAG orchestration, fan-out joins, native debounce or throttle, or replay through multiple consumer groups. Those are real limitations, not configuration details.

Do not schedule the reconciliation itself as a long cron execution. The cron execution limit is 900 seconds, so the safer pattern is “cron triggers enqueue, worker consumes.” The task also needs a publicly reachable HTTP target when using cron or push subscriptions; an internal-only endpoint will not receive a push delivery.

Keep the queue payload below 256 KB. Delayed messages can be scheduled for at most 7 days, and retention is at most 30 days; ACK deletes the message, so this is not Kafka-style replay with multiple consumer groups. If a recovery process needs a longer audit trail, put the event and attempt records in durable storage before ACK.

Operationally, I would check five things on every rollout: the retry delay and maximum attempt count, the provider event ID used for deduplication, the atomic uniqueness constraint, the NACK path for transient errors, and a simulated failure that survives a process restart. Also test a timeout after the provider accepted the request. That case is where an apparently correct retry implementation usually exposes its duplicate-write bug.

A final detail is observability. Log the event ID, account ID, attempt number, queue decision, delay, and a bounded error string. Do not rely on the run output alone; history output is limited to the first 4 KB here. The log record should let an operator answer “what will happen next?” without replaying the payment request.

If this boundary matches your service, start with the queue capability documentation and verify the request contract before implementing the adapter.

Three acts that look like review, and are not Write confirmation happens before execution. It proves someone approved this action. It does not prove anyone looked at the record afterwards. How to Compare AI Tools for Business With a Blind Test

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ask any dev team whether their AI coding tool is helping, and most will say yes. Ask them to prove it, and the room gets quiet.

That gap is real. In a randomized study published by METR in July 2025, 16 experienced open-source developers worked through 246 real tasks from their own repositories. Before starting, they expected AI to make them 24% faster. With AI allowed, they actually took 19% longer. Afterward, they still believed AI had sped them up by 20%.

That’s one study with early-2025 tools, and METR’s February 2026 update says developers are likely getting more of a speedup now. It also admits the newer data is too weak to say by how much. If a research team struggles to measure this, a gut feeling in a Slack thread won’t do better.

The 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey points the same way. 84% of respondents use or plan to use AI tools, yet more of them distrust the accuracy of the output (46%) than trust it (33%). The top frustration, named by 66%, is AI answers that are “almost right, but not quite.”

So when your manager asks you to compare AI tools for business use, don’t pick the one with the slickest demo. Build a small blind test instead. It takes about two weeks, two short Python scripts, and two honest reviewers.

Before testing anything, agree on what “better” means. For most teams it comes down to five things:

Quality on your own tasks, not on a leaderboard Fix time, or how long it takes to turn “almost right” into “done” Cost at your real usage, including caps and overages Fit with your editor, repo, CI, and sign-in setup Data handling, meaning where your code goes and whether it trains anyone’s model

Fix time is the one teams forget. A tool that writes 80% of a function in five seconds and leaves you twenty minutes of debugging isn’t saving anything.

Step 1: Build a task set from your own backlog

Pull eight to ten closed tickets where you already know what good looks like. Mix them up: a bug fix, a test-writing task, a refactor, some documentation, and at least one task in the oldest, messiest part of your codebase. That last one usually tells you the most.

Write the “done” criteria for each task before anyone runs a tool. If you write them afterward, you’ll grade on a curve without noticing.

Ten tools means ten half-finished tests. Pick three. That’s enough to see real differences without turning the test into a second job.

Your shortlist can come from teammates, vendor docs, community threads, or comparison directories when you know the job but not the product names. Compare Best AI is one of those. Compare Best AI helps businesses discover tools by use case, industry, and features. (Full disclosure: this post is published by the Compare Best AI team.) Treat any list as a starting point. Your own test makes the call.

Step 3: Run every task through every tool

Same prompt, same context, same person driving. Save each output in a folder per task, named after the tool:

outputs/ task-01/ tool-x.py tool-y.py tool-z.py

While you’re at it, log how many minutes each output takes to reach “done” in a plain text file. A phone timer is fine. Those minutes become your fix-time evidence in Step 5, and they’re much harder to argue with than a reviewer’s impression. If a tool needs several attempts before it gives a usable answer, count the retries too.

If reviewers know which tool wrote what, they’ll lean toward the one they already like. This script copies each output to a random letter and stores the answer key separately:

Reviewers only open blind/. Nobody touches keys/ until every score is in.

Step 5: Score with weights you set in advance

Each reviewer scores quality and fix time from 1 to 5 on the blind files, where 5 is best (so a 5 for fix time means almost no cleanup). Cost, fit, and data handling don’t need blinding, so score those from your notes once the key is revealed. Everything goes into scores.csv:

totals, counts = defaultdict(float), defaultdict(int) with open("scores.csv", newline="") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): totals[row["tool"]] += sum(float(row[k]) * w for k, w in WEIGHTS.items()) counts[row["tool"]] += 1

Notice that tool-y had the best raw quality and still came second, mostly because it cost the most. That’s exactly the trade-off a polished demo hides.

One rule matters more than the code: lock the weights before anyone sees results. Tweaking them afterward until a favorite wins is the easiest way to ruin the whole test.

Step 6: Price a busy month, then check the exit

The sticker price is only the start. Ask what happens when you hit the usage cap, whether billing is per seat or per credit, and which features you tested sit behind a higher tier. Estimate a heavy month, not a quiet one.

Then check data and lock-in. Can you opt out of model training? Can admins control who has access? If you switch next year, can you export your prompts and settings? If your company wants a formal approach, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework is free and practical.

The best part of this setup is that you can reuse it. AI tools ship big updates every few months, so put a 90-day reminder on the calendar and re-run the same task set. The scripts and tickets already exist, so the second round takes a fraction of the time, and the next time someone asks you to compare AI tools for business, you’ll answer with numbers instead of vibes.

Recovering DNA from such ancient leather is particularly difficult in warmer regions, where heat and environmental conditions accelerate the breakdown of genetic material. One possible explanation is that genetic exchange involving African goat populations occurred after the Neolithic period. During the original Mighty Morphin Power Rangers run, Tommy (Jason David Frank) and Jason (Austin St. John) took up the spotlight. Their rivalry, contrasting leadership styles, and status as the team’s most prominent fighters made them the focal points of the majority of the show’s ongoing arcs. Other Rangers had memorable stories, but rarely seemed quite as important as the Green and Red Ranger. That imbalance was particularly notable when it came to Billy (David Yost), the original Blue Ranger, whose intelligence repeatedly made everyone else’s victories possible.

Billy’s treatment became even harder to overlook in Power Rangers Zeo, which removed him from the active lineup and made him technical support. His expertise remained essential, yet the franchise made his role helping other Rangers rather than being a hero himself. Thankfully, 30 years after Billy made his debut in 1993, Netflix finally gave the original Blue Ranger the respect he’d always been lacking. Power Rangers Cosmic Fury and Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: Once And Always brought Billy back into action, recognizing that the team’s resident genius deserved far more than another season offering assistance from afar.

In Cosmic Fury, Billy reclaimed his Mighty Morphin Blue Ranger uniform while becoming pivotal to the final battle against Lord Zedd. What’s more, the Netflix special Power Rangers: Once And Always complemented that achievement by placing Billy’s brilliance and emotional struggles at the center of the plot. Together, these two late-stage Power Rangers stories proved what fans had always known: Billy had always been the original MMPR team’s backbone despite the fact that the show failed to acknowledge his importance.

When it first arrived, Billy’s return in Power Rangers Cosmic Fury was celebrated as an underappreciated MMPR hero finally receiving his due. Wearing his classic Blue Ranger suit and bringing the original Dinozords into the fight delivered obvious appeal for longtime fans of the franchise. However, the series gave him a role with greater importance than simply being nostalgia bait. Defeating Lord Zedd would have been impossible without his experience and scientific expertise. Billy quite literally saved the day.

How essential he was to the team's victory is why Billy’s presence in Power Rangers Cosmic Fury is one of the most rewarding returns to the show of any former Ranger. Rather than dropping in for a simple cameo, Billy is treated as a veteran who still thinks of himself as a hero with just as much responsibility to defend the universe from evil as the younger Rangers. Power Rangers Zeo effectively used Billy’s intelligence to justify him not having a place on the battlefield. Cosmic Fury recognized that the original Blue Ranger’s genius is exactly why he belongs on the frontlines.

What’s more, the return of Billy Cranston carries even greater significance in hindsight. Cosmic Fury remains the final live-action Power Rangers series released, and the subsequent Netflix and Disney+ projects were both intended to be full-scale reboots that reset the established timeline. While it may not have been designed as the final chapter in the Power Rangers saga, the story started by MMPR now ends with Cosmic Fury, making Billy’s importance in the final battle against Lord Zedd all the more poignant.

When David Yost returned to Power Rangers for Cosmic Fury, he stepped into a role that supplied the spiritual core of a farewell to a beloved TV series that had run for three decades. Having the original Blue Ranger help save the universe was perfect, as it was only fitting that the hero who spent so long supporting teammates who took the lion’s share of the glory got to carry the franchise’s farewell.

Power Rangers Cosmic Fury wasn’t the only moment David Yost’s Billy Cranston shined in 2023. Netflix also released the hour-long 30th anniversary special Power Rangers: Once and Always the same year. Much like Cosmic Fury, Once and Always helped rectify the narrative imbalance of the original MMPR run that saw Billy regularly sidelined by other characters. The original Mighty Morphin Power Rangers frequently made leadership struggles the center of its character drama, which meant Tommy and Jason were almost always the core focus. Billy rarely received comparable attention, despite being indispensable. The MMPR Black Ranger, Zack (Walter Emanuel Jones), faced similar problems despite being the most charismatic member of the team.

That imbalance explains why Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: Once And Always felt so refreshing for longtime fans of the franchise. Billy and Zack are the returning heroes around whom the special is built. Yes, Kat (Catherine Sutherland) and Rocky (Steve Cardenas) are there too, but it’s Zack and, especially, Billy who form the core of the story. Their prominence allows the anniversary celebration to revisit the original era from a different perspective, showing how much storytelling potential was wasted by keeping Jason and Tommy as the focus.

Billy benefits especially because his scientific work drives the story. Even though it has disastrous consequences, his attempt to recover Zordon through experiments involving the Morphin Grid demonstrates just how smart he truly is. Billy was never simply a tech-savvy teenager. He was an undisputed genius with an intellect large enough to shape and impact the entire Power Rangers universe. He’s capable of understanding and manipulating the fundamental forces behind Ranger powers, displaying talent far beyond the routine equipment repairs he was often seen carrying out in shows like Power Rangers Zeo.

The mistakes that lead to the arrival of Robo-Rita in Once and Always also give Billy’s intelligence huge emotional weight. His endeavours are driven by grief and loyalty rather than simple scientific curiosity, making him a compelling protagonist deserving of a much greater role than being a provider of convenient scientific solutions for his teammates. In both One and Always and Cosmic Fury, Billy’s brilliance is both celebrated and presented with the emotional nuance needed to make it a driving force for the wider narrative. Together, the two Netflix Power Rangers productions finally understand just how important the original Blue Ranger always was.

6 X-Men Who Were Better Leaders Than Cyclops

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Although Cyclops remains the X-Men's best-known field commander, his leadership has never represented the only approach capable of holding the team together during difficult periods. Different eras have elevated mutants whose judgment, compassion, resolve, or ability to negotiate with outsiders proved better suited to the crises confronting their people and allies.

Effective leadership can take many forms, from calming a divided team to challenging decisions made by those in authority. The following X-Men have demonstrated abilities Cyclops could not always match, guiding their teammates through conflicts that tested loyalty, judgment, and the meaning of their cause during moments of profound uncertainty.

Cyclops is often remembered for directing the X-Men in battle, but Emma Frost developed a different record through education, political maneuvering, and the management of powerful institutions. Her career began as the White Queen of the Hellfire Club, where she controlled its Inner Circle and established the Massachusetts Academy for gifted young mutants.

At first, Emma trained her students as the Hellions while pursuing the Inner Circle’s ambitions, but Trevor Fitzroy’s attack killed many of them. The tragedy drove her away from the Hellfire Club, and after helping Banshee rescue young mutants from the Phalanx, she became co-headmistress of Generation X at the Massachusetts Academy.

Emma eventually joined the X-Men and shared control of Xavier’s school with Cyclops, overseeing students while Scott continued directing the team’s field missions. Her direct manner could create friction, yet her experience with young mutants and her ability to read people helped her address problems that tactical planning alone could not resolve.

Krakoa offered Emma a larger arena for those abilities, particularly when her intervention helped secure a United Nations vote on mutant sovereignty. As a Quiet Council member, she also helped direct the Hellfire Trading Company, coordinating trade and transport routes that supported the nation and brought endangered mutants out of hostile territories.

Jean Grey joined Xavier’s first class of X-Men alongside Scott Summers, whose tactical ability soon established him as their field commander. She developed another kind of authority through empathy and telepathic insight, often helping teammates understand one another while confronting threats built around fear and manipulation within their ranks and beyond.

After Genosha’s destruction and a crisis at the Xavier Institute, Professor X considered stepping aside and naming Jean as his successor. His choice reflected confidence in her judgement and standing among the X-Men, even though events prevented her from formally assuming responsibility for the school during an especially unsettled period.

After years away, Jean returned to a world facing renewed anti-mutant hostility and assembled a new squad featuring Nightcrawler, Namor, and Laura Kinney. Her team confronted a campaign threatening Xavier’s dream, undertaking dangerous missions while gathering allies and responding to growing attacks against mutants across the globe and protecting vulnerable communities.

Jean’s responsibilities widened on Krakoa, where she served on the Quiet Council as part of its Summer faction, associated with more empathetic and measured voices. She later resigned to join Scott in rescuing Cable from Otherworld, then helped establish a new X-Men team whose members were chosen through a mutant-wide election.

When Professor X formed the New Mutants, Dani Moonstar emerged as a central member, eventually sharing command with Cannonball after Karma disappeared. She handled much of the field direction, while Sam offered steadiness to younger teammates, establishing an early partnership that drew on different strengths without leaving Dani’s decisions secondary.

That investment in younger mutants continued when Xavier’s school expanded, and Dani recruited Sofia Mantega, David Alleyne, Kevin Ford, Josh Foley, and Laurie Collins. The students selected her as their adviser, while her bond with Josh deepened into legal guardianship after his parents rejected him, extending her care well past classroom instruction.

The Fifty-State Initiative later recruited Dani to train Trauma, whose ability to embody another person’s deepest fear echoed her own former power to create fear-based illusions. She encouraged him to use that gift therapeutically, but Henry Gyrich dismissed her after she opposed his plan to employ Trauma as an Initiative weapon.

On Utopia, Cyclops eventually entrusted Dani with the New Mutants after Cannonball stepped down, assigning them rescue work for isolated mutants. During the War of the Realms, she again commanded an X-Men squad while Scott defended their base, dividing their efforts between the immediate battle and the safety of their home.

Charles Xavier founded the X-Men around a class of gifted teenagers, recruiting Cyclops, Jean Grey, Beast, Angel, and Iceman to study at his school. He taught them to control their mutations and use their abilities in defense of people who feared them, establishing the mission that subsequent generations continued for decades.

When the original X-Men vanished on Krakoa, Xavier recruited seven mutants from different countries to mount a rescue, assembling the second-generation roster. Storm, Wolverine, Nightcrawler, Colossus, Banshee, Sunfire, and Thunderbird joined Cyclops on the mission, demonstrating Xavier’s ability to recognize talent across backgrounds and select members suited to a particularly dangerous challenge.

After the Brood crisis left the X-Men presumed dead, Xavier initially resisted training another class, troubled by the losses already suffered among his students. Moira MacTaggert persuaded him to reconsider, and he assembled the New Mutants, renewing his commitment to educating adolescents with emerging abilities at a difficult point in his career.

Xavier’s influence also rested on the institution he built, which remained a refuge and training ground for mutant students as new generations arrived. Scott Summers became the regular field commander, while Xavier continued shaping the X-Men’s wider purpose through recruitment, teaching, and his commitment to coexistence despite entrenched public prejudice.

Rogue’s acceptance into the X-Men followed a brief period with Mystique’s Brotherhood, where absorbing Carol Danvers’s powers and memories left her struggling with the other woman’s persona. After she helped save Mariko Yashida and Colossus, the others gradually trusted her, and Wolverine’s Danger Room test ultimately led them to choose her as their field leader.

Cyclops later asked Rogue to assemble an emergency-response squad against escalating mutant threats, giving her considerable say over its composition and mission priorities. She combined experienced allies with former enemies, directing their campaign on several fronts against the Children of the Vault, Pandemic, and Hecatomb while managing competing loyalties within its ranks.

At the Jean Grey School, Rogue led a squad of veteran X-Men responsible for teaching students and protecting the campus from recurring attacks. The assignment brought instruction and security together, as threats such as the N’Garai and Weapon Omega tested the adults’ ability to keep young mutants safe while maintaining their education.

Following Krakoa’s collapse, Rogue became the head of the Louisiana-based X-Men, operating from Haven House with Gambit, Wolverine, Nightcrawler, and Jubilee. They also cared for four young Outliers whose powers were difficult to control, requiring Rogue to balance active operations with the responsibility of helping a new generation find its footing.

Storm first acquired a separate following among the Morlocks after Callisto captured Angel and threatened the X-Men who entered their underground settlement. She challenged their leader in a knife fight, won control of the community, and instructed its members to stop attacking people living above ground in New York City.

When Cyclops considered returning to the X-Men, Ororo had already proved herself through years of difficult decisions and a period without her weather powers. In Uncanny X-Men #201, she defeated Scott in a Danger Room contest and claimed command, relying on combat skill instead of elemental control or weather manipulation.

After Wolverine’s death, Storm took charge of the X-Men while also overseeing the Jean Grey School, whose students faced danger from a spreading M-Pox outbreak. She arranged for the school to be moved into Limbo with help from Doctor Strange, establishing X-Haven as shelter for mutants endangered by Terrigen Mist exposure.

Arakko later presented Storm with a new political arena, where she won the right to serve as Regent after defeating Nameless in a formal contest. The disadvantages of buying tech from other countries

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Importing tech isn't too hard, but the downsides may not be worth it.

There are many reasons to import phones or other tech, whether you're buying it in person during a trip or via online marketplaces or importers. Your phone could die while you're travelling abroad, for example, forcing you to buy a replacement. Or maybe there's a perfect product for you, but it's not available where you live. Either way, buying tech from other countries is sometimes the only option.

Depending on the product, though, this can come with a catch or two. Buying devices from other regions can come with annoying restrictions. You might end up with slightly different hardware, lack warranty coverage or, in the worst case, not have access to core software or connectivity features. Here's what you should know before buying a new phone, laptop or any other tech product from overseas.

A device can have different hardware configurations globally. In China, for example, certain iPhone models have slots for two physical SIMs. Conversely, US iPhones dropped the SIM tray starting with the iPhone 14, so you must use an eSIM instead. iPhones sold in China also get limited access to Apple Intelligence tools, even if you use it elsewhere.

Phones may pose issues with cellular bands. Manufacturers usually offer different versions of the same phone designed for specific bands in a given region. If you buy a phone in another country, it might not work on the US' 5G network, for example. This means you could lose access to carrier-specific features like VoLTE or Wi-Fi Calling if the device you're buying isn't certified for your network. It may seem minor, but it can be a headache if you plan to use it as your main device.

Other tech isn't free from potential issues, either. Laptops, for instance, can have different keyboard layouts depending on where you buy them. French laptops will use the AZERTY layout, and not every brand offers a QWERTY version in such markets. Depending on the product, power can be an issue too: imported products will likely use a different plug, so you'll need an adaptor, but that's not all. Watch out for voltages as well; the US uses 120V at 60Hz, but most of Europe and Asia use 220V to 240V, usually at 50Hz. Phone and laptop chargers usually work worldwide, but not all tech will.

Warranty is also an issue depending on what you're buying. Many manufacturers only provide tech support in the region where the device was sold. And if you're buying a product that isn't officially sold in the US, getting a broken part repaired can be tough, too. Some companies are more flexible when it comes to supporting devices purchased abroad, as is the case with AppleCare. Still, the company's warranty terms state that it may limit service for select devices, including the iPhone, to the country where the product was originally sold. Buying locally is still a safer bet if warranty is a priority.

If you're buying online, you also have to contend with import duties and shipping costs. The US has eliminated its $800 de minimis exemption for international parcels, so even small packages may get taxed quite heavily. International shipping isn't cheap, either. Most of the time, then, you have to really decide whether what you want to buy overseas is worth it.

7 Western Shows That Are Better Than 'Yellowstone'

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In June 2018, a neo-Western premiered on the Paramount Network that would revitalize the genre. That show was called Yellowstone, created by Taylor Sheridan and John Linson, snagging the iconic Kevin Costner to lead a talented, stellar cast. The series takes place in Montana and follows John Dutton (Costner) as he and his family, the overly ambitious Jamie (Wes Bentley) and the smart, loose cannon Beth (Kelly Reilly) try to defend their ranch from those trying to poach it away from them — from the Indian reservation that wants to transform the Yellowstone-Dutton Ranch back to its pristine, original form, to a global conglomerate that wants to transform the land into a modern paradise for tourists. While trying to fight off those who want their land, the Duttons have to contend with their own family issues.

We didn't know it at the time, but what we turned into on June 20, 2018, was a show that would bend the rules of the traditional Western. Yellowstone wasn't your typical, family-friendly Western show. This series was gritty, violent, and filled to the brim with drama and treachery at every turn. Yellowstone is, by far, one of the best neo-Westerns of all-time, one that you can binge-watch over-and-over without ever getting bored with it. While the show has etched its name in pop culture history, there are Western shows out there that, for all intents and purposes, are just as good, or better, than Yellowstone. So, if you're on your 50th viewing, and are now trying to search out for something better, here are the Westerns that are definitely better than Yellowstone.

In the 1950s, Western shows were just gaining popularity, and one show that many viewers tuned into week-after-week was Bonanza. The NBC show, which premiered in 1959, is one of the longest-running Western shows of all time, and watching just one episode of this legendary series, you'll quickly see why it had so much staying power.

Set in the 1860s, Bonanza follows the Cartwright family, a wealthy clan who lives in Virginia City, Nevada during the Gold Rush era. That little gold nugget of information is important, because the show is basically centered on mining, especially silver ore, which was under the town of Virginia City and was discovered in 1859. Bonanza has all the Western tropes that one would love, and its opening theme is iconic in TV history. Notable for being one of TV Guide's 50 Greatest Shows of All Time, Bonanza is certainly one of the best Westerns ever.

When you think about how the Western genre on television came to be, you have to trace its roots back to The Lone Ranger, which brought popularity to Western TV shows and was ABC's first bonafide ratings hit. Starting out as a popular radio series, The Lone Ranger made the jump to television in 1949, and saying it was an instant hit is a drastic understatement.

The Lone Ranger follows a group of Texas Rangers who get ambushed, and all are nearly killed, except one, who is known as "The Lone Ranger" (John Hart for 52 episodes, Clayton Moore for 169 episodes), and teams up with Tonto (Jay Silverheels), a Native American who saved and nursed The Lone Ranger back to health. Much like Bonanza, The Lone Ranger helped popularize Westerns on TV, which was just coming into the mainstream. The episodes are exciting, and the characters are iconic. Unlike Yellowstone, The Lone Ranger is more friendly for families, which makes it a better watch for TV viewers who want to introduce Westerns to the little ones.

Before Yellowstone became the vanguard of the neo-Western, you could make an argument that FX's Justified was the show that popularized the sub-genre. Based on the novel Fire in the Hole by Elmore Leonard, Justified is a unique throwback to the Westerns of old, but with a more modern twist that contemporary Western fans would come to expect, especially after watching Yellowstone.

The series follows Raylan Givens (Timothy Olyphant), a Deputy U.S. Marshal who does his work the way Marshals did in the 1800s. This way of investigating makes him a frequent target for criminals, especially Boyd Crowder (Walton Goggins), a career criminal known for robbing banks. Just by reading that, you get the sense of what Justified is. Having an old-school Western show set in the modern age was a bold move for the show creators and runners to make, but it was one that paid off thanks to the awesome acting and intriguing storylines. If it wasn't for Yellowstone, we would be talking about Justified being the vanguard of neo-Westerns, without question.

When you think of Westerns, most people think of gunfights in the middle of town, and law enforcement officers trying to hunt down criminal bank robbers. While most of the best Westerns have these tropes, not all in the genre has to be action-packed, gun-fighting bonanzas. Take Little House on the Prairie, for example, the famed NBC Western that went the historical drama route to its claim to fame.

Based on Laura Ingalls Wider's Little House on the Prairie novel, the series follows the Ingalls family, who move to Walnut Grove, Minnesota in the 1840s, and most of the show revolves around their lives, which is told through the lens of Charles (Michael Landon) and Caroline's (Karen Grassle) middle daughter, Laura (Melissa Gilbert), and her maturation from child to adulthood. Little House on the Prairie is well-known for its story of "strength through family," with the Ingalls and other Walnut Grove townsfolks finding a way to make it through the strength of family and community. This is completely separate from what Yellowstone represents, which saw family as more of a hindrance than anything else. If you're looking for a Western drama that is more family-focused, Little House on the Prairie is definitely your show.

By the late 1950s, Westerns were the staple on television, especially for ABC, which already had a massive Western hit on their hands with The Lone Ranger. Enter Maverick, a Western comedy created by Roy Huggins which is just as iconic as the series it replaced.

More comedy-focused than The Lone Ranger, Maverick follows Bert Maverick (James Garner), a poker-playing rounder who is always on the lookout for games with high stakes. Most of the storylines within Maverick center on Maverick's high-stakes poker-playing, and this is where the comedy is born. Maverick wasn't always about poker, however. Make no mistake, this was a Western in the 1950s, so you know there were villains that were able gunslingers as well. Maverick is a truly exciting Western series, one that we hope today's Hollywood will not touch and remake. It's that timeless.

With Yellowstone being a massive hit, there was little doubt that the show was going to spawn a franchise. The first show to spin-off from its parent series was 1883, created by Taylor Sheridan, and, if one gives it a chance, would see that it actually exceeds its parent show in both story and tone.

1883 is a prequel that follows the Dutton family at the end of the Civil War. As they pack up and leave Tennessee, they begin to head west, eventually settling in Montana and putting up stakes in the land that would later become the massive Yellowstone-Dutton Ranch. Filmed as a miniseries, 1883 is a great look at how the Dutton family came to be and the struggles they endured to make a home and living for themselves in those tough times. By ditching the heightened drama and toxic vitriol of Yellowstone, 1883 is a more traditional Western drama that follows the rules, not aims to break them. So, for fans of old-school Westerns who were turned off by Yellowstone's breakneck drama, 1883 is a show that's more your speed.

Set in Dodge City, Kansas after the Civil War, Gunsmoke follows U.S. Marshal Matt Dillion (James Arness), who protects the citizens of Dodge City from criminals. When one talks about traditional Westerns, Gunsmoke is the show that is often brought up as an example of how to do it right, and exciting. You'll get it all here, from drama to exhilarating gun-fights, all packed in a single episode. With its strong Western tone, and great plotlines, there is no question Gunsmoke is certainly better than Yellowstone.

10 Best The Godfather Parodies, Ranked (Including Modern Family)

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather has provided movies with an abundance of parody opportunities. Released in 1972, the crime epic established a remarkably distinctive cinematic language, from Marlon Brando's gravelly delivery and intimidating presence to the elaborate rituals of loyalty, family gatherings, and underworld negotiations. Yet, The Godafther's influence extends well beyond gangster movies, with countless filmmakers borrowing its imagery, dialogue, performances, and instantly recognizable musical themes.

Part of what makes The Godfather such an effective target for comedy is the contrast between its characters' deadly seriousness and the situations surrounding them. Vito's quiet authority, Michael's increasingly elaborate schemes, and the formalities of organized crime are all wonderfully easy to exaggerate. Even relatively minor details, such as kissing a mob boss's hand or making somebody an offer they cannot refuse, can become hilarious when transplanted into an entirely different setting.

The Godfather’s most recognizable sequences provide an endless supply of opportunities for visual comedy. The wedding celebrations, infamous horse-head incident, and climactic baptism montage have all become familiar enough to audiences that a relatively straightforward recreation can deliver an immediate hilarious punchline.

Animaniacs has a knack for sending up popular movies within a short scene. Its recurring "Goodfeathers" segments, which follow a trio of pigeons modeled on characters from gangster cinema, introduce an especially memorable take on Vito Corleone with the Godpigeon.

An obese, 96-year-old pigeon, the Godpigeon is the boss of the Goodfeathers and makes his authority known by expecting the trio to kiss his foot whenever he appears. His raspy, largely unintelligible mumbling is a direct nod to Brando's performance in The Godfather, and he usually needs Bobby to translate what he's saying.

Despite being treated like an intimidating criminal patriarch, the Godpigeon is generally jovial and would much rather enjoy life as a pigeon than get caught up in the trio's problems. The Godpigeon also appears in several Goodfeathers segments in Animaniacs, so the joke isn't limited to one fleeting appearance.

South Park finds an appropriately petty reason to recreate one of The Godfather Part II's most chilling sequences. In "Toilet Paper," the boys toilet-paper their teacher's house, but Cartman becomes increasingly concerned that Kyle's guilt will eventually urge him to confess. His solution is to take Kyle out on a boat at Stark's Pond and kill him.

The setup deliberately echoes Michael Corleone's attempt to have his brother Fredo murdered during a fishing trip in The Godfather Part II. The accompanying music also references the original film's "Godfather Waltz," giving Cartman's ludicrous plan the sort of ominous atmosphere usually reserved for a major betrayal within a criminal empire.

Of course, Cartman isn't Michael Corleone. He's a vindictive child trying to cover up a school prank, and his murderous plan is undermined by the fact that he only has a Wiffle bat.

Angelica Pickles may be a toddler, but she already understands the value of having people owe her favors. In Rugrats in Paris: The Movie, she adopts the persona of the "Bobfather," complete with a tuxedo and her beloved doll Cynthia positioned in her lap. It's an unmistakable take on Vito Corleone's introduction in The Godfather.

Angelica's fellow babies come to her with requests, and she treats their problems like business propositions. The scene climaxes with Phil and Lil producing the broken head of a wooden rocking horse they found in their crib. They ask the Bobfather what happened, only for Angelica to explain that it's what they get for wiping their boogers on Cynthia.

It's a wonderfully silly adaptation of the infamous horse-head scene, transforming one of cinema's most disturbing threats into a punishment for a pair of toddlers with poor manners.

Jim Abrahams’ Mafia! is an entire comedy built around sending up gangster movies, with The Godfather and The Godfather Part II among its principal targets. It depicts the Cortino family's rise from Sicily to the criminal underworld of America, with the story moving between generations in a deliberately familiar gangster-movie structure.

Mafia!'s most direct Brando parody comes through Lloyd Bridges as the older Vincenzo Cortino. Bridges gets to send up the famous tomato-and-death scene, while the movie repeatedly turns familiar mafia conventions into opportunities for ridiculous sight gags.

Even the family's journey to America gets a joke, when Ellis Island officials attempt to rename the immigrant Vincenzo after his Armani windbreaker. It may not be as consistently sharp as Abrahams' Airplane!, but its affectionate mockery of gangster cinema gives it plenty of entertaining material.

One of the funniest uses of The Godfather in modern animation comes from Disney's Zootopia, which introduces Mr. Big, an Arctic shrew who happens to be one of the city's most intimidating crime bosses. The character recreates Brando's Corleone with a tiny body, a raspy whisper, a formal tuxedo, and an entourage of enormous polar bear enforcers.

This contrast is particularly funny, juxtaposing the gangster grandeur with the diminutive creature. Like The Godfather, Judy Hopps and Nick Wilde are brought before Mr. Big during his daughter Fru Fru's wedding reception.

The entire sequence deliberately recreates the atmosphere and staging of the original film's opening, with Judy and Nick discovering that their investigation has taken them into considerably more dangerous territory. The filmmakers even recreated elements of the original film's set and dialogue.

Homer Simpson's brief career as a Krusty the Clown impersonator takes an unexpected turn when he is mistaken for the real Krusty and becomes embroiled with the Springfield Mafia. In The Simpsons season 6, "Homie the Clown," Homer is captured by Fat Tony to repay Krusty’s debt.

The episode's climactic sequence features Homer attempting to entertain Fat Tony and his associates with a dangerous bicycle stunt. While Fat Tony and his associates are already a Godfather spoof, the episode’s best reference sees Homer hit his head on a series of hanging wine glasses that ring out Nino Rota's famous "Love Theme” from The Godfather.

The mob standoff is perfectly executed and prompts some genuine tension. Of course, The Simpsons quickly undermines this, with the reveal that Krusty’s debt is just $48 becoming the biggest joke.

The Muppets have tackled plenty of famous movies, and their Godfather spoof gives Kermit the Frog a suitably imposing new identity as the Frogfather. In this 1999 Movie Mania short, Fozzie Bear visits him to ask for revenge against Statler and Waldorf, whose constant heckling has finally gone too far.

The premise is already funny because Fozzie's request is essentially a complaint about two elderly audience members who won't stop telling jokes at his expense. Kermit plays the part with the serious demeanor of Brando's Vito Corleone, treating the matter as though it concerns a genuine threat to the family business.

Naturally, the Muppets can't resist turning the idea of a mob hit into something considerably sillier. The Frogfather dispatches his henchmen to carry out a classic, non-lethal execution – smashing cream pies straight into the faces of Statler and Waldorf to permanently silence their heckling

Before Fat Tony became one of The Simpsons’ most recognizable supporting characters, he made his debut in "Bart the Murderer." The season 3 episode sees Bart stumble into a job at a mafia social club after a series of unfortunate events leaves him working as the group's bartender.

The episode draws on both The Godfather and Goodfellas, with Bart becoming increasingly comfortable around the mobsters as they treat him like one of their own. He serves drinks, listens to their stories, and enjoys the perks of his new employment, even as the gang's criminal activities become harder to ignore.

Things take a darker turn when Principal Skinner disappears after Fat Tony and his crew threaten to deal with him. The parody draws much of its humor from dropping a schoolboy into the criminal organization and letting him enjoy the lifestyle before discovering the consequences.

Marlon Brando parodying his own most famous role is an irresistible premise, and Andrew Bergman's The Freshman makes excellent use of it. Brando plays Carmine Sabatini, a powerful New York importer whose resemblance to Vito Corleone is so striking that Matthew Broderick's film student, Clark Kellogg, is immediately taken aback when they meet.

Clark initially accepts a job with Carmine after being introduced by Victor, a small-time crook played by Bruno Kirby. His work soon becomes considerably stranger than he anticipated. One of his first assignments is collecting a Komodo dragon from the airport and delivering it to a secretive gourmet club that specializes in endangered animals.

Brando wholly leans into the mannerisms and screen presence that made Vito Corleone so recognizable. Carmine is imposing, sentimental, and capable of making even an ordinary conversation feel like a negotiation. It's a wonderfully silly extension of his most iconic role.

Modern Family's "Fulgencio" perfectly recreates The Godfather’s famous baptism montage. Like the original, it intercuts the religious ceremony with a series of acts of revenge.

As Phil renounces Satan and all his works during the baptism, his son Luke is busy settling various suburban grievances on his behalf. He releases rats into Karen Sullivan's party, causes Lee Cooper's camper to roll into a lemon tree, shoots balloons covering Phil's billboard, and gets school bully Reuben to admit that he has been lying – who wakes up to discover the head of a stuffed zebra in his bed.

The sequence also plays with the original Godfather's closing image when Phil closes a door on Claire after she starts asking questions. It’s so unexpected but so perfect that it stands as the greatest Godfather parody.

How much new business did Bloom win on Nov. 5? Less than the headlines may suggest, because just one of the two deals is new. I think the other still matters, though, and the stock looks much more reasonably priced than it did in October.

Google won't buy the fuel cells itself. American Electric Power plans to invest around $4 billion in the 702-megawatt project near Cheyenne, Wyoming, the utility said on Oct. 14. It'll sell the plant's output to Google under a 20-year power purchase agreement, and it expects the plant to be fully running by late 2028.

The fuel cell order came months earlier. In January, an AEP subsidiary agreed to buy solid oxide fuel cells for around $2.65 billion for a plant near Cheyenne. The purchase used most of an option for 900 extra megawatts under AEP's 2024 supply deal with Bloom.

For a sense of scale, that one order is worth over half of Bloom's full-year revenue forecast of $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion for 2026.

So, Google's news didn't add to Bloom's orders. The fuel cells were bought in January, and AEP had already named Google as the plant's customer in October.

That still means something, I think. A fuel cell order backed by a 20-year power contract with Google looks more likely to become revenue on time than one linked to an unnamed buyer.

NTT DATA's deal is the new business, and the companies called it Bloom's biggest international project so far. NTT's data center arm plans to use the fuel cells as its main power source, with the relationship meant to reach gigawatt scale across its global portfolio.

What the release didn't say matters, too. It gave no dollar value, no project locations, and no timeline beyond first shipments starting in 2027. This deal shouldn't show up in Bloom's 2026 revenue, and Bloom hasn't said how much of it falls in 2027 or later.

But 900 megawatts is a lot of fuel cells -- nearly a full year of output at the 1 gigawatt of annual production capacity Bloom's Fremont, California, plant had going into 2026. The company planned to double that capacity by year-end.

Bloom needs deals like this, because its quarter-to-quarter growth slowed sharply in the third quarter. Second-quarter revenue of $1.07 billion was 42% above the first quarter's $751 million. Third-quarter revenue then climbed 16% sequentially to $1.24 billion, up 138% year over year. And October's raised outlook (management's third raise this year) implies a sequential gain of 9% to 25% in the fourth quarter.

That's still strong growth. Keeping it up into 2027 and 2028, though, depends on deals like NTT DATA's moving from announcement to shipment.

Based on the midpoint of management's adjusted earnings forecast for 2026 ($3.10 to $3.40 a share), Bloom stock cost around 52 times earnings at its Nov. 5 close. The same forecast had it at around 67 times earnings at the $217.77 close on Oct. 22. On next year's expected earnings, shares now trade at around 31 times earnings.

Still, 52 times earnings is a premium price. It assumes Bloom keeps growing quickly well past this year.

But Bloom landed a big new deal on Nov. 5 as its shares got much cheaper.

Of course, a slower U.S. data center build-out might delay some deliveries. And October's weak spot hasn't gone away. Bloom's operations burned $27 million in cash in the third quarter, mostly because it booked revenue faster than it could bill customers for it.

Is Bloom stock worth buying after a 16% one-day drop? I'd consider buying some shares at this price, a few at a time. And I'd maybe add more when billing catches up with revenue.

10 Apple products and features that transformed under Tim Cook's leadership

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple changed a lot in the last 15 years.

When Tim Cook became Apple's CEO in 2011, he began leading what was already one of the world's most successful technology companies. The iPhone was driving Apple's growth, the iPad was still relatively new and the Mac was at the center of its computing business. Cook inherited an enormous ecosystem, then he spent 15 years finding new ways it could be put to use.

By the end of Cook's tenure, Apple had introduced entirely new product categories, moved the Mac to its own processors, turned the iPhone into a wallet and satellite communicator and built a services business worth more than $100 billion a year. Meanwhile, some of Apple's biggest changes weren't new products, but rather familiar ones that slowly evolved into something else.

There are now more than 2.5 billion active Apple devices, while the company brought in $416.2 billion in net sales in fiscal 2025. Apple's announcement on Cook's transition lays out the scale of the company he leaves behind. But the more interesting story is how under his leadership, Apple products took on entirely new forms and jobs.

When the Apple Watch launched in 2015, it was primarily an iPhone companion for notifications, communication, apps and activity tracking. Apple began expanding its health and fitness capabilities with Apple Watch Series 2, which added built-in GPS, swim tracking and more workout features.

The bigger shift came with Apple Watch Series 4 in 2018. With this, Apple added an ECG app and fall detection, giving the Watch capabilities that went beyond basics like tracking steps. The ECG could record a heart rhythm directly from the wrist, while fall detection could automatically contact emergency services if the wearer appeared to have taken a hard fall and remained immobile.

The watchOS 11 update in 2024 added sleep apnea notifications, the Vitals app and Training Load, giving you more ways to track and interpret health and fitness data. In 2025, the Apple Watch Series 11 introduced sleep scoring and hypertension notifications. While the watch started as an accessory for your iPhone, it now monitors a whole suite of metrics that the iPhone can't.

AirPods could have been just a pair of wireless earbuds for your iPhone, but Apple kept giving them more reasons to exist within the larger Apple ecosystem. Automatic pairing, seamless switching between devices, active noise cancellation and spatial audio made them an attractive option for all your music and other audio.

Apple has given AirPods an important role beyond entertainment, though. Starting with AirPods Pro 2, Apple added a scientifically validated Hearing Test, Hearing Aid functionality for people with mild to moderate hearing loss and Hearing Protection. These hearing health tools make support more accessible to lots of people. Meanwhile, the AirPods Pro 3 added heart rate sensing to track your workout effort with the buds you're likely already wearing while exercising.

Under Cook, Apple took a familiar product and expanded what it could do, without asking people to adopt an entirely new device.

For years, Apple built its own chips for the iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch while relying on Intel processors for the Mac. That changed in 2020, when Apple announced a two-year transition to its own silicon. The move gave Mac computers a common architecture with Apple's other products, providing developers with a more unified platform for building software across the ecosystem.

The first Apple silicon Macs arrived later that year. Apple put the M1 chip in the MacBook Air, 13-inch MacBook Pro and Mac mini, combining the CPU, GPU, Neural Engine and other components into a single system-on-a-chip. This helped Apple push the Mac toward the same performance and power efficiency that had come to define its mobile devices.

The difference between Apple's processors and Intel's isn't just speed. After Apple took over this core piece of Mac hardware, it had holistic control of the hardware design, chips inside and macOS itself. This helped the Mac overcome its midlife crisis.

What started as a convenient way to snap a picture became a sophisticated camera system with multiple lenses, computational photography, Night mode and some serious video capabilities.

The iPhone 11 was a big turning point. It featured an Ultra Wide camera, Night mode and Deep Fusion, which uses machine learning to process photos pixel by pixel for more detail and less noise. The iPhone 12 Pro then added Apple ProRAW, combining Apple's computational photography with the flexibility of a RAW file for those who wanted more control over their images.

Apple's more recent iPhones have continued this progress. The iPhone 15 Pro brought a 48MP main camera, multiple focal-length options, 48MP ProRAW and professional video features including ProRes recording to external storage. Our iPhone 15 review highlighted the camera as a major part of the upgrade. Later, our iPhone 17 review covered more camera improvements, including a new Center Stage front camera and upgraded rear cameras. For many people, their iPhone camera is the way they take pictures, and what all other smartphone cameras are judged against.

Apple Pay is easy to take for granted because it feels so normal now. But in 2014, putting a payment system directly into the iPhone people already carried everywhere gave the device a new role: replacing the cards in their wallet. Apple Pay eventually expanded beyond in-store purchases to apps, websites and other Apple devices like the Apple Watch.

10 years after its launch, the company stated that Apple Pay was used by hundreds of millions of people across 78 regions, at millions of websites and apps and tens of millions of stores. It was also supported by more than 11,000 banks and "network partners" at that milestone. Apple Pay later expanded to third-party iOS and desktop browsers, providing more ways to check out online without typing your card details into every site.

That idea of leaving your physical wallet behind has grown beyond payments, too. Wallet can hold other items like event tickets, transit cards, keys and even eligible IDs. In 2025, Apple started expanding its Digital ID for use with participating US states, plus support for Japan's My Number Card. Expanded Apple Wallet ticket features have also added venue maps, parking directions and other event information.

Some of the iPhone's biggest changes under Cook had more to do with connectivity than the phone itself, as Apple kept expanding the ways your iPhone can connect to the world around it. The iPhone 12 brought 5G to Apple's phones in 2020, while MagSafe introduced a ring of magnets that made wireless charging easier and created an all-new suite of snap-on accessories.

The iPhone 14 added something even more ambitious: direct satellite connections to message emergency services when cellular and Wi-Fi coverage isn't available. Apple also added the ability to share a location through Find My via satellite. In 2024, satellite connectivity was expanded to support Messages via satellite for messaging friends when you have no other options.

Not everything that changed under Tim Cook came in a box. Apple's services business grew from a relatively small part of the company into a $109.2 billion business, making it a core part of Apple's revenue alongside the iPhone.

The category is broader than just subscriptions. It includes the App Store, advertising, cloud services, Apple Music, Apple TV, Apple Pay and more. In 2025, the App Store alone averaged more than 850 million weekly users, while Apple said developers had earned more than $550 billion through the platform since 2008. Apple's services revenue also hit a quarterly record in early 2026, topping $30 billion.

Hardware still accounts for most of Apple's revenue, but the company's various services provide a huge business built around what people do after they buy an Apple device.

Apple had been selling digital content long before the Apple TV streaming service (formerly called Apple TV+) arrived in 2019. But with it, the company was no longer just offering access to content made by others. Apple TV+ launched with an original catalog of shows, movies and documentaries, including The Morning Show, See and For All Mankind.

Apple quickly built a reputation around those originals. CODA became the first streaming film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture in 2022, while shows like Ted Lasso and Severance became some of the service's biggest hits.

Apple TV+ earned a record 81 Emmy nominations across 14 Apple Original titles in 2025, led by Severance with 27 nominations and The Studio with 23. Both shows also took home awards that year. By September 2025, Apple said its originals had accumulated 620 wins and 2,816 award nominations.

Vision Pro is different from almost everything else Apple released under Cook. Instead of putting another screen in front of you, Apple built a computer that adds your digital life into the space around you. When Apple unveiled Vision Pro in 2023, it called the device its first "spatial computer," with visionOS and an interface controlled by your eyes, hands and voice.

What may be more interesting than the $3,499 headset is where Apple thinks computing could go next. Apps can float around a room instead of being confined to a traditional display, which provides immersive environments with three-dimensional content. More than 600 apps and games built specifically for Vision Pro were available when it launched.

As our M5 Vision Pro review found, the device is unlikely to become the next big Apple platform unless future revisions make it more accessible. But that's why it's worth a mention here: Vision Pro is Apple trying to create a new platform instead of solely relying on what's worked in the past.

The premise of an AirTag is simple: attach one to something you don't want to lose and your iPhone will help you find it. Its bigger innovation is how the tracker taps into Apple's existing Find My network, which uses nearby Apple devices to detect an AirTag and anonymously report its approximate location to its owner. An AirTag doesn't need its own cellular connection or GPS to be useful.

Apple describes AirTag as "the bestselling item finder", and continues expanding what it can do. The second-generation AirTag, released in 2026, added a longer Bluetooth range and more precise finding. Apple's Share Item Location feature also lets you securely share an item's location with participating airlines to help recover lost luggage.

The second-generation AirTag's primary usefulness comes from Apple's enormous network of devices, giving it access to a vast web to help locate a lost item.

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

As the saying goes, good things come to those who wait. Well, as Rivals fans, we've done our fair share of waiting. After its first season premiered in October 2024, the sleeper hit series quickly became a favorite, both for longtime fans of Jilly Cooper and new viewers discovering her world for the first time. Now, after a five-month hiatus between Part 1 and Part 2, the raunchy, soapy series is finally paying off the promise it made back in May of this year. Despite some issues with pacing — which can be almost entirely blamed on the bizarre split-season release — Rivals Season 2 proves it can not only embrace the glitz and glamor of Cooper's world, but also forge its own path and create something new.

Following the shocking death of Monica Baddingham (Claire Rushbrook), all of Rutshire is in shambles. Tony (David Tennant) is spiraling deeper into the darkness; Rupert (Alex Hassell) has been thoroughly shaken after it's revealed that Monica spent her dying moments with him, and the O'Haras are on the brink of collapse with the secret of Maud (Victoria Smurfit) and Tony's affair hanging over them like the Sword of Damocles. Although Part 2 has its share of celebratory moments — there's a bonfire night, a debauched toga party, and a wedding celebration for the ages — the ghost of Monica and the fallout from Episode 6 haunt this half of Season 2.

For much of Episodes 7 to 10, the show loses some of that bubbliness that has become signature to the series. There's still big hair and tons of sex with some globe-trotting tossed in, but there's a sense that some light has left Rutshire. Your mileage may vary with how successful this tonal shift is, especially for those who enjoyed the show for its levity; Part 2 is full of fights, tears, threats, and broken hearts. If you're familiar with Cooper's books, this is probably less of a surprise, but even those who know the 1988 novel from back to front will be hit with shocking twists they never expected.

Rivalry, sex, and deceit are the best themes for an addictive TV show.

The person who shines the most in this darkness is Tennant, whose Tony Baddingham is absolutely riveting to watch and tops even his book counterpart. He's darker than he's ever been, but he's not twirling his mustache and cackling maniacally; he's a man who has been utterly torn apart and is at his most dangerous. We Tracked The World's Longest Airbus A321XLR Flight: Here's The Detour It Takes Every Day

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

On October 25, IndiGo started operating its flights between Mumbai Airport (BOM) and Amsterdam Schiphol Airport(AMS) with the Airbus A321XLR, replacing the leased Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners previously used on the route. At approximately 3,707 nautical miles (6,865 km), this is now the world's longest scheduled route operated by the A321XLR, with a scheduled flight time of around 10 hours from India to the Netherlands.

However, the actual distance flown is considerably longer due to airspace restrictions across the Middle East and South Asia. Using the Simple Flying Flight Tracker, we examined the routing of flight 22 from Amsterdam to Mumbai on November 1, as well as the flight times recorded during the aircraft's first 11 days on the route. Interestingly, the data shows that although the A321XLR is smaller and generally slower than the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner, its average flight time to Amsterdam has been shorter.

On November 1, IndiGo flight 22 departed Amsterdam at 2:31 PM local time, operated by an A321XLR registered VT-NLC. According to our flight tracking data, the aircraft spent approximately nine hours and nine minutes in the air before landing in Mumbai at 4:10 AM local time the following morning. Rather than following the shortest possible route toward India, the aircraft followed a more southern trajectory through Europe and the Middle East.

After departing Amsterdam, the aircraft headed southeast across Central Europe before continuing toward Turkey and Iraq. From there, the route passed through the Gulf region and Oman, avoiding Iranian airspace, before continuing toward India's western coast and eventually Mumbai. The tracking data shows how the aircraft avoids both Pakistan and Iran, despite these countries lying along the more direct route between Mumbai and Amsterdam.

The distance between Mumbai and Amsterdam is about 3,707 nautical miles (6,865 km) along the shortest route. However, according to Aerospace Global News, the necessary detours can increase the actual distance flown to approximately 4,200 nautical miles (7,778 km), or around 13% longer. This puts the flight much closer to the A321XLR's advertised maximum range of 4,700 nautical miles (8,704 km).

IndiGo's 8-hour Bali flights showcase the A321XLR's potential to transform aviation route planning as we know it.

One of the main reasons for IndiGo's detour is the ongoing closure of Pakistani airspace to Indian airlines, in place since April 2025 following rising tensions between the two countries. According to the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), the restrictions apply to aircraft registered in India, as well as those owned, operated, or leased by Indian carriers. Pakistan has since extended the restrictions several times.

At the same time, the ongoing conflict in the Middle East has also made flying through Iran increasingly difficult. Although Iranian airspace is not subject to the same ban for Indian airlines, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) currently advises operators to avoid Iranian airspace at all altitudes.

For IndiGo, Pakistan's restrictions and the security situation in the Middle East considerably reduce available routing options between India and Europe. The airline must therefore follow a longer route, which increases fuel requirements and operating costs. The implications are particularly significant for the A321XLR, as the aircraft has far less flexibility to carry additional fuel and passengers on such a long flight than a widebody such as the Boeing 787-9 previously used on the route.

We tracked a Widerøe Dash 8 through 17 sectors in one day. The shortest flight lasted seven minutes.

Although Airbus advertises the A321XLR with a range of up to 4,700 nautical miles (8,704 km), the actual range depends on several operational factors, including passenger weight, cargo, fuel reserves, and weather conditions. The additional distance flown between Mumbai and Amsterdam consequently puts the aircraft much closer to its operational limits. To ensure that the flight can be completed nonstop, IndiGo reportedly restricts the number of seats it makes available for sale.

According to avicionline, IndiGo is currently selling fewer than 140 seats on the Mumbai-Amsterdam leg, despite its A321XLRs having a total capacity of 195 passengers. The aircraft are configured with 12 IndiGoStretch business class seats and another 183 economy class seats, meaning that over 25% of capacity is reportedly unavailable for sale. However, this restriction is not necessarily identical in both directions, as the aircraft's fuel requirements may differ depending on weather and other operating conditions.

Compared with the 787-9 previously operated on the route, this represents a considerable reduction in available capacity. The Norse Atlantic Airways aircraft offered 338 seats, including 56 premium and 282 economy class seats, according to IndiGo's original route announcement. While the A321XLR already has 143 fewer installed seats, additional restrictions on the Mumbai-Amsterdam leg mean almost 200 fewer seats may be available than on the previous widebody operation.

Interestingly, analysis of Flight Tracker data indicates that in the first 11 days of A321XLR operations, the aircraft spent less time in the air on flights from Mumbai to Amsterdam than the Boeing 787-9. Between October 25 and November 4, flight 6E21 recorded an average airborne time of nine hours and 36 minutes, approximately 20 minutes shorter than the average of nine hours and 56 minutes recorded by the 787-9 during its final nine flights. However, the opposite was true in the other direction: the A321XLR averaged nine hours to fly from Amsterdam to Mumbai, compared with eight hours and 31 minutes for the 787-9.

Looking at the individual flights, the fastest A321XLR service from Mumbai to Amsterdam was recorded on October 28, when VT-NLD completed the journey in nine hours and 19 minutes. The longest was the first flight on October 25, when the same aircraft spent nine hours and 52 minutes in the air. For comparison, the longest 787-9 flight during the period examined took 10 hours and 15 minutes on October 20. On the return journey, the fastest A321XLR service took eight hours and 32 minutes, while the longest required nine hours and 14 minutes, compared with a range of eight hours and 19 minutes to eight hours and 43 minutes for the 787-9.

However, this does not necessarily mean the A321XLR is operating more efficiently or at higher speeds than the 787-9. The two aircraft were operating at different times of day, with the 787-9 previously scheduled to depart Mumbai at 12:25 PM and Amsterdam at 8:35 PM local time, compared with 6:25 AM and 2:15 PM for the A321XLR. The comparison also covers two different periods around the start of the winter schedule, meaning wind conditions, air traffic restrictions, and the actual routings flown may have differed. The 787-9 also generally cruises faster than the A321XLR, making it difficult to attribute differences in airborne time to the aircraft types themselves.
