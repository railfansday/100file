Autonomous combat drone maker Shield AI buys radar startup Echodyne for $5.3B

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Defense technology startup Shield AI Inc. said today it has agreed to buy the Bill Gates-backed radar startup Echodyne Inc. in a massive deal valued at $5.3 billion. The acquisition is intended to enhance Shield’s autonomous drone warfighting capabilities, it said.

Echodyne is a 13-year-old startup that’s best known for its Metamaterials Electronically Scanned Array or MESA technology, which is basically just a fancy name for its next-generation solid-state radar systems that can be integrated with a wide range of weapons and monitoring systems. The technology is designed to detect much smaller targets than traditional radar, including the kinds of compact autonomous drones that have terrorized soldiers on the battlefields of Ukraine and in other modern conflicts.

The deal comes just a couple of months after Echodyne landed a $250 million contract with the U.S. Defense Department’s counter-drone task force JIATF-401, which will see it provide drone tracking technology to the U.S. military.

Echodyne’s advanced radar systems look like a natural fit for Shield AI, which is the developer of the X-Bat and V-Bat vertical takeoff and landing systems, known as VTOL drones. Shield’s V-Bat hit the headlines in the last couple of years after assisting the U.S. Coast Guard in tracking down drug traffickers looking to bring illicit cargoes into the U.S. via speedboats, and notably participated in a number of large maritime drug busts. Shield AI is also the developer of Hivemind, an autonomous software system for piloting unmanned drones, as well as pilotless boats and spacecraft.

Shield AI Chief Executive Gary Steele said the deal will enable his company to accelerate its goal of branching into three different areas of unmanned systems. “We are building a new-generation defense technology company consisting of three fast-growing, high-margin, mutually supporting business units: autonomy software, autonomous systems and mission systems,” he said.

Echodyne co-founder and CEO Eben Frankenberg said Shield AI is a natural partner for his company because it shares the view that autonomous systems must be able to sense their environment to achieve their desired mission outcomes. “By joining Shield, we’re creating a company with the size and breadth to deliver even more value to our broad range of defense, government and commercial customers around the globe,” he insisted.

According to Steele, Echodyne will continue to operate as a standalone entity, as a separate business unit within Shield AI’s mission systems business. Frankenberg will remain with the company, where he will report to Steele as part of its senior executive team. Echodyne is not going to layoff any staff, and nobody will be asked to relocate either, he said. In the longer term, the companies expect tighter integration of Echodyne’s radar systems with Shield AI’s drones.

The deal will see $4.8 billion paid out upon closing, with the other $500 million dependent on Echodyne meeting certain performance targets, which have not been disclosed. Shield AI said it was able to fund the deal with a combination of its own cash and a $2.5 billion cash injection that came via a Series H funding round led by Blackstone and JPMorgan Chase, which was also announced today.

Shield AI has a history of making decisive acquisitions. Its V-Bat and X-Bat drones are heavily based on technology developed by a company called Martin UAV Systems Inc. that it acquired in 2021. In June, it purchased Aechelon Technologies Inc., a developer of high-fidelity simulation and physics-based sensor modeling software, for an undisclosed price.

Steele added that the company may not be done with acquisitions, and will continue to look at making more deals in order to accelerate its growth strategy.

Forget ‘The Traitors,’ Alan Cumming’s 2-Season Apple TV Flop Demands a Weekend Binge

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Alan Cumming is one of the most versatile actors ever. He began as a Tony-winning stage actor and won the hearts of every millennial in films like the Spy Kids trilogy, Romy and Michele's High School Reunion, and Spice World. He then switched to television, where his role as shark lawyer, Eli Gold, on The Good Wife netted him three Emmy nominations. The role that Cumming is perhaps most known for right now is his hosting duties over on The Traitors, a show which has landed him six Emmy wins. Cumming might be crushing his over-the-top Shakespearean-like leadership, complete with a stunning array of colorful costumes, but there's one other binge-worthy project that never got to flourish properly that he shines in.

Schmigadoon! first premiered on Apple TV back in 2021. The parody musical comedy, created by Cinco Paul and Ken Daurio, is an inventive take on the musical art form. The first season, consisting of just six episodes, pays tribute to the Golden Age of musicals of the 1940s and 1950s. With a focus on Brigadoon, and nods to Oklahoma, South Pacific, and Carousel, the show follows two New York-based doctors named Melissa (Cecily Strong) and Josh (Keegan-Michael Key). They decide to go backpacking to try and fix their relationship problems, but on their trip, they discover the mystical town of Schmigadoon. The kooky village seems to exist in some ephemeral past, and there are all kinds of stereotypical, yet hilarious characters who have the tendency to break out into elaborate song and dance musical numbers. Melissa and Josh realize that they can't leave the town until they find true love and break the spell. The second season of Schmigadoon! tackles an entirely different era of musicals. Released in 2023, the six-episode season is aptly named Schmicago, and features parodies of musicals from the 1960s and 1970s, like Chicago, Cabaret, and Sweeney Todd.

Every one of these four started as a cast recording people played until the disc wore out, and every one of them eventually turned into a film. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for it being the best of the four.

You work like the clock is against you, because in your head it always is. Hamilton is three hours about a man who cannot stop producing, cannot stop arguing and cannot leave a single thing alone, and you recognised yourself in it immediately.

People made up their minds about you early and they were wrong. Wicked takes the most famous villain in American children's fiction and asks what the story looks like from her side, which is a question you have asked about yourself more than once.

You cannot walk past something unfair. Les Mis is a show about a man who spends his entire life trying to make one wrong right, and about everybody around him deciding what they are prepared to lose for other people.

You are not interested in saving it all for later. Rent is a year in the life of people with no money, no plan and a very clear sense that the time they have is the only thing they genuinely own.

Schmigadoon! is a completely unique show because it's so brilliantly written. There are plenty of fun storylines, and some of the plots that you might already love from these traditional musicals get turned on their heads in a delightfully tongue-in-cheek way. These homages to past memorable musicals are a tad irreverent, but they also focus on the elements that made these shows so popular in the first place. The series is truly at its best during the big-budget musical numbers that are sprinkled throughout each episode. With songs in Season 1 like 'Corn Puddin,' which landed the series an Emmy for Outstanding Original Music and Lyrics, "You Can't Tame Me," "I Always, Always, Never Get My Man," and Kristin Chenoweth's showstopping performance of "Tribulation," Schmigadoon! definitely features catchy, toe-tapping music that will have you wanting to sing along.

It's time to get on your feet and dance!!

Both seasons of Schmigadoon! offer a completely stellar cast of performers. Key and Strong are grounded anchors in this fantastical world, but there are a plethora of other talented actors including Chenoweth, Fred Armisen, Dove Cameron, Aaron Tveit, Ariana DeBose, Jane Krakowski, and Martin Short. Despite this stacked cast, Cumming is a true standout. In Season 1, he portrays the mayor of Schmigadoon, a closeted gay man named Aloysius Menlove who sings the amusing "Somewhere Love Is Waiting For You." Season 2 offers Cumming the chance to chew on even more scenery in an altogether different role as Dooley Blight, a Sweeney Todd rip-off. Through several dark, yet hilarious songs, Cumming manages to make the character even more memorable. He never fails to land each comedic beat, and his beautiful baritone nails every song he takes on.

Although the show was a hit with reviewers, Schmigadoon! was disappointingly cancelled after Season 2 and never seemed to gain the traction with audiences that it needed. In an interesting twist, the show has found new life in a stage musical also written by Paul that debuted on Broadway in April. The show won four Tony Awards this year, proving that the magic of Schmigadoon was transferable from screen to stage. Cumming definitely has his dance card full right now, with his recent role in the miniseries, Tip Toe, as well as his continued participation in the massive hit that is The Traitors, but Schmigadoon! is still certainly worth checking out to get another glimpse of Cumming's immense talents.

Schmigadoon! is available to stream on Apple TV in the U.S.

New Hampshire

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The penultimate state! Wow. Not only did it feel amazing to leave Vermont (finally!) but walking into such a highly anticipated state was surreal. Also Singe’s home state and homecoming! Even though I had never hiked in a lot of the prior states, I had at least visited a majority of them. But from the moment I stepped foot into New Hampshire, everything was brand new to me. I was trying to convey that New Hampshire and Maine would be new states for me but what I ended up saying was, “New state forever. Or…wait. How would a normal person say that?” To which Loon said, “How about ‘I’ve never been here before.'” lol.

Singe, Loon, and I walked across the Connecticut River into New Hampshire on a sweltering summer day in early August. Our first order of business? Jump right into the river. Before strolling into Hanover, right after the bridge, you can take a left, walk to the Dartmouth dock, ignore all the signs about it being for students and staff only, and jump right in. It felt AMAZING. I cannot emphasize enough how hot the sun was and how exposed the concrete bridge is. The river was full of weird cold pockets that we enjoyed to the max. Once we had cooled down, we finally walked into Hanover! I was super excited because I’m a little nerd who loves college campuses and Hanover being both a college town and a hiker town had always stuck with me.

Hanover was so cute and Dartmouth was so beautiful. Shocking. We found the Dartmouth Outdoors Club and left our packs in their care. Then we walked around Hanover and cashed in on some awesome hike friendly deals! We got free pizza for hikers and were completely entranced by the Housecleaning Games on ESPN 8: The Ocho. My favorite part? A poster that said “Ouchtown: Population YOU.” Incredible. We got handmade ice cream at Red Kite Candy which remains one of my most memorable ice cream stops on trail. I had Strawberry Balsamic. Thank you for asking. We grabbed our packs and walked around campus a little before getting picked up to stay with one of Singe’s good friends in town. It was so exciting to get a look into Singe’s life and meet the characters and friends she’s been talking about all trail!

We had an awesome night getting to know one of Singe’s oldest friends and having a homemade pot roast. YUM. Also this night, I got an email that I got off the waitlist for a marathon the first weekend of October. In Connecticut I started toying with the idea of running a marathon after trail just to see what would happen. When I received this email offering me a spot, I went for it. Turns out, this is the same marathon that Loon’s friend, who we have seen multiple times on trail, was training for! Done and done. The rest of the hike, this marathon was a sort of bit. I would forget I signed up and someone would mention it and I’d be like “oh RIGHT! The marathon! Yes, of course.” What a bit.

The next day, we were able to leave most of our things at Singe’s friend’s house because we were staying with another Singe friend connection the next night! We walked over Velvet Rocks, right off the campus of Dartmouth, and to our pick up spot near the Ice Cream Man’s house where I discovered my new favorite Key Lime popsicle. We got to hang out with the cutest dog’s at Singe’s friend’s parents’ house and enjoy their company and a delicious home-cooked meal.

We were back to it with full packs this time. We were coming up close to Singe’s house and sections of trail she’s done several times. The plan was for Loon and I to spend the night out on trail but Singe was going to get picked up by her partner and stay at her house. We would join them the next day. It feels weird to be apart from Singe for even a night but I loved having a night with just Loon. We plotted to stay at Hexacuba Shelter because it seems weird and cool. Like I’ve said many times before, you have to look for reasons to get excited for the day. Hexacuba is a shelter in a hexagon shape with a pentagon shaped privy. Obviously, we had to see that. It turned out to be just the two of us at the shelter that night so we threw up our tents inside to have some protection from the bugs. We had the sweetest night camping and laughing together.

We climbed up and over Smarts Mountain and onto the fire tower the day before Hexacuba and Cube Mountain the morning after Hexacuba. I loved both! On top of Cube Mountain, we found a rude comment someone had left on a post of mine that had been shared and honestly had a field day with it. This person’s mean comment (and others they would continue to leave on my posts later) became one of our tramily’s favorite inside jokes. We took it well and not seriously but a little reminder: If you don’t have anything nice to say, don’t say it! This can really hurt people! Especially online!

Anywho! After clearing these two mountains, covering more ground, and taking a perfect midday lake swim, Singe picked us up and we went back to her house! It was so fun to see where our trail bestie lived after talking about it for so many miles. Spoiler: It’s beautiful. We took a zero with her and got to see some of New Hampshire that’s off trail too. I didn’t know I was going to fall in love with New Hampshire so quickly! There was a lot of anticipation because as soon as we left the comfiness of Singe’s house, we would be entering the Whites. But of course we were able to scheme a slack-pack and were able to not bring full packs over Mt Moosilauke by dropping our things off at The Notch Hostel a day early.

I was quite nervous and intimidated of the Whites before we started. But step by step, we ascended the mountain. I actually flew up to the top pretty fast. It’s difficult, but I think I had it built up so much in my mind that it was better than I expected. I did fall in a little tiny creek on the way up (lol) but it didn’t get me down. And when I broke the treeline? I was so speechless and energized that nothing really mattered. I kept squealing to myself out of excitement. The terrain is gorgeous and we had a beautiful day. I loved being able to see the path ahead of me and the progress I was making pushing to the summit. There were a ton of people hiking at the top but I was in my own little world taking it all in…until I heard a group to my left singing out “You’re a bear and I’m a girlllll.” AKA the Song of the Summer AKA the song I sang to a bear when I had a close encounter back in New York. I looked over and our trail cousins, The Binguses were sitting together serenading me. What a welcome to the top! This was the first of several mountain top reunions that kept happening in the Whites. So much fun. I went over and had lunch with them and waited for Singe and Loon to summit. We hadn’t seen the Binguses since we were all in New York City together in early July! We also got to meet a new Bingus they’d picked up along the way in Vermont.

We all hung out together at the top. Apparently some kind of eclipse was happening but not a full eclipse so we didn’t end up sticking around to see it from the top since the weather looked like it might be turning too. We all bunched up together for a picture on the summit that a day hiker took. It looked like she was taking several. She said to us, “How many do you want?” So we figured we’d have good options. But when she returned the phone, we had exactly three pictures. lol. Us Golden Girls and the Binguses all tackled the steep and endless descent together. We stopped a few times to look at the pretty waterfalls that follow the trail. It was so fun to be back with them again for this hike. We all got to the parking lot and waited for the shuttle to the Notch. A few of the Binguses weren’t staying at the hostel so they found a hitch into town.

We got picked up to head to the Notch by the owner, Serena, who also happens to be Singe’s good friend and climbing partner in normal life! She was awesome! Please stay at the Notch! They are so great and have their systems figured out so smoothly. Serena told the 6 of us in the shuttle (3 Golden Girls + 3 of the Binguses) that a local outdoor apparel company, Burgeon, wanted to invite 6 hikers for a pan pasta dinner tonight. So of course we were like, “we’ll we’re 6 hikers…” And voila! We checked in, showered, and an hour later we were on our way to have one of the best meals of my life. Three workers at Burgeon ate with us in their on-site stock room. They were cool and fun and we had a great time. This was one of the coolest and most exciting trail magics we had. Loon asked if they needed a Colorist to work for them since I work in fashion as a colorist! Thanks for trying to get me a job, Loon!

After the dinner, Singe’s partner offered to pick us up and take us to Super Secret Ice Cream. An ice cream shop in Bethlehem, NH that Singe had been talking up since probably day 2 of me knowing her. As an ice cream connoisseur and an AT Dairy-Blazer, I couldn’t wait. We left the boy Binguses behind and Loon, Singe, Outlaw, and I got ice cream. I am absolutely obsessed with this place. The branding, the colors, the unique small-batch flavors, the ice cream flights, the posters–all of it is perfect. New favorite ice cream. I think it’s extremely tragic that I live so far away from it and also probably for the best. If you’re even remotely close though, take the detour! Best ice cream I’ve had on trail and also in years hands down.

All of this together made for such a magical day and one of the standouts from trail. Tackling a hard and anticipated mountain but feeling strong? That view?! Reuniting with our friends unexpectedly! Surprise trail magic pasta dinner?! AND SUPER SECRET ICE CREAM?! What a way to kick off the Whites.

The next day we slackpacked the Kinsmens with The Notch. They have a deal that if you stay two nights, the slackpack is included. Yes please! The Kinsmens were some of the most difficult parts of the Whites. It wasn’t my worst day there, but quite a challenge. The final ascent up is basically bouldering/rock scrambling. I was so glad to not have my full pack weight, but I did feel extremely rushed trying to catch the shuttle back to the Notch. The options were a 4pm or 7pm. We got a tiny bit of a later start that ate into our hiking time. Thus, I ended my hike around 4:10pm while feeling extremely rushed and stressed the last part of the day. The trail was super rocky and frustrating. We missed the shuttle but since Singe did know the staff well, they came and picked us up. Thank you! When we got back to the hostel, someone had decided to make dinner for everyone as trail magic. What a godsend. Two covered dinners in a row. I was so incredibly exhausted after the Kinsmens that I probably would have cried if I had to figure out my own dinner. Moosilauke felt like a warm up but the Kinsmens felt like an ass-kicking. I was worried more of that was to come but Singe told me she thinks the Kinsmens are some of the hardest 4kers in the Whites and it shouldn’t feel worse than that which really helped. Tomorrow, we’d be back to full packs and really getting into it!

There is a challenge in New Hampshire hiking called the New Hampshire 48. This refers to a group of 48 prominent peaks all above 4k. The AT goes right over a few of them, and grazes along the side of a few others. Some people choose to tag the summits and take the detours to hit as many summits as they can. In hiking this pursuit of summits is called “Peak Bagging.” While we went through the Whites, Loon was all in on peak bagging as many of the 48 as she could. She was a rockstar! Singe and I supported her and encouraged her but did not have the same drive. I was too worried about my ankles on all the rocky ups and downs that I didn’t want to risk anything by doing extras. Because of this, Singe and I sometimes had slightly different days than Loon. Like for example, when Loon walked an extra like 7 miles to get 4 extra peaks in a day and Singe and I walked 5 extra miles the next day to be able to go get ice cream. It’s important to know where your priorities are.

Still, on the AT, I got my fair share of the 48 done. The trail follows a section of the Whites called the Pemigewasset Loop (or Pemi Loop) for about 10 miles after the Kinsmens. Most notably, it follows along Franconia Ridge which is an exposed ridge walk that goes over multiple peaks. You can see the circle of the Pemi Loop through the mountains while up on the ridge. I absolutely loved Franconia Ridge. It’s like nothing else on the trail. It most closely reminded my of a hike I had done in the Dolomites! And yet it’s here! Just sitting up in New Hampshire! Wow! Franconia is known for being beautiful but also extreme due to the exposure. Like everything in the Whites, it’s quite steep (like straight up rock ladders for miles) to get up to the top and then open to strong winds and weather with nowhere to hide. We were lucky to have gorgeous weather throughout the Whites. While we did have some wind (including being pushed over by the wind a few times), it was nothing unmanageable or unpleasant on Franconia. Still, the exposure is tough. There was even a Search and Rescue on this section the same day we were up there. But we got through it just fine along with my new can of Honey Mustard Pringles that we became obsessed with and didn’t last more than 6 miles. We were in such awe the whole time. When I first got to the top of the ridge, I ran into McMurphy! Another mountain top reunion! Loon started a little further back to get more peaks, so we also reunited with her on a summit!

Towards the end of the ridge, the novelty had worn off a little and we were ready to get out of the wind and set up camp for the night. We went down and then up and over Mount Garfield where I had another summit reunion! I ran into Sniper, a woman I had met in Georgia and North Carolina. She hiked the AT years ago and is attempting a Yo-yo of the full trail this year! This means she hiked from Georgia to Maine and will turn around and hike all the way back down to Georgia. Incredible!! It was so cool to run into her and see her still crushing it! After leaving her and wishing her the best on the rest of the journey, I made my way down to the Garfield Campsite where of course we ran into several other friends!

The Whites are known for their Hut-to-Hut system where you can stay in little lodges along the way. This is awesome for a stand alone trip, but not really an option for thru-hikers. They are quite expensive (especially multiple nights in a row) and require reservations far in advance. That would be nearly impossibly to schedule on a thru-hiker’s flexible and ever-changing timeline. Stealth camping is also not allowed/highly discouraged in the Whites due to the extreme weather and the delicate nature of the alpine plants. There are designated campgrounds throughout the Whites that each have caretakers. They are all first come first serve, but the caretakers are great at finding spots for everyone so we were never too stressed about it. At this first campsite, we registered for a thru-hiker pass with the caretaker that allows us to camp at all the campgrounds for a discounted price and also get free pastries and soup when we stop into the huts! Wahoo! We opted to stay in the shelter the first night because of the wind.

The next day we started off our hike by basically having to walk straight down a sort of rocky waterfall. The Whites are on another level. We made it to the Galehead Hut for lunch where we cashed in our free soup pass. Then we continued up South Twin Mountain. For some reason, when we summited, I could not get “Breathe” by Faith Hill out of my head and I continued to sing it as my anthem the rest of trail. South Twin was a long push up, but the descent felt flat compared to the climb. What a treat! We went to a campsite a little off the path since there weren’t too many options otherwise. You’d either have to do a really short day or a really long one. Given the strenuous terrain, we opted for a short day. Singe and I got to the campsite around 3pm while Loon went off to go bag three more peaks! It felt really nice to have time to relax and recover. I don’t think I got to camp before 4pm since North Carolina. We stayed in the shelter this night too and watched a beautiful sunrise from the comfort of our sleeping bags the next morning.

Our next day in the Whites was a bit of a Choose Your Own Adventure. This was one of the only nights on trail (the only night on trail?) that Singe, Loon, and I were all apart. I had a field day telling people we were all apart because we were fighting. Nobody believed it for a second. We had a bit of an easier day planned passing by Zealand Hut and aiming for Ethan Pond Shelter. But Singe’s friend told us she could meet us at the trailhead for Zealand Hut and take us to lunch in Bethlehem and then return us to trail. Loon decided to pass and stick with the original plan since she was already doing so many extra miles and peaks but all Singe had to tell me was we could get Super Secret Ice Cream again and I was in. Singe left a little before me in the morning but we met up at Zealand Hut. It was super special to be there with her because it was the first place she decided she ever wanted to Thru-Hike back in college. <3 We took a side trail that was super flat about 2.5 miles to a parking lot. I timed myself on how long it took from Zealand to the lot because I had the idea that I might try to return later for Work-For-Stay if the timing worked out. We had a great lunch and ice cream of course and were returned to the trailhead. It felt like we snuck off campus in the middle of the day with our cool older sister. We were cutting it close but I wanted to try for the work for stay at Zealand since it’s a unique thru-hiker experience and I didn’t know when else the timing would work out to try. I ZOOMED through the 2.5 miles back up to Zealand in record time and ta-da! I was granted one of the three work for stay spots that night! Singe ended up camping solo sort of in between Loon at Ethan Pond and myself at the hut.

Two more hikers I knew rolled in and we hung around the pretty waterfall waiting for our duties to begin. We got leftovers from the dinner that night and then were assigned the tasks of sweeping, wiping down tables, and reorganizing the freezer for the hut croo. In exchange, we got the dinner leftovers and were able to sleep in the dining room which was much appreciated since it rained that night. We had to get up super early so the staff could make breakfast for the paying guests. We hung around outside until breakfast leftovers were set out and then we descended on those. From there we made our way to the AMC Highland Center where Loon, Singe, and I met back up for a zero. I had a job interview over the phone the next day and I was touched by Singe and Loon’s efforts to make sure we were somewhere I could take the call with reliable service. We were excited for a rest too since the Whites are so demanding even when dropping the number of miles you’re doing each day. The Highland Center was also super neat because we got to run into our friend, Mosey, who we met during the first half of trail and was an original Bingus! The Binguses also trickled in over the next day and we got to catch up with them as well!

The interview went well (though did not ultimately land me a job…but that’s story for Maine) and I was feeling excited. There was no laundry so we did our best to freshen our clothes up in the bunkhouse sink. We enjoyed taking over the on-site library to do our nails and watch TV on our phones together. It was nice to have a little break before the Presidential Range or “The Prezies.” We actually lucked out by taking a zero for my interview because it let some bad weather pass and gave us a perfect weather window later!

After our time at the Highland Center and mapping out our next stretch, we made our way back up into the range. We made our way to the Mizpah Hut and Nauman Campground. Mizpah was one of my favorite huts. There was something more remote, cozy, and mellow about it more than the others I had seen. The Croo was also very friendly. We checked on a work-for-stay just to see but they didn’t have any so we camped at the Nauman Campground next door. Singe, Loon, and I got an entire tenting platform to ourselves! We didn’t know it then, but this would be the last night the three of us would camp together without anyone else. We had a magical time. Somehow, it wasn’t too cold so we decided to cowboy camp on our platform under the clear sky. We watched a beautiful sunset and had such a fun and special night. We were laughing so much. This night was such a highlight on trail. We slept under the stars and I remember waking up at different times during the night and seeing how the milky way was moving across the sky. What an experience.

The next day we got breakfast leftovers at Mizpah Hut with another female hiker who was traveling with her partner. I think she enjoyed having some girl time with us and we loved it too! We made our way from the hut up to the Mount Pierce summit (President Pierce not Pierce Brosnan apparently) and were officially in the Prezies! The first summit was not so bad but we were hyped! We could see the others stretching before us, most of which we’d tackle that day! We took a moment at the summit to talk about how excited we were. Before we continued on, Singe said, “Does anyone have words they’d like to share about Pierce before we continue?” which made me laugh all day thinking about it.

Mount Eisenhower was next and it was so beautiful! We had a gorgeous day and could see Mount Washington’s summit for the first time! Though it was quite windy, Eisenhower was one of my favorite mountains in the Prezies because the 360 views are so stunning! What a view of Washington! After a snack break and some photos, we kept it moving before getting too cold. We went up and over Little Monroe and Mount Monroe where we got a look at Lakes of the Clouds Hut where we would be staying. We felt like we had such a magical day hopping from summit to summit and squealing about the views and the weather. Such good time, in fact, that we decided to go ahead and summit Mount Washington that afternoon! Originally we were going to wait until the next day but when you get good weather and you have the time…don’t waste that opportunity. We decided to go up the 1.5 trail to the summit with slackpacks and take a trail around the next day.

Washington was….fine. Loon, Singe, and I pressed play on the Hamilton soundtrack at the same time to get us up the mountain. It’s so strange to make your way up this infamous beast of a mountain only to be met with a bunch of ill prepared tourists who drove or took the Cog Railroad up. It was crowded and touristy, but hey, at least we weren’t hit with 100mph winds! We didn’t stay terribly long before making our way back to the hut. We knew 3 of the Binguses were making their way to the hut tonight too and we were excited to see them. Our friend, Mosey, was also able to work on-site at Lakes of the Clouds that day and spend the night too! Google, TikTok, and Meta have each filed suit against California in an attempt to block enforcement of a law that protects kids under 16 from “psychologically exploitative features” on social media platforms.

Governor Gavin Newsom signed the law, Assembly Bill 1709, into law this past September. “We want our children–and every California ​kid–to grow up in a world where technology supports their wellbeing, rather than exploits their vulnerabilities,” Newsom said at the time.

AB 1709 prevents socmed platforms from being able to employ features like recommendation algorithms, infinite scroll, and video autoplay on accounts of users under the age of 16. Because of how Google, TikTok, and Meta have designed their platforms, it effectively bans kids under 16 from having accounts at all on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and Facebook.

According to Reuters, the three big tech companies all said (in separate lawsuits) that California is wrongly forcing them to use a state-mandated method of delivering content to children, instead of leaving things up to parents’ discretion.

Their lawsuits claim violations of Section 230 (aka the “safe harbor” law that lets platforms off the liability hook for anything their users post) and First Amendment free speech protections.

“Parents ‌are best ⁠positioned to make the right decisions for their families,” Google spokesperson Jose Castaneda told Reuters. “Overly broad laws like this actually break the systems we’ve built to help kids find high-quality, age-appropriate videos.”

In its suit, TikTok said protected speech “cannot be suppressed solely to protect the young from ideas or images that a legislative body thinks is unsuitable.”

Meta’s suit, meanwhile, said California doesn’t have the right to override its editorial standards.

“We are challenging this law because it violates the First Amendment by letting the government dictate how ideas are organized, displayed or shared with Californians,” a Meta spokesperson told Courthouse News. “Under the law, we would be forced to either remove features that keep teens’ experiences age-appropriate, or cut thousands of California teens off from family, friends and their communities.”

It's time for investors to ratchet down their future growth expectations for XRP.

Future price targets for cryptocurrency XRP (XRP -6.61%) tend to be wildly bullish. Even though XRP is currently trading for just over $1, it's not uncommon to see price targets as high as $100. And, until recently, crypto bulls were convinced that XRP was heading to $1,000.

Unfortunately, XRP has consistently overpromised and under-delivered. In more than a decade of trading, XRP has never traded higher than $3.84.

XRP still has a chance to move higher over the next few years, but investors need to ratchet down their expectations. I predict XRP will only reach $2 by the end of 2030.

On the surface, a price target of $2 might sound underwhelming. After all, XRP traded as high as $3.65 last summer before eventually hitting a brick wall. Earlier in 2025, XRP hit $3.40 before giving back those gains.

But just think of the phenomenal growth rate XRP will need to regain the $4 price level over the next few years. To move from its current price of $1 to a future price of $4, XRP will need to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of more than 40%.

To put that number into context, Bitcoin has delivered a 37% CAGR over the past decade, while Ethereum has delivered a 23% CAGR. So investors are asking XRP to grow faster than either Bitcoin or Ethereum, the two market leaders.

With that in mind, a more realistic growth rate for XRP may be 20%. That's still faster than the growth rate of many established tech companies. If XRP grows at a 20% CAGR over the next four years, it will double in value to $2 by the end of 2030.

Of course, it will take a lot for XRP to achieve a 20% growth rate. First and foremost, new use cases need to be found for the XRP token, which is primarily used now as a bridge currency for cross-border transactions. One of the most interesting new use cases could be real-world asset (RWA) tokenization on the XRP blockchain ledger.

Secondly, the regulatory environment for crypto needs to remain supportive, and new crypto legislation needs to be passed to make it easier for financial institutions and banks to embrace blockchain-based payment technology. It looked like this crypto legislation would pass this year, but the Clarity Act fizzled in the Senate in September.

If both catalysts deliver as planned, XRP could finally see a breakout. But keep your expectations in check. XRP is not going to hit $10, $100, or $1,000. Instead, XRP will be fortunate to double in value and reach $2 by the end of 2030.

While there’s no evidence that proxy access was used to carry out attacks on the White House, experts say it could open the door for hackers to exploit vulnerabilities or grant outsiders an unmonitored back door into high-security systems.

Residential proxy services route internet traffic through consumer devices (such as smartphones, laptops or home routers) to make it appear that the activity is being performed by a real person. While proxies can be used for relatively benign purposes — web scraping is by far the most common use case — they’re also popular for activities like malware attacks, identity theft and creating fake social media accounts.

For example, earlier this year, Synthient, another company that tracks proxies and VPNs, documented how one botnet infected more than 2 million devices using a residential proxy server. If a similar vulnerability existed in the White House proxy, hackers could probe the infected device’s internal systems and install malicious payloads.

In its report, Layer3 found traffic from six distinct IP addresses exiting the White House network from at least 28 known residential proxy services between Oct. 5 and Nov. 3, with most activity occurring in the evenings between 5 p.m. and midnight EST. This suggests the culprit may be an employee who connected a personal device to a Wi-Fi network with a compromised app or a proxy SDK (software development kit) running in the background. Third-party SDKs can convert a device into a “proxy node.”

Commercial proxy networks sell access to these nodes, so anyone with a few dollars could rent a trusted IP address — in this case, making it appear as though traffic originates from the Executive Office of the President.

Researchers at Synthient provided more details about the case, posting on X that several White House staffers are running “a Windows 11 machine with InfaticaSDK installed.” They pointed directly to Rave, a popular app that lets users watch streaming services with friends, as the likely delivery mechanism for the residential proxy traffic detected on the White House network.

Riley Kilmer, co-founder of the cybersecurity firm Spur Intelligence, wrote in a LinkedIn post that different types of proxy companies had been discovered in the White House, including one called 9Proxy, which is often used by criminals for banking fraud.

The White House didn’t respond to CNET’s request for comment.

Because smart TVs, connected appliances and home routers usually have minimal user oversight, a single unvetted app or out-of-date firmware update can turn a household of IoT devices into an exit node for commercial proxy networks.

Proxies can generally “glom” onto consumer devices by embedding networking code directly into software, either consensually (users willingly install it) or nonconsensually (by exploiting unsecured device access points).

It starts when app developers look for revenue by integrating those proxy SDKs into their free apps. Then, when you install the app, the fine print of the terms and conditions of service often includes a clause that allows the app to add your IP address to a third-party proxy pool.

A June report from Spur Intelligence found that roughly one in three apps available on Samsung and LG smart TVs were selling users’ IP addresses. To avoid ads, users often accept the terms when downloading the app — a type of unnoticed consent. Once installed, the SDK runs continuously in the background, registering the device’s IP with the proxy provider’s gateway, unless you delete the app or opt out. (The report notes that Amazon and Roku have explicitly banned this practice.)

Using a free VPN could expose you to the same risk, allowing your IP address to be used by someone else for nefarious activities. The FBI’s guidance on protecting yourself against proxies specifically calls out free VPNs as one of the primary ways bad actors gain access to an IP address.

In some cases, you don’t need to download anything to get your IP thrown into a shady proxy pool — you just have to buy the wrong device. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services on Tuesday finalized its annual outpatient hospital payments rule with a lower-than-proposed rate bump and its major, industry-opposed policy shifts moderated, but largely intact.

The 2027 Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment (OPPS) and Ambulatory Surgical Center (ASC) Payment Systems rule outlines a 2.3% reimbursement increase for those meeting applicable quality reporting requirements. That’s below the proposed 2.4% increase and reflects a 3.2% projected hospital market basket increase and a 0.9 percentage point productivity adjustment.

The agency is also moving forward with a substantial and controversial cut in 340B drug reimbursements, though not quite to the extent that it initially proposed.

The final rule adjusts these drug payments down from the current bar of average sales price (ASP) plus 6% to ASP minus 28%, as opposed to the proposed 33.4% below ASP. To make the reimbursement change budget neutral as required by statute, OPPS payments for non-drug services will be boosted by 7.5%.

CMS said it tweaked the proposed 340B reimbursement cut based on 340B ceiling price data from the Health Resources and Services Administration, with the baseline reductions informed by a drug acquisition cost survey it conducted earlier this year. Those changes will reduce Original Medicare drug payments by $4.35 billion and the drug payments from beneficiaries by $1.1 billion (or $900 on average per beneficiary who receives an affected drug from a hospital outpatient department) in the policy’s first year, CMS said.

The rate adjustment did little to calm hospital industry groups who’d railed against the 340B reimbursement changes as a threat to safety-net care and a gift to for-profit hospitals that do not participate in the subsidy program but would benefit from the budget-neutral offset. The American Hospital Association, the Association of American Medical Colleges and America’s Essential Hospitals each condemned the finalized policy in reaction statements, with the latter two issuing more forceful rebukes.

“CMS has based its reimbursement to 340B hospitals on the results of a hasty and faulty survey, failing to follow the clear requirements of the Medicare statute,” AAMC Chief Health Care Officer Jonathan Jaffery, M.D., said. “The drug payment cuts disproportionately affect and target non-profit and public hospitals serving low-income patients, while redistributing billions of dollars to hospitals that do not share the same safety-net and academic missions. These cuts threaten access to critical services made possible by the 340B program and will lead to worse health outcomes for patients.”

“Rather than strengthening and preserving safety net funding, CMS is instead prioritizing hospitals that do not serve the safety net and nodding to the pharmaceutical industry's interests,” AEH’s statement reads. “These cuts are landing at a time when essential hospitals and their mission are more important than ever, as millions of people risk losing their Medicaid coverage in the coming months.”

The Federation of American Hospitals, which represents for-profit hospitals that do not participate in 340B, struck a different tone, calling the rulemaking “a win for patients, taxpayers and the hospitals that communities depend on.”

“By bringing Medicare drug payments more in line with actual costs, this reform will lower seniors’ out-of-pocket expenses and redirect billions in Medicare dollars where they’re needed most—supporting access to care for patients served by small and rural hospitals across the country,” Charlene MacDonald, the group’s president and CEO, said in a statement. “We applaud the Administration for advancing a smarter, fairer approach to Medicare payment.”

Additionally, CMS finalized an increase in an annual OPPS conversion factor to non-drug items and services, speeding up the agency’s effort to offset a prior $7.8 billion recoupment to 340B hospitals it was forced to make after previously reducing 340B rates contrary to law. But while this accelerated payback—often described by non-340B hospitals as a clawback—was proposed as an increase from 0.5% to 3%, the final rule brings the conversion factor to 2%.

AHA Senior Vice President of Public Policy Analysis and Development Ashley Thompson described both 340B policy changes together as a “continued assault” on the subsidy program that “will further stress hospitals’ ability to care for their patients.” AAMC’s Jaffery said the combined policies’ impact “will be devastating to patients,” and that his group “remains committed to pursuing all available avenues to reverse these payment cuts.”

Further rankling the hospital industry was the final rule’s continued push toward site-neutral payment policy and a phase-out of the inpatient-only list.

For the former, the agency will apply Physician Fee Schedule payment rates for some imaging services to off-campus provider-based departments. In line with the proposed rule, CMS said it expects the plan to trigger first-year reductions in Medicare Part B expenditures and beneficiary cost-sharing obligations of $260 million and $70 million, while also reducing a market incentive for provider consolidation.

“Both policies are misguided,” AHA’s Thompson said, “and overlook the unique role hospital outpatient departments play in delivering care to patients in communities nationwide. “CMS is committed to spending taxpayer funds wisely and efficiently, so [that] America’s seniors can continue getting the care they need,” CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., said in a release. “By right-sizing payments and rooting out waste, today’s reforms will drive affordability in Medicare for current beneficiaries while ensuring it will still be there to serve their children and grandchildren.”

A ratio means little when it is built on very few parcels. One return out of two parcels is 50%. Ten returns out of twenty is also 50%. They are not the same customer. Always look at the total number of parcels next to the percentage.

After the SOC Assessment: Common Findings and How to Fix Them

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

_ Got your SOC assessment report back? Here are the findings that show up most often and a practical plan to remediate them._

The report lands in your inbox. Forty pages, a heat map, and a list of findings that wasn't there last week. Now what?

This is the stage where an assessment either pays off or collects dust. Let's walk through the issues that appear most often, how to rank them, and how to turn them into progress. If you're working with a team offering SOC consulting, this is also where good advisors earn their keep.

Every environment is different, but certain patterns repeat.

1. Blind spots in log coverage. Critical systems, cloud workloads, or identity platforms aren't feeding the monitoring tool. Teams often assume they are, because someone set it up years ago.

2. Noisy, untuned alerts. Analysts drown in low-value alerts, so real ones get missed or delayed. Alert fatigue is one of the biggest silent risks.

3. Playbooks that exist only in theory. Documents are outdated, never tested, or unknown to half the team.

4. Weak escalation paths. Contact lists are stale. Nobody's sure who can authorize isolating a server at midnight.

5. Thin after-hours coverage. Strong daytime performance, thin or no coverage overnight and on weekends.

6. Limited detection mapped to real attacker behavior. Rules focus on a few familiar threats while whole categories of technique go unwatched.

7. Inconsistent documentation in tickets. Investigations lack notes, making handoffs and post-incident reviews painful.

8. No meaningful metrics. The team counts alerts but can't say how fast it detects or responds.

9. Tool sprawl and poor integration. Analysts bounce between consoles, wasting time and losing context.

10. Skills and burnout concerns. High turnover, limited training, and heavy workloads.

If you recognize several of these, you're in very ordinary company.

Resist the temptation to start fixing everything. Sort first.

Use two simple lenses: risk (how bad is it if this goes unaddressed?) and effort (how hard is the fix?). That gives you four buckets:

Be honest about risk. Findings tied to detection of serious threats outrank cosmetic documentation issues.

These won't solve everything, but they build momentum and visibly reduce risk.

You don't have to do it all alone. Outside support can make sense for specific needs:

When evaluating remediation help, look for advisors who explain trade-offs and teach your team along the way, not those who create dependency. The goal is a stronger in-house capability or a well-managed partnership, not a permanent black box.

Remediation projects often fade after the first burst of energy. A few habits help.

Name an owner. Someone senior should be accountable for progress.

Track it visibly. A simple board or dashboard works.

Review monthly. Short check-ins catch stalls early.

Celebrate wins. Acknowledge when a gap closes. It reinforces the work.

Retest. Verify that fixes actually work. A closed ticket isn't proof.

Executives want to know three things: how risky is it, what will it cost, and how will we know it's working. Frame your plan around those.

Show the findings in terms of business impact, such as slower detection of account compromise or exposure of customer data. Present a phased roadmap with rough costs and expected risk reduction. Offer a small set of metrics that will demonstrate progress over time.

Treating the report as the finish line. The assessment is the start.

Fixing the easy stuff only. Quick wins matter, but don't ignore the hard, high-risk items.

Buying a tool to solve a process problem. Often the issue is workflow or tuning, not software.

Skipping validation. Test that changes behave as intended.

Blaming individuals. Findings usually reflect systemic gaps, not personal failures.

Picture a healthcare services firm whose assessment flagged missing identity logs, noisy alerts, and no formal after-hours coverage. They spent the first month updating contacts and tuning the five loudest rules. By month three they'd onboarded identity logs and run a tabletop exercise that exposed unclear authority for isolating systems. By month nine they had an on-call rotation and a simple set of response metrics.

Nothing dramatic, just steady, prioritized work. At their reassessment, three domains had moved up a maturity level, and the team could point to numbers instead of impressions.

A report is only as valuable as what happens next. Sort findings by risk and effort, grab the quick wins, plan the heavier lifts, and keep ownership clear. Do that consistently, and the next assessment becomes less of an interrogation and more of a progress check.

The Same TSA Scanner That Lets Laptops Stay In Your Bag Is Quietly Ruining Other Gear

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

While some passengers might not directly notice it, airport security checkpoints across the United States are changing. For most travelers, the most visible benefit is simple: laptops, tablets, and approved liquids can increasingly stay inside carry-on bags, removing one of the most familiar airport security rituals of the past two decades. However, this same technology that makes the checkpoint feel more convenient also creates new problems for some travelers.

The Transportation Security Administration (TSA) has been rolling out advanced computed tomography, commonly known as CT, scanners as part of a major security technology upgrade. In March 2022, the agency awarded two orders totaling up to $781.2 million for base and full-size CT X-ray systems for airport checkpoints, while later procurement added even more units to the program. These machines are designed to improve threat detection by creating three-dimensional images of carry-on bags.

TSA officers can rotate and inspect those images in ways that were not possible with older two-dimensional X-ray machines. For the average passenger, this means fewer items need to be removed from bags, while for travelers carrying analog film, overstuffed carry-ons, or other sensitive items, the story is less straightforward.

The new CT scanning equipment represents a major shift from the traditional X-ray equipment passengers have become used to at airport checkpoints. Older systems produced flatter images, making it harder for officers to distinguish between items layered together inside a bag. This is one reason laptops and other large electronics often need to be removed and screened separately.

The introduction of newer CT systems, which use more advanced imaging and algorithms to create 3D views of carry-on content, is increasingly changing this. According to TSA, officers can view and rotate the image on three axes to identify possible threat items more effectively. The agency has described the technology as both a security upgrade and a way to improve the passenger experience. Today, these scanners are already available at some of the largest hubs in the United States, including Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD), and New York JFK Airport (JFK), among many others.

For travelers, the most obvious change is the reduced need to unpack their hand luggage at the conveyor belt. TSA has stated that under current screening procedures for CT lanes, laptops are allowed to remain in bags. In some airport press releases, the agency has also noted that passengers screened through CT units do not need to remove 3-1-1 liquids or laptops. Travelers no longer need to place laptops at the top of a backpack just to remove them quickly, and toiletry bags do not always need to be kept in an outside pocket.

The downside is that CT scanners do not treat every item the same way, and their more powerful imaging is useful for security screening, but it can be a problem for undeveloped photographic materials. This is where the new technology creates a completely different experience for analog photographers than for passengers carrying digital equipment. The new CT scanners can damage or destroy unprocessed film rolls, and the stronger imaging can cause damage in a single screening. CT scanners can fog unprocessed rolls, reduce image quality, and cause permanent damage, forcing analog photographers to change how they move through security.

However, this does not mean that every piece of camera equipment is at risk; digital cameras, lenses, batteries, and memory cards are not affected in the same way. The concern is specifically undeveloped film, including rolls carried separately, film already loaded inside a camera, disposable analog cameras, and instant materials. For most travelers, this may sound like a niche problem, but analog photography has become increasingly popular again among travelers who want a different look from digital smartphone photos. Many unaware passengers might also fail to realize there is a risk until after their trip, when the damage is already done.

Airport security technology has changed greatly over the last 25 years.

As a result of the new scanners, the most important rule for travelers carrying analog materials is simple: do not allow it to go through a CT scanner if it can be avoided. TSA’s own guidance recommends placing undeveloped film and cameras containing undeveloped film in carry-on bags, or taking undeveloped film to the checkpoint and asking for a hand inspection.

That means film should not be buried at the bottom of a suitcase. Instead, it should be packed so it's easy to remove before reaching the scanner. It is also advisable to separate unprocessed rolls from cameras where possible and place them in transparent, quart-sized zipper bags on top of the carry-on. That makes it easier for TSA officers to inspect without delaying the process and the entire line behind.

The hand inspection is not especially complicated, but it does take a bit of time, since TSA officers generally take the film to a separate area, visually inspect each roll, swab it for explosive residue, and then manually clear it. The process typically takes three to 10 minutes, with passengers advised to arrive around 15 to 20 minutes earlier if they know they will need the extra step. It is important to note that travelers should clearly tell the TSA officer that they are carrying undeveloped photographic materials and would like a hand inspection.

TSA PreCheck Touchless ID is available at 65 airports nationwide and supports more than 100 participating airlines

The new CT machines have also changed the carry-on versus checked-bag decision process. Many passengers assume that fragile or valuable items should stay in the cabin, but when flying with film, there is another reason to avoid checked baggage. Checked luggage is also screened using similar powerful equipment, and passengers generally do not have the same opportunity to request a hand inspection once the bag disappears behind the check-in counter.

For that reason, film travelers should keep undeveloped rolls, disposable cameras, and cameras containing film in their hand luggage. This does not mean placing them deep inside a trolley that may need to be gate-checked later. The safest option is to keep film in a personal item or small carry-on that will remain accessible through the checkpoint.

There is also a practical difference between domestic and international travel. TSA guidance applies in the United States, where passengers can request a hand inspection. Procedures abroad may vary, and foreign airport security staff may not always handle film requests in the same way. For travelers connecting internationally, that means planning ahead and expecting the same conversation more than once.

Besides the potential risk to analog film, newer CT scanning equipment can also create problems for travelers carrying oversized or overstuffed bags, since the scanners have fixed tunnel openings. This means a bag that does not physically fit through the machine cannot be screened in that lane.

That matters because many passengers have become used to a certain amount of flexibility around carry-on sizes. Most major US airlines use a standard domestic carry-on limit of around 22 x 14 x 9 inches (56 x 36 x 23 cm). However, in practice, the strictness of the enforcement has often depended on the airline policy, gate agent, aircraft type, and how full the flight is. Soft-sided bags and expanded trolleys could sometimes slip through, especially when passengers were not asked to place them in a sizing frame.

However, the CT scanners at security lanes are now changing that dynamic, and if a carry-on is too large for the scanner, the issue arises at the security checkpoint rather than at the gate. In that sense, the new scanning technology has become an indirect baggage-size enforcement tool for airlines, and while passengers might not be trying to break a rule, an expanded carry-on suitcase that once passed through older equipment may now be pulled aside before screening can even continue.

The discovery of two live smoke grenades hidden inside a jar of peanut butter in a passenger's checked luggage highlights the importance of screening.

For passengers, the CT rollout at TSA checkpoints is making security increasingly convenient for the average passenger but less forgiving for travelers carrying certain items. Laptops and liquids staying in the bag is a real improvement, as it avoids the need to unpack half a bag in public, but it does not mean passengers can stop thinking about how their carry-on is packed.

A carry-on now needs to be packed not only to meet the airline’s baggage rules but also to fit within the physical limits of the checkpoint itself. For most travelers, the best strategy is to keep laptops and liquids where they fit naturally, while making sure the bag remains within airline carry-on dimensions and is not expanded beyond its normal size. For passengers traveling with analog cameras and photo rolls, it is important they remain accessible and separated where possible. This ensures it is ready to be handed to an officer for a manual inspection before the bag enters the scanner.

The best approach is therefore not to assume every checkpoint will work the same way. Travelers should still arrive prepared for traditional lanes, especially at airports where older and newer machines operate side by side. At the same time, those carrying analog photographic materials or borderline-sized bags should plan around the newer system.

It was the kind of thinking that used to govern most of football. A few years ago – although it could really have been many examples from the last 50 – a promising manager was being pursued by one of the game’s wealthiest clubs. He had built up a fine team with an upwardly mobile club, and it looked like they could be on the brink of something special… but that one thought wouldn’t go away.

“These opportunities don’t always come along, so you have to seize them.”

The manager ended up taking it, but not quite seizing it. He didn’t perform to the level required, albeit amid a lot of debate around the circumstances he walked into. His career has never reached the same heights, but the key point is that its trajectory was, in that moment of acceptance, drastically changed.

The old thinking was wrong. That job just wasn’t right for him.

He’s not the only one. There are a few examples, from Graham Potter to Julen Lopetegui, and there’s much more to it than just “backing yourself”.

Unai Emery is at a very different point of his own career, having experienced historic Europa League success  – with three different clubs – over 22 years, but it’s impossible not to wonder whether any of this weighed on his own thinking as he now discusses a new deal with Aston Villa.

The Basque will be all too aware of the noise around Manchester United’s interest, and the latest developments – including Villa’s very ‘LinkedIn’ post about "long-term strategic direction" – all felt very pointed ahead of Sunday’s trip to Old Trafford. Many in football even believe that one reason his club resorted to the absurdity of last week’s legal threat against Jamie Carragher was aggravation about their manager consistently being linked to “a bigger club”.

This, it should be acknowledged, is still Manchester United. It’s Sir Alex Ferguson, Sir Matt Busby and his babes, and so much glory and legacy. Despite all the current problems at Old Trafford, there is a history and consequently inspiration for the future; of what you might be able to do with that.

Others in football believe Emery might previously have been open to joining such a club, primarily because of the will to finally win a major league and the Champions League.

That isn’t to say either are indefinitely impossible at Villa given the club’s rapid recent growth, but their own fans have been frustrated over regulatory constraints.

And yet such strands lead into the nub of this, where this new thinking is warranted.

Emery’s record above Europa League level might raise considerable questions over whether he would suit a club of United’s scale, and those at Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain have long insisted his outlook didn’t fit.

Far more relevant, however, might be that United just don’t suit Emery.

There are numerous persuasive arguments as to why he should absolutely sign that contract and stay at Villa.

None of them are to deepen these debates about club size, or even Emery’s own career.

It’s more that the 55-year-old is in the very rare modern position of having earned an almost ideal job.

At Villa, everything is built around him, in a way you just don’t really see at this level any more. The only comparison in the Premier League or any of Europe’s wealthiest clubs is at Arsenal, where Mikel Arteta was only given such authority after initially proving himself. Emery has almost complete control, and those circumstances have ensured everything is in tune. The work of a very good manager is consequently greatly amplified, so that Villa consistently over-perform.

That can even be seen in how they’ve responded to the great frustration of the summer. They’re already in far better shape than would have been expected. Emery might have had his Europa League winners ripped up, but it is already impressive how he has built a coherent unit. Sure, there are still issues and gaps that are difficult to fill amid constraints, but there’s a bite there. This is personified in Nicolas Jackson.

And, from that, and a relatively volatile Premier League, there is a real opportunity for top five again. That can bring further growth… but maybe more? He’s already had more at Villa than most managers will ever enjoy, since he is an adored club legend.

By contrast, links to United have been met with scepticism by tranches of the Old Trafford support. They are also fully aware that the actual problems are way above the manager. United are unlikely to give Emery the keys to the club, in the way Villa have done. Sir Jim Ratcliffe has already been insistent on building a modern sporting director culture, even if this iteration has so far failed.

Worse, a U-turn on that, to make someone like Emery manager, would actually just look even more indicative of dysfunction; that they’re going from extreme to extreme again. And that’s even before you get to the amount of rot that still needs to be cleared at Old Trafford. United are not yet close to being a club who just need a coach coming in and imposing his influence to get them winning. They require a patient rebuild, but the wait can derail coaches.

Put bluntly, if this comes down to a chance of winning the league or Champions League, there’s an argument that Emery specifically has a better opportunity at Villa, certainly in the medium-term. There would be so much more work to do at Old Trafford, with a lot more risk.

Maybe most pertinently, he would be giving up a very good thing, a very rare thing, that has the potential for so much more. And it’s that context that is also so key to such debates. It’s not just that the thinking has started to change. So has the reality. With more and more clubs looking to head coaches in wider infrastructures, the jobs are much more transitory. Such opportunities actually come along more frequently, but they’re far more contoured than before.

Better-run clubs than United are now geared in such a way that they only suit certain profiles, or certain ideologies. Tactical shifts can take time, and be painful, as currently being seen at Newcastle United. On Premier League pitches, the game is so systemised and optimised that the margins have become very thin. Hence wild swings in results when clubs aren’t in tune.

At Villa, it’s almost note-perfect for Emery. That isn’t something to be given up easily.

angelo renna edited by christina vergopoulou November 5, 2026 3 min

Designed by Renna Studio and architect Angelo Renna, Firenze Little Island is an architectural proposal for a demountable, seasonal public floating platform on the Arno River in Florence, Italy. Sited along the Pescaia weir, where water flow is regulated, the project introduces an amphibious public space designed for cultural gatherings, orchestral performances, and recreation during the calmer summer months between May and September.

The installation, conceived by Renna Studio’s architect Angelo Renna, utilizes a lightweight, reversible modular structure comprising prefabricated floating pontoons topped with a timber beam substructure and finished decking. Encircling the central open-air deck is a segmented, circular planter ring approximately one meter (3.3 feet) high, accommodating 39 mature trees. This planted perimeter acts as both a spatial boundary and an environmental buffer, moderating the localized microclimate through natural shading and air circulation. Engineered for seasonal disassembly ahead of autumn and winter flood conditions, the pontoons, decking modules, and segmented planters are designed to be broken down, transported, and stored, with the root systems maintained in municipal nurseries until reassembly. Originally proposed with municipal backing in 2024 and supported by Heritage Superintendent Antonella Ranaldi in early 2026 before municipal plans paused, the proposal articulates a circular, low-impact strategy for re-integrating the historic riverfront into the civic fabric of Florence.
